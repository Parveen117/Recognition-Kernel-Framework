# Native operator mathematics: development sequence

Monty Dabas | Active canonical programme | 26 September 2026

## Current mathematical advance: version 0.5

The new chapter `theory/WEIGHTED_OPERATOR_COMPLETION.md` links seven theorem
families, rather than adding another parallel engine:

1. **WC1:** finite weighted rewrite contracts give a complete normal-word algebra
   and a faithful regular action, even when its basis is infinite.
2. **WC2:** compatible native dagger extends; products and functional calculus
   have explicit tail bounds and ordered noncommutative derivatives.
3. **WC3:** two exact residuals certify a true inverse and its error in the
   completion, including power-sharpened cases.
4. **WC4:** perturbations of an operator have certified inverse and derivative
   bounds without an eigenvalue or positive-pairing premise.
5. **WC5:** hidden-sector elimination retains both returning memory and the
   hidden source, and is transitive when the required pivots exist.
6. **WC6:** imperfect hidden/visible solves produce a certified full response
   with separate residual, memory-tail and hidden-source error contributions.
7. **WC7:** noncommuting quadratic operator equations have a constructive
   existence, uniqueness-in-ball and residual-error theorem under checked bounds.

These are written results under specified assumptions. Exact finite certificates
support their implementation, not the other way around. Norm completion,
Neumann series, Schur elimination and contraction are established methods; the
advance here is their explicit, reusable integration with native normal forms,
retained memory and target contracts.

## Next mathematical obligations, in dependency order

**A. Derivations and unbounded actions on a native regularity scale.** Starting
with WC1 weights, construct stronger weighted domains, prove derivative action
bounds between levels, closability and composition on a shared core. Use those
theorems before asserting a differential generator or a continuum limit. A
failed bounded-weight contract is not a rejection of an unbounded native action.

**B. Multi-object completion and intertwiners.** Extend weighted normal forms
from one object to typed finite/infinite object families with local units,
admissible aperture exhaustion and uniform action estimates. Keep the identity
at each object and the integer residue fibre; do not flatten them into one
untyped matrix alphabet.

**C. Spectral separation with target witnesses.** Combine local resolvent
certificates with algebraic/jet data and declared marker banks. Prove which
operator changes remain invisible to each readout. Add conditions for global
spectral continuation only after local invertibility, contour/domain and
uniform tail controls are present.

**D. Operator-family selection for the physics targets.** Bind one source family,
its native propagation and a calibrated target to the common mathematics.
Source amplitude, physical length, time and probe coupling are separate inputs
or derived quantities with explicit proofs. Validate a predeclared observable
without changing the presentation to fit its answer.

## Advancement criteria

A stronger operator tool solves a larger specified class, preserves more
load-bearing native information, needs fewer unproved adapters, or delivers
sharper certified error bounds. More files, more names, or a larger test count
alone do not meet those criteria.

Every next theorem therefore needs a statement, full proof, source dependency,
constructive consequence, a failure witness for at least one dropped assumption,
and an example that could not be handled by the previous release. The current
infinite normal-word inverse and nonlinear ball certificate provide such
examples for the 0.4-to-0.5 step.
