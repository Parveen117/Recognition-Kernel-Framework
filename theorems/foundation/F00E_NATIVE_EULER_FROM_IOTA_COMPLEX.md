## 2. Factorial approximants

For `z in C_Sigma`, define the finite native factorial polynomial

\[
E_{\Sigma,N}(z)
=
\sum_{n=0}^{N}\frac{z^n}{n!}.
\]

The coefficients `1/n!` belong to the radial rational subfield already embedded
in `R_Sigma`.

### Lemma 2.1 — Native factorial-tail convergence

For every `z in C_Sigma`, the sequence

\[
\bigl(E_{\Sigma,N}(z)\bigr)_{N\ge0}
\]

is Cauchy in `C_Sigma`.

### Proof

Choose a natural number `m` such that

\[
m>2|z|_\Sigma.
\]

For `k >= n+1 >= m`, multiplicativity of the cut-complex norm gives

\[
\frac{|z|_\Sigma^k}{k!}
=
\frac{|z|_\Sigma^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}
\prod_{j=n+2}^{k}\frac{|z|_\Sigma}{j}.
\]

Every factor in the product is at most `1/2`. Therefore

\[
\sum_{k=n+1}^{\infty}\frac{|z|_\Sigma^k}{k!}
\le
2\frac{|z|_\Sigma^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}.
\]

The last expression tends to zero because, from index `m` onward, successive
terms decrease by at least a factor `1/2`. Thus every factorial tail is native
Cauchy. Completeness of `C_Sigma` supplies the limit. ∎

### Definition 2.2 — Native Euler exponential

\[
\boxed{

=
\lim_{N\to\infty}E_{\Sigma,N}(z).
}
\]

This is the first appearance of an exponential object. It is generated by

## 3. Exponential group law

### Theorem 3.1 — Native addition law

For all `z,w in C_Sigma`,

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Exp}_\Sigma(z+w)
=
\operatorname{Exp}_\Sigma(z)
\operatorname{Exp}_\Sigma(w).
}
\]

### Proof

The factorial series are absolutely controlled by the native radial majorants
proved above, so their Cauchy product is lawful. Grouping the product by total
degree gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Exp}_\Sigma(z)
\operatorname{Exp}_\Sigma(w)
&=
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}
\sum_{k=0}^{n}
\frac{z^kw^{n-k}}{k!(n-k)!}\\
&=
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}
\frac{1}{n!}
\sum_{k=0}^{n}
\binom{n}{k}z^kw^{n-k}\\
&=
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(z+w)^n}{n!}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The finite identity consumed in the second line is the binomial theorem in the
commutative cut-complex field. The final series is the definition of
`Exp_Sigma(z+w)`. ∎

### Corollary 3.2 — Unit, inverse and nonvanishing

### Corollary 3.3 — Dagger covariance

### Proof

## 4. Native derivative and the Euler equation

The derivative is now defined inside the completed field. It is not imported as

### Lemma 4.2 — Unit tangent of the exponential

\[
\lim_{h\to0}
\frac{\operatorname{Exp}_\Sigma(h)-1}{h}

### Proof

For nonzero `h`,

\[
\frac{\operatorname{Exp}_\Sigma(h)-1}{h}

The tail is bounded by a factorial majorant tending to zero with `|h|_Sigma`.

### Theorem 4.3 — Native Euler differential equation

\[
\boxed{
\partial_\Sigma\operatorname{Exp}_\Sigma(z)
=
\operatorname{Exp}_\Sigma(z),
\qquad
\operatorname{Exp}_\Sigma(0)=1.

### Proof

By the addition law,

\[
\frac{\operatorname{Exp}_\Sigma(z+h)-
      \operatorname{Exp}_\Sigma(z)}{h}
=
\operatorname{Exp}_\Sigma(z)
\frac{\operatorname{Exp}_\Sigma(h)-1}{h}.

Apply Lemma 4.2. ∎

### Theorem 4.4 — Native analytic uniqueness

`Exp_Sigma` is the unique native power-series solution of

\[
\partial_\Sigma F=F,
\qquad
F(0)=1.
\]

### Proof

Therefore `F=Exp_Sigma`. ∎
