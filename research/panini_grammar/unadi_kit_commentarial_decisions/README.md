# PG194 · Why kuṭaru carries k and what remains open in kīnāśa

Monty · 11 November 2026

**One of the two outstanding marker questions now has a traditional answer.** At 4.80, PG181 found that the kuṭādi treatment of the root already prevents ordinary guṇa locally. Śvetavanavāsin explicitly draws this comparison and explains the repeated marking as a *jñāpaka*: it indicates that the inherited ṅit treatment does not apply invariably. Nārāyaṇa supplies the same kind of argument in verse and points to guṇa in other words, including *koṭara* and *koṭīra*. The local *u* in *kuṭaru* and the *o* elsewhere play different parts in his explanation.

The lexical witnesses also diverge. Ujjvaladatta glosses a cloth dwelling. Nārāyaṇa gives a bird, and Śvetavanavāsin gives a bird and a carpenter. The register preserves the lemma and the marker argument with each source’s own sense.

**At 5.56 the effect of k remains open.** Śvetavanavāsin includes both *kliśū vibādhane* and *kliśa upatāpe*, since the sūtra supplies no distinguishing condition. This broadens the earlier Ujjvaladatta reading on that commentator’s account. He refers to the function of kit in general. His specific statement about placement after the final vowel belongs to the **m** of the augment *nām* under 1.1.47. Nārāyaṇa independently gives the long ī, the loss of l and the augment in the Yama reading. These operations can be recorded while the contribution unique to k is still unknown.

The primary numbers correspond to Śvetavanavāsin 4.84 and 5.63, and to Nārāyaṇa 4.85 and 5.68. The complete reprint supplies printed p. 136 for the latter passage. No additional finite or nominal positions are credited.
