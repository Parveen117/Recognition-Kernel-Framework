# Reading the interchange

Each entity ID is local to its frame. An implicit entity has an unfilled lexical identity; its absence from the written text is retained. Case belongs to an event-role edge at its expression site. A null case means no separate nominal case is asserted on that edge. A participant can recur on another event with a different role. The agreement entry links a finite event to its controlling participant. The source-row array records direct evidence for the particular edge; contextual roles complete only the bounded example.

The schema checks shape, required fields, case labels and agreement slots. A second document check verifies reference integrity, uniqueness of IDs, the agreement-controller edge and source-case consistency. These are inspections of written example records. A grammar analyzer that accepts raw sentences, builds attachments or selects role edges is a downstream component.

## Two compositions

In the promise, the prior actor and the current dative share one entity ID and the first event precedes the second. In the causative, the instigator bears simultaneous hetu and kartṛ titles and the outer event causes the inner event. Event order in the former does not automatically establish causation; the formal link types differ.

The inherited carrier is extended by labelled event, participant, agreement and expression edges. Its ordinary projection retains entity identity, case and event incidence while forgetting construction history. On each exhibited frame, projecting after joining by entity ID gives the same incidence relation as joining the projected, still-identified edges. This follows by literal equality of the matching endpoint IDs. A projection that erases the IDs before joining can identify the cow with its yield or the causer with the maker; the ordinary projection specified here preserves them. Inverse incidence gives all compatible input histories in the displayed domain, with no imposed probabilities or physical clock.
