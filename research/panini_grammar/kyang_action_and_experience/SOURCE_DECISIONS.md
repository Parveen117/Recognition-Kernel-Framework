# Source decisions

## H1. The jaw-motion condition

3.1.15 pairs romantha with turning/chewing and tapas with practicing. For romantha the source adds the condition of moving the jaws in rumination. Kāśikā gives the cow example and rejects the insect in the cited other sense. Kaumudī reports more than one interpretation of the insect’s physical action; the selected positive condition remains rumination.

## H2. Tapas has its own pada rule

The vārttika at 3.1.15 gives tapasya-ti. Bālamanoramā explicitly describes P as overriding the A otherwise attracted by ṅ. Nyāsa offers its rationale and likewise excludes default A on this derivation. Morphology retains kyaṅ and the nominal s, while pada is P. This gives the complementary marker/ending diagnostic to PG144’s kyac and A on citraṅ.

## H3. Emit the incorporated material

3.1.16 names bāṣpa and ūṣma for expelling/emitting that material; the phena extension is specifically a vārttika. The emitted thing is the incorporated nominal input and the emitter controls the verb. The root label ūṣma follows the source field; no independently supplied n-final nominative is substituted.

## H4. Production is an action, not an instrumental case

3.1.17 names śabda, vaira, kalaha, abhra, kaṇva and megha in the karoti sense; its exposition also names the three selected day/weather extensions. Distinguish the action meaning of karaṇe here from the 1.4.42 grammatical designation of the most effective means. Kaumudī also records a separate ṇic formation on śabda. Only the kyaṅ formation is selected here.

## H5. Experience belongs to the subject

3.1.18 gives sukhāyate and duḥkhāyate when the experiencer is the one to whom the pleasure or pain belongs. Its negative example involves another person’s pleasure. Merely seeing or describing someone else’s feeling does not instantiate the self-related experience frame. This certificate is separate from the desire relation of 3.1.8.
