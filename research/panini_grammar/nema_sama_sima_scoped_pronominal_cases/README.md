## Three different lexical decisions

*Nema* denotes a half or one of two portions in the Śabdakaustubha's discussion of 1.1.27. Ṛgveda 10.48.9 puts the locative and the accusative in the same line. Its word text isolates *némasmin* and *némam*. Preserve the locative final n across the continuous-text junction. The word address here belongs to the first token. The accusative corroborates the lexical identification and earns no new position. For M nominative plural, 1.1.33 inherits optionality specifically before *jas* from 1.1.32. On the pronominal path 7.1.17 substitutes śī for jas and yields *neme*. The ordinary path retains jas and yields pause *nemāḥ*. The two paths share one input. Outside jas this decision gives no ordinary alternative to the selected dative *nemasmai*. The middle perfect *neme* belonging to √nam has a separate finite history.

*Sama* in the sense of all has the pronominal licence. In the refrain at Ṛgveda 8.40.1 the plural *same* goes with *anyake*. The number of recurrences is immaterial to the grammatical position. The Kāśikā restricts the class to the synonym of *sarva*. In 1.3.10 the genitive plural *samānām* concerns equal or corresponding sets. The level-ground expression with locative *same* supplies another ordinary-adjective control. Neither receives the pronominal ending on its equality or level sense. The spelling *same* alone cannot decide between that locative and the all-inclusive plural. This decision supplies no generic licence for compounds or proper names.

*Sima* has a positive whole/all use. In RV 1.115.4 night spreads a garment, with a recipient in the dative. RV 1.95.7 has the ablative as the source from which something bright is taken. The commentary glosses the latter by reference to the totality of beings. The continuous junction before *navā* shows nasal assimilation. The word register restores ablative *simasmāt*, independently quoted in the grammatical commentary. Keep the Nighaṇṭu attribution concerning capability and bounded scope as a separate semantic observation. Which other occurrences instantiate those readings remains Open. The Śabdakaustubha's discussion of an Atharvan accent condition does not assign that accent throughout the paradigm.
