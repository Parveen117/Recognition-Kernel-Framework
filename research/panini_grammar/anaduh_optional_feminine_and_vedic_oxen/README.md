# PG242 · Two feminine draught-animal stems and the Ṛgvedic strong vowel

Monty · 12 November 2026

The Bhāṣya gives two feminine forms for the draught animal: **anaḍuhī** and **anaḍvāhī**. Its addition makes *ām* optional in the feminine. The Kāśikā connects the feminine suffix with *gaurādi*. The ordinary masculine has *anaḍvān* in the nominative and *anaḍvan* when directly addressed.

| Formation | Nominative singular | Nominative dual | Nominative plural |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Masculine animal | anaḍvān | anaḍvāhau | anaḍvāhaḥ |
| Feminine animal | anaḍuhī / anaḍvāhī | anaḍuhyau / anaḍvāhyau | anaḍuhyaḥ / anaḍvāhyaḥ |
| Neuter *svanaḍuh*, a possessor of fine oxen | svanaḍut (also final *-d* in the archive) | svanaḍuhī | svanaḍvāṃhi |

The **ī** at the end of *svanaḍuhī* belongs to the neuter dual. It has a different source from the ī of the feminine singular. The Prakriyāsarvasva works through all three neuter nominatives, including *num* in the plural.

The Zürich Ṛgveda corpus has three tokens under *anaḍváh-*. The strong vowel bears transmitted acute in **anaḍvā́ham** (10.59.10b) and **anaḍvā́hau** (10.85.10c). Its locative plural **anaḷútsu** (3.53.18b) differs from ordinary *anaḍutsu* even after accent is removed. The three source words occupy existing case positions.

The three gendered profiles add **72 nominal positions and 99 readings**. The paired feminine alternatives share the same 24 requests.
