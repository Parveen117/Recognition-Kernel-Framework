## H07. Perfect boundary readings

Each primary root factor has ṃ/ñ before kṛ, ṃ/m before bhū
and m before as. Each P1sg kṛ length alternative retains both
nasal readings. Named A therefore has 45 readings per profile;
ordinary P, with two root factors, has 94. Across six A and
two P profiles perfect gives **458 readings/paths on 72 keys**.

Auxiliary path counts are kṛ 188, bhū 180, as 90. Their
root/preverb source remains the primary's source; no primary
prefix is reassigned to the auxiliary.

## H08. Caṅ factors and all nine secondary endings

Use līlap for every named A and the ā/puk P branch; use
līlay for the ordinary retained-ī P branch. The pinned thematic
secondary tuples are

\[
P=(at,atām,an,aḥ,atam,ata,am,āva,āma),\quad
A=(ata,etām,anta,athāḥ,ethām,adhvam,e,āvahi,āmahi).
\]

Caṅ a intervenes before tiṅ, so these are not direct-abhyasta
plural endings. A2pl dental dh follows a and has no additional
retroflex alternative. Prefix/aṭ decisions are applied to the
whole copied constituent, never to an already printed present stem.

## H09. Final words and local multiplicities

Representative 3sg words are ālīlapata for honour,
udalīlapata for the two ud-prefixed named A frames, and
udalīlapat/udalīlayat for ordinary P. All aorist paths have
one primary long-ī copy, ṇic-lopa, aṭ and no outer iṭ.
There are **90 readings/paths on 72 aorist keys**.

Across both labels the 144 cells partition into 54 singleton,
18 doubleton, 54 five-word, 16 ten-word and two fourteen-word
cells, giving **548 readings/paths**. Their repeated words remain
labelled by lexical and sense identity.
