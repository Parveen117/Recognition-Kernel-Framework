# PG213 · Three future schedules for the twelve Uṇādi-related root entries

Monty · 12 November 2026

The twelve Dhātupāṭha entries treated in PG206 now have **405 new finite positions** in *luṭ*, *lṛṭ* and *lṛṅ*. With the earlier present-system forms, **945 of 1,350** positions are written for these fifteen root–pada profiles across the ten declared lakāras.

| Root or pair | *Luṭ* 3sg | *Lṛṭ* 3sg | Condition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| *nam*, bow or sound | nantā | naṃsyati | Aniṭ. Root-final m stands before dental t or the s of *sya*. |
| *ṣam* 01.0963 and divādi *sam* 04.0132 | samitā | samiṣyati | Seṭ places i after root-final m. The two roots coincide here; only the latter has present-system śyan. |
| Both *styai* teaching forms | styātā | styāsyati | Aniṭ; final ai gives ā before these non-śit affixes. |
| Long *māh* and short *mah* | māhitā / mahitā | māhiṣyati / mahiṣyati | The inherited radical vowel remains visible before iṭ. |
| Curādi *naṭ*, causative formation | nāṭayitā | nāṭayiṣyati | The inherited ṇic gives ay before the future iṭ. |

The Bālamanoramā’s printed entry for *ṇam* gives **nantā**. Under 8.3.24 the root-final nasal becomes anusvāra before a consonant; 8.4.58 then yields dental *n* before *t*. The following *s* of the other future class leaves the anusvāra, giving *naṃsyati* and conditional *anaṃsyat*. This difference is visible even within a single root and person. The *sam* roots supply a control in which an intervening iṭ keeps their root-final *m*.

Each of the 405 selected words occurs in the pinned comparison archive. It includes eleven additional voiced *-d* spellings in active conditional third singular, giving 416 archive readings. The written word-final selection is *-t*. The three remaining lakāras for these roots, āśīrliṅ, liṭ and luṅ, have 405 requests in total.
