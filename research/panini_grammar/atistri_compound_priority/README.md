# PG204 · Rule priority in the three genders of atistri

Monty · 12 November 2026

*Ati + strī* ‘one who or that which has gone beyond a woman’ shows how the same lexical word inflects when it becomes subordinate in a compound. The ending of the inner word shortens. The outer gender can be masculine, feminine or neuter. The source archive supplies all three paradigms.

| Outer gender | Nominative plural | Accusative singular | Accusative plural | Dative singular |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Masculine | *atistrayaḥ* | *atistriyam / atistrim* | *atistriyaḥ / atistrīn* | *atistraye* |
| Feminine | *atistrayaḥ* | *atistriyam / atistrim* | *atistriyaḥ / atistrīḥ* | *atistraye / atistriyai* |
| Neuter | *atistrīṇi* | *atistri* | *atistrīṇi* | *atistriṇe / atistraye* |

### Four case readings differ from the archive

The archived masculine and feminine tables give **atistriyaḥ** for nominative and vocative plural. The Bālamanoramā explicitly derives masculine **atistrayaḥ**. Guṇa before *jas* under 7.3.109 produces *e*, which becomes *ay* before a vowel under 6.1.78. It takes priority over *iyaṅ*. The digitized Kaumudī’s prose itself has *atistriyaḥ* after *jasi ca*, but gives *he atistrayaḥ* for plural address. Its verse limits the domain of *iyaṅ*. The Kaumudī says the feminine generally follows the masculine here and specifies different treatment in the accusative plural, instrumental and certain obliques. The feminine nominative and vocative plural in the table follow this rule application. In the accusative plural **atistriyaḥ** retains its independent, explicit licence from *śas*.

These decisions give **72 positions and 87 readings**. The archive and the selected account agree at 68 positions. Each of the other four has its own source comparison. Counting both source policies yields 91 position–form pairs. Only the selected 87 occur in the grammatical table.

The Kāśikā on 7.1.73 and 7.1.74 explains the neuter *num* and the optional masculine treatment of compatible obliques. Thus *atistriṇī* is neuter direct dual, while *atistriyau* belongs to the masculine and feminine. The three genders all give genitive plural *atistrīṇām*. The inner lexical meaning alone cannot select the requested outer form.
