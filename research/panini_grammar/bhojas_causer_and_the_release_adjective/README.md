# PG983 · What is the causer in Bhoja’s example?

Bhoja places *nakhāni mocayanti nakhamucāni dhanūṃṣi* under **hetu** in the second prakāśa. The page first divides kartṛ into independent actor, causer and self-agent. Immediately after the bow example it starts a separate paragraph for the self-agent. Thus the finite plural in the bow example belongs to the causer paragraph; the adjacent calf construction of PG981 cannot supply its voice. *Nakhāni* is the internal object of the expansion. The neuter plural bows control the finite plural. The compound agrees with those bows. Mādhava, in contrast, expressly identifies an agent-forming ka of the mūlavibhuja group and explains the bows as outside the fist. Kāśikā 3.2.5 includes this very example in the supplementary list. These accounts answer different questions about the same compound. [Dvivedī edition, I, p. 73, PDF leaf 176](https://archive.org/details/sringaraprakasabhojarewaprasadadwivedisantanasadashivakumaradwivedivol1ignca/page/n175/mode/1up) · [Mādhavīya, Benares p. 345](https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.369319/page/n363/mode/1up) · [Kāśikā source register](../compound_case_derivation/SOURCE_EXCERPTS.json)

**Finite path.** Start with simple tudādi √muc, release, no preverb, and the expressed inducing participant. Select ṇic in the causative sense under 3.1.26. The root takes guṇa u → o. The ṇic present stem appears as *mocaya-* before the primary parasmaipada endings. There is no present-class nasal of *muñcati* in this derived present. The same input with one, two or several referents gives respectively **mocayati, mocayataḥ, mocayanti**. Only the third plural is read on Bhoja's page. It supports the P choice in this frame. An alternative A or additional result entitlement remains open. The self-agent desiderative of the calf requires san and has a different controller.

**Compound path.** Take the object upapada *nakha* with √muc. The supplementary ka prescription at 3.2.5 licenses the agent derivative on this listed lexical combination. The k marker prevents the ordinary guṇa on this path (1.1.5); the suffix contributes a. Internal case loss yields *nakha-muc-a*. Monier-Williams 102965 independently records the noun as neuter. The finite paraphrase with ṇic has o, while the lexical compound retains u. No surface *-aya-* enters the compound, and the causer paraphrase by itself cannot decide its entire pre-deletion history. The printed plural is syncretic in isolation. Its role in Bhoja's expansion supports nominative here. Reserve that plural from new credit because the Kāśikā excerpt already displays it. **The other two agreement paradigms of amocya.** MW entry 14345 marks all three genders for the privative adjective which PG982 located in Raghu’s judgment about the horse. Carry PG982's simple √muc + ṇyat, the necessity restriction on velar substitution under 7.3.65 and the outer nañ into the feminine and neuter. Feminine *amocyā* has ṭāp. Neuter *amocyam* lacks it. The latter coincides with the poem’s masculine accusative but carries its own gender and case. Both additional paradigms are lexical extensions. The verse attests only the masculine context.
