# PG957 · The rope and the lion under kurac

Under 3.2.162 the Kāśikā gives *chidurā rajjuḥ*, a rope disposed to break. The rope is the thing that undergoes cutting in the corresponding transitive event. In the description of its disposition the rope itself controls the feminine nominative singular. The kṛt register owns that example. The Mādhavīyadhātuvṛtti at √chid, entry 1041, reports the vṛtti's karmakartṛ interpretation, then quotes a lion described as **करीन्द्रदर्पच्छिदुरं मृगेन्द्रं**. It attributes to Ātreya the judgment that the vṛtti's statement is usual (*prāyikam*). The quoted lion breaks an elephant lord's pride. Here lion and what gets broken are different participants. This is positive evidence for a second reading of the derivative under a named commentary policy.
