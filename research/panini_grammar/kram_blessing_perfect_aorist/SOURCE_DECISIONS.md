## H02. Perfect iṭ is an affix-specific query

Kāśikā and Siddhāntakaumudī **7.2.13** restrict the liṭ aniṭ class to the named
krādi roots, subject to separately specified exceptions. SK explicitly states
that the restriction covers both root-dependent and affix-dependent iṭ
prohibitions. Kram is not in that named class. Applying that perfect-specific
restriction gives iṭ before its val-initial liṭ endings, including the admitted
A endings. This is the stated application of the source's niyama, not a new
literal quotation of every kram perfect word. Do not carry PG77's root-facing
future prohibition forward as an immutable root tag.

The P **thal** question additionally uses **7.2.61–63**. Bālamanoramā
**7.2.62 directly gives चक्रमिथ** and explains why kram is not nityāniṭ in
tās: despite the A restriction of 7.2.36, its P tās is seṭ. Thus the
7.2.62 no-iṭ premise fails. There is no independent cakrantha option in the
selected P thal position, and 7.2.63 does not create a free iṭ switch when
the prerequisite no-iṭ domain is absent. P va/ma and A se/dhve/vahe/mahe
have mandatory iṭ; vowel-initial liṭ endings do not require that augment.

For āśīrliṅ and luṅ the same perfect-specific restriction is unavailable.
PG77's **7.2.36** source-cause analysis therefore continues to block iṭ in
the admitted basic A paths. Kāśikā quotes **प्रक्रंसीष्ट**, and Nyāsa
explicitly identifies its kartari A licence as 1.3.42. P āśīrliṅ instead
lacks iṭ because yāsuṭ begins with y, outside val; P sic receives iṭ.

## H03. Short a and nasal retention require their own guards

Kāśikā **7.2.5** explicitly prohibits iṭ-initial P sic-vṛddhi after m-final
roots; Bālamanoramā **7.2.36** applies this to **अक्रमीत्**. Thus short a is
mandatory here, not an optional 7.2.7 grade. The long ā of P perfect cakrāma
has a different source, **7.2.116** with ṇal's ṇit; it is not present-system
7.3.76. No śit present vikaraṇa occurs in any new request.

Kram's m follows a, not ik, so **1.2.11** does not give A liṅ/sic kit. Kram
is not the ṛ-final or gam exception of 1.2.12–13. Nor does it satisfy the
anudātta-upadeśa/tanādi domain of **6.4.37**. Root m is retained until the
actual late phonetic query. At liṭ, root-final m is a single consonant: the
initial kr cluster is not the root-final cluster excluded by **1.2.5**.

**8.2.26** explicitly contrasts nasal contacts with its literal
examples **अमंस्त / अमंस्थाः**: a preceding nasal is not jhal. Its exact
application here is that kram's m does not license sic-s deletion before
ta/thās. **8.2.27** also fails because the preceding root/aṅga ends in m,
not a short vowel. **8.2.25** still removes sic-s before dhvam. This leaves
m+dh and gives a different nasal readout at A luṅ 2pl; see H07.
