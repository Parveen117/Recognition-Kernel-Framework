# PG257 · The other four ghu roots in their separate present stems

Monty · 12 November 2026

The Bhāṣya asks why *ghu* must include these roots even before *śit* affixes, where their present stems have visibly changed. It gives **praṇidayate**, **praṇidyati** and **praṇidhayati**. For the fourth root, Maitreya supplies **praṇiyacchati** in an example about a garland. These directly cited forms distinguish all four roots in the present.

| Root and teaching | Meaning | Present after *pra + ni* | Imperfect after *pari + ni* |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| *dheṭ*, 01.1050 | drink or suckle | praṇidhayati | pariṇyadhayat |
| *dāṇ*, 01.1079 | give | praṇiyacchati | pariṇyayacchat |
| *deṅ*, 01.1117 | protect | praṇidayate | pariṇyadayata |
| *do*, 04.0043 | cut or break up | praṇidyati | pariṇyadyat |

The Kāśikā uses a calf and its mother to illustrate the sucking root. Its teaching *dheṭ* differs from the reduplicating *ḍudhāñ*. The cutting root *do* differs from *dāp* in reaping, which the definition expressly excludes. A second excluded root is *daip* in cleansing. The specific middle for *dāṇ* with *saṃ* and the case condition of 1.3.55 belongs to a different request.

There is also a source spelling to preserve. The selected Kāśikā field at 1.1.20 has **prāṇidātā**, while the Bhāṣya at the same address gives **praṇidātā**. The future and its source readings are treated separately in the next packet. Here the four present-system lakāras supply **288 positions and 300 readings** for the two ordered prefix pairs.
