## Designation origin is a source-clause identity

At **1.2.43**, प्रथमानिर्दिष्ट describes the applicable compound rule's
wording. The object member of **कष्टं श्रितः** does not need to appear in
the sentence nominative to receive the designation. Nyāsa makes the compound
relation essential: borrowing an unrelated द्वितीया clause for कुमार्याः
would wrongly designate or shorten a component of राजकुमारी.

At **1.2.44**, the main एकविभक्ति clause excludes the पूर्वनिपात consumer
while retaining a shortening use under **1.2.48**. Nyāsa explicitly asks
what licenses निस् ordering in निष्कौशाम्बि and supplies the **च supplement**.
It rejects the explanation that simply excluding कौशाम्बी from the ordering
designation must itself fix निस् first. B02 therefore keeps two clause
origins within 1.2.44. Neither one untyped उपसर्जन flag nor one origin-sūtra
number is sufficient to represent the source distinction.

The compound's कौशाम्बी ending has feminine-affix provenance. 1.2.48 does
not shorten every apparently feminine word ending in ई; its स्वरितत्व and
उपसर्जन qualifications remain admission evidence. B02's negative ordering
query does not mutate the component sequence, while the separate shortening
query may succeed.

use. Kāśikā contrasts the partial loss of the सीयुट् स्, and distinguishes
an affix-triggered operation from the merely sound-triggered आय्/आव्

deleted by लुक्/श्लु/लुप्. Nyāsa's **गर्गाः** discussion includes both

source's **पञ्च/सप्त/पयः/साम** counterexamples preserve the पद designation
of the suffix-ended community and its proper consequences. In B04 a final
base sound changes through a पद consumer; changing a base sound does not
by itself identify the operation as the blocked अङ्ग effect.

For **कार्यते**, the inner णिच् disappears by ordinary **6.4.51 लोप**,

## Ordinary exclusivity and admitted Vedic dual use

Kāśikā **3.4.115/116** explains why the ordinary perfect and blessing-liṅ
designations are exceptions to the general तिङ् designation rather than
unrestricted coexisting labels. Kaumudī 115 explicitly states the exclusion
of the ordinary sārvadhātuka view. The nonblessing examples in 116 keep the
meaning gate visible; unknown आशिष् evidence cannot certify either branch.

**3.4.117** refers to the entire preceding designation discussion, not just
the nearest liṅ rule. Its examples give consumer-specific consequences:
वर्धन्तु has णि-lopa, स्वस्तये avoids अस् → भू, विशृण्विरे receives
श्नु/शृ, and उपस्थेयाम uses both salopa and etva. B08 takes only the last
attested chain, with an explicit Vedic-source admission. It does not create
a universal Vedic optionality flag or globally turn both designations on for
every suffix. The two named consumers retain the same suffix identity.
