# PG47 — Designation origin, deletion cause and consumer histories

Author: Monty. 4 November 2026.

These eight written histories instantiate the common interaction interface.
Addresses identify token occurrences; `d` identifies a designation record and
`q` its particular consumer. The source clause and policy belong to `d`, not
only its sūtra number. `APPLIED`, `BLOCKED` and `OPEN` describe the named local
query. They do not certify a complete paradigm or an unrestricted Sanskrit
derivation. Inputs described as given remain explicit admission evidence.

Use the full operator/history carrier and its typed zero-reading from the
packet's [native continuation](NATIVE_CONTINUATION.md). A designation query
selects a history coordinate; it does not erase the other coordinates or
identify a deleted suffix with no suffix ever having existed.

## B01 — The compound rule designates the object member

**Sources:** 2.1.24, 1.2.43 and 2.2.30; Kāśikā's **कष्टं श्रितः →
कष्टश्रितः** witness. The admitted frame supplies an accusative member
`a = कष्टम्`, a related श्रित member `b`, and their तत्पुरुष relation.
Their sentence cases are retained separately from the wording of 2.1.24.

|Step|Addressed event or query|Retained result|
|---|---|---|
|1|Interpret the nominative **द्वितीया** in 2.1.24 under 1.2.43|`d(a)` is उपसर्जन relative to this compound relation, origin `1.2.43 / 2.1.24-द्वितीया`|
|2|Ask whether `d(a)` licenses consumer `q = 2.2.30`|`APPLIED`: the designation carries the ordering use|
|3|Commit the component order `a ≺ b`|The compound tree retains both source addresses and the accusative relation; the sentence nominative of `b` does not make `b` the designated member|

This closes the **component-order query**. The quoted source word is the
comparison witness; inflectional deletion, internal phonology, accent and
outer inflection are downstream obligations, not generated by step 3.

**Open control:** if the relation to a श्रित-class member is unknown, the
word द्वितीया in an unrelated rule cannot license the designation. Record
`OPEN(compound-rule relation)` without assigning `d` or changing the order.
Nyāsa's राजकुमारी discussion explains why borrowing a case rule from another
compound relation would give the wrong designation and shortening behavior.

## B02 — One designation can shorten without licensing reordering

**Sources:** 1.2.44, 1.2.48, 2.2.18's निरादि supplement and 2.2.30;
Kāśikā/Nyāsa's **निष्क्रान्तः कौशाम्ब्याः → निष्कौशाम्बिः**.
The supplied compound frame contains `n = निस्` in the departure meaning
and `k = कौशाम्बी`, with the latter fixed in the ablative relation while
the departure expression varies in case.

|Step|Addressed event or query|Retained result|
|---|---|---|
|1|Apply the main एकविभक्ति clause of 1.2.44 to `k`|`dK = उपसर्जन(k, origin=1.2.44/एकविभक्ति, excluded_consumer=पूर्वनिपात)`|
|2|Under the declared Nyāsa policy, use the च supplement for `n`|A separate `dN` records the source's positive ordering support for निस्; it is not inferred from step 1's prohibition|
|3|Query 2.2.30 on `dK`|`BLOCKED`: no order event moving कौशाम्बी first is committed; the source excludes **कौशाम्बीनिर्**|
|4|Query 2.2.30 on `dN`|`APPLIED`: retain `n ≺ k` under this separately recorded supplement|
|5|Query 1.2.48 on the compound ending in feminine-affix-bearing `k`|`APPLIED`: shorten the final ई belonging to the addressed compound ending, giving the local **निस् + कौशाम्बि** stage|

The ordinary label उपसर्जन is shared; its **source-clause origin and consumer
permissions differ**. A record storing only `origin_id = 1.2.44` would still
lose the distinction between the main clause and the च supplement. No
permutation is produced by the failed query in step 3.

The source's prefix junction and final nominative realization remain
downstream from this designation/shortening history. If feminine-affix
provenance or the fixed ablative relation is missing, step 5 or step 1
respectively returns `OPEN`; a final visible ई alone is insufficient.

## B03 — Deleted यञ् does not license the blocked base effect

**Sources:** 4.1.105, 2.4.64, 1.1.62–63, 1.4.13, 7.2.117 and 6.1.197.
Input `g = गर्ग` is supplied as a गर्गादि entry in the admitted masculine
plural गोत्र sense. `y` is its actual यञ् token selected under 4.1.105.
The plural, nonfeminine and गोत्र gates of 2.4.64 are all affirmative.

1. Record `aṅga(g; cause=y)` with the base/affix ownership relation. Select
   the 2.4.64 **लुक्** event on `y`; retain its यञ् origin and mark its
   entire active affix value absent. The deletion cause is `LUK`, not an
   untyped null value.
2. Ask whether 1.1.62 restores the **7.2.117 initial-vowel वृद्धि** query
   on `g` using the deleted `y`. The 1.1.63 exception matches: this is the
   base effect caused by the very affix whose लुमत् deletion is recorded.
   Result: `BLOCKED`; no अ → आ event is committed by that query.
3. Ask the **6.1.197 initial-accent** query using the same deleted affix.
   Nyāsa 1.1.63 and Kāśikā 6.1.197 block it too. Its location outside the
   textual अङ्ग chapter does not make the base effect independent of `y`.

This is a history of **pending consumer queries after admitted लुक्**,
not an instruction to erase or reverse any arbitrary earlier वृद्धि event.
The source comparison form is **गर्गाः**. Outer inflection and the full
accent realization remain downstream obligations. If the deletion record
does not say whether the cause was लुक्, श्लु, लुप् or ordinary लोप, the
exception gate is `?`: return `OPEN(deletion cause)` instead of applying the
1.1.62 default.

## B04 — The same deletion class can preserve a पद consumer

**Sources:** 1.1.24, 7.1.22, 1.1.62–63, 1.4.14 and 8.2.7;
Kāśikā/Nyāsa's **पञ्च** witness. Input has `p = पञ्चन्`, its original
numeral identity and षट् designation, and an actual nominative-plural
`j = जस्` token belonging to this expression.

|Step|Addressed event or query|Retained result|
|---|---|---|
|1|Apply 7.1.22 to `j`|Entire जस् is inactive by लुक्; origin, number/case and owner remain in history|
|2|Use 1.1.62 to query 1.4.14 on the whole `p + j` expression|`APPLIED`: पद designation survives; its bearer is the suffix-ended community, not merely the pre-affix अङ्ग|
|3|Check 1.1.63 against that particular consumer|It does not block this पद designation; this is the explicit अङ्गस्य counterexample in the commentary|
|4|Query 8.2.7 on the designated पद, addressing `p`'s final न्|`APPLIED`: that final न् becomes inactive and the local unaccented readout is **पञ्च**|

An operation can affect a segment inside the base and nevertheless be
licensed as a **पद consumer**, as the source here explicitly establishes.
Testing only “does the output change a base letter?” would incorrectly erase
the distinction from B03. The original numeral stem and deleted जस् remain
recoverable in history after the unaccented readout.

This closes the displayed **पद/n-lopa chain** for the supplied form. Accent
and a complete numeral paradigm remain separate obligations. Missing षट्
origin, actual जस् identity or whole-affix deletion evidence gives `OPEN`
at the corresponding step; it is not inferred from the displayed word पञ्च.

## B05 — Ordinary णि-lopa retains the causative grade relation

**Sources:** 3.1.26/32/67, 3.4.113/114, 6.4.51, 7.2.115 and 1.1.62–63;
the **कार्यते** witness in Kāśikā/Nyāsa 1.1.63.
Supply the admitted causal/passive frame and addresses `r = कृ`,
`c = णिच्`, `y = यक्`, and outer `t = ते`. Marker identities and the
passive ending choice belong to the supplied history.

1. 3.1.26 owns `c` on the root in the causative meaning; 3.1.32 designates
   the resulting aggregate as a derived धातु. The outer `t` has the
   sārvadhātuka view under 3.4.113, supporting the passive यक् selection
   at 3.1.67.
2. `y` is a different suffix token: its own ārdhadhātuka designation under
   3.4.114 and non-इट् environment license **6.4.51 on `c`**. Record its
   deletion as ordinary `LOPA`, not लुक्/श्लु/लुप्. The inner `c`, middle
   `y`, and outer `t` are not one designation bearer.
3. Query the causative णित्-dependent grade relation of 7.2.115 on `r`
   through deleted `c`. 1.1.62 supports it; 1.1.63's लुमत् gate is false.
   Result: `APPLIED`, retaining the addressed **कृ → कार्** relation and
   its causative affix cause. With the supplied `य` and `ते` readouts the
   displayed local assembly is **कार्यते**.

Step 3 is the source's **retained grade relation** after ordinary deletion.
It does not require reapplying a grade operation already recorded earlier;
the query may recognize an existing grade event with the same cause. Full
causative admission, marker preprocessing, accent and all other paradigm
members are outside the closed local query. If `c` is missing from the
history, a superficially similar कार् string cannot manufacture its cause;
the query returns `OPEN(causative provenance)`.

## B06 — Ordinary perfect designation is exclusive for its consumer

**Sources:** 3.4.113–115 and 6.4.64; **जग्ले** in Kāśikā/Nyāsa.
Supply a classical perfect history with an admitted reduplicated
आ-final base at address `g` and an adjacent vowel-initial, कित् ending `e`
whose origin is a लिट् substitute. The upstream reduplication and ending
selection are supplied evidence rather than rederived here.

1. The general तिङ् designation at 3.4.113 is a candidate for `e`.
   3.4.115 is the specific लिट् clause. Kāśikā's एव continuation and
   Kaumudī's explicit formulation select **ārdhadhātuka only** in this
   ordinary domain; do not store simultaneous unrestricted global labels.
2. Consumer **6.4.64** asks for the ārdhadhātuka, vowel-initial, क्ङित्
   suffix view on `e` and final आ on `g`. With the supplied evidence,
   its deletion event addresses that आ, not the ending or the reduplicant.
   Result: `APPLIED`, consistent with the source's **जग्ले** witness.
3. A request to invoke ordinary sārvadhātuka-conditioned विकरण behavior
   merely because `e` is तिङ् is `BLOCKED` by the declared exclusive view.

The local conclusion is the **designation/आ-lopa query**. It does not
establish all perfect forms, marker inferences or accent. If only a surface
ए is supplied without its लिट् origin, return `OPEN(ending provenance)`;
surface equality cannot select 3.4.115.

## B07 — Blessing and ordinary liṅ need different designation records

**Sources:** 3.4.113, 3.4.116 and 7.2.79. Kāśikā supplies
**लविषीष्ट** as the blessing witness and **लुनीयात्** as the nonblessing
control. The two inputs contain distinct affix addresses and supplied
meaning/pada histories; they are not interchangeable surface spellings.

|Input and query|Designation event|Consumer result|
|---|---|---|
|`lA`: admitted ordinary-language liṅ in आशिष्|3.4.116 assigns exclusive ārdhadhātuka status to the liṅ suffix|The sārvadhātuka prerequisite of 7.2.79 is false: `BLOCKED` for its nonfinal-स् deletion query|
|`lO`: ordinary nonblessing liṅ from the source control|3.4.116 is inapplicable; the otherwise admitted तिङ् view follows 3.4.113|With the relevant यासुट्/सुट्/सीयुट् internal स् addressed, the 7.2.79 query is `APPLIED`|
|`l?`: liṅ with its आशिष् evidence missing|The specific-exception gate is `?`|`OPEN(meaning class)`; neither the exclusive blessing view nor the residual ordinary view is certified|

Only the **designation and named salopa consumer** close here. The printed
forms are source witnesses, not newly generated complete endings. In
particular, Nyāsa 3.4.116 has a printed-form note and a थास् explanation;
the present history does not silently resolve that ending-level material.
Augment insertion, all other ending operations, word realization and accent
remain separately owned obligations.

## B08 — Vedic liṅ carries two consumer views in one retained history

**Sources:** 3.4.117, 3.4.103, 7.2.79 and **6.4.67**, with its authority
6.4.46 and inherited conditions from 6.4.66. Kāśikā/Nyāsa's admitted
Vedic witness is **उपस्थेयाम**. Supply `u = उप`, `s = स्था`, and a
P-liṅ ending history `l` with its यासुट् and मस् components. The Vedic
attestation and source policy are positive admission evidence.

|Step|Addressed event or query|Retained result|
|---|---|---|
|1|Record 3.4.117's locally admitted उभयथा behavior on `l`|Both consumer views retain the same suffix origin; the rule is not restricted to the immediately preceding blessing liṅ clause|
|2|Use the sārvadhātuka view for 7.2.79|`APPLIED`: delete the addressed nonfinal स् of the liṅ augment; do not delete a final स् merely by this rule|
|3|Use the ārdhadhātuka view for 6.4.67 with the supplied consonant-initial क्ङित् liṅ conditions|`APPLIED`: the addressed स्था base receives **ए**, consistent with **स्थेयाम**; this does not rewrite another affix token|
|4|Retain both results and their different consumer evidence|The local outcome contains both salopa and etva in one history; choosing one global designation would lose one of the source's required effects|

The claim closes the **two designation-consumer queries**, not every step of
the accented Vedic word. Ending preparation/final-स् treatment, prefix
junction and accent are downstream obligations. The visible witness does
not allow arbitrary Vedic clauses to acquire every effect of both classes.
If the register or source-qualified attestation is unknown, return
`OPEN(Vedic admission)` before either exceptional consumer view is committed.

Nyāsa 3.4.117 literally cites **एतेर्लिङि 7.4.24** for its etva discussion.
That reference addresses prefixed **इण् shortening**, not स्था → स्थे.
The operative consumer here is **एर्लिङि 6.4.67**, verified against the
separate sūtra and Kāśikā/Kaumudī/Nyāsa fields. The erroneous reference is
preserved in the literal archive and explained in [source notes B](SOURCE_NOTES_B.md).
