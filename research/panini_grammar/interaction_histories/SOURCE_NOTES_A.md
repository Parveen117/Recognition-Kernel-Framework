Author: Monty. 4 November 2026.

The current scope is **31 Kāśikā, eleven Kaumudī and ten Nyāsa fields**,

## Already fixed relations are not overwritten

Kāśikā 1.1.49 and Kaumudī restrict the substitution interpretation to an
otherwise undetermined genitive relation. Nyāsa makes the restriction concrete
with उपधाया गोहः: the latter relation identifies the penultimate as a part
of the named root. Its interpretation is already determined. Reading every
genitive as replacement would instead misdirect the operation to the whole
root or, through the usual final-sound rule, to its ह्. A01 preserves the
part–whole relation and does not use 1.1.49 to erase the explicit उपधा target.

Kāśikā 6.4.89 independently confirms the penultimate target, the vowel-initial
affix restriction and the source-specific गोह्-form domain. Its other
realization and asiddha discussions remain beyond A01's relation control.

## Replacement length, marker identity and specified extent

Kāśikā 1.1.55 uses भू and शि as different reasons for whole replacement.
Nyāsa explicitly says an इत् marker does not itself create the relevant
अनेकाल् status. Thus the surviving single इ of शि still needs its
retained शित् reason; it cannot be explained by counting a deleted श् as
an ordinary output segment. A02 contrasts this with the final इ under
1.2.50 in its source-admitted गोणी formation.

For 1.1.53 and 6.3.25, the actual first-member ऋ is replaced by आनङ्;
the ङ् specifies the final-sound domain. Nyāsa 6.3.25 states both that the
न् subsequently undergoes 8.2.7 and that its presence prevents र्
accompaniment. This supports A03's retained आन् history before n-lopa.
The archived Nyāsa field contains damaged question marks and an incorrect
embedded 1.1.50 reference for उरण् रपरः; the archive is not corrected.
The local argument and the cited text, not that damaged number, support the
stated n-retention decision.

तातङ् is an express limit to generalizing A03. Kāśikā and Nyāsa 1.1.53
explain its different ङित् function and the competing rules' available
domains before whole replacement is selected. PG47 does not encode one
unqualified marker-priority flag from the आनङ् example.

## Spans, direction and inherited authorities

Nyāsa 1.1.64 treats the last vowel as the beginning of the टि span,
including subsequent sounds. Nyāsa 1.1.65 restricts उपधा to the single
sound immediately before the final sound. A04 retains this distinction even
when the two addresses begin at the same vowel. A source-provided word is
not by itself an analysis of its sound segmentation or compound boundaries.
The single-vowel case invokes 1.1.21's explicit transfer of initial/final
behavior rather than requiring a fictitious neighboring vowel.

At 1.1.66, Nyāsa explains why adjacency applies even where only one
plausible इक् target would otherwise be found. The अग्निचिदत्र control
therefore blocks replacement across an intervening consonant. It is not
merely an instruction to choose the nearest match among distant candidates.
A05 uses the source's दध्युदकम्, where both adjoining vowels belong to
इक् and direction is directly material.

At 1.1.67, Nyāsa retains the inherited adjacency condition and distinguishes
several cases where locative and ablative instructions coexist. Its discussion
of 7.2.82/83 supports the target interpretation in A06. The closing
सार्वधातुके यक् example expressly prevents converting every inherited
धातोः into a replacement-owner relation: the affix-after-root authority and
marker functions constrain that interpretation. The present cases therefore
do not define a general case-ending parser from one syntactic template.

Kāśikā and Nyāsa 1.1.54 explain the initial आन replacement after आस्.
The latter also supplies its laṭ/शानच्/adādi/śap-luk history. A06 names
those as admitted prerequisites, so आस् is not confused with A01's अस्.
The separate 8.1.28 instruction has its own whole-word अनुदात्त scope
from 8.1.18. Direction and replacement extent remain separate fields;
1.1.54 cannot erase that inherited सर्वम् authority.

## Admission precedes substitute comparison

Nyāsa 1.1.50 is explicit that another prescription must admit the substitute
family. Otherwise दधि/मधु could trigger endless unnecessary replacements
merely through likeness. A07 therefore records the 7.3.52 family before
selecting क् for च्. The comparison is source-qualified, with phonetic
features supplied as evidence. Missing features yield Open rather than a
tie broken by list order.

The same source separately treats semantic likeness, duration and place
priority when likeness dimensions compete. Those domains are not replaced
by this one consonant example or a numerical similarity score.

These are written address and dependency histories. No generator, grammar
test, certificate replay, navigation suite or operator suite supplied their
results. Complete parsing, lexical admission, full word/accent realization
and unrestricted conflict scheduling remain downstream work.
