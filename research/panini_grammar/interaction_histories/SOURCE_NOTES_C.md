## Equal strength is a supplied relation

Kāśikā 1.4.2 expressly gives वृक्षेभ्यः/प्लक्षेभ्यः as the collision of
7.3.102 and 103, and gives their independent domains वृक्षाभ्याम् and
वृक्षेषु. Its statement excludes general/exception, नित्य/अनित्य and
internal/external unequal-strength pairs from this tie-break. The written
interface therefore retains the source judgment instead of deriving it from
rule-number order or from identical output addresses alone.

The भिस् example makes the address point concrete: 7.1.9 replaces the affix,
whereas 7.3.103 changes the stem vowel. Their dependency conflict survives
that difference. Kāśikā 7.1.9 explicitly explains its **भूतपूर्व** basis
for being available even after proposed ए substitution. It does not license
every historical sound to satisfy every present predicate. Kaumudī identifies
the ऐस् replacement as सर्वादेश because of अनेकाल्.

Nyāsa 1.4.2 and 7.2.8 make **ईश्वरः** an explicit example of the earlier
specific prohibition defeating the later general इट् prescription. The
occasion is वश्-initial कृत्, with वरच् independently supplied by 3.2.175.
The general rule's admitted domain is not enough to admit its action after
the decisive prohibition is known. Kāśikā's ईशिता/ईशितुम् controls prevent
extending the local result to every suffix after ईश्.

## Continuation policies remain distinct

Nyāsa 1.4.2 distinguishes जाति and द्रव्य readings. On the former, a
previously competing operation may act if it becomes applicable after the
later one; on the latter, the operation defeated in that conflict remains
defeated. The source does not turn the distinction into a free optional
choice at every step, nor does this field provide a worked pair of divergent
surface words for the two policies.

C02 consequently records both continuations of the same वृक्षे history.
The जाति path performs a fresh sound-conditioned admission query; the द्रव्य
path retains the defeat reason. Both block the requested lengthening here.
This agreement does not identify the policies globally. A concrete
source-attested divergence history is retained as an open obligation.

## Visibility is indexed by the consumer

Kāśikā and Nyāsa 8.2.1 expressly prevent **हलि च 8.2.77** from defeating
the earlier guṇa in **विस्फोर्यम्/अवगोर्यम्** through 1.4.2. The later
instruction's असिद्ध status is relative to its earlier competitor. Nyāsa
also explains why असिद्ध does not mean physically unmaking an already
existent event; this supports keeping a history while restricting its view.

The same source separately preserves the exception relation in दोग्धा and
दोग्धुम्. C06 therefore cannot be read as “all later operations are ignored,”
or as a universal reversal of source order. The history stops after the
addressed guṇa decision; it does not independently reconstruct every
derivational or accented detail of those nominal word witnesses.
