## H02. Denial is a meaning, not a prefix-only switch

Kāśikā 1.3.44 says that jñā in apahnava occurs with a prefix, not bare,
and gives **śatam apajānīte**. Apa is the explicitly counted witness; the
rule is not rewritten as an unconditional apa→A lookup or an exhaustive
list containing apa alone. A negative sentence such as the source's
na tvaṃ kiṃcid api jānāsi is not thereby the lexical denial sense.

Nyāsa at this rule explicitly uses 7.3.79's jā substitution, śnā and
6.4.113's ī. Its witness and the clean Kāśikā form control the spelling.
The pinned Kaumudī field instead prints **अपजीनीते**; preserve that literal
fault and do not silently turn its missing ā into an alternative stem.

## H03. Intransitivity requires the intended role, not an absent accusative

Kāśikā 1.3.45 admits **sarpiṣo jānīte** and **madhuno jānīte** with the
substance construed as a means of knowledge-guided engagement, not an object
of knowing. Rule 2.3.51 gives the genitive to this instrument in the selected
non-vid sense. Its separate mithyājñāna explanation is preserved in the
archive but does not create another table or an unreviewed meaning alias.
The control **svareṇa putraṃ jānāti** has an instrument and a karman: mere
instrument presence is insufficient for 1.3.45.

Kāśikā explains the independent role of 1.3.45 when principal fruit is not
agent-directed; 1.3.76 already addresses bare jñā when it is. Bālamanoramā
and Tattvabodhinī emphasize prefixed **sarpiṣo 'nujānīte**, where 1.3.76's
absence-of-prefix condition is unavailable. Keep both rationales as source
evidence. The counted bare frame fixes non-agent-directed principal fruit;
the anu frame supplies the actual prefixed engagement. Neither gains an
unsupported fruit-condition requirement from the later bare-root rule.

Rule 1.3.76 and its 1.3.77 upapada qualification are comparison dependencies,
not extra counted paradigms. PG12 admitted only the ordinary P laṭ profile
of 1090043; the bare A positions here are new. Its old P words remain frozen.

## H04. Anādhyāna blocks the otherwise tempting fallback

Kāśikā 1.3.46 calls this the transitive extension for sam/prati. Kaumudī
distinguishes **saṃjānīte** in attending to an object from **pratijānīte**
in acknowledgement. Ādhyāna here is **utkaṇṭhā-pūrvaka smaraṇa**, remembrance
with longing, not a generic prohibition of every thought or recollection.

Kaumudī and Bālamanoramā explicitly split the expression through
**yogavibhāga**: the anādhyāna restriction also blocks 1.3.45's route in
the selected sam/prati remembrance context. Thus the source's **mātaraṃ
saṃjānāti / mātuḥ saṃjānāti** does not acquire A just by presenting the
object relationally with a genitive. Rule 2.3.52 retains the intended
remembrance relation and its śeṣa presentation. It is not the instrument
genitive of 2.3.51.

This is an explicitly adopted traditional scope resolution, not a new
universal rule that any later exception defeats every earlier rule. The
controlled remembrance frames select residual P under 1.3.78 after the
relevant A channels have been discharged; their words receive zero new
position credit here. Unknown meaning/role data remains HOLD instead of
defaulting to A or P. The lexical row's broad prefix gloss is not enough.
