## H01. Exact lexical and current derivational recipient

Kāśikā specifies the current ṇyanta of karoti. Select
ḍu-kṛñ `1080010`, class eight, karaṇa, with one current ṇic.
The class-five hiṃsā kṛ `1050007` is a separate lexical entry
and receives no credit. The upapada words mithyā and suṣṭhu
are independently supplied sentence constituents, not upasargas;
all three finite tables here have no prefix.

Tattvabodhinī interprets underlying kṛ here through utterance
as niḥsaraṇa, intransitive, and its ṇyanta as transitive
uccāraṇa, niḥsāraṇa. The current controller c emits the
spoken unit q, the expressed karman. This source-specific role
analysis is retained; an unmentioned independent causee is not
invented. There is no karmavat, passive yak or nested ṇic.

The first source example is padaṃ mithyā kārayate: repeated
fault in accent or form. Kāśikā explicitly tests the missing
mithyā, kṛ and repetition guards. The suṣṭhu control fixes
the other admitted premises while replacing the upapada; the
single-use control explicitly retains mithyā but has sakṛt.
All counted requests select an other-directed principal result;
1.3.71 still licenses A in the first. The two P requests supply
positive ordinary frames, not mere failure to establish A.

Unknown recurrence or upapada stays HOLD. A pronunciation defect
alone is insufficient; neither a bare kṛ headword nor a repeated
correct performance entails this special A. This is a bounded
input certificate, not automatic inference of intention from text.

## H03. Semantic abhyāsa does not manufacture phonological copies

Kāśikā defines abhyāsa as repeated doing. Nyāsa and
Tattvabodhinī explain that the A ending signals that recurrence;
8.1.4 does not require repeating the whole finite word in this
construction. Thus the counted present word is kārayate, with
no extra whole-word duplication or intensive yaṅ.

The future, blessing and other selected contexts concern the
same repeated-event predicate. The aorist's 6.1.11 copy is a
separate morphological operation, and the perfect auxiliary has
its own copy. Semantic recurrence is therefore a typed input
coordinate, not the phonetic abhyāsa object and not a clock.
No alternative discourse-level repetition is counted here.

## H04. Positive root contrast and excluded lexical domains

The source also gives padaṃ mithyā vācayati as the non-kṛ
control. Archive adādi vac `1020058` and the separate curādi
vac `1100380`, but do not infer either full paradigm from that
one contrast. The adādi record contains restricted-usage reports
for plurals, while the curādi entry has optional lexical ṇic.
Their broader admission/usage questions stay outside this target.
The literal source control receives zero new position credit.

Kāśikā's karoti recipient and the class-eight lexical record
fix the counted kṛ identity. Its final ṛ is not an ṛ-it
marker and not an original upadhā ṛ. Neither 7.4.2's ṛdit
prohibition nor 7.4.7's original-upadhā substitution supplies
an extra caṅ branch. Preserve Nyāsa 7.4.7's literal mislink
of uraṇ raparaḥ to 1.1.50; the catalogue identifies that rule
as 1.1.51. The source text is not silently corrected.

## H05. Root and current affix owners

Ṇic's ṇit status licenses 7.2.115 vṛddhi of final ṛ;
1.1.51 gives the r-bearing result **kār**. This is not
7.2.116's consonant-final light-upadhā case. Later ṇic i
becomes ay where retained. Basic kṛ's u-vikaraṇa and kuru
allomorphs do not replace this current derived stem.

For caṅ, Kāśikā 7.4.1/93/94 explicitly gives acīkarat.
Shorten kār to kar before copy, apply san-like i and its
light-copy lengthening, then 7.4.62 changes copied k to c.
Original root k stays k. The resulting **cī|kar** factor is
independent of whether the semantic recurrence certificate says
ASAKṚT or SAKṚT.
