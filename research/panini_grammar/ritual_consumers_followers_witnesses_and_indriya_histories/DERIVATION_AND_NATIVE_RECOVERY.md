# PG796 · Derivation and native recovery

Rule 5.2.85 gives correlated `ini` and `ṭhan` readings for the person by whom the śrāddha has been eaten: `śrāddhī` and `śrāddhika`. Kāśikā's equal-time requirement blocks carrying yesterday's meal into a new `śrāddhika` designation. This is an explicit guard, not an invented completion shortcut.

Rules 86–87 retain two kinds of `pūrva`. Bare `pūrva` admits a supplied prior action and displays singular, dual and plural `pūrvin`; a stem ending in `pūrva` carries its own inner action, as `kṛtapūrvī kaṭam` and `bhuktapūrvī odanam`. The compound is made before the outer `ini`; identical final material does not erase action or object.

Rule 88 extends `ini` to the iṣṭādi list. PG796 constructs the two explanatory outputs only, `iṣṭī yajñe` and `pūrtī śrāddhe`; the rest of the printed list remains genuinely unconstructed and Open.

Rule 89 stipulates Vedic `pariparin` and `paripanthin` for an adversary. The frozen Kāśikā and Vasu fields differ in the second quoted spelling/accent presentation (`paripanthino` versus anusvāra `paripaṃthino`); both stay source-labelled. Rule 90 stipulates `anupadī` as a searcher after cows or camels.

Rule 91 narrows `sākṣin` to the technical onlooker. Its singular, dual and plural are accepted; giver and receiver are source-explicit negative controls for this name. Rule 92 stipulates `kṣetriya` and gives four domain families: disease/leprosy curable only in another body, poison transferred to another body, weeds to be removed from a crop field, and an adulterer to be restrained in another's marital domain. These senses remain distinct histories.

Rule 93 stipulates finally accented `indriyam`. Five analyses are named by the rule: Indra's sign, seen, created, enjoyed and given by Indra. Vasu also instantiates the rule's open-ended `iti` policy with “difficult for Indra to subdue.” PG796 accepts that sixth source-given analysis without treating arbitrary glosses as licensed.
