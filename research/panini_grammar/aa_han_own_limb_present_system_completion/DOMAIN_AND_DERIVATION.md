## Imperfect

After adādi śap-luk, A apit sārvadhātuka endings have 1.2.4 ṅidvat.
Actual jhal initials allow 6.4.37 root-n lopa: han|thās → ha|thās,
han|dhvam → ha|dhvam. Before a qualifying vowel initial, 6.4.98 removes
root a and 7.3.54 supplies gh before n: han|āthām → ghn|āthām and
han|i → ghn|i. Vahi/mahi do not have a jhal initial; retain han.

The prefix ā is distinct from the past aṭ at the root edge; their vowel
seam coalesces under 6.1.101. Keep both owners. The A first-singular i
survives: 3.4.100's P restriction does not delete it. The six new words
are āhathāḥ, āghnāthām, āhadhvam, āghni, āhanvahi, āhanmahi.

second-person endings are sva, āthām and dhvam, yielding āhasva,
āghnāthām and āhadhvam. In particular han|sva has an actual s initial
and the apit ṅidvat condition, so 6.4.37 gives ha|sva. The digital

The first-person āṭ here is not the imperfect's past aṭ.

## Optative and the partial-lopa boundary

3.4.102 supplies ending-owned sīyuṭ; 3.4.107 supplies the separate suṭ
at its named t/th target. Keep their source identities. On the
sārvadhātuka domain, 7.2.79 removes the specified nonfinal s occurrences.
The actual root-adjacent initial is then ī. Apply 6.4.98, followed by
7.3.54. Apply PG19 E4's ending changes, including 3.4.105 ran and
3.4.106 a, and the addressed 6.1.66 y-lopa queries.

Kāśikā 1.1.62 expressly cites āghnīya: sīyuṭ-s lopa is partial-affix
lopa, so its old s is not restored as an actual jhal initial by
pratyayalakṣaṇa. The selected root is ghn-, not a hypothetical ha-
obtained from that hidden s. Retaining source history therefore does not
make every query inspect every historical sound as currently present.

The six new words are āghnīthāḥ, āghnīyāthām, āghnīdhvam, āghnīya,
āghnīvahi and āghnīmahi. Dental dh in āghnīdhvam follows the stated
scope: ordinary liṅ īdhvam is not 8.3.78's ṣīdhvam, luṅ or liṭ.

## Full native construction and typed zero-reading

Reuse the exact PG14 N1–N5 construction pinned in SOURCES.json:
the native cut field C with derived iota and dagger, the free cut-path
coefficient algebra B = C⟨z_e,z_e*⟩, and the history-graded right module
M_lambda = ⊕_h e_h B. This is a specified full operator/history carrier;
lambda is not a scalar clock or the depth of a table. Pair by
⟨x,y⟩_B = Σ_h x_h* y_h. Keep cuts for admitted, excluded and open input
histories, and the complementary cut Q = 1 − P.

Each new guarded operation changes the actual owned grammar state and
appends the source/operation witness: (h,a) ↦ (h', (1+z_e)a).
Events include root-n lopa, root-a lopa, h→gh, ending-owned āṭ/pit,
separate sīyuṭ/suṭ deletion and the augment/prefix seam. Guards inspect
the retained grammatical history with the rule's declared visibility,
not the residue coefficient. Their source/target grades are explicit in
the derivations above. No other event order is silently admitted.

Let epsilon take the empty-word coefficient, pi_0,M apply epsilon to
each coefficient while keeping h, and Pi_0,op apply it entrywise to an
admitted operator. For every new edge,

    pi_0,M(T_e,lambda(e_h a))
      = e_h' epsilon((1+z_e)a)
      = e_h' epsilon(a)
      = T_e,0(pi_0,M(e_h a)).

The existing algebra-homomorphism and dagger identities extend this to
each displayed typed finite composition, its cuts/complements and the
pairing. Conversely every displayed zero-level derivation uses exactly
these same labelled guarded edges, each with empty-word coefficient 1;
its lifted history survives. Thus the selected finite branch relation,
including Open/Excluded channels, is recovered exactly on this domain.

The liṅ example makes the interface concrete: pi_0 retains the deleted
s event and its owner, while the actual-initial query continues to see ī.
Neither zero-specialization nor a surface-word observation reinstates s
as the current phoneme. Equal laṅ/loṭ words such as āhadhvam retain
different mood/ending histories. No Sanskrit reverse derivation,
unrestricted rule completeness, runtime implementation or physical clock
is inferred from the pairing or this algebraic adapter.
