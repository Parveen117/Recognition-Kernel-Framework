# PG947 · The ordinary case panel of the offering ladle

The input is the free feminine noun *juhū*, a ritual ladle, derived with kvip in PG945's Kāśikā policy. Its reduplication makes the base polysyllabic. Mādhavīya 732 actually cites *juhvau* under 6.4.83. This supplies a root-specific check on the glide before a vowel-initial sup ending. The long ū before consonants is independently visible in PG946's *juhūbhiḥ*. The short vocative *juhu*, the accusative *juhūm* and the locative *juhvām* were recorded in ritual clauses at PG945. These anchors license the following unaccented nadī-class inflection as a written derivation. Direct textual attestation is separately recorded for the earlier contexts. **Three outcomes at a vowel boundary.** In NOM/ACC dual, long ū and the dual ending give *juhvau*. NOM plural with jas likewise gives *juhvaḥ*. In ACC plural, śas is a different ending: 6.1.102 yields long pūrvasavarṇa ū, whereas the 6.1.105 prohibition applicable to jas does not apply here. This explains why NOM and ACC agree in the dual and diverge in the plural. ACC singular has the separate *am* operation of 6.1.107. The addressed singular has shortening and vocative consonant loss, as in the actual PG945 address. [Vowel-junction contracts](../compound_sound_accent/RULES_6_1_B.md)

**Oblique cases.** For the selected nadī reading, āṭ on the ṅit endings (7.3.112) gives the DAT *-vai* and ABL/GEN *-vāḥ* outcomes. In LOC singular the replacement of ṅi by ām is owned by 7.3.116. At the dual ending os the base glide yields *juhvoḥ*. By contrast GEN plural has nuṭ under 7.1.54, giving *juhūnām*. Its dental n has no retroflexion trigger. The consonant-initial bh forms retain ū, and the s of the locative plural undergoes ṣatva after ū. Feminine gender alone would be insufficient to import the uncontracted inflection of a different iy/uv-class root noun.
