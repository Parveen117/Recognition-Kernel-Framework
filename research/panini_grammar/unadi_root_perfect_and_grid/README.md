# PG218 · Perfect reduplication and closure of the fifteen-profile grid

Monty · 12 November 2026

*Mah* ‘honour’ and *māh* ‘measure’ can both appear as **mamāha**. The dual separates them. Short *mah* gives **mehatuḥ** while long *māh* gives **mamāhatuḥ**. Before a weak perfect ending, 6.4.120 selects short medial *a* between single consonants, substitutes *e*, and removes the reduplicant. The long vowel of *māh* falls outside that condition.

The Kāśikā expressly distinguishes a sound substitution triggered by the perfect itself from one already present in the root. It gives *nematuḥ* for *nam* and *sehe* for *sah*. This explains why the original ṇ of the two *naṭ* entries and ṣ of class-one *ṣam* allow **neṭatuḥ** and **sematuḥ**. The two *sam* entries share the complete perfect although their aorists are *asamīt* and *asamat*.

| Root | 3sg | 2sg | Condition visible at 2sg |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| *nam* | nanāma | nanantha / nemitha | Unaugmented *thal* or *iṭ* with *e* and loss of the copy. |
| Either *styai* | tastyau | tastyātha / tastyitha | Root ā remains before unaugmented *tha* and drops before *iṭ*. |
| Short *mah* | mamāha | mehitha | The selected seṭ form receives *e* under 6.4.121. |
| Long *māh*, P | mamāha | mamāhitha | Long ā retains the copy. |
