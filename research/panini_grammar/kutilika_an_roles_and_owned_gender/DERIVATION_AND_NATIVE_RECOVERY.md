# Recover the actors and the actual aṇ

## The selection roles and witnesses

The 4.4.18 lemma cites the lexeme *kuṭilikā*
in its own selection case. The supporting usage
in Kāśikā and Siddhāntakaumudī makes
*kuṭilikayā* a third-case means. Retain that
actual usage address until the taddhita
incorporates it. The predicate is bare
hṛñ 01.1046 haraṇe, with the attested
supporting P3sg kartari *harati* and the
specific source meanings below. The lexical
base and the noun formation have no tiṅ pada
of their own.

The Kāśikā actor and target are case-marked
distinctly. In Siddhāntakaumudī the two
accusatives *mṛgān* and *aṅgārān* match the
hunter and smith frames respectively. These
policies genuinely exchange the roles in the
hunt. The masculine suffix and outer
agreement follow each admitted actor and
do not treat an accusative as its nominative
controller. Bhāṣāvṛtti's first example
repeats the deer and specifies movement by
*gatyā*. It also prints the feminine use. Its
latter phrase leaves the object unexpressed.

The coal reading in PG36's frozen Kāśikā
and Vasu excerpts and the Puṣpā text is
*aṅgārān*. In the Sanskrit Documents
Kāśikā display it is *aṅgārāt*, an ablative
singular on the literal form. We retain that
printed form and leave its textual and
constructional status Open. It supplies
no second tool and no second independent
accusative witness. In the accepted
accusative plural, the final dental n
belongs to the object noun's own ending.
It remains dental by 8.4.37 even though
that noun contains r. Bālamanoramā describes
the named iron pincers for picking up
embers or heated iron. The specific tool
is the means. Vasu's English “forge”
is retained at the English gloss
address.

The reported Padamañjarī clause contains
*saṃjñāyāṃ kan* and a problematic
*uparamaṇamu* string in the digital witness.
This is evidence of a proposed inner analysis.
The exact earlier base and the status of
that spelling need an independently read
witness. The present nominal base is fixed
lexically, with its internal ik available to
the following aṇ.

## Outer affix, gender and case

The base reads *kuṭilikā*. Delete the
supporting instrumental sup under 2.4.71.
4.4.18 selects aṇ on this matched means
and sense. Preserve the terminal ṇ as the
original marker when only a sounds.
7.2.115/117 changes the first u to au.
The nominal input has the bha designation
before the vowel-bearing taddhita under
1.4.18. Its final ā elides at this
taddhita boundary under 6.4.148.

    kuṭilikā | sup_means(INS) | aṇ
      → kuṭilikā | empty | aṇ
      → kauṭilik | a
      → kauṭilika .

The ik belongs to the original nominal
material. 7.3.50's ṭha-to-ika does not
run on aṇ, and the neighboring 4.4.17
option does not appear in the history.
The expressly attested feminine now
selects ṅīp because 4.1.15 names aṇ.
The taddhita's own a elides at this
next boundary, leaving *kauṭilikī*.
The source base ā-stem, masculine
derived a-stem and feminine derived
ī-stem therefore require three case
tables with different ending owners.
There is no operative ṣ in these
stems. The vowels and retroflex ṭ
alone supply no ṇatva trigger, so
the case tails preserve dental n.
