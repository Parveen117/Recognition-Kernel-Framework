# PG266 · Sowing, carrying, piling and smearing after two preverbs

Monty · 12 November 2026

Four further verbs named in 8.4.17 have both *pra + ni* and *pari + ni* presents in the Kāśikā. Their eight active profiles add **288 positions, 312 readings**, across the present, imperative, imperfect and ordinary optative.

| Dhātupāṭha | Sense in the lexical commentary | Present 3sg | Imperfect 3sg |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 01.1158 *vap* | scatter seed over a field | praṇivapati | praṇyavapat |
| 01.1159 *vah* | carry, convey | praṇivahati | praṇyavahat |
| 05.0005 *ci* | gather, pile | praṇicinoti | praṇyacinot |
| 02.0005 *dih* | build up, specifically smear | praṇidegdhi | praṇyadhek |

The Mādhavīya supplies the entire augmented word *praṇyavapat*. The *i* of *ni* becomes *y* before *a*. The commentary also records a separate use of bare *vap* with hair as its object. Kṣīrasvāmin gives *praṇivahate* beside *praṇivahati*, and prints *praṇivapate* in brackets beside *praṇivapati*. The table above follows the active examples. The direct combination *pra + vah* has its own pada rule at 1.3.81.

With *ci*, the class-five affix has an optional loss of *u* before *v* and *m*, as in *praṇicinvaḥ / praṇicinuvaḥ*. With *dih*, the Nyāsa explains *degdhi* through the changes *h → gh*, *t → dh*, and *gh → g* before *dh*. The Padamañjarī explicitly excludes *yaṅ-luk* for the verbs named through their inflected forms. The Mādhavīya supplies precisely the intensive **pranidedegdhi** with dental *n* after *pra*. The ordinary perfect has its own reduplication and will be treated with its own forms.
