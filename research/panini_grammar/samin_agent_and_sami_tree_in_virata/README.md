# PG245 · The tranquil agent and Arjuna’s śamī tree

Monty · 12 November 2026

In the Virāṭaparvan Arjuna points out a great, dense **śamī** at 4.5.12a. The feminine demonstrative and adjectives agree with the tree. The Kāśikā also gives masculine **śamī**, the nominative of *śamin*, someone habitually tranquil. The masculine form loses its final *n* in the nominative. Kṣīrasvāmin and the Mādhavīya keep the agent and tree under separate derivations.

| Reading | Nominative singular | Accusative singular | Instrumental singular |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Habitually tranquil, masculine | śamī | śaminam | śaminā |
| Habitually tranquil, feminine | śaminī | śaminīm | śaminyā |
| Habitually tranquil, neuter | śami | śami | śaminā |
| Tree, feminine, already in PG230 | śamī | śamīm | śamyā |

The root belongs to the eight in 3.2.141. Under 7.3.34 its *a* stays short before the agent suffix. Its feminine **śaminī** also spells the neuter nominative or accusative dual. The printed root commentary gives *ghinuṇ* for the agent and *ṅīṣ* in the *pippalyādi* group for the tree. The Kṣīrataraṅgiṇī XML’s agent-suffix spelling is recorded independently.

All eight Virāṭaparvan lines containing the literal sequence **शमी** are located in the accompanying table. One free tree word is nominative singular and five are accusative singular, including *śamīṃ* before *k*. In **śamīvṛkṣam** and **śamīmūle** the tree is the first compound member. The case belongs to the whole compound. These eight source observations add evidence at two of the previously written tree cases. The three adjective schedules add **72 new nominal positions and 73 readings**.
