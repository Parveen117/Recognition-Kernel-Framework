# PG250 · The later action on the gerund and two nouns for acquired property

Monty · 12 November 2026

The Bhāṣya makes the sequence of actions explicit. The person asks first and makes the exchange afterwards. Northern teachers allow **apamitya yācate** even with the later action on the absolutive. The ordinary order also gives **yācitvā apamayate**, ‘having asked, he exchanges’. The Kāśikā gives a second pair with carrying, *apamitya harati* and *hṛtvā apamayate*. Rule 6.4.70 independently licenses **apamitya** and **apamāya** for the exchange root. The Mādhavīya gives *pramāya* for the measuring root.

| Input to 4.4.21 | Prescribed suffix | Neuter noun | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| *apamitya*, the cited absolutive | kak | āpamityakam | property obtained by barter |
| *yācita*, something requested | kan | yācitakam | something borrowed or obtained on request |

The Nyāsa explains how the indeclinable first input satisfies the instrumental semantic relation without showing an instrumental ending. The second can be paraphrased with the explicit instrumental *yācitena*. The Kāśikā’s pairing assigns the two different affixes in order. Optional *apamāya* is licensed as a gerund; the taddhita rule expressly names *apamitya*.

Śrīharṣa’s *Naiṣadhīyacarita* 7.56 has **vibhūṣaṇaṃ yācitakaṃ**, a borrowed ornament. The lotus and moon are the two subjects of *dhattaḥ*. The object gives the neuter accusative singular of the adjective here, while dictionaries also record the neuter noun for a borrowed thing. The two neuter case schedules add **48 positions with 50 readings**. The literary token occupies an existing case shape.
