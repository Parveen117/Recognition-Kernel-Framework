## H06. Yuj's internal śnam and the strong/weak boundary

Reuse exact PG7 and PG22's source-qualified 3.1.78/1.1.47 schedule:
put the separately owned mit affix after the last root vowel, giving
**yu | na | j**. It is not a suffix after j. The established nitya
argument gives śnam precedence over simple light-upadhā guṇa; after
insertion the old root u is no longer that aṅga's upadhā. Weak
6.4.111 removes only śnam's a, leaving **yu | n | j**.

In P laṭ/laṅ the three singular endings are strong; other slots are
weak. Ordinary P loṭ has strong 3sg and all first persons. A laṭ/laṅ
is weak throughout, and ordinary A loṭ has only the three āṭ-bearing
first persons strong. All vidhiliṅ positions are weak. Original ending
and effective marker features survive later ending loss. The six yuj
profiles therefore have **32 strong and 184 weak** present-system keys.

The inherited consonant readout is address-sensitive:

| Actual following material | Work after weak śnam-a lopa | Factor/example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| vowel, y, v or m | j retained; infix n→ṃ→ñ | yuñj, yuñjvahe |
| t/th | j→g, then k; infix nasal→ṅ | yuṅkte, yuṅkthāḥ |
| dh | j→g; nasal→ṅ | yuṅgdhve |
| s | j→g→k; addressed affix s→ṣ; nasal→ṅ | yuṅkṣe, yuṅkṣva |

Rules 8.2.30, 8.3.24, 8.3.59, 8.4.55/58 have different recipients
and ordered views. Strong yunaj retains its a and j before vowel/m;
before t/s the final j yields k, giving yunakti/yunakṣi. Pra does
not turn śnam's n into ṇ: the prefix rule 8.4.14 requires the specified
ṇopadeśa root, which yuj is not. In weak forms the late ñ/ṅ cannot
be fed back to an earlier ṇatva query. Kāśikā's prayunakti/prayuṅkte
pair directly anchors the two nasal outcomes.

## H07. Complete present endings and the deleted singular ending

Apply the pinned athematic endings to the owned layouts. A non-short-a
3pl uses **7.1.5 at**, not the thematic ant series: yuñjate/yuñjata
and yuñjatām. A duals retain ā before the ending; P weak duals
retain consonant-initial tas/tām or thas/tam. First-person plural v/m
contacts preserve the j and the infix nasal as yuñjv-/yuñjm-.

At P laṅ **3sg and 2sg**, 3.4.100 first removes the ending i.
Then **6.1.68** deletes the resulting apṛkta t/s after root-final j;
the strong layout remains yunaj. Pada-final 8.2.30/39 and the selected
devoiced-pause 8.4.56 policy yield **ayunak**. These are distinct
agreement keys with the same word. No ending t/s or extra infix nasal
is appended after that loss. Laṅ 1sg instead has am, hence ayunajam.

Ordinary loṭ first-person āṭ is ending-owned, not past aṭ. It gives
yunajai/yunajāvahai/yunajāmahai in A and yunajāni/yunajāva/yunajāma
in P. At P 2sg, 6.4.101 hi→dhi gives yuṅgdhi. Vidhiliṅ uses the
separate P yāsuṭ or A sīyuṭ contract, including its prescribed
s/y-lopa and ī sequence: yuñjyāt versus yuñjīta. The ordinary loṭ
profile adds no tātaṅ alternative. All these ending paths remain
independent of the special-versus-residual pada cause.

## H08. Kṣṇu's root u, uvaṅ and first-person guṇa

Adādi **śap-luk 2.4.72** leaves the root directly before tiṅ. In A
laṭ/laṅ and non-first-person ordinary loṭ, 1.2.4/1.1.5 blocks guṇa.
Before a vowel, **6.4.77 uvaṅ** acts at final root u, with 1.1.53
retaining the rest of kṣṇu. Thus kṣṇute, kṣṇuvāte, kṣṇuvate and
kṣṇuve have different ending seams. Before consonants retain root u:
kṣṇuvahe/kṣṇumahe contain their original v/m-initial endings, not
optional root-u deletion. The affix-u condition of 6.4.106/107 fails.

Only ordinary loṭ's three first persons have strong vowel-facing
āṭ: **7.3.84 u→o**, then **6.1.78 o→av**, giving
**kṣṇavai, kṣṇavāvahai, kṣṇavāmahai**. Do not transport the P
hal-facing 7.3.89 au into them. The remaining thirty-three present
positions are weak; twenty face a vowel and receive uvaṅ, thirteen
retain u directly. All nine A vidhiliṅ positions face the resolved
ī interface, giving kṣṇuvīta through kṣṇuvīmahi.

Short iṭ is absent from this sārvadhātuka domain. The optional ending
īṭ of exact ru/tu/stu under 7.3.95 does not include kṣṇu. After u,
8.3.59 gives **kṣṇuṣe/kṣṇuṣva** at the addressed ending s. Present
and ordinary vidhiliṅ dh remains dental; no ārdhadhātuka blessing
or sic retroflexion is imported.
