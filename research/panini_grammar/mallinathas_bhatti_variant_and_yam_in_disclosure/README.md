# PG866 · Mallinātha locates the Bhaṭṭi variant and the aorist of yam in disclosure

### The actual address of āhadhvaṃ mā raghūttamam

The phrase sought in S1 occurs in Mallinātha’s *Sarvapathīnā* on Bhaṭṭi. In K. P. Trivedi’s 1898 edition, canto VII, stanza **103**, printed p. **253**, the verse body reads *modayadhvaṃ raghūttamam*. Immediately below it Mallinātha gives **आहध्वं मा रघूत्तमम्** and labels it *iti kvacit pāṭhaḥ*, a reading found in some copies or sources. Trivedi independently draws attention to that very variant in his discussion of stanza 103 on notes pp. 111–112. The two appearances in the volume constitute one edition with an editorial discussion.

The same stanza is **7.101** in the 1891 Calcutta text with Jayamaṅgalā and Bharatamallika (printed pp. 458–459), and in the *Candrakalā–Vidyotinī* edition (pp. 57–58). The initial gathering, the command concerning the best of the Raghus, the fear clause, and the closing clause concerning Sītā and Daśānana fix the correspondence. Calcutta prints *modayadhvam* in its verse body. PG775 correctly recorded the reading of the editions it examined. Its exclusion cannot be carried over to Mallinātha’s commentary. **The independent locator for the exact S1 quotation is closed at this variant.** Its carrier is the commentary. The page supplies no manuscript siglum for Mallinātha’s *kvacit*. The wording in the apparatus under the verse, including the alternative for the first vowel of the fear clause, is separately located.

Recovering a verse address settles where a commentator placed the disputed words. It does not identify the authorial reading, assign the variant to a named manuscript, decide whether its separate mā is pronominal or prohibitive for all witnesses, or transfer the explicit luṅ from the adjacent *modāhadhvam*. Those are the residual S1 questions.

On Candrakalā’s selected fear token the two preverbs precede yam. Middle second plural supplies dhvam. The kit of sic deletes root-final m under 6.4.37. Sic-s disappears before dh under 8.2.25, leaving dental dh after short a. In the augmented luṅ the adjacent vowels of upa, āṅ and aṭ coalesce to ā. With māṅ aṭ would be blocked; the final long vowel by itself cannot decide between those histories. Continuous *no-* in the printed verse is external na + u. In the separate marriage clause upa + yam admits 1.3.56 and **optional** kit under 1.2.16. Its witnessed third singular with nasal and s has the non-kit path. The two meanings, voice grounds and kit decisions cannot be interchanged.

### A complete ordinary aorist on the direct Kāśikā disclosure domain

At 1.2.15 the Kāśikā supplies ud + ā + yam, revealing another person’s concealed fault. Its own disclosure examples give middle under āṅ, with the signified fault left unexpressed as a direct object. Haradatta’s explanatory paraphrase describes what the agent makes known. The following counterexamples express foot or water as an object and illustrate drawing up, with a different licensing discussion. PG107 explicitly reserves non-gandhana inputs. PG86’s optional marriage aorists instead have upa. The selected nine-cell schedule below is for ordinary augmented luṅ, affirmative actor-middle, ud + ā on the disclosure sense, with the middle already admitted. No agent-result or sense licence is exported to another prefix.
