# PG26 — Ending-owned īṭ and the actual guṇa/vṛddhi query

Author: Monty. 2 November 2026.

## M1. Resolve the recipient before choosing īṭ

Retain D4's ending substitution, first-person āṭ, modal augment and
non-final s-lopa. The optional **7.3.95 īṭ** is admitted precisely
for exact tu/ru/stu before a hal-initial sārvadhātuka tiṅ. It has
no effective-pit requirement. It is not short iṭ under 7.2.35 and
not a generic consequence of a seṭ lexical label.

By 1.1.46, īṭ is an initial member of its qualified ending. Its ṭ
is a retained it marker, leaving pronounced ī. Root, īṭ, yāsuṭ,
sīyuṭ and original tiṅ keep different owners. Select at most once
at this resolved boundary; do not repeatedly augment the new ī.

| Context after ending refinement | īṭ choices for ru/tu/stu |
|---|---|
| I/B ordinary P tu, tām, hi, tam, ta | absent or present |
| I/B ordinary P antu or first-person āṭ | absent only |
| I/B ordinary stu A tām, sva, dhvam | absent or present |
| other ordinary stu A slots | absent only |
| O P yā/yus, all nine slots | absent or present |
| O A resolved modal ī, all nine slots | absent only on this source view |
| B P selected tāt at 3sg/2sg | absent or present |

Hal includes y. This is why O P admits īṭ, unlike PG23's
val-sensitive rudādi iṭ. The ordinary 3pl antu/atām is vowel-initial
after jh replacement; historical jh does not license an extra augment.
First-person āṭ similarly blocks the hal field even though it is pit.

The O A view continues PG19/25's actual resolved boundary. A schedule
that queries historical s of sīyuṭ before its mandatory lopa is a
different history policy, not a second counted reading. Kāśikā's bare
स्तुवीत does not establish that alternative chronology: the same word
is already the admitted augmented ordinary P 2pl. The absence of a pit
condition is independently witnessed by weak tām and A sva/dhvam.

## M2. Short-u roots: strengthening follows the augment choice

For these non-abhyasta, śap-luk roots, **7.3.89** supplies u→au
before effective-pit, actual hal-initial sārvadhātuka. Before a vowel
in the strong context, ordinary 7.3.84 instead gives u→o. 6.1.78
then gives av at the vowel seam. Weak u is retained before hal and
has uv under 6.4.77 before an actual vowel.

| Owned input | Choice and actual context | Stem/readout |
|---|---|---|
| ru∣tu, strong | no īṭ; t first | rau∣tu → rautu |
| ru∣tu, strong | īṭ chosen; ī first | ro∣ī∣tu → ravītu |
| ru∣tām, weak | no īṭ | rutām |
| ru∣tām, weak | īṭ chosen | ru∣ī∣tām → ruvītām |
| ru∣hi, weak | absent/present īṭ | ruhi/ruvīhi |
| ru∣ā∣ni, strong | ā first; no īṭ | ro∣ā∣ni → ravāṇi |
| ru∣yā∣t, weak | absent/present īṭ | ruyāt/ruvīyāt |
| ru∣tāt, separately weak | absent/present īṭ | rutāt/ruvītāt |

Tu/stu have the same owned decision pattern. In particular their
strong augmented tu readings are **tavītu/stavītu**. The literal
Bālamanoramā स्तुवीतु is retained as a source conflict in R;
it is not used to cancel the strong guṇa prescription.

The optional augment must precede this root query. Strengthening ru
first to rau and only then inserting ī produces the wrong seam
rāvītu. That path is excluded by the selected schedule, not accepted
as an additional optional word. M1's īṭ never makes a weak ending pit.

## M3. Ūrṇu has its own option

**7.3.90** permits both u→au and u→o for exact ūrṇu before
strong hal. Here that field occurs only at ordinary P 3sg:
**ūrṇautu/ūrṇotu**. It does not borrow ru/tu/stu's īṭ.

All first persons are vowel-facing, so only guṇa/av survives:
ūrṇavāni and ūrṇavai. All weak fields retain u before hal and
uv before a vowel. Thus ūrṇu P O is **ūrṇuyāt**, A O
**ūrṇuvīta**, and B tāt is **ūrṇutāt**. The apṛkta rule
7.3.91 and ārdhadhātuka ṅit option are outside this mood domain.
A two-way strong-tu choice is not a root-wide vowel toggle.

## M4. Sū's prohibition and the two i-vowel roots

For exact class-2 **sū**, 7.3.88 prohibits guṇa before directly
following sārvadhātuka tiṅ. It still applies at strong first-person
āṭ, producing **suvai/suvāvahai/suvāmahai** by 6.4.77, rather
than a manufactured sav- stem. Weak vowel contexts likewise use uv;
weak hal retains ū. Short-u su is a different lexical entry and
has ordinary strong saut-/sav- behavior.

For **iṇ**, strong guṇa gives e, with ay before first-person ā.
Weak vowel-facing i instead uses the specific **6.4.81 yaṇ**:
yantu. Weak consonant fields retain i: ihi, itām, iyāt, itāt.
The unprefixed ordinary hi form is ihi, explicitly given by the
Kaumudī/Bālamanoramā passage at 7.4.69.

For **vī**, strong guṇa gives e, with ay before ā. Weak hal retains
long ī; before a vowel 6.4.77 gives **iy**, hence viyantu.
The single-vowel aṅga fails 6.4.82's anekāc field, and it is not
iṇ. Therefore vīhi/vīyāt differ from ihi/iyāt, and viyantu
from yantu. No separate ī root is conflated with this ID.

## M5. Fourteen ā roots retain their root vowel

These are non-abhyasta roots without śnā. They do not satisfy
6.4.112/113's stem class, and dāp is not ghu. Retain ā before hi,
y and tāt as well as ordinary t: yāhi/yāyāt/yātāt;
dāhi/dāyāt/dātāt for exact dāp. Do not transplant PG25's
hāk y-lopa or class-3 māṅ/hāṅ weak-ī construction.

Before ordinary antu and first-person āṭ, apply the owned ā/a
coalescences under 6.1.101: yāntu, yāni, yāva, yāma.
The modal y remains: yāyāt and yāyuḥ. No laṅ jus option is
transported to loṭ; Bālamanoramā 3.4.111 explicitly excludes it.

## M6. Local completeness

D2 partitions short u, long sū, iṇ/vī and the fourteen ā roots.
M1 exhausts every admitted augment fibre; M2–M5 exhaust the strong,
weak, vowel/hal and lexical-exception queries after that fibre is chosen.
The only multiplicities are the stated īṭ pairs and ūrṇu's ordinary
P 3sg pair, plus B's independent ending refinement. They are counted
per resulting ending/context in P, not multiplied as global toggles.
