# PG26 — Owned vowel seams and nasal readout

Author: Monty. 2 November 2026.

## S1. Root and ending vowels retain different owners

Use M's completed branch, not a present-tense surface stem. Strong e/o
before an actual vowel gives ay/av by 6.1.78; retained weak i/ī/u/ū
has the source-specific yaṇ or iyaṅ/uvaṅ of M4/M2. Thus ravītu
contains strong root av plus ending-owned ī, whereas ruvītām
contains weak root uv plus ending-owned ī. Neither ī is a new root
vowel, nor does its presence alone imply a strong ending.

The fourteen long-ā roots take their owned 6.1.101 coalescences before
antu/āṭ. Hnu/ūrṇu/stu A first-person ai and āvahai/āmahai
follow their strong av construction; sū instead retains the prohibited-
guṇa uv route. No thematic short a is inserted behind śap-luk.

At O P 3pl, retain 6.1.96's modal yā+us→yus, then final-s
readout: ruyuḥ/ruvīyuḥ, yāyuḥ, iyuḥ and vīyuḥ. At O A,
īy keeps y before vowels and loses it before consonants under 6.1.66.
The final n of ran remains dental by 8.4.37; all O A dhvam outputs
remain dental dh on this sārvadhātuka domain.

## S2. Hi, sva and tāt

Every admitted root is vowel-final after marker preprocessing. Ordinary
hi therefore remains hi: no 6.4.101 jhal-final dhi is admitted.
Short u here belongs to the root, not an affix; 6.4.106's affix-u
hi-luk does not give a false tu/ru/stu imperative without hi. Long
ā is not the short-a field of 6.4.105 either.

After the inherited iṇ vowel field, 8.3.59 supplies sva→ṣva:
ūrṇuṣva, stuṣva/stuvīṣva, hnuṣva and sūṣva. An inserted
īṭ changes the immediate trigger but not the affix-s owner. B tātaṅ
has its own ṅit/weak source state, and no surviving hi target.

## S3. The first-person n and the plural n are different sites

Apply 8.4.1/2 to the actual first-person ni site. The eligible new
outputs are **ravāṇi, kṣavāṇi, śrāṇi, drāṇi, rāṇi and
prāṇi**. The admitted intervening vowels/semivowels do not block them.

The n of **kṣṇavāni** and **ūrṇavāni** stays dental: the retained
root ṇ interrupts the permitted intervening class. Its being retroflex
is not a license to spread ṇatva through it. All other P first-person
ni outputs in this packet are dental. The n in lexical snu/snā is
retained from its own preprocessing history, not newly generated here.

At ordinary P 3pl, non-final n before t first becomes anusvāra under
8.3.24. 8.4.58 restores the nasal matching dental t later. Kāśikā
explicitly explains why that restored n does not feed the earlier ṇatva
query again. Hence **ruvantu, kṣuvantu, ūrṇuvantu, śrāntu,
drāntu, rāntu and prāntu**, all with dental n at this ending.
This is the inherited local tripādī schedule, not arbitrary suppression
of every n after r/ṣ.

## S4. No additional sound-choice fibre

These root-vowel/ending contacts create no savarṇa consonant pair in
8.4.65's optional-collapse field. The selected profile excludes optional
doubling. Every M branch therefore has one S readout. The śam/am Vedic
examples, ārdhadhātuka ṣīdhvam retroflexion and prefixed assimilation
routes do not enlarge this finite domain.

Together S1–S3 cover every vowel seam, sva contact, ni site and ordinary
plural nasal in P. All remaining consonants are retained under the
inherited ending and final-s rules. Thus no unlisted surface option
changes P's count under the declared source/sound policy.
