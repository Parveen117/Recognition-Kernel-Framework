## R1. Īṭ is optional, hal-sensitive and ending-owned

Kāśikā, Kaumudī and Bālamanoramā 7.3.95 give the īṭ
prescription without a pit requirement. This differs from short iṭ
under 7.2.35.
Bālamanoramā retains tiṅ and explains that the completed ant ending
blocks īṭ. Its ruvītaḥ example gives the weak augmented path;
its ruvīyāt gives the P modal-y path. Kāśikā/Kaumudī 7.2.79
specify removal of the nonfinal modal s, supplying the resolved ī view
used for O A. No universal priority algorithm is inferred.

The Kāśikā/Tattvabodhinī token स्तुवीत can already be an
ordinary P loṭ 2pl augmented reading. It does not by itself prove an
additional O A history in which īṭ precedes a subsequently removed
sīyuṭ s. The selected resolved-boundary policy is explicit, as in
PG19/25; history equivalence with a competing schedule remains open.

Tu's सौत्रः identity and its classical luk license remain qualified.
The same source discusses Vedic śam/am and an alternative Āpiśali
recension; neither is silently included in D. Modern corpus-level
classical-use attestation is not supplied merely by a rule derivation.

## R2. Strong/weak and actual sound are independent fields

Kāśikā 7.3.89 supplies the strong, non-abhyasta, luk and actual-hal
conditions, and contrasts first-person yavāni/ravāṇi. It explicitly
blocks vṛddhi at stuyāt through the effective ṅit/pit account.
Kaumudī/Tattvabodhinī 7.3.89 and Kaumudī/Bālamanoramā 7.3.91
support that distinction. Thus choosing īṭ does not make a weak
tām/y/tāt ending strong.

Kāśikā/Kaumudī 7.3.90 give ūrṇu's own guṇa/vṛddhi option;
Tattvabodhinī contrasts vowel-facing ūrṇavāni. The apṛkta source
at 7.3.91 supports the weak-marker explanation, but its apṛkta
operation is not used in I/O/B.

The ordinary strong īṭ path uses 7.3.84 guṇa and 6.1.78 av,
just as PG10's ravīti/stavīti. Therefore PG26 constructs **ravītu,
tavītu and stavītu**. Bālamanoramā 7.2.6 instead prints **स्तुवीतु**
beside स्तौतु. That token conflicts with the strong-tu rule account;
retain it literally, select the rule-derived av form, and do not count
uv as an additional alternative. This is an explicit source decision,
not an exact-word witness or a runtime comparison result.

## R3. Sū, iṇ and vī

Kāśikā/Bālamanoramā 7.3.88 restrict the prohibition to the
luk-vikaraṇa sū identity. The lexical passage in Bālamanoramā
7.2.78 explicitly has sūtām/suvātām/suvatām/sūṣva/sūdhvam,
then invokes the prohibition at āṭ and gives suvai/suvāvahai/
suvāmahai. Short-u su is a different ID.

Kāśikā 6.4.81 supplies yantu and distinguishes iṇ's yaṇ from
generic iyaṅ and guṇa. Kaumudī/Bālamanoramā 7.4.69 explicitly
give etu/itāt/itām/yantu/ihi and ayāni, with separate iyāt and
āśīrliṅ īyāt. The host liṭ-copy lengthening is not an operation
of this packet.

Kaumudī/Bālamanoramā 6.4.66 supply vīhi, vetu/vītāt/viyantu
and vayāni. Their neighbouring prefixed ik/iṅ and separate ī-root
accounts remain outside the exact vī contract. The host ārdhadhātuka
ā-to-ī rule is not applied to these modal derivations. Vī's explicit
sārvadhātuka-use qualification is retained.

## R4. The fourteen ā roots and nasal history

Kaumudī/Bālamanoramā 3.4.111 list the ā roots, distinguish pā in
protection and dāp in cutting, qualify khyā's use and explain mā's
unprefixed intransitive sense. Bālamanoramā explicitly blocks transport
of laṅ's optional jus to yāntu under loṭ's limited laṅvat rule.
The host laṅ operation is not a new paradigm here.

For prāntu and the other r/ṣ-containing ordinary plurals, Kāśikā
8.3.24 and 8.4.58 give the anusvāra/late-restoration order. 8.4.58
expressly explains why the restored n cannot feed ṇatva again.
8.4.2's permitted intervening class also distinguishes ravāṇi from
ūrṇavāni: retained lexical ṇ is not an admitted intervening sound.

## R5. Other literal conflicts retained

- Kaumudī 7.3.95 prints **रूवीयात्**; Bālamanoramā there has
  **रुवीयादिति**, i.e. ruvīyāt + iti, and the weak-u/īṭ
  construction supports that short-u reading.
  The same Bālamanoramā entry's later unaugmented-vṛddhi sentence
  expressly retains **पिति**. Read it as a restricted general reminder,
  not a prescription for the immediately discussed weak modal slots;
  7.3.89/91 do not license an added rauyāt.
- Bālamanoramā 7.2.6 prints **स्तवै स्तवावहै स्तवावहै**; the last
  slot is constructed as stavāmahai from its owned ending. Its
  **स्तुवीद्वे** is also retained; it is not a model for deleting dh
  in the loṭ stuvīdhvam construction.
- The Nyāsa 7.3.95 field has repeated/damaged examples and a local
  reference **7.4.78** attached to बहुलं छन्दसि. The cited text is
  2.4.73; PG26 neither applies 7.4.78 nor silently fixes the archive.
- Tattvabodhinī 7.3.88 prints **षूझ्** where D's pinned teaching
  record is षूङ्. The lexical ID and ṅit A license are not changed.

PG10's six missing separate full-word witnesses (su, ku, śrā, rā,
dāp, prā) remain historical evidence gaps. New written consequences do
not retroactively turn those old comparison cells into literal witnesses.

## R6. Claim boundary

JSON parsing and set/hash arithmetic are provenance bookkeeping, not
Sanskrit tests, independent validation or proof-assistant checking. The
complete shared source entries include many outside-domain examples;
