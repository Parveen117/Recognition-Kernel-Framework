# PG921 · The imperfect of √rudh and the reported siege

Kāśikā 3.2.111 gives *aruṇad yavanaḥ sāketam* in a qualification concerning a past event outside immediate perception, publicly known and within the speaker’s possible observation. The Yavana is the agent and Sāketa the object of obstruction. The second example changes the place under siege. It repeats the grammatical request. This use of an imperfect is distinct from the mixed today/yesterday example in the preceding question. The commentary’s examples witness a grammatical analysis; their wording alone establishes neither a date for the siege nor eyewitness testimony. **Construction.** Add śnam under 3.1.78 after the root vowel by 1.1.47. The morphology preserves one root, one infix and a separate ordered arrangement of their sounded portions. The three P singular strong endings keep the infix vowel and permit ṇatva after the initial r. In the other six P cells and all nine A cells the weak ending conditions the infix a deletion (6.4.111). The resulting nasal next to the root-final stop goes through the anusvāra and homorganic dental path of PG7. Its later dental realization cannot be read back into the earlier ṇatva decision. The P plural *arundhan* ends in a second, ending-owned n. The vowel before it belongs to the third-person ending.

In P 3sg and 2sg the single-consonant secondary ending disappears under 6.1.68. The remaining stop belongs to *rudh*. Final voicing and optionally devoiced pause give the t/d pair. P 2sg additionally admits ru under 8.2.75, with the original sip history, and visarga at pause. A 3sg has its own weak ending and cannot inherit any of these three forms. At the three dental junctions in each pada the tables distinguish *nddh* and *ndh*. Preserve the voicing/aspiration and the optional deletion (8.4.65) separately from stem selection. A 1du *arundhvahi* and A 2pl *arundhvam* share initial segments but have different original endings and different availability of the dental-cluster fork. The printed historical source excerpt in those stages earns no earlier *rudh* imperfect table credit. Mādhava quotes representative middle forms and the second-singular ru option.
