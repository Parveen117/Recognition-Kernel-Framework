# PG272 · Shared third-person futures and four middle blessing clusters

Monty · 12 November 2026

The periphrastic future of these four roots has the same third singular, dual and plural under either pada. Under *pari + ni + vah*, for example, these are *pariṇivoḍhā, pariṇivoḍhārau, pariṇivoḍhāraḥ*. The second singular distinguishes P *pariṇivoḍhāsi* from A *pariṇivoḍhāse*. Across the four roots and both prefix orders exactly **24** pairs of positions share a periphrastic-future word. In the simple future, conditional and benedictive all person-matched readings differ between padas.

| Root | Middle *lṛṭ*, 3sg | Middle benedictive, 3sg | Middle benedictive, 2pl |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| *ci* | praṇiceṣyate | praṇiceṣīṣṭa | praṇiceṣīḍhvam |
| *dih* | praṇidhekṣyate | praṇidhikṣīṣṭa | praṇidhikṣīdhvam |
| *vap* | praṇivapsyate | praṇivapsīṣṭa | praṇivapsīdhvam |
| *vah* | praṇivakṣyate | praṇivakṣīṣṭa | praṇivakṣīdhvam |

The Mādhavīya explicitly gives the middle *vakṣīṣṭa* and *vakṣyate*. The active blessing of the same root, *uhyāt*, starts with a vowel; here the middle *vakṣīṣṭa* preserves a consonant at the boundary with *ni*. In the second plural the *ci* selection has retroflex *ḍh*, whereas the three consonant-final roots retain dental *dh*. Kāśikā 8.3.78 differentiates the qualifying preceding *iṇ* from its consonantal counterexample *pakṣīdhvam*.

These four lakāras add **288 middle positions and 288 readings**, taking the four-root middle selection to **576 of 720**.
