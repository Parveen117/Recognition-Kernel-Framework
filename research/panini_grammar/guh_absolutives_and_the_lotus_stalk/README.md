# PG992 · Having embraced, having concealed and the lotus held by its stalk

**Monty · 26 November 2026.** Three operations produce a long ū in this family: a guṇa-conditioned vowel substitution, a vowel change before a deleted consonant and the retention of an earlier causative grade. The same surface vowel cannot identify the affix.

At Bhāgavata 3.22.24 the emperor embraces the daughter with **both arms**. The finite continuation lies across the verse boundary. At 3.22.25 he wets her hair with tears (underlying *āsiñcat*, written *āsiñcad amba*). The two intervening present participles describe his condition and the shedding of tears. In 4.9.49 Sunīti embraces her **son** and gives up **her distress**, *jahau ādhim*. The son is the object of the absolutive; distress belongs to the finite predicate. Each verse supplies a direct simple *upaguhya*. One nonfinite construction covers both occurrences. [Bhāgavata 3.22.24–25 and 4.9.49](https://gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de/gretil/corpustei/transformations/html/sa_bhAgavatapurANa.htm)

In *nigūhayati*, ṇic creates a guṇa-eligible vowel-initial environment and 6.4.89 supplies ū. When lyap follows the derived stem, 6.4.51 removes ṇi in the selected an-iṭ environment. The root is already long when the light-vowel condition for 6.4.56 is evaluated. The Kāśikā reports a view that uses the rule's special wording to prevent unwanted -ay-, then gives the different-locus explanation: length applies with ṇi as condition; the possible replacement of ṇi applies with lyap as condition. Record both explanations of why *nigūhya* survives. The short *niguhya* in the simple print and the long causative are different requests. An attribution nuance for PG991: Kāśikā's isolated *nigoḍhā* supplies a consonant-initial grade control. Its bare surface alone cannot distinguish luṭ from the nominative of a homophonous agent noun. The finite reconstruction also uses PG987's luṭ evidence. **The woman concealing the paired objects.** Bhāgavata 4.25.24 has *nigūhantīm* agreeing with the woman described in the surrounding accusative construction. The masculine dual *stanau* belongs to her act of covering. The end of the garment is instrumental. Use simple ni + guh, present actor participle śatṛ, thematic śap, feminine ṅīp and obligatory num under 7.1.81. The accusative case and singular number come from the feminine controller. The paired objects license no dual ending on this participle. [Bhāgavata 4.25.20–24](https://gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de/gretil/corpustei/transformations/html/sa_bhAgavatapurANa.htm)

**The remembered embrace.** At 1.5.19 the compound's final member denotes the action of embracing Mukunda's feet. The neuter action construction admits lyuṭ under 3.3.115 with ana under 7.1.1; the ensuing *upagūhana-* has long ū. At the internal junction *aṅghri + upagūhana* gives *aṅghryupagūhana*. No additional nominal reading follows from it. **The lotus in the suitor's hands.** In Raghuvaṃśa 6.13a–d *upagūḍhanālam* qualifies the play-lotus *līlāravindam* that someone twirls. Internally the stalk is held in his hands, with upa + guh and patient kta. The kta branch has no iṭ and retains its own history of consonant substitution and contraction (8.2.31, 8.2.40, 8.3.13), with 6.3.111 lengthening the preceding u. Externally the whole possessive adjective agrees with the neuter singular lotus in the accusative. The selected case fibre below is neuter. Other genders and the standalone participle require separate keys. [Raghuvaṃśa 6.13, Scharpé pāda index](https://gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de/gretil/1_sanskr/5_poetry/2_kavya/kragh_iu.htm)
