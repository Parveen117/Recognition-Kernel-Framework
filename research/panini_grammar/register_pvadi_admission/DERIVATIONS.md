## C01 — Neither a missing tag nor a present tag decides alone

H01 withholds three untagged entries under its chosen report; H03 admits
one tagged entry under an explicit exception. The admission predicate
must take source authority and the exact lexical referent, not just a
Boolean `vedic` field. A policy-specific withholding is not a universal
linguistic impossibility judgment.

## C02 — A quoted Vedic word is not a complete Vedic engine

H01's three witness paths select the attested sārvadhātuka branch.
3.4.117, accent and other Vedic overrides remain open. Do not count the
tail register's twelve withheld classical entries as newly completed
classical paradigms merely because a comparison lane returns forms.

## C03 — शॄञ् and शॄ cannot share an erased lexical identity

Keep 1090015 / U and 1090021 / P separately, with their different source
admissions. A normalization that drops ñ and then joins by remaining
śṝ is insufficient for pada. Source-specific numbering 17 is not the
modern lexical ID 09.0017, which denotes a different root.

## C04 — Keep membership, applicability and target distinct

Shortening needs the selected pvādi membership and the actual śit
affix. The broad jñā exception can block it after membership succeeds.
The weak-ending ī belongs to śnā, not to a reversal of root shortening.
The same word therefore requires more than a single short/long flag.

## C05 — No unlabelled union of endpoint policies

Do not overwrite PG12's vrīṇāti with vriṇāti or classify one policy
as an implementation error solely because the other is in an oracle.
Keep the narrow delimiter, broad delimiter, lexical ID, selected profile
and actual observed pair in any replay adjudication. Preserve the OCR
and damaged Nyāsa spellings separately from adopted grammar.

## C06 — The gaṇa is part of the shortening domain

The class-5 dhūñ entry 1050010 does not become a 7.3.80 target just
because the kryādi dhūñ entry 1090020 shortens. Padamañjarī explicitly
excludes the similarly named bhvādi group, and Kāśikā locates pvādi
in kryādi. This is a qualification of the rule's domain, not a global
ban on shortening elsewhere.
