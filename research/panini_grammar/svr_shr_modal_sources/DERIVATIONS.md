## H01 — The cited Dhātupradīpa separates svṝ and vṝ

The selected witness is Maitreya-rakṣita's *Dhātupradīpa*, edited with
annotations by Srish Chandra Chakravarti, Varendra Research Society,
1919. The title-page text distinguishes author and editor; the website's
catalogue “author” field alone does not. Three scanned pages were
visually inspected and archived without image alteration:

| Printed page / viewer | Selected evidence | Role |
|---|---|---|
| [125 / 135](DHATUPRADIPA_P125.jpg) | पूञ् पवने, entry 10, पुनाति/पुनीते; the subsequent pū/lū/ṛ-final sequence through pṝ 17 | Start and continuation of the relevant kryādi subdivision. |
| [126 / 136](DHATUPRADIPA_P126.jpg) | **स्वॄ हिंसायाम्**, entry **18**, **स्वृणाति**; next **वॄ वरणे**, entry **19**, **वृणाति** | The headword, sense and present word are separate for the two entries. |
| [127 / 137](DHATUPRADIPA_P127.jpg) | The P declaration for the śṝ-onward group and the later plī boundary discussion | Pada and local order; svṝ 18 lies before either discussed endpoint. |

The decisive literal fragment is `स्वॄ हिंसायाम् । १८ । स्वृणाति ।`.
It gives a short present vowel. The same page also has the non-present
word स्वूर्णः; that does not turn the preceding present into स्वूर्णाति.
The page's neighboring vṝ entry owns वरणे. The website OCR drops or
misreads several root vowels and even the neighboring entry number;
the scan, not the OCR, supplies the adopted transcription.

The modern `dhatu/data.txt` object 1090070 instead supplies svṝ,
वरणे, and a variant link to 9.23; object 1090023 actually has vṝ.
Its label inside the link also says svṝ. This is a concrete mismatch
between that metadata and its cited witness. It is not repaired by
replacing the meaning of every vṝ or every svṛ entry elsewhere.

## H07 — A conditional ir/ur branch must recheck the following sound

Keep the residual question separate: suppose another source actually
licensed an unshortened svṝ/śṝñ śnā profile. PG62 does not admit such
a profile merely because a program generates it. Even under that
hypothesis, two following-affix states cannot share one unconditional
8.2.77 result.

| Stage | Explicitly conditional state | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
|Q1|If ir/ur is licensed and śnā remains n-initial.|8.2.77 has a following hal and can lengthen the root i/u.|
|Q2|If 3.1.83 is instead licensed and replaces śnā by āna.|The root is followed by ā, not hal; 8.2.77 cannot supply that lengthening.|
|Q3|Perform the admitted affix substitution before its tripādī readout.|Do not carry a lengthening conditioned by the discarded n into the āna branch.|

K 8.2.77 explicitly requires the following hal; K 8.2.1 explains
tripādī visibility and even discusses 8.2.77 against earlier guṇa.
Thus the T165 long **svūrāṇa/śīrāṇa** readings cannot be justified by
first lengthening before nā and then replacing it with āna in that
same claimed chain. If that hypothetical śānac branch were separately
licensed and no other lengthener admitted, its conditional readout
would have short u/i (**svurāṇa/śirāṇa**). These are diagnostic
conditional strings with **zero admission or position credit**.

1.1.56 is not a slogan that restores all old sounds or ignores all
new ones. K distinguishes sthāni-al-dependent operations and preserves
the substitute's own properties. 1.1.57 additionally has its specific
paranimitta/pūrvavidhi conditions; “pūrva” is not just an earlier
sūtra number. A wider positive source and its rule-relative view would
still be needed to settle that hypothetical unshortened profile.
The selected H02 profiles already have ṛ and do not enter this fork.

## C03 — Retained hi uses the relevant view, not just the final ī

K 6.4.105's counterexamples are लुनीहि/पुनीहि, and it explains
that ī-substitution is asiddha there, leaving original long ā in
that rule's view. The short-a requirement still fails. For H03's
short-root śnā branch the same argument retains hi. 6.4.106 also
fails: the vikaraṇa is not the required u-ending affix. This is a
rule-relative 6.4.22 argument, not merely “the final string has ī”.

For a separately admitted śānac control such as मुषाण, the affix
really has final short a, and 6.4.105 can elide hi. K/Tattvabodhinī
3.1.83 explain that contrasting construction. Śānac remains an
affix substitute with its own markers and designation obligations.
