## H02. Six meanings do not make six independent morphology tables

Kāśikā, Kaumudī, Bālamanoramā and Nyāsa distinguish the six terms of
1.3.47. **Upasaṃbhāṣā** and **upamantraṇa** are senses of the verbal use;
bhāsana, jñāna, yatna and vimati qualify its discourse or intended setting.
They cannot all be flattened into a string prefix or a generic “speaking” bit.

| Term | Selected source interpretation and witness | Count treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bhāsana | Illuminating discourse; śāstre vadate, including supported responses to objections | P1 |
| Upasaṃbhāṣā | Upasāntvana, conciliating; bhṛtyān/karmakarān upavadate | P2 |
| Jñāna | Understanding that supports the speech; śāstre vadate | Separate semantic certificate, P1 morphology, zero extra credit |
| Yatna | Utsāha revealed regarding a field/house; kṣetre/gehe vadate | Separate semantic certificate, P1 morphology, zero extra credit |
| Vimati | Nānāmati/incompatible dealings; kṣetre vivadante | P3, with the stated event restriction |
| Upamantraṇa | Upacchandana/entreaty; upavadate | Separate recipient/sense certificate, P2 morphology, zero extra credit |

The root's generic “clear/articulated speech” gloss does not discharge these
conditions. Conversely 1.3.47 has no universal akarmaka restriction: the
conciliation/entreaty witnesses retain recipients construed as objects, while
topic locatives and pragmatically revealed effort have their own roles.
The control yatkiṃcid vadati supplies no licence merely from the verb's
presence. The aliases above reuse word morphology only, not the same meaning
or identical role tensor. They do not silently enlarge the counted grid.

## H03. Joint utterance needs a participant condition in addition to the root gloss

Kāśikā 1.3.48 inherits vad and interprets samuccāraṇa as sahoccāraṇa.
Its conventional vyaktavāc participant scope is human speakers; **saṃpravadante
brāhmaṇāḥ/kṣatriyāḥ** are the positive witnesses. **Saṃpravadanti kukkuṭāḥ**
and the single-speaker **brāhmaṇo vadati** are local controls. Kaumudī
explicitly says manuṣyāṇām and contrasts birds. Nyāsa/Padamañjarī discuss
recognizable animal calls and learned parrot speech while retaining that
traditional scope. No modern biological impossibility claim follows.

Thus vad's lexical vyaktāyāṃ vāci cannot stand in for a human-agent
certificate. The counted sam+pra sequence is an actual source witness, not
an extra universal prefix requirement of 1.3.48. P4 excludes vipralāpa so
that H05's optional branch is not accidentally lost in a mandatory A label.

## H04. Anu keeps the participant guard but drops joint utterance

Kāśikā and Bālamanoramā 1.3.49 retain vad and vyaktavācām while explicitly
dropping samuccāraṇa. They admit **anuvadate kaṭhaḥ kalāpasya** and the
Maudga/Paippalāda parallel. Anu expresses similarity: the speaking resembles
the comparison party's speaking/study; it need not be simultaneous with it.
Akarmaka concerns this verbal construal, not the absence of a printed
accusative. The compared person and similarity relation remain in the input.

The source's repeated-yajus example **pūrvam eva yajur uditam anuvadati**
has a karman and fails this rule. **Anuvadati vīṇā** fails the participant
condition. These are local source controls with other A licences absent;
neither proves that every transitive anu+vad use is universally P.

Bālamanoramā first offers the tulyārtha genitive rule 2.3.72, then rejects
that explanation for the avyaya anu and selects **śeṣa genitive**. Preserve
the complete field, including the correction; Tattvabodhinī/Padamañjarī also
retain the similarity relation. This packet does not count noun declensions
or turn the genitive into a new karman. No joint-speech guard is copied from
H03 into P5.

## H05. Vipralāpa restores joint utterance and admits a local P/A option

Kāśikā 1.3.50 explicitly retains vyaktavācām **samuccāraṇe** and defines
vipralāpa as mutually contradictory speech. It gives **vipravadante /
vipravadanti sāṃvatsarāḥ**, with mauhūrta and Kaumudī's vaidya parallels.
It calls this **prāptavibhāṣā**: A is already available from 1.3.48.
Nyāsa confirms that explanation. No contradiction gives mandatory
saṃpravadante in the source's joint human example; birds and speech occurring
krameṇa are its local negative controls. Sequence alone is not simultaneous
joint utterance.

The counted vi+pra chain witnesses the rule; 1.3.50 is not rewritten as
requiring those prefixes in every conceivable use. The option is scoped to
this event certificate, not all vad speech or all disagreement. P3's vimati
certificate is deliberately outside this vipralāpa event; it is not a global
priority decision for overlapping semantic domains.

The electronic **Tattvabodhinī 13050** instead literally reads
“विभाषा विप्रलापे । अप्राप्तविभाषा ।”. This classification differs from
the explicit Kāśikā/Nyāsa explanation. Count the latter policy, corroborated
by Kaumudī's P/A witnesses; preserve the Tattvabodhinī field without silently
repairing it. Reconciliation or independent edition collation is open.
The local anuvṛtti map is consequently **48: joint; 49: not required;
50: joint again**, rather than one monotonically inherited boolean.
