# PG27 — Vac's source filter and vid's coupled auxiliary

Author: Monty. 3 November 2026.

## A1. A grammatical construction still needs a usage license

Kaumudī 3.4.111's vac paragraph records three usage readings:
anti-family restriction, all-plural restriction, and jhi-family restriction.
Tattvabodhinī distinguishes them explicitly. The **selected PG27 view
is the first, anti-family reading**. In I/B it excludes the 3pl antu
continuation. O 3pl instead receives jus by 3.4.108 and does not enter
that anti family on this view. Its admitted result vacyuḥ is a written
consequence of this source selection, not a newly found literal word witness.

| Source view | I 3pl | O 3pl | B 3pl | Other plural slots |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| anti-family, selected | Excluded | admitted | Excluded | admitted |
| original jhi-family | Excluded | Excluded | Excluded | admitted |
| every plural | Excluded | Excluded | Excluded | Excluded |

These are labelled source policies, not alternatives to print together
and not freely switchable features of one fixed input. The source host
3.4.111 is not itself the vac usage rule; no new positive application
of its laṅ-jus operation is counted here. Prefix, Vedic and other
formation contexts are outside this basic-kartari claim.

The old PG11 present manifest included all nine vac slots and its
implementation comparison included vacanti. A comparison label cannot
override this newly applied source restriction. **Current admitted
coverage removes that one basic laṭ 3pl cell**: carry PG26's historical
9,460 forward with a **−1 scope correction**, then add the 997 newly
admitted PG27 positions, giving **10,456**. Frozen PG11 and PG26 records
remain unchanged and dated. Their historical counts are not described
as if this later source filter had already been applied.

The two new Excluded loṭ slots are not counted as new admitted positions.
All 999 PG27 slot requests nevertheless have a written disposition on
the selected view. Missing information and a source-declared exclusion
remain different native channels.

## A2. The optional 3.1.41 package

For exact class-2 vid in knowing under I/B, Kāśikā 3.1.41 prescribes
the following optional package:

1. Introduce ām on vid and retain its owner.
2. Suppress the otherwise available root guṇa in this branch, yielding
   **vidām**, not vedām.
3. Record luk of the original loṭ ending while retaining its request,
   controller, semantic profile and source history.
4. Append the matching loṭ construction of **kṛ** as its auxiliary.

The four clauses belong to one nipātana. They are not four independent
optional switches. The simple vid branch remains available in every
admitted I/B position. No such optional branch is introduced for O.
The ordinary laṭ ending option under 3.4.83 is also not transported to
loṭ or O merely because the root is vid.

Kāśikā explicitly generalizes its cited kurvantu to all persons/numbers
and explicitly gives vidāṅkurutāt. PG27 retains that all-slot reading;
Padamañjarī also records dissent about the generalization. Tattvabodhinī's
prefixed reciprocal A examples belong to a different semantic/pada domain
and are not additional profiles here. Bhū/as auxiliaries from liṭ are
not added to this loṭ-specific package.

## A3. Controller-matched auxiliary reuse

Let κ=(modal meaning, P, person, number). Reuse the previously written
basic kṛ loṭ constructor only with the same κ. Its ordinary tuple is

\[
 (karotu,kurutām,kurvantu,kuru,kurutam,kuruta,
   karavāṇi,karavāva,karavāma).
\]

In B add **kurutāt** at 3sg and 2sg, keeping the corresponding ordinary
word. The auxiliary supplies the finite exponent of the original vid
request; it does not introduce nine independent controller choices.
Śnam from class-7 vid cannot enter this class-2 construction.

Thus I 3sg has simple **vettu** plus the vidām + karotu construction;
I 2sg has **viddhi** plus vidām + kuru. B 3sg/2sg additionally have
simple **vittāt** and vidām + kurutāt. O retains **vidyāt** and its
ordinary weak paradigm. Native assembly and zero-specialization preserve
these construction branches rather than identifying them by a root name.

## A4. Two members and the external nasal seam

Vidām and the auxiliary keep separate member boundaries. The original
tiṅ's total luk retains the relevant pada designation under the admitted
1.1.62/1.4.14 interface; it does not restore its sounds. This differs
from treating partial s-lopa as restoration of a historical consonant.

Every auxiliary starts with k. At this supplied saṃhitā seam,
8.3.23 gives final m→anusvāra. **8.4.59**, retaining 8.4.58's
parasavarṇa prescription, permits either retention or homorganic ṅ:

\[
 \operatorname{Join}(vidām,a)
   =\{vidāṃ\mathbin{\cdot}a,\ vidāṅ\mathbin{\cdot}a\}.
\]

The dot marks the retained member boundary; it is not a spoken segment.
For example, vidāṃ·karotu/vidāṅ·karotu and
vidāṃ·kurutāt/vidāṅ·kurutāt. This is external nasal optionality,
distinct from internal 8.4.58's mandatory readout and from 8.4.65's
consonant collapse. Optional pronunciation doubling remains excluded.
This seam adds no further independent finite-verb position.

Each ordinary auxiliary position therefore adds **two readings**. I
adds 18; B adds 18 plus four for its two auxiliary tāt slots. Vid has
27 I readings, nine O readings and 33 B readings over its 27 admitted
positions. A source policy that fixes only the ṅ spelling would have a
different readout count and is not silently substituted for this one.
