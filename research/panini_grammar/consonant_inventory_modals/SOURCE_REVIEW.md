## R1. Num and lexical identity

Kāśikā 7.1.58 locates num at the teaching-form stage and explains why
irit is not automatically idit. Kaumudī/Bālamanoramā 3.4.112 give
cakṣ's traditional nonfinal-i marker account without num, and 7.2.78
distinguishes the short-idit kas/vṛj entries from their non-idit/long-ī
counterparts. PG11's contrary modern cakṣ annotation remains visible.

Bālamanoramā gives ṛ-upadhā pṛj but does not supply PG11's missing
exact full-word witness. Kaumudī's **पृङ्क्त** is retained as printed;
it is not silently changed into पृङ्क्ते. The new modal pṛṅktām is
a written rule consequence, not a correction to that historical witness.

## R3. Single-intervener permission and anusvāra's hal status

Kāśikā, Kaumudī, Bālamanoramā and Nyāsa 8.3.58 explicitly explain
the **separate**, not combined, num/visarga/śar permissions. They use
niṃssva as a negative example because num-origin anusvāra and root s
both intervene. S2 constructs the bounded window cut and target-owned
replacement, including its single-intervener positive control windows;
it does not add complete nominal paradigms.

Padamañjarī 8.3.5 explicitly treats anusvāra as hal for optional
8.4.65 reduction of following ss. Together with Kāśikā/Kaumudī/
Laghukaumudī 8.4.65, this supplies S4's two non-doubled readings of
short-idit kas/nis at loṭ 2sg. The host sam/suṭ operation of 8.3.5
is not applied or counted as new operative reach. The collapse follows
the earlier ṣatva decision; no early query is rerun on its shortened
word. PG11's old option counts are not silently claimed to have
included this later written sound-choice analysis.

among the rejected forms on the first reading and explains how the

pretending the resulting vacyuḥ is a separately quoted word witness.

The current rollup therefore has the explicit **−1** scope correction;

## R5. Vid's loṭ construction and the seam policy

Kāśikā, Kaumudī, Bālamanoramā, Nyāsa and Laghukaumudī 3.1.41
supply ām, guṇa prohibition, original-loṭ luk and loṭ-kṛ as one
optional package. Kāśikā expressly generalizes all persons/numbers and
gives the tāt example. Padamañjarī records dissent about that scope;
the selected generalization is not described as unanimous commentary
agreement. Tattvabodhinī discusses an alternative account of the luk
through 2.4.81 and prefixed reciprocal A uses. PG27 adopts the explicit
3.1.41 nipātana account, not those outside-domain constructions.

Kāśikā 1.1.62/1.4.14 supplies the retained total-tiṅ-lopa/pada
interface. 8.3.23 and 8.4.58/59 then distinguish a pada-final nasal
seam from internal nasal readout. Both anusvāra and homorganic ṅ are
kept before auxiliary k. Source citation spelling with ṅ does not
silently remove the licensed 8.4.59 choice.

The source's special laṭ ending option 3.4.83 is not an operation of
PG27. Nor does the host 3.4.111 source location add its laṅ operation.
New operative IDs are **3.1.41, 8.3.58 and 8.4.59** on the explicitly
bounded construction/query/seam domains.
