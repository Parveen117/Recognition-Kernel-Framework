# PG27 — Consonant contacts, single-intervener scope and late collapse

Author: Monty. 3 November 2026.

## S1. Actual contact retains constituent ownership

Apply PG7/11/22/24's bounded readout to M's actual segments. Before
jhal, root c/j receives its velar under 8.2.30. Before an ending s,
the resulting ku trigger licenses 8.3.59 ṣatva. Before dh, 8.4.53
supplies the voiced stop; before khar, 8.4.55 supplies its voiceless
counterpart. Thus nij gives niṅktām/niṅkṣva/niṅgdhvam, while
vac gives vaktu/vagdhi. Actual modal y/ī blocks these ordinary
root-to-tiṅ contact queries.

Kaś's ś instead receives ṣ under 8.2.36. Before t this gives ṣṭ;
before s, 8.2.41 gives k and the affix s becomes ṣ. Before dh it
gives ḍḍh through the inherited ṣṭutva/voicing view. Hence
kaṣṭām, kakṣva and kaḍḍhvam. The pair ḍḍh follows a vowel,
so it has no 8.4.65 collapse on this non-doubled profile.

Cakṣ first has the 8.2.29 k-lopa at its root kṣ cluster before
the actual jhal-initial ending. This yields caṣṭām and caḍḍhvam.
At sva, the *remaining root ṣ* then becomes k by 8.2.41 and the
ending s becomes ṣ. The result **cakṣva** resembles the original
root's kṣ but has different sound-source owners: original k is in
Smriti, current k descends from original ṣ, and current ṣ belongs
to the ending. No root-preserving shortcut identifies those histories.

For the s-final roots ās, kas and nis, use PG11/24's explicitly
selected broad affix-dh reading of 8.2.25. Direct loṭ dhvam deletes
root s: ādhvam, kadhvam, kandhvam and nindhvam. In the last two,
root num survives and has dental readout. O's modal ī separates root
s from dhvam and blocks this direct-contact deletion. Kāśikā's narrower
sic reading remains a recorded source disagreement.

## S2. 8.3.58 admits each listed intervener, not arbitrary combinations

For a completed ṣatva query retain target s's ādeśa/pratyaya owner,
the preceding iṇ/ku trigger and the *ordered* intervening record.
In addition to direct contact, the selected 8.3.58 contract admits one
listed intervening kind: num-origin anusvāra, visarga or śar. It does
not close this permission under concatenation of different interveners.

On the bounded window set

\[
 \mathcal W=\{\varnothing,[num],[visarga],[śar],[num,śar],other\},
\]

the admitted extension cut is 1 on the first four fields and 0 on the
last two, provided the trigger/target-owner premises hold. For the true
fields, replace only the identified target s by ṣ. Kāśikā's sarpīṃṣi,
sarpīḥṣu and sarpiṣṣu illustrate the respective single-intervener
windows; these are local query controls, not new nominal paradigms or
additional verbal positions. Missing provenance gives ?, not 0.

For exact nis, the loṭ sva query sees i + **num-origin anusvāra +
root s** + ending s. Both Kāśikā and Bālamanoramā expressly reject
ṣatva here and give **niṃssva**. Dental root s is not silently changed
to ś or ṣ. Kas with num also retains dental ss; its a is not an iṇ
trigger in the first place. Ordinary kas/ās have no earlier iṇ/ku
trigger either. By contrast īr's r immediately before ending s gives
īrṣva.

## S3. Nasal history and ṇatva

Before actual jhal, 8.3.24 hides nonfinal n as anusvāra. Weak śnam's
deleted a is not restored for this query under the inherited 1.1.58
contract. Later 8.4.58 supplies the nasal appropriate to the following
yAy consonant: niṅktām versus niñjātām, riṅktām versus riñcantu,
and kṣunttām versus strong kṣuṇattu. Before s, anusvāra remains.

Strong śnam n before its surviving a is visible to 8.4.1/2. The
eligible new stems are **riṇac, kṣuṇad, chṛṇad, tṛṇad, kṛṇat,
vṛṇaj and pṛṇac**. Weak late nasal restoration cannot feed that
earlier ṇatva query again. Num's n before a root-final palatal is
likewise already hidden at the earlier query; vṛñj is not vṛṇj.

All D3 first-person ni endings retain dental n: the intervening root
palatal/dental stop blocks a further r/ṣ-to-ni query. Ordinary plural
ending n also retains the inherited late dental restoration before t.
The final n of O A ran remains dental under 8.4.37. No O A dhvam
retroflexion from an ārdhadhātuka ṣīdhvam context is admitted.

## S4. Complete local collapse choices

After the fixed contacts, use 8.4.65: a jhar immediately after hal
and before a savarṇa jhar may disappear. A nasal is not the deleted
jhar. Nasal + tt or nasal + ddh therefore admits the root-stop
deletion while preserving the ending's consonant. The strong doubled
stop following a vowel does not qualify.

There is at most one eligible collapse site per constructed word.
The separate two-member vid construction has A4's external nasal
option and no additional internal collapse site. Optional consonant
doubling and unrestricted multi-boundary phonetics remain outside D.
