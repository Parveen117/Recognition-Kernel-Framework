# PG27 — Num, śnam and the target of deletion

Author: Monty. 3 November 2026.

## M1. Lexical num precedes ending strength

For D2's seven exact short-idit entries, 7.1.58 introduces an owned num
after the last root vowel under 1.1.47. The pronounced n remains part
of the root history after marker-lopa. Cakṣ's nonfinal teaching i does
not meet the selected terminal-idit interpretation. Long ī in vṛjī̃
and pṛcī̃ is also not short i. Retain all contrary lexical annotations.

Thus the morphological stems are kans, nins, ninj, śinj, pinj, pṛnj
and vṛnj; their eventual anusvāra/palatal/velar nasals belong to S's
readout. Num is not an inflectional weak/strong switch. Its n occupies
the upadhā in the palatal-final roots, blocking a false 7.3.86 guṇa
of their earlier i/ṛ at strong first persons. Hence niñjai and vṛñjai,
not manufactured neñjai or varñjai.

Without num, class-2 vṛj and pṛc have a light ṛ upadhā: strong
āṭ first persons use vr̥j→vraj and pṛc→parc under 7.3.86 with
1.1.51's r accompaniment. Their other I/B slots and all O retain ṛ.
Class-2 vid similarly has ved before ordinary strong endings, but vid
before weak endings. Its optional ām branch has the separate prohibition
in A2. Vac's a, īr's long ī and ās's long ā require no such guṇa.

## M2. Śnam changes the aṅga query

For D3, 3.1.78 replaces śap with śnam. Retain one root and one mit
affix; place pronounced na after the root's last vowel by 1.1.47.
The resulting root-with-mit layout is the aṅga relative to tiṅ. Reuse
PG7's specific nitya insertion-before-laghūpadha-guṇa account. Do not
query guṇa on the isolated pre-infix root and then insert śnam.

Examples before phonetic readout:

| Root | With strong śnam | With weak śnam |
|---|---|---|
| ric | ri-na-c | ri-n-c |
| kṣud | kṣu-na-d | kṣu-n-d |
| vṛj, class 7 | vṛ-na-j | vṛ-n-j |
| indh | i-na-n-dh → i-na-dh | i-n-dh |
| añj | a-na-n-j → a-na-j | a-n-j |

6.4.111 deletes only śnam's a before effective kit/ṅit/ṅitvat
sārvadhātuka. It retains that a at ordinary strong P 3sg and at āṭ
first persons, including A. Thus A indh has **inadhai/inadhāvahai/
inadhāmahai**, not a reused weak indh stem in every slot.

## M3. Root n and affix n cannot be merged

For indh, bhañj, und, añj and tañc, 6.4.23 deletes the original
root-owned n immediately after identified śnam. It does not delete
śnam's n. Apply this in the retained layout before weak a-lopa; the
early root n has not yet acquired a late palatal pronunciation.

The operation also matters in strong contexts: indh→inadh at āṭ
cannot be justified by 6.4.24's weak-ending condition. Bālamanoramā
6.4.23 expressly identifies that distinction. PG27 uses the particular
6.4.23 contract, without claiming a general interchangeability of the
two n-lopa rules or a universal asiddhavat schedule.

In a weak indh output, the printed n has changed ownership: the original
root n is in Smriti; the surviving n is śnam's. Printing the same
letters as the original root does not mean that nothing happened.
Likewise weak class-2 idit vṛj and class-7 vṛj can print vṛñj
while retaining different nasal owners and different strong continuations.

## M4. Actual weak consonants and hi

All D roots are consonant-final after their licensed preprocessing.
At ordinary P 2sg, 6.4.101 replaces hi with dhi before the consonant
readout. This includes vac→vagdhi, vid→viddhi, ric→riṅgdhi and
kṛt→kṛnddhi/kṛndhi. No root/affix u-hi-luk route is relevant here.

The selected B tāt is a whole ending replacement under 7.1.35 and
has its own weak marker state. It is not a hi form followed by tāt.
Thus strong kṣuṇattu alternates in B with weak kṣunttāt/kṣuntāt;
the strong stem is not copied into that option. M2/M3 are reevaluated
for the chosen ending, and S counts any ensuing collapse locally.

O uses weak stems throughout. In vac, 6.1.15 requires kit; the O
ṅit/ṅitvat interface does not supply it. Hence the admitted O readings
use **vacyāt**, not an imported āśīrliṅ **ucyāt**. Vid's ordinary
O uses vidyāt; class-7 vid A uses vindīta. The actual modal y/ī
intervenes before final tiṅ, so a final-tiṅ-only jhal/dh query cannot
skip it and trigger an ordinary contact operation on the root.

## M5. Finite stem partition

The D2 partition is num/non-num plus exact lexical guṇa conditions;
the D3 partition is strong/weak śnam plus the five original-root-n
deletions. There are no optional morphological stem choices in these
main paths. A2 supplies vid's independent *whole-construction* choice;
S supplies the explicitly admitted local sound options. These stages,
with A1's usage filter, exhaust the selected domain without a software
form table or a new grammar runtime.
