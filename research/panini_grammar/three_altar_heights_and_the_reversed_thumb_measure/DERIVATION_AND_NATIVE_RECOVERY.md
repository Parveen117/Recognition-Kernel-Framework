# PG785 · The standard and the thing measured

At 5.2.37 the Bhāṣya makes pramāṇa qualify the supplied base. The possessor of the measure is the referent of the derivative. The first two affixes concern a vertical extent, and mātrac also applies to a volume. The Kāśikā distinguishes conventional measure names with deleted affix, necessary luk on dvigu and the supplementary mātrac in doubt. A deleted measure affix still has a recorded occasion and base. PG755’s half-month, month and Aṅgiras cases retain their previous owner. Bhaṭṭi’s palm is a height standard applied to the approaching person. PG782 recorded the literary locator. Its case panel credited the breadth predicate alone, so the single masculine agreement on the distinct palm epithet is new here.

In TS 5.6.8.2–3 the same building prescription first counts bricks and then measures height. Bhaṭṭabhāskara supplies agnim in construing the prescription. The three height adjectives accordingly have accusative masculine readings with a common understood object. Morphology in isolation could also yield neuter -am. The three metres are instrumental. The distinct successive destinations are the world here, the intervening space and the world yonder. The Pada separates all three height words. Bhaṭṭabhāskara expressly invokes 5.2.37 and accounts for the short a in grīva- by 6.3.63. The Āpastamba commentary labels height and brick count independently. Its landmark in the third height gloss is āsya. The Vārāha print at p. 78 has चबुक at that position. These witnesses cannot be superimposed as if the Saṃhitā offered three spellings of one word. In Taittirīya Āraṇyaka 1.25.1 an altar bed is dug to knee depth. Its overt feminine ending governs the adjective. This gives a second direction of vertical extent on a different object.

At 5.2.38 aṇ is added to the permitted choices with a human or elephant as standard. The first syllable grades. On the elephant route Nyāsa appeals to 6.4.164 for retention of n in hāstina. Where a numeral supplies the first member the inner affix is selected and elided. For a moat measured by people, 4.1.24 yields both the long ā and the long ī forms. Its own commentary distinguishes this measure from being purchased and from aggregation. For the elephant measurement the n-final feminine has its own 4.1.5 operation. In Kaṭha 2.1.12 puruṣa and the thumb occupy the reverse semantic positions to the human-measure example. Size is predicated of puruṣa. The standard is aṅguṣṭha. The text gives no modern unit conversion.

PG271’s relation already spells out cinvīta as well as cinute for the bare root. The three building directions and the separate Āraṇyaka present provide a primary comparison with zero repeated finite credit. The two new active predicates have different prefix histories. For ji, guṇa gives e before the class vowel and 6.1.78 realizes ay before a. The Padapāṭha records abhi apart from jayati. For ruh, grade of u gives o before the class-one present ending sequence, with ā retained before r and i of the preceding abhi becoming y at the vowel junction. Its observed third-person singular is abhyārohati. The three instruments and destinations belong to separate repetitions of this predicate. They do not furnish three new finite positions.
