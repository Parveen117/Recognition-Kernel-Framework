# PG22 — Deleted vikaraṇas, internal śnam and modal ending features

Author: Monty. 2 November 2026.

## S1. Actual marker query

In ordinary loṭ P, 3sg and all first-person endings have the strong
query; the other five are weak. In loṭ A, the three first-person
endings are strong and the remaining six weak. This follows PG19's
3.4.87 apit hi and **3.4.92 āṭ plus pit**, not a fixed laṭ slot list.

All O endings are weak: P has 3.4.103's explicit ṅit even in the
originally pit slots; A is apit sārvadhātuka by 1.2.4. An inherited
pit flag by itself cannot decide 6.4.111/112/113. On a B tātaṅ
branch, PG20's source-specific ṅit/pit override gives the weak query.

The root/vikaraṇa/tiṅ boundaries remain distinct. A rule must query
the owned ending and its actual prescriptions, not a string labelled
“imperative” or “optative”. Source-specific prohibitions remain attached.

## S2. Adādi: retain the śap-luk record

2.4.72 wholly deletes śap on the exact ad/as entries, retaining its
identity and luk operation. PG8's 1.1.63 restriction prevents a deleted
śap from licensing an otherwise unavailable guṇa. The actual outer tiṅ
is available for its own query; no thematic a is manufactured.

Ad remains ad before sound readout on every admitted slot. As retains
its a in the strong loṭ contexts, but **6.4.111** removes that root-owned
a in the weak contexts. Thus ordinary 3sg is as∣tu, weak 3du is
s∣tām, and O starts from s∣yā. The 2sg e/dhi path is H's own
source-qualified continuation. PG8's laṭ sip-specific s deletion is
not copied into every modal ending.

## S3. Juhotyādi: copy before strength, retain both owners

2.4.75 gives śap-ślu; 6.1.10 licenses the bounded root copy, with
6.1.4/5 naming the earlier occurrence abhyāsa and the pair abhyasta.
PG9's 7.4.59/60/62 and 8.4.54 procedures give the owned interfaces
**ju∣hu**, **da∣dā**, **da∣dhā**. Copy UIDs and copied-from links
remain separate from original root UIDs. Strength never mutates the copy
merely because it strengthened the original root.

Hu's original root u takes guṇa before strong loṭ endings: ju∣ho∣tu
gives juhotu; ju∣ho∣ā∣ni gives juhavāni by 6.1.78. Weak vowel
contexts have 6.4.87's v: ju∣hu∣atu gives juhvatu. Weak consonant
contexts retain u, including O's yā and H's lexical hu→dhi context.
This u belongs to the **root**, so 6.4.106/107's affix-u operations
cannot erase hi or add optional u-lopa here.

Dā/dhā are the exact ghu entries of 1.1.20. Weak 6.4.112 removes
the original root ā before vowels **and consonants**, since 6.4.113
expressly excludes ghu. The ordinary weak interfaces are da∣d and
da∣dh. Strong loṭ preserves ā. H's special hi route supersedes the
generic weak-ā-lopa completion and supplies e plus whole-abhyāsa lopa.

No ślu/reduplication is invented on as merely because 6.4.119 also
mentions it. That rule's abhyāsa-lopa component is applicable only where
an abhyāsa actually exists.

## S4. Rudhādi: an infix inside one retained root

3.1.78 and 1.1.47 put śnam after the last root vowel. Retain one
original root, the distinct mit affix and their interleaved layout. PG7's
Kāśikā/Kaumudī/Bālamanoramā contract gives śnam nitya precedence
over the competing root light-upadhā guṇa; after placement, the old root
vowel is no longer the aṅga upadhā. It is insufficient simply to treat
the left fragment as an ik-final root before a following śit suffix.

| Root | Strong owned layout | Weak layout after 6.4.111 |
|---|---|---|
| rudh | ru∣na∣dh | ru∣n∣dh |
| bhid | bhi∣na∣d | bhi∣n∣d |
| chid | chi∣na∣d | chi∣n∣d |
| yuj | yu∣na∣j | yu∣n∣j |
| śiṣ | śi∣na∣ṣ | śi∣n∣ṣ |
| piṣ | pi∣na∣ṣ | pi∣n∣ṣ |
| hiṃs | hi∣na∣s | hi∣n∣s |

In hiṃs, the idit input first receives root-owned num by 7.1.58.
After śnam placement, **6.4.23** deletes that following root n.
The new n of śnam remains. Resolve that root-n query before weak
śnam-a lopa under the inherited local 6.4.22 policy. The two deletions
have distinct targets and are not a rule deleting every visible nasal.

All late n/ṇ/ṅ/ñ/ṃ and stop changes belong to C's readout, with
each predecessor owner retained. Readout cannot become a new lexical root.

## S5. Endings and complete optative factors

Retain PG19's tiṅ owners, loṭ substitutions and resolved O augments:
P yā after non-final s-lopa, A īy. Retain **3.4.107**'s suṭ on
the addressed t/th of either pada as well as the distinct yāsuṭ/sīyuṭ
owners. Sārvadhātuka **7.2.79** removes the admitted non-final s
from both the augment and the internal suṭ position; the lopa histories
remain. PG20's āśīrliṅ two-s schedule is not used here.

In loṭ P 3pl, **7.1.4** gives at after the abhyasta hu/dā/dhā,
whereas ad/as/rudhādi take ant. A uses at on this non-short-a domain.
In O P 3pl, **3.4.108 jus overrides the ordinary ant/at route**,
including after abhyasta; Kāśikā 7.1.4 states that jus blocks at.
Thus juhvatu and juhuyuḥ have different actual ending routes.

For P define the full tuple

\[
 O_P(K)=(Kyāt,Kyātām,Kyuḥ,Kyāḥ,Kyātam,Kyāta,Kyām,Kyāva,Kyāma).
\]

For A define

\[
 O_A(J)=(Jīta,Jīyātām,Jīran,Jīthāḥ,Jīyāthām,Jīdhvam,
 Jīya,Jīvahi,Jīmahi).
\]

P's 3pl seam uses **6.1.96**: bhind∣yā∣us → bhind∣y∣us,
then bhindyuḥ with the inherited final-s readout. The y remains;
it is the non-padānta ā plus us that receives pararūpa. A's
augment y is deleted before consonants and retained before vowels; ran's
final n remains dental. These non-short-a factors do not use 7.2.80's
thematic iy. PG19's resolved-augment schedule and dental -īdhvam
remain; no āśīrliṅ dh/ḍh branch is imported.

P2 lists every K/J factor after C's ordered phonetic projection. For
example dadhīdhvam has an intervening augment vowel before dhv, so it
does not satisfy dhā's direct dhv trigger of 8.2.38.

## S6. Stem/ending exhaustion

**Proposition S6.** The two luk, three ślu and seven śnam roots
have a complete owned pre-readout continuation at every D2 slot.

**Proof.** S1 fixes actual ending features. S2–S4 exhaust the three
disjoint root families and their exceptional operations; S5 exhausts the
ending/augment series and at/ant/jus choices. H resolves the remaining
hi target and B substitute; C resolves their finite sound continuations. □
