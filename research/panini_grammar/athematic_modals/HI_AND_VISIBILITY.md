# PG22 — Hi, dhi, etva and the retained source view

Author: Monty. 2 November 2026.

## H1. Address the actual ending first

The input is a native history with an owned P 2sg hi from 3.4.87,
its apit prescription, exact lexical identity and the source/augment view.
For B, select the ordinary ending or PG20's whole tātaṅ replacement
before resolving the dependent stem and hi queries. Retaining original
hi in memory does not make actual tāt a new target of a hi rule.

On the ordinary branch **6.4.101** substitutes dhi for the whole hi
after hu or a jhal-final aṅga in the admitted consonant-initial context.
Kāśikā gives juhudhi, bhinddhi and chinddhi, and contrasts krīṇīhi.
Its rudi-hi/svapi-hi examples retain h after intervening īṭ; a root's
historical consonant alone cannot bypass an actual augment boundary.
Those additional roots and their īṭ pathways remain outside this packet.

The literal hu disjunct matters: hu ends in u, not jhal, but still
admits juhudhi. Conversely hu's root u is not śnu/u-affix u and does
not satisfy PG21's 6.4.106 hi-luk. The ending owner and root identity
must both remain available.

## H2. Ghu and as receive e on the ordinary hi branch

**6.4.119** has two different targets: final-sound etva on the exact
ghu/as aṅga, and whole-abhyāsa lopa where an abhyāsa exists. The
final-sound scope follows 1.1.52. Kāśikā and Tattvabodhinī explain
the lopa's śit interpretation and hence the whole target under 1.1.55.
Do not delete only the last sound of da, or delete an imagined copy of as.

For the two admitted ghu roots the source construction is

| Input to the special hi route | Owned operations | Result |
|---|---|---|
| da∣dā∣hi | original root ā→e; whole copied da deleted | dehi |
| da∣dhā∣hi | original root ā→e; whole copied da deleted | dhehi |

The full copy identity survives as Smriti after lopa. This special hi
route does not first commit the generic 6.4.112 weak-ā-lopa outcome
and then attempt to recover e from a now-consonantal string. The original
root final is ā for the addressed 6.4.101 query; etva does not turn
these into dhi forms.

As has a different path. Final **s**, not a, receives e by 6.4.119;
6.4.111 separately deletes the root a. The addressed 6.4.101 query
still sees the earlier s and replaces hi by dhi, giving **edhi**.
Its two root positions and its two operations remain distinct.

## H3. Why visible e cannot answer the dhi query

Kāśikā **6.4.22** explicitly uses edhi and śādhi: etva/śābhāva
are asiddha to the indicated jhal-based dhi query. Nyāsa 6.4.119
likewise identifies the s→e change and its invisibility to that query.
PG22 admits this particular source-qualified dependency, not a global
rule that every later operation is hidden from every earlier one.

After etva and the applicable lopa, the local visible windows for
as and dā are both e∣hi. Their original queried finals are s and ā,
respectively. The first meets the jhal disjunct of 6.4.101 and the
second does not. The full words and lexical histories are different;
the equality is only of the displayed local observation.

**Proposition H3.** A current-window-only predicate on e∣hi cannot
decide the two source-qualified dhi admissions. Retaining the addressed
original sound view resolves this pair.

**Proof.** The window predicate would have one value on e∣hi, but
the source contracts require true for as and false for dā. Their retained
original finals supply precisely the missing distinction. N4 states the
same obstruction for the native cuts and their zero-reading. □

This is a failure of one declared observation. A procedure retaining
lexical identity plus the relevant rule history can reconstruct the view;
no impossibility claim about all surface analyses is intended.

## H5. Tātaṅ does not receive a second hi application

Write the teaching substitute as **tātaṅ**, its pronounced form as tāt.
Kāśikā 6.4.101 names juhutāt and bhintāt and excludes renewed dhi
after the later tātaṅ prescription. Kaumudī 6.4.119 explicitly excludes
etva on that branch and gives stāt. The inherited original hi/tu remains
in history but licenses neither e nor dhi on the actual tāt target.

All twelve tāt branches therefore reconstruct the weak stem: ad∣tāt,
s∣tāt, ju∣hu∣tāt, da∣d∣tāt, da∣dh∣tāt, or the appropriate weak
śnam interface. In particular dehi/dhehi/edhi do not become
*detāt/*dhetāt/*etāt. C then resolves the new junction rather than
copying the ordinary branch's phonetic flags.

H1–H5 exhaust the ordinary hi and admitted B replacements on D2.
Other 6.4.118/120 roots, īṭ-bearing adādi, Vedic variations and a
general ābhīya scheduler remain explicit later work.
