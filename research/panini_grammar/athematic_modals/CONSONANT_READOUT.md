# PG22 — Owned consonants, nasal order and dependent collapse

Author: Monty. 2 November 2026.

## C1. A bounded readout, with every predecessor retained

Readout takes a copy of S/H's completed owned morphology. Retain PG7's
non-doubling policy, the chosen pause convention and the source-qualified
local visibility decisions. Its main stages are the applicable 8.2 root/
ending operations; 8.3.24 nasal hiding; the admitted ṣ/ṇ/contact queries;
8.4.53/55 voicing/devoicing and 8.4.58 parasavarṇa; then optional
8.4.65 collapse. The ṣ∣dhi contact in C4 has its own declared local
source schedule. This is not a universal ascending-number algorithm.

A late result is never fed back as a fresh pre-Tripādī stem. In
particular a restored n cannot restart an earlier ṇ query. The deleted
śnam a is not supplied by sthānivadbhāva to the nasal readout, following
PG7's bounded 1.1.58/source contract.

## C2. Dhā's bhaṣ exception is lexical and local

On da∣dh after weak original-ā lopa, **8.2.38** makes the abhyāsa d
into dh before direct t/th and, by its inherited extension, s/dhv.
Kāśikā identifies reduplicated dadhāti, requires its actual jhaṣ-final
weak state, and expressly permits the relevant ā-lopa and abhyāsa-jaś
views for this query. These permissions are recorded, not inferred from
a blanket slogan about asiddha.

| Actual context | Bhaṣ and subsequent contact | Word |
|---|---|---|
| da∣dh∣tām | dha∣dh∣tām; root dh→t before t | dhattām |
| da∣dh∣sva | dha∣dh∣sva; root dh→t before s | dhatsva |
| da∣dh∣dhvam | dha∣dh∣dhvam; root dh→d | dhaddhvam |
| da∣dh∣yāt | no direct t/th/s/dhv | dadhyāt |
| da∣dh∣īdhvam | augment ī intervenes before dhv | dadhīdhvam |

**8.2.40** changes t/th after jhaṣ to dh but expressly excludes
dadhāti. Thus dhā gives dhattām/dhattāt while rudh gives
runddhām/runddhāt before optional collapse. This is not a shared
string rule “dh before t”. Strong dadhātu and first-person dadhā-
retain ā and fail 8.2.38's jhaṣ-final condition. Dā is not dhā and
has no bhaṣ operation: dattām, datsva, daddhvam.

## C3. Rudh, bhid, chid and yuj

Strong śnam retains its a. In rudh, r reaches the exposed infix n
through the admitted intervening sounds by 8.4.1–2, giving ruṇa-.
Weak śnam has n immediately before a root-final jhal: 8.3.24 first
gives anusvāra, hiding it from the earlier ṇ query. Later parasavarṇa
restores a nasal appropriate to the actual following stop.

Consequently ru∣na∣dh∣tu → **ruṇaddhu**, whereas
ru∣n∣dh∣antu → **rundhantu**, with dental n. Restoring the deleted
a or rerunning ṇatva after parasavarṇa would alter this source schedule.

Before t/th, rudh admits 8.2.40's dh on the ending, then jaś on
the preceding root dh: n∣dh∣tām → n∣d∣dhām. Bhid/chid instead
have root d before t and receive car, giving n∣t∣tām. Before dhi
or dhvam their final d remains voiced. Before sva these three dental
roots yield nt∣sva: **runtsva, bhintsva, chintsva**. The n and t
intervene between the root vowel and s; no adjacent iṇ/ku ṣatva
premise is supplied there.

Yuj has three different owned continuations:

| Following context | Root-final and nasal readout | Example |
|---|---|---|
| weak vowel or O y | original j remains; n before it gives ñ | yuñjantu, yuñjyāt |
| t/th | 8.2.30 j→g, car k; nasal becomes ṅ | yuṅktām |
| dhi/dhvam | j→g; nasal becomes ṅ | yuṅgdhi, yuṅgdhvam |
| sva | j→g→k; adjacent affix s→ṣ; nasal becomes ṅ | yuṅkṣva |
| strong vowel | internal na remains before j | yunajāni, yunajai |

The adjacent-affix ṣ query in the sva row uses PG7's source-qualified
velar view and 8.3.59. The weak dental n cannot be copied unchanged
into every surface word; nor may ku replace j before every vowel/y.

## C4. Śiṣ, piṣ and hiṃs keep their different sibilants

Śiṣ/piṣ have original root ṣ. Before t/th, contact gives ṣṭ;
before a vowel or y, ṣ remains. Anusvāra before that sibilant stays
ṃ because ṣ is not yay: **śiṃṣṭām, śiṃṣantu, śiṃṣyāt**.

The special dhi junction takes the Kaumudī 6.4.23 reading: jaś on
ṣ gives ḍ, and the admitted retroflex contact gives ḍh on dhi.
Nasal hiding/restoration then yields śiṇḍḍhi/piṇḍḍhi, with optional
ḍ deletion. Kaumudī explicitly prints both शिण्ढि and शिण्ड्ढि;
Kāśikā 8.4.65 explains the ḍ/ḍh collapse using śiṣ and piṣ.
This licenses this junction's contact order rather than pretending that
the main stage list alone proves every cross-stage interaction.

Strong first singular has the actual sequence śinaṣ∣ā∣ni or
pinaṣ∣ā∣ni. Root ṣ reaches ending n through ā, giving
**śinaṣāṇi, pinaṣāṇi**. The first n, in śnam before ṣ, remains dental.
No comparable final ṣ trigger exists in hiṃs.

Hiṃs enters as idit hisi, receives root-owned num, and then loses that
root n by S4's 6.4.23. Its surviving śnam n and original root s give
hi∣n∣s. Before t or a vowel the weak output is **hiṃstām/hiṃsantu**;
O is hiṃsyāt. The original s is neither an affix s nor a replacement s
licensed by the reviewed 8.3.59 contract, so it does not become ṣ simply
because the earlier vowel is i. In hi∣n∣s∣dhi, however, jaś makes
s into d, then parasavarṇa restores n: **hinddhi**, optionally hindhi.

## C5. The collapse cut and every ordinary site

For the selected non-doubling profile, 8.4.65 requires a preceding hal,
a deletable jhar and a following savarṇa jhar. The nasal is hal but not
jhar; the adjacent stop pair supplies the two jhars. A sibilant and stop
are not made savarṇa merely by sharing a place. Stop voicing/aspiration
need not match for the admitted pairs.

| Root(s) | Ordinary I/B slots with one collapse site | Sites across profiles |
|---|---|---:|
| rudh | P 3du, 2sg, 2du, 2pl; A 3sg, 2pl | 6 |
| bhid | same six slots | 6 |
| chid | same six slots | 6 |
| śiṣ, piṣ, hiṃs | each P 2sg | 3 |
| ad, as, hu, dā, dhā, yuj | none | 0 |
| **Total** | each site has keep/delete alternatives | **21** |

Examples are runddhām/rundhām, bhinttam/bhintam,
chinddhvam/chindhvam and śiṇḍḍhi/śiṇḍhi. Strong ruṇaddhu,
bhinattu and chinattu have a vowel before the pair; addhi, dattām
and dhaddhvam likewise fail the preceding-hal condition. Yuj's k∣t
and g∣dh pairs are not savarṇa. No O factor creates one of these sites.

## C6. Ending refinement can create a collapse site

Rudh ordinary P 3sg has ruṇaddhu: preceding a prevents 8.4.65.
Its B tātaṅ continuation is weak ru∣n∣dh∣tāt. After the admitted
ending/voicing/nasal steps it is runddhāt: now n precedes savarṇa
d∣dh, so both **runddhāt/rundhāt** occur. Bhid/chid similarly give
bhinttāt/bhintāt and chinttāt/chintāt.

This is a dependent branch construction:
ending choice → actual marker query → stem → sound site → keep/delete.
It is not a global choice of “collapsed spelling” attached to every root.
Copying the ordinary 3sg's false collapse flag to its tāt continuation
loses a source-licensed word. N5 makes that failure explicit.

There are two tāt words at each eligible P singular for these three
roots, and one for each of the other nine. The per-singular family
therefore has 3·2+9=15 words, giving **30 added readings** over the
twenty-four eligible B positions. Every admitted word has at most one
optional site; multiple interacting sites remain outside the claim.

## C7. Finite sound completion

**Proposition C7.** S/H's eighteen profiles admit exactly the C5
ordinary optional sites and the C6 tāt sites on the declared policy.

**Proof.** C2–C4 exhaust the root-final classes d/s/u/ā/dh/j/ṣ
and the ending contexts t, dh, s, vowel and O y/ī. At each resulting
cluster apply C5's three conjuncts. The listed sites meet all three;
the remaining clusters either have a preceding vowel, lack a jhar pair
or have a non-savarṇa pair. C6 repeats that classification after the
new weak stem rather than borrowing the ordinary classification. □
