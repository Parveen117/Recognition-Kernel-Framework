## Three visibility decisions kept separate

1. **Edhi:** H uses the explicit 6.4.22/119 source view to recover
   original s for 6.4.101 despite current e.
2. **Dhā:** C2 uses 8.2.38's stated permission for weak-ā lopa and
   abhyāsa jaś, with the exact lexical exclusion in 8.2.40.
3. **Rudh and śnam:** C retains PG7's non-restored deleted a and
   nasal-hiding-before-ṇatva order. C4 separately states the source's
   ṣ∣dhi contact sequence.

These are different addressed dependencies. Treating any one as permission
to expose or hide every previous event would exceed the evidence.

## Literal witnesses and anomalies

Kāśikā 6.4.101 directly prints juhudhi/bhinddhi/chinddhi and its
tātaṅ examples. Kāśikā 6.4.119 prints dehi/dhehi/edhi. Kaumudī
3.1.78 and 6.4.23 give the additional rudh/śiṣ modal anchors stated
above. The complete tables are derived; they are not asserted to be
486 individually quoted Sanskrit positions.

The pinned **Nyāsa 6.4.119** has the damaged string `देहि चेहि`.
It is preserved verbatim; Kāśikā/Tattvabodhinī supply dhehi. The
**Kaumudī 6.4.101** entry prints `अद्धिः`; that anomalous final
visarga is retained in the archive and does not attest the standalone
addhi spelling used here. Addhi is derived from ad plus whole dhi.
PG7's Bālamanoramā 3.1.78 anomaly `भिन्त्त्थः` also remains raw
and is not used as a modal word witness.

No differing byte sequence is silently corrected to make an expected
form agree. Independent edition comparison and Sanskrit review remain
open. No oracle comparison, grammar program, certificate replay or
proof-assistant formalization was performed for PG22.
