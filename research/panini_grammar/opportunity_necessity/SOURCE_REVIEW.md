## Opportunity and yad

Kāśikā 3.3.167 requires the inherited praiṣādi meaning and explicitly
contrasts kālaḥ pacati bhūtāni. It also preserves the bhāva-lyuṭ phrase
kālo bhojanasya through the local vā-asarūpa permission. Nyāsa and
Padamañjarī explain why the post-strī restriction is not a universal
ban. K generalizes that witnessed opportunity interface to its four
declared roots through 3.3.115, with action meaning supplied, not guessed
from a noun's spelling.

Kaumudī's synonym reading adds anehas; Prakriyāsarvasva supplies avasara
and the reversed order bhoktum kālaḥ. Padamañjarī records a narrower
reading as well as the semantic extension. D selects a five-head
lexicon under the latter source policy; it does not claim every synonym
or every ordering has been resolved.

Kāśikā/Kaumudī/Nyāsa 3.3.168 identify the **yad word**, not the
conditional conjunction yadi, and say that liṅ overrides tumun.
The genuine Tattvabodhinī 3.3.168 field repeats the praiṣādi restriction.
Bālamanoramā's 3.3.168 field is empty; its field keyed to 3.3.159
actually bears the 3.3.168 heading. The latter is recovered only with
its actual subject label and corroborating witnesses. Tattvabodhinī also
has a duplicate/displaced field at 3.3.159. Neither becomes evidence
for PG28's different 3.3.159 desire construction.

## Eligibility and the case contrast

3.3.169 licenses kṛtya, tṛc and liṅ with a qualified actor, expressed
or understood. Kāśikā's kanyāyā voḍhā contrasts with kanyāṃ vahet.
Nyāsa gives the h/t route and 6.3.112 o-substitution. K5 adopts that
specific forward ṣṭutva query permission; it does not import a universal
late-rule visibility policy.

Kāśikā 2.3.65/69 supports the adopted tṛc-object genitive and explicitly
distinguishes the tṛn scope. Tattvabodhinī 3.3.169 prints kanyāṃ voḍhā
and the damaged-looking वोढ्वया. Its literal text is preserved; the
accusative phrase is not used to license all tṛc objects or silently
reinterpreted as a proved luṭ example. That source/parse reconciliation
remains open. The new complete vah P/A O paradigms are written consequences
of its exact U entry, qualified pada inputs and PG19's ending interface;
only the source's vahet/vaheḥ examples are literal modal witnesses here.

## Necessity, indebtedness and optional actor case

Kāśikā/Nyāsa/Padamañjarī 3.3.170 explicitly distinguish semantic upādhi
from mandatory upapada. Prakriyāsarvasva independently supplies bare
kārī. Kaumudī's short display अवश्यङ्कारि is retained without
silently normalizing its ending or assuming a masculine nominative;
K3 constructs the explicitly selected masculine kārī independently.
The compound's mayūravyaṃsakādi explanation is context, not a newly
constructed compound operation or an extra counted rule.

Kāśikā 2.3.70 contrasts debt śataṃ dāyī with necessity
avaśyaṃkārī kaṭasya. Its future condition addresses the prescribed
aka/in routes, not any arbitrary mention of future time. Tattvabodhinī's
gamyādi discussion is retained; D excludes that additional future route
from its necessity-genitive claim. The same-form dāyī necessity/debt
pair in R4 is a rule-derived control, not two quoted source phrases.

2.3.71 gives optional genitive for the unexpressed **actor** of ordinary
kṛtya, with instrumental in the other branch. Its two-karman/ubhayaprāpti
restriction is not discarded; D admits a single selected karman.
3.3.171's exceptional agent-kṛtya discussion is retained without adding
the bhavya/geya lexical inventory to K's ordinary karman domain.

## Morphology and provenance

The new kṛt interfaces use 3.1.95–97, 3.1.120/124/133, 3.3.115,
3.4.67/70/114 and their owned marker consequences. Tavyat and tavya
are separately retained under their shared unaccented TAV target;
their accent distinction is not certified away. Ṇyat/kyap/yat differ
in guṇa/vṛddhi/tuk and ā→ī behavior. Yuk in dāyin, śnam in finite
bhuj and anīyar's n have different owners.

The nominal readout is restricted to the 39 targets in V2. Sup,
anaṅ, ī-lengthening, ṭāp and lopa are explicit, not guessed from a
stored word. The full sup declension, all kṛtya roots, gender assignment
from meaning and general external sandhi remain outside this packet.

Kāśikā/Kaumudī/Bālamanoramā 3.1.7 and 1.3.62 were freshly read for
the T149 handoff: same-actor desired action and the same underlying
pada **condition** must survive into san. These are companion adapters,
not a new independent full san grammar here.

Written exhaustion is source-relative on D's named channels. It does
not establish all-commentary equivalence, a universal conflict scheduler,
runtime correctness or whole-grammar closure.
