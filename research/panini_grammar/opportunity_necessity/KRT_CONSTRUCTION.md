# PG29 — Owned nonfinite bases and nominal readout

Author: Monty. 3 November 2026.

## K1. The ārdhadhātuka boundary: tumun and bhāva-lyuṭ

Every admitted suffix retains its source family and teaching markers.
These root-prescribed non-tiṅ, non-śit kṛt continuations are ārdhadhātuka
under 3.4.114. No śnam, u, ślu/reduplicated present stem or śap is
carried across from the corresponding finite paradigm. The exact roots
are aniṭ in the inherited 7.2.10 account where an iṭ question arises.

Tumun's pronounced part is tum. For the opportunity license 3.3.167:

| Root | Owned continuation | Base |
|---|---|---|
| bhuj, eating | PG28 E4's bhuj→bhoj→bhog before tum; devoicing before t | bhoktum, reused |
| kṛ | ṛ→ar by 7.3.84 and 1.1.51, then tum | kartum |
| dā | retained ā before tum | dātum |
| vah | the separately sourced h/t deletion and o route in K5 | voḍhum |

The resulting m-final kṛt expression is avyaya by 1.1.39. 2.4.82
suppresses its nominal sup/āp realization where applicable; there is
no independent tiṅ person or grammatical gender ending. The actor link
remains in the semantic input, not in a new finite exponent.

For the separate bhāva channel, 3.3.115 prescribes lyuṭ in neuter
action meaning. Retain l/ṭ markers and substitute pronounced yu→ana
by 7.1.1. The results are:

| Root | Root/ana junction | Action base |
|---|---|---|
| bhuj | light-upadhā u→o, 7.3.86; j before a stays j | bhojana |
| kṛ | ṛ→ar; r-to-affix-n ṇatva under 8.4.1–2 | karaṇa |
| dā | ā+a→ā, 6.1.101 | dāna |
| vah | vah+ana, no ṣṭutva/ḍh-lopa boundary | vahana |

These are the selected action meanings. 3.3.117's instrument/location
uses of lyuṭ are source context only, not extra admitted senses.
Genitive singular ṅas→sya after a by 7.1.12 gives the four R's
opportunity nouns. There is no retroactive śnam in bhojanasya.

## K2. Tṛc is an agent construction, not luṭ's tās

3.3.169 licenses the eligible-agent tṛc family, alongside the general
3.1.133/3.4.67 interface. Retain c as a marker and pronounced tṛ.
The bases are **kartṛ, dātṛ, voḍhṛ**. Kṛ's guṇa and K5's vah
contact use this exact affix, not a borrowed finite future word.

These kṛt bases have prātipadika status under 1.2.46. For masculine
nominative singular, retain su from 4.1.2, its non-vocative setting
and sarvanāmasthāna status under 1.1.43. By 7.1.94, final ṛ receives anaṅ. Its ṅ-marked
extent replaces only the last sound under 1.1.53: kartṛ→kartan,
dātṛ→dātan, voḍhṛ→voḍhan. 6.4.8 lengthens the n-penult before
the sarvanāmasthāna su. Then 6.1.68 removes its apṛkta s and
8.2.7 removes the prātipadika-final n. Read **kartā, dātā, voḍhā**.

These are three nominal nominatives, not new luṭ 3sg positions. R3's
genitive object is addressed to their tṛc owner. PG16's kartā has a
different source/ending history and retains finite accusative government.
The nominal route is not obtained by stripping or re-labelling luṭ.

## K3. Ṇini: two bases with retained necessity/debt tags

3.3.170 prescribes ṇini on R1's completed inputs. Retain initial ṇ
and the final nasalized vowel as teaching markers; pronounced **in**
belongs to the suffix. Ṇit is still available after marker lopa.

- Kṛ+ṇini: 7.2.115 gives ṛ→ār under 1.1.51, hence **kārin**.
- Dā+ṇini: 7.3.33 introduces yuk before this ñ/ṇit kṛt continuation;
  its k-marked placement is at the root's right boundary, giving
  **dā-y-in = dāyin**. The y belongs to yuk, not an imagined finite
  causative -aya- stem. No such yuk enters plain dātum/dātṛ.

For masculine nominative singular, 6.4.13 lengthens the suffix's i
before non-vocative su. 6.1.68 su-lopa and 8.2.7 n-lopa yield
**kārī, dāyī**. Preserve the n owner and the distinct semantic tag.
This packet does not claim every gender/case of in-stems or every
possible lexical source of a printed -in word.

## K4. Root-dependent kṛtya catalogue

3.1.95 supplies the kṛtya classification. 3.1.96 licenses tavyat,
tavya and anīyar. For unaccented readout, tavyat and tavya share the
TAV family's pronounced tavya, but their distinct source suffixes and
markers remain two history routes. They are not silently declared the
same Sanskrit suffix. Accent-sensitive comparison is outside D.

| Root | TAV | anīyar | Further y family |
|---|---|---|---|
| kṛ | kartavya | karaṇīya | kārya (ṇyat), kṛtya (kyap) |
| dā | dātavya | dānīya | deya (yat) |
| vah | voḍhavya | vahanīya | vāhya (ṇyat) |

These ten base targets exhaust the stated tavyat/tavya, anīyar and
applicable yat/ṇyat/kyap families for the three selected roots. They do
not enumerate every kṛtya formation, exceptional nipātana or lexical
meaning in Sanskrit.

TAV uses the same root-facing guṇa/contact as tumun/tṛc. Kṛ+anīyar
has kar+anīya and actual r-conditioned n→ṇ; vah+anīyar has vahanīya.
Dā+anīyar retains ā through ā+a coalescence, yielding dānīya. The
ī-substitution reserved for yat is not imported into this suffix.
For kṛ/vah, 3.1.124 prescribes ṇyat; retained ṇit invokes 7.2.115
on final ṛ or 7.2.116 on vah's a-upadhā, yielding kārya/vāhya.
No h→ḍh occurs before y on this admitted readout.

3.1.120 gives kṛ's optional kyap against ṇyat. Its k blocks guṇa
under 1.1.5; its p licenses tuk after short ṛ by 6.1.71, giving
**kṛ-t-ya**. Ṇyat and kyap have different marker effects even though
both display y. They are not an arbitrary strong/weak stem toggle.

For dā, 3.1.97 licenses yat. 6.4.65 first changes ā→ī specifically
before yat; 7.3.84 then gives e, producing **deya**. Substituting
the ṇini yuk route here would manufacture the wrong affix history.

## K5. Vah's h/t contract has its own primary witness

Nyāsa 3.3.169 explicitly derives the voḍhavya contact using 8.2.31,
8.2.40, 8.4.41, 8.3.13 and **6.3.112**. Adopt that addressed
early ṣṭutva visibility contract for vah's t-initial TAV/tṛc/tumun:

\[
vah\mid tX\to vaḍh\mid dhX\to vaḍh\mid ḍhX
\to va\mid ḍhX\to vo\mid ḍhX.
\]

The root-owned ḍh is deleted; the ending-owned ḍh survives. 6.3.112
changes vah's a to **o**, not 6.3.111's generic short-vowel lengthening.
Thus voḍhavya, voḍhṛ and voḍhum have one selected contact route.
Kāśikā 6.3.112 expressly witnesses voḍhā/voḍhum/voḍhavyam.
This does not generalize PG28's separately qualified tṛh-hi schedule
or resolve all commentarial visibility conventions.

## K6. Bounded adjective and noun readout

For each of K4's ten a-final bases, the selected nominative-singular
agreement interface has three genders. Masculine su gives final s→ru→ḥ
at pause (8.2.66/8.3.15). Feminine ṭāp under 4.1.3–4 gives ā,
with a+ā coalescence and āp-conditioned su-lopa. Neuter su→am by
7.1.24 followed by 6.1.107 gives final am. The expressed karman,
not the unexpressed agent, supplies the kṛtya adjective's agreement.

Add the three masculine tṛc nominatives, two masculine ṇini nominatives
and four neuter lyuṭ genitives. This gives **30+3+2+4=39** nominal
readout targets. No full 24-cell nominal declension is claimed. The
subanta boundary and suppressed markers remain in history at every step.
