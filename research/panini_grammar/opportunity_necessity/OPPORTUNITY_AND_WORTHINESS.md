# PG29 — Addressed opportunity and eligibility relations

Author: Monty. 3 November 2026.

## C1. 3.3.167 needs an opportunity relation

Let H be the governing time head and e its addressed action. Require
the retained dependency H→e, an admitted opportunity meaning, and one
of the inherited praiṣa/atisarga/prāptakāla qualifications. Then 3.3.167
licenses tumun at e. The relation is lexical and semantic, not temporal
measurement by a grammar-event counter.

3.1.92 gives upapada status to what the prescription names in the
locative within this dhātu domain. The locative belongs to the rule's
statement; it does not demand that the time head itself appear with
a locative ending in the resulting phrase. Kāśikā also requires the
relevant semantic connection, which D retains as H→e.

Kāśikā gives kālo bhoktum, samayo bhoktum and velā bhoktum.
Kaumudī adds anehas; Prakriyāsarvasva explicitly gives bhoktum avasaraḥ
and bhoktum kālaḥ in the opposite order. Thus the selected dependency
can survive the two witnessed orders. This is not a theorem of unrestricted
word-order freedom or arbitrary long-distance dependency resolution.

The counterexample kālaḥ pacati bhūtāni lacks the required praiṣādi
reading: “time cooks beings” is not converted into an infinitive merely
because kāla is printed. The semantic predicate is indispensable.

## C2. A locally witnessed lyuṭ alternative

Kāśikā explicitly retains kālo bhojanasya by vā-asarūpa 3.1.94;
Nyāsa/Padamañjarī explain that the post-strī restriction is not an
absolute ban on every such alternative. Prakriyāsarvasva also retains
the lyuṭ construction. Adopt that local permission, not a global rule
that every unlike suffix survives every later exception.

K1 constructs bhāva-lyuṭ for all four D2 roots: bhojana, karaṇa,
dāna and vahana. They denote the selected action here, not an instrument,
vehicle, gift object or every other lexical sense of the same noun.
Noun number/gender and the opportunity relation supply genitive singular:
bhojanasya, karaṇasya, dānasya, vahanasya. This is the residual relation
to the time head under 2.3.50, not the action's agent/object genitive
under 2.3.65.

The declared no-yad opportunity interface therefore has two tagged
channels: an indeclinable tumun complement and a genitive bhāva noun.
Their coincident English paraphrase does not identify their Sanskrit
syntax. K's form constructors realize each of these channels on D2.

## C3. 3.3.168: yad is a dependency, not the conjunction yadi

The sūtra's locative **yadi** refers to the **yad word** in its
commentaries. It is not an instruction to search for the conditional
conjunction yadi. Require the relevant yad dependency together with C1's
opportunity premises. Then O replaces the addressed tumun complement:

\[
  (H\to e,\ Opportunity,\ Praiṣādi,\ Yad\to e)
       \longmapsto O_e.
\]

The quoted instance is kālo yad bhuñjīta bhavān. Bhavān controls
third person; being addressed does not turn it into a second-person
form. If the supplied meaning is instead “time cooks”, Tattvabodhinī
explicitly rejects the attempted extension even with yad.

On this addressed verbal-complement channel the relation is replacement,
not {tumun,O}. The separately admitted no-yad nominal phrase in C2
is not silently extended to all yad-relative nominal configurations;
that larger syntax remains outside this finite contract. A yad belonging
to another clause does not discharge this clause's guard.

Bālamanoramā's field stored at 3.3.159 discusses this 3.3.168 subject;
it is used only with that corrected subject label and the corroborating
properly keyed witnesses, never as evidence for the 3.3.159 desire rule.

## C4. 3.3.169: eligibility keeps expression roles distinct

Supply Eligible(a,e), with a the actor of the selected action. An overt
arha word is not required; the source allows the qualified actor to be
expressed or understood. This is a grammar condition, not an independent
judgment of a person's qualifications.

The construction licenses three **families**:

\[
 \{O_{actor},\ tṛc_{actor}\}
 \ \sqcup\ \{K_{karman}:K\in\mathcal K(root)\}.
\]

K4 defines the finite root-dependent kṛtya catalogue; it is not one
unrestricted “kṛtya suffix”. Kṛtya here expresses the selected karman
under 3.4.70, while tṛc expresses the qualified actor under 3.4.67.
The ordinary O kartari route has its own controller and pada license.

Kāśikā's vah examples contrast kanyāyā voḍhā with kanyāṃ vahet
and kanyā voḍhavyā/vāhyā/vahanīyā with an unexpressed instrumental
agent. In isolated case labels the tṛc object's form is kanyāyāḥ;
the source's kanyāyā before voiced v includes sentence sandhi. This
packet supplies the case relation without claiming a new complete kanyā
declension or all external joins.

Tattvabodhinī also prints kanyāṃ voḍhā. Its accusative display is
retained as a source discrepancy/possible parse issue; it is not used
to erase the tṛc versus finite-luṭ distinction established by 2.3.65/69.
No general permission for both object cases with every agent noun is
inferred. R3 gives the adopted source-qualified case branches.

## C5. Completing PG19's missing kṛtya projection

PG19 D3 deliberately constructed only finite moods under 3.3.163–166.
For kṛ/dā/vah, append K4's role-correct kṛtya family to the ordinary
3.3.163 praiṣādi expression relation. In the supplied later context,
3.3.164 retains it alongside O and I. With the specified later sma,
3.3.165 excludes both O **and the kṛtya family**, leaving I.

This restriction applies to the selected 3.3.165 domain; it is not
“sma deletes all nonfinite words”. 3.3.166's adhīṣṭa/sma rule is
the separately inherited O→I restriction. Neither extension changes
PG19's already correct finite-position counts. It fills an explicitly
unbuilt expression channel, subject to the three lexical adapters here.

**Proposition C6.** On D's completed opportunity/eligibility inputs,
these addressed relations preserve the event and participant identities,
produce only their declared expression types and are finite. K realizes
the selected nonfinite bases; V supplies vah's missing O adapter.

**Proof.** C1–C3 distinguish time meaning, lexical dependency, relevant
yad and the separate nominal channel. C4 chooses from a finite catalogue
without changing the actor/karman graph. C5 carries the named source
exceptions before realization. All changes are addressed substitutions
or tagged choices; none infers a missing premise from a surface word. □
