# PG29 — Necessity, indebtedness and case government

Author: Monty. 3 November 2026.

## R1. 3.3.170 qualifies meaning without requiring an overt word

Kāśikā explicitly treats āvaśyaka and ādhamarṇya as **upādhis**,
not mandatory upapada words. Nyāsa explains why bare kārī/hārī can
occur. Prakriyāsarvasva likewise gives kārī separately from avaśyaṃkārī.
The positive guards are therefore supplied Necessity(a,e) or Debt(a,e),
not the presence of avaśyam or a money word in a string.

On the declared kṛ/dā necessity inputs and dā debt inputs, 3.3.170
licenses actor-expressing ṇini. K3 constructs kārin/dāyin and their
masculine nominatives kārī/dāyī. Necessity and debt retain different
semantic tags even where they choose the same affix and printed word.
No free compound operation is inferred from an overt avaśyam: the
source's separate mayūravyaṃsakādi compound explanation remains an
unbuilt adapter here. Its word is a cited witness, not a new compound
paradigm counted by this packet.

## R2. 3.3.171 adds the applicable kṛtya family

Under the same supplied necessity/debt qualification, 3.3.171 licenses
the appropriate kṛtya alternatives. For the declared transitive uses,
3.4.70 makes these karman-expressing; ṇini is actor-expressing. Thus
the relation is a dependent union over **expression role**, not a list
of interchangeable endings attached to one already fixed subject.

The commentaries discuss agent-expressing exceptional kṛtyas such as
the bhavya/geya class when explaining why an explicit rule is needed.
That discussion is retained; those lexically exceptional adapters are
not claimed by K4's ordinary three-root catalogue. The rule does not
turn all kṛtyas into agent nouns or abolish the ordinary bhāva/karman
restriction. For the present single-karman domain, select karman only.

## R3. A suffix and its meaning jointly determine unexpressed-case rules

Start with the expressed role, then apply case prescriptions to the
remaining participants under 2.3.1. Do not assign genitive to every noun
near every kṛt word.

| Addressed construction | Unexpressed karman | Unexpressed actor |
|---|---|---|
| finite kartari O or inherited luṭ | accusative, 2.3.2 | actor already expressed by the finite verb |
| tumun indeclinable | accusative, 2.3.69 blocks the general kṛt genitive | no new finite controller; any additional actor-expression adapter is outside this table |
| tṛc agent noun | genitive, 2.3.65 | actor expressed by tṛc |
| necessity ṇini, without future-prescribed in exception | genitive, 2.3.65 | actor expressed by ṇini |
| debt ṇini | accusative: 2.3.70 blocks genitive | actor expressed by ṇini |
| ordinary karman-expressing kṛtya | selected karman expressed by kṛtya | genitive or instrumental, 2.3.71 and 2.3.18 |

The source's tṛn exclusion in 2.3.69 does not include tṛc merely
because both may print -tā. Its scope is stated explicitly in Kāśikā.
The construction behind the word must be retained.

2.3.70 is also not a ban on every -in or -aka noun. Kāśikā distinguishes
the future-prescribed aka/in cases and the debt in case; its positive
necessity counterexample is avaśyaṃkārī kaṭasya. Tattvabodhinī discusses
the additional future gamyādi issue. D's necessity channel excludes
that independently future-prescribed route; it does not claim that every
future-sounding necessity phrase must have genitive.

For kṛtya, the actor's optional genitive does not imply an optional
genitive for the expressed object. The single-karman restriction matters:
2.3.71's double-karman/ubhayaprāpti commentary is retained as a boundary,
not a solved extension to every vah/causative frame.

## R4. Same form, different obligations

Construct two admitted dā histories, both ending in dāyī. In the first,
the actor necessarily performs a giving action without the selected
debt/future exception; in the second, the actor owes the giving.
The first selects an object genitive, the second an accusative. For
the supplied śata object, śatasya dāyī versus śataṃ dāyī illustrates
the contrast. The first is a rule-derived control, not a quoted
Kāśikā phrase; the second is the source's debt witness.

Another pair prints kartā: K2's tṛc nominative versus PG16's luṭ
third singular. Their unexpressed karman takes genitive versus accusative.
The nominal ending contains anaṅ/su/lopa history; luṭ retains tās,
whole-ending ḍā and its own deletion. Printed ā does not decide the type.

N3–N4 prove why preserving only the words, or appending one unaddressed
"genitive" coordinate across both word sectors, is inadequate for linear cut
transport. This is a local grammar observer result, not a universal
claim that semantic interpretation requires a fixed number of channels.

## R5. Typed relation theorem

**Proposition.** On D's completed single-karman inputs, the case relation
is nonempty and preserves the already chosen expressed role. Necessity
and debt remain distinguishable after identical morphology. Unknown
semantic qualifiers do not authorize the union of their cases.

**Proof.** R3 exhausts the admitted expression families. Genitive is the
default 2.3.65 branch only where no named exclusion applies; 2.3.69/70
remove it on the stated subdomains. R2's karman-expressing kṛtya has the
explicit 2.3.71 actor option. No branch changes the actor into the object
or promotes a nominal nominative to a tiṅ controller. □
