# PG825 · Kaiyaṭa’s printed objection and the possessor of both sons

Monty · 25 November 2026. PG824 established Haradatta’s explicit acceptance of a dual after ekaśeṣa. The independent original Kaiyaṭa witness is now identified in print. A separate Padamañjarī example licenses an outer possessive compound paradigm.

## The direct Pradīpa witness

Read the **right column under प्रदीपः** on printed p. 321, scan leaf 372. The lemma introduces the argument for mentioning kṛt and taddhita and the limiting force of enumeration. Kaiyaṭa asks whether the lack of a dual makes *ubhaya* deficient in inflection, potentially giving the taddhita an unwanted indeclinable designation. He then includes the place-name examples *godau* and *varaṇā*. His solution restricts which taddhitas the list admits. This is direct evidence that the **no-dual premise occurs in his argument**. It provides neither a dual token nor a command to declare *ubhaya* indeclinable. The passage beneath the Uddyota heading belongs to a different commentary. PG824's opposing ekaśeṣa argument in the Padamañjarī is directly attested as well. Which policy governs a request stays part of its source history.

The scanned title identifies the Navāhnika first volume, second printing, Bombay, Nirṇayasāgara, Śaka 1839 / 1917. The archive's 1937 item label remains a catalogue note. The scan and its title page support the edition and passage independently of OCR.

## One or more possessors, each with two sons

Haradatta's Padamañjarī 11027 supplies the two-son paraphrase *ubhau putrāv asya* and the first member used in *ubhayaputra*. Restrict this construction to the bahuvrīhi interpretation of 2.2.24, with a masculine external referent. First retain the link between the two sons and the possessive referent. The free dual expression describes the sons. The internal nominal members lose their sup markers in the compound under 2.4.71. Record *ubhaya* at the internal stem address, as specified by the source. Do not install a free word *ubhau* or substitute its nominative ending at that address. The outer referent supplies the number for the whole expression. Thus two possessors with two sons each can be addressed in the dual even on a policy denying the independent stem *ubhaya* a dual.

The resulting a-final compound has an ordinary masculine ending. The dat.sg is consequently *ubhayaputrāya*. The *smai* belonging to a request for free *ubhaya* would give the wrong agreement. The two holders’ nom./acc./voc. share *ubhayaputrau*, with distinct original case or address histories. The r in the last member conditions ṇ in *ubhayaputreṇa* and *ubhayaputrāṇām*. Accusative plural retains final n under the padānta restriction. For ablative singular the final dental has the two pausal outcomes under 8.4.56. The [eight-by-three panel](CASES.tsv) retains both on one key. A chosen possessor reading is sufficient for this paradigm. No other compound sense, feminine formation or Vedic accent is credited.
