## S2. Two tāsi analyses are not one automatic it rule

Kāśikā 3.1.33 explains idit as preventing nasal deletion and gives
mantā/saṅgantā. Bālamanoramā there instead calls tāsi's i
uccāraṇārtha. Nyāsa expressly distinguishes the Jayāditya and Vāmana
accounts; Padamañjarī discusses how asiddha/Tripādī visibility changes
the need for an idit marker. These are not reconciled by pretending both
sources say the same thing.

PG16 selects Bālamanoramā/Vāmana's pronunciation-aid analysis for its
realized tās. Original teaching evidence and the source-policy label
remain in state. We do not infer a nasalized it vowel from plain i in a
modern transcription. Future work on nasal A aṅgas must separately
establish the relevant 6.4.24/visibility branch; these eight roots do not
close that larger adjudication. The source contexts are preserved under
3.1.33 and 7.1.58 rather than altered to match our selection.

## S3. Future time is word meaning, not a timestamp switch

Kāśikā 3.3.13 licenses lṛṭ in the residual future and the included
purpose setting; 3.3.15 gives anadyatana luṭ, the mixed-future exclusion
and a paridevana discussion. Nyāsa 3.3.5 explicitly separates the
word's future-general vivakṣā from anadyatana understood through another
sentence word. Its concluding distinction is the basis of F3's two
positive semantic constructors.

Consequently a general future meaning is not epistemic uncertainty, and
the actual event's tomorrow date does not by itself establish the verb's
anadyatana constructor. This qualified policy also avoids elevating a
publisher's modern blanket prohibition on a “tomorrow + lṛṭ” sentence
into the only traditional account.

Mahābhāṣya 3.3.15's yogavibhāga and jñāpaka explicitly acknowledge
lṛṭ in anadyatana while discussing sat substitutes. We retain that
evidence and exclude the participial procedure from D10. The packet
does not claim that a single finite table settles every future use.
Tattvabodhinī 3.3.15's traditional day-boundary account is retained as
calendar-adapter evidence, not equated without argument to UTC dates.

## S5. The 8.2.25 dispute and a publisher attachment error

Siddhāntakaumudī 8.2.25 explicitly states s-lopa before a dh-initial
affix and illustrates **edhitādhve**. That is the selected luṭ A
procedure. Kāśikā's entry discusses a sic-restricted reading and then
a broader Bhāṣyakāra position. It is not accurate to cite the entire
Kāśikā entry as an unqualified unanimous extension to all s-final stems.
Our domain is the owned tās-s before dhve in E5.

The publisher's `balamanorama.txt` and `tattvabodhini.txt` entries keyed
82025 begin with **ho ḍhaḥ** material, not a commentary on *dhi ca*.
They are archived with a misattachment flag and **not used** as evidence
for the future 2pl rule. A source key is not enough to authenticate its
contents. Correctly located Kaumudī text is sufficient for this bounded
adjudication; no quote checker or repaired source snapshot was invented.

## S6. Commentary procedure tails and textual variants

Kaumudī and Bālamanoramā at key 74070 begin with abhyāsa material,
then continue the edh procedure into luṭ. The exact edhitāsāthe
discussion occurs in that **procedure tail**. It is evidence for the
derivation, not an assertion that the literal sūtra 7.4.70 governs a
luṭ dual ending. The Kaumudī tail repeats edhitārau; the raw repetition
is preserved in SOURCE_EXCERPTS, not treated as an extra grammatical slot.

The source entries also contain spelling/typing irregularities, including
a Bālamanoramā future plural spelling and small variants in the Nyāsa
future-time passage. We derive displayed forms from the stated rules and
the selected procedure, without silently replacing those archival bytes.
The edition's teaching form, a literal commentary word and our IAST
normal-form output have separate labels in the evidence.

## S7. Reading the theorem branch's existing future work

The companion branch was inspected again at
`bee6b0297824e632ac8ca6f7d88d823c0a54ae07`. Its latest visible
development remained T118, already reviewed in PG15. T97 and T112
were reread directly; T97's note points to T100 for gam's luṭ extension.
No predecessor certificate was run and no companion code was imported.

Useful inherited work is acknowledged rather than presented as newly
discovered: T97 already develops bhū/gam/nī future P paths; T112
develops six A positions for labh/edh in both futures. PG16's purpose
is the completed semantic/source/native construction and the remaining
nine-slot interfaces in this eight-root domain, not to rename those
earlier finite forms as wholly new coverage.

Two qualifications are additive:

1. T97's phrase “both are ārdhadhātuka” is too coarse as a state type.
   The source-bearing future sya/tās is ārdhadhātuka while the tiṅ
   ending is sārvadhātuka under 3.4.113. The distinction supports, rather
   than invalidates, its useful root-guṇa route on these forms.
2. T112 notes that omitting ending ṭi-e in lṛṭ A 1sg can leave the
   same surface e, but attributes a+i→e to 6.1.97. The a+i route is
   **6.1.87**; the actual ending i→e followed by a+e route uses
   **6.1.97**. Surface neutrality does not establish that these are the
   same rule history. The same precision is needed in the dual chain.

T112 explicitly leaves 2du/2pl/1du open. E2/E5/D9 supply those written
paths, including luṭ's 8.2.25 decision. D1/D2 also add selected kṛ/hṛ/pac
profiles and complete the common construction. No modification of T97,
T100, T112, their expected hashes or historical PASS labels is required.

## S8. What has not been adjudicated

This packet does not complete all seṭ/aniṭ/veṭ lists, all roots,
prefixed or derived future stems, bhāva/karma futures, accent, Vedic
variants, all asiddha interactions, future participles or lṛṅ. The
two tāsi analyses and broad source-tradition reconciliation remain
explicit outside-domain obligations. Written local completeness in D10
is not a global Sanskrit oracle or a numerical whole-grammar percentage.
