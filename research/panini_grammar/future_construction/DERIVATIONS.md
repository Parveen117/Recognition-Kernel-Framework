# PG16 — The 216 written positions and their derivations

Author: Monty. 2 November 2026.

These are written consequences of F1–F6, A1–A6 and E1–E6. A source
literally printing a form is distinct from our obtaining it by the stated
construction. The tables make no claim of 216 independent quotations,
software comparisons or executed cases. Each U-root P/A row presupposes
the corresponding completed principal-result frame in F5.

## D3. bhū: generic future versus encoded anadyatana

Take bhū 1010001, ordinary sattā, kartari, P, speaker-singular. A completed
FutureGeneral ν selects lṛṭ; 3.4.78 selects mip. Then

\[
 bhū+sya+mip\to bhū+i+sya+mi\to bho+i+sya+mi
 \to bhav+i+sya+mi\to bhav+i+syā+mi\to bhaviṣyāmi.
\]

The respective actions are iṭ 7.2.35, root guṇa 7.3.84, 6.1.78,
7.3.101 on the a-final future aṅga and later 8.3.59. The augment i
keeps 1.1.6's protection. If instead the verb encodes FutureAnadyatana
with the required temporal evidence, 3.3.15 selects luṭ:

\[
 bhū+tās+mip\to bhū+i+tās+mi\to bho+i+tās+mi
 \to bhav+i+tās+mi\to bhavitāsmi.
\]

There is no 7.3.101 action on the s-final aṅga. These are two meanings
and two licensed constructions, not a suffix replacement on one word.

## D4. gam: exceptional iṭ with an exact affix/pada guard

Take gam 1011137, ordinary gati, kartari, P, third singular.
FutureGeneral gives gam+sya+ti. Although gam is aniṭ, the s-initial
affix and P conditions of 7.2.58 supply i, after which 8.3.59 gives
**gamiṣyati**. The present-system gaccha substitution is not invoked.

For FutureAnadyatana use gam+tās+tip. Rule 7.2.58 cannot apply to
t-initial tās, so no i is inserted. Whole-ending ḍā substitution, its
marker treatment and 6.4.143 give gam+t+ā. Then 8.3.24 gives gaṃtā
and 8.4.58 gives **gantā**. The later dental n cannot justify earlier
nasal-upadhā deletion. Other P slots follow the same root/t junction;
for instance gam+tās+vas gives **gantāsvaḥ** after the final-s process.

## D5. nī: dual A and the difference between sya and tās interfaces

Take nī 1011049 with principal result accruing to the agent, two
addressees as agent-controller, ordinary kartari. Pada is A, ending āthām.
In lṛṭ there is no iṭ: nī+sya+āthām → ne+sya+āthe. The short-a
interface licenses 7.2.81, then 6.1.66 and 6.1.87:

\[
 ne+sya+āthe\to ne+sya+iythe\to ne+sya+ithe
 \to nesyethe\to \mathbf{neṣyethe}.
\]

For the completed anadyatana interpretation, nī+tās+āthām instead
gives ne+tās+āthe. Rule 7.2.81's preceding-a guard is false at the
s-final aṅga, so the result is **netāsāthe**. Both words agree with
two addressees, but their ending-junction histories differ.

## D6. kṛ and hṛ: iṭ before sya in both padas

Select kṛ 1080010 in karaṇa, or hṛ 1011046 in haraṇa. With principal
result accruing to the agent and speaker singular, F5 gives A. Rule
7.2.70 overrides the aniṭ block before sya. Guṇa gives kar/har and
the ending's iṭ becomes e:

\[
 kṛ+i+sya+iṭ_{ending}\to kar+i+sya+e
 \to kar+i+sye\to \mathbf{kariṣye};
\]

\[
 hṛ+i+sya+iṭ_{ending}\to har+i+sya+e
 \to har+i+sye\to \mathbf{hariṣye}.
\]

The displayed i before sya is the **augment**, whereas the original
ending i is the target of ṭi-e. Before tās there is no 7.2.70 exception:
kar+tās+e → kar+tāh+e → **kartāhe**, and analogously **hartāhe**,
using 7.4.52. Changing β to the admitted non-agent principal-result
frame gives P; with speaker singular the same root/affix iṭ decision
then yields **kariṣyāmi/hariṣyāmi** or **kartāsmi/hartāsmi**.
No global “A means aniṭ” or “P means seṭ” rule can model this.

## D7. pac: distinct root/affix consonants

Take pac 1011151, ordinary pāka, a P principal-result frame and third
singular. Neither future receives iṭ. Lṛṭ gives pac+sya+ti; 8.2.30
changes root c to k before the jhal s, and 8.3.59 changes the owned
affix s to ṣ after that k: **pakṣyati**. Luṭ gives pac+tās+ḍā;
ṭi-lopa precedes the phonetic readout, and c→k before t yields
**paktā**. The same printed k in both formations does not make the
following s and t the same affix or identify their ending paths.

In the A principal-result frame with several addressees, pac+tās+dhvam
gives pac+tās+dhve; 8.2.25 removes the tās-s and 8.2.30 changes c
before the retained t: **paktādhve**. This contains an initial root/t
junction and a later ending-dh junction with different owners.

## D8. labh: bh→p versus t→dh followed by bh→b

Take labh 1011130, ordinary prāpti, lexical A, third singular.
Aniṭ gives labh+sya+ta; ta→te and 8.4.55 bh→p before s produce
**lapsyate**. No s→ṣ condition holds. For luṭ, whole-ending ḍā and
ṭi-lopa yield labh+t+ā; 8.2.40 gives labh+dh+ā, then 8.4.53
gives **labdhā**. The two derivations require different rules in a
different sequence; a memorized “labh future stem” would conceal them.

For A second plural, labh+tās+dhvam → labh+tās+dhve. Delete the
tās-s by 8.2.25, change the root-adjacent t to dh by 8.2.40, and
then bh to b by 8.4.53. The result is **labdhādhve**. The two dh
occurrences have different ancestry; only the first was substituted
for affix t. For first dual no such ending-s deletion occurs:
labh+tās+vahe → **labdhāsvahe**.

## D9. edh: the three restored A positions and two iṭ owners

Take edh 1010002, ordinary vṛddhi, lexical A. Both futures receive iṭ
by 7.2.35; 1.1.6 protects its i. Lṛṭ uses the thematic construction,
giving edhiṣyethe, edhiṣyadhve and edhiṣyāvahe in 2du/2pl/1du.
The three luṭ paths are different:

\[
 edh+i+tās+āthām\to edh+i+tās+āthe
   \to \mathbf{edhitāsāthe};
\]

\[
 edh+i+tās+dhvam\to edh+i+tās+dhve
   \to edh+i+tā+dhve=\mathbf{edhitādhve};
\]

\[
 edh+i+tās+vahi\to edh+i+tās+vahe
   =\mathbf{edhitāsvahe}.
\]

The first does not meet 7.2.81's preceding-a condition; the second
uses the selected 8.2.25 source procedure; the third does not meet
7.3.101's a-final-aṅga condition. In first singular,
edh+iṭ_augment+tās+iṭ_ending becomes edh+i+tās+e, then
**edhitāhe** by 7.4.52. A single unowned token named “iṭ” cannot
represent both occurrences correctly.

## D10. Bounded construction theorem

Let D be the set of completed frames satisfying F1–F5, the chosen
commentary policies and the spelling profile in README. Forget only
irrelevant participant names to index the twelve root/pada profiles;
retain ν, person and number. Let R be the relation of the explicitly
constructed future selection, affix, ending and phonetic arrows.

**Theorem D10.** R supplies exactly one selected written word reading for
each of the 216 profile/ν/person/number positions. Every displayed word
has an owned, source-labelled derivation. Its native lift and its zero
reading have exactly the same labelled accepted branches and the same
Open/Excluded classifications for partial or conflicting inputs.

**Proof.** F3 chooses one future for each completed ν. F5 chooses one
pada and original ending for each completed β/controller. A3 exhausts
the eight entries' iṭ possibilities in these two affix domains. A4–A6
cover every root-specific junction required by these entries. E6
partitions and constructs every ending position. The tables D1/D2
substitute each of the twelve profiles into all nine slots in each
future, exhausting 12·9·2 positions. Their actions preserve ownership,
original evidence and ordered history, so PG14 S4 gives a lawful
composite. N3 supplies the typed specialization identity and exact
branch recovery; F6 gives the partial-input guard behaviour. □

The theorem is about this specified relation and its adopted source
interpretations. It is not an independent proof of all Sanskrit
soundness, a test result, a confluence theorem, or a claim that the
publisher's entire root inventory has been given future paradigms.
