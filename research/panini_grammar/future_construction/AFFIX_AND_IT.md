# PG16 — Owned future affixes, iṭ and the root interface

Author: Monty. 2 November 2026.

## A1. Affix identity and grammatical class

After F3 and F5, 3.4.78 selects a tiṅ member with its original identity.
Rule 3.1.33 inserts sya for lṛṭ and tās for luṭ. Its lṛ designation also
includes lṛṅ in the source; retaining the exact lakāra label prevents this
packet from silently admitting conditional forms. Sya/tās replace the
present-system śap-type route here. We do not first build gaccha, naya,
kuru or another present aṅga and append a future suffix to it.

| Owned constituent | Class in PG16 | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| sya or tās | ārdhadhātuka, 3.4.114 | May receive iṭ by 7.2.35 and its exceptions |
| Original finite tiṅ ending | sārvadhātuka, 3.4.113 | Supplies the appropriate ending operations |
| iṭ augment of sya/tās | Owned by that following affix | Its position and restrictions survive marker lopa |
| A 1sg ending iṭ | A tiṅ member, a different occurrence | 3.4.79 acts on its ṭi; it is not the preceding augment |

The finite future is not one untyped object to which “ārdhadhātuka” can
be applied wholesale. In particular 3.4.115–116's special treatment of
liṭ/āśīr-liṅ does not convert these future tiṅ endings into ārdhadhātuka.
This distinction matters for 7.2.35, 7.2.81 and 7.3.101.

## A2. The teaching i in tāsi

The selected procedure follows Bālamanoramā on 3.1.33: tāsi's final i is
**uccāraṇārtha**, a pronunciation aid, giving the realized affix tās.
It is not an inserted iṭ before the affix and not the first-person ending.
Keep the teaching record, the chosen analysis and the realized sound span
as distinct fields.

Kāśikā on 3.1.33 instead explains an idit purpose, protecting a nasal from
lopa. Nyāsa and Padamañjarī explicitly discuss Jayāditya/Vāmana's different
analyses and their visibility assumptions. PG16 records that disagreement
in [S2](SOURCE_REVIEW.md); it does not manufacture an it-saṃjñā simply
because an editorial teaching form ends in the letter i.

Our gam is P and has m before the late Tripādī operations. The n in gantā
is introduced later through anusvāra/parasavarṇa and cannot be fed backward
into 6.4.24. Neither this example nor the chosen source policy proves the
complete equivalence of both tāsi analyses on nasal A roots outside F4.

## A3. iṭ as a guarded augment

Rule 7.2.35 admits iṭ before a val-initial ārdhadhātuka affix. Both sya's s
and tās's t qualify. The ṭ marker places the realized i at the beginning
of its **following affix** under 1.1.46; marker lopa does not reassign it
to the lexical root. Rule 7.2.10 blocks the general augment after the
specified ekāc anudātta-upadeśa roots. Ekāc is a predicate of the original
root teaching form, not a count performed after future guṇa or sandhi.

Two explicit exceptions supply the positive branches needed here:

- **7.2.58:** gam receives iṭ before a **s-initial** ārdhadhātuka in
  **parasmaipada**. Kāśikā gives gamiṣyati and expressly contrasts the
  t-initial gantāsmi/gantāsvaḥ/gantāsmaḥ. This does not make gam seṭ
  before every future affix, nor does it license the same result in A.
- **7.2.70:** ṛ-final roots, and han in its separate domain, receive iṭ
  before **sya**. For our kṛ/hṛ this supplies i in both P and A. Tās
  is not sya, so the luṭ branch retains the aniṭ prohibition.

| Exact root | lṛṭ sya | luṭ tās | Guard evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| bhū | iṭ | iṭ | seṭ; 7.2.35 |
| gam | iṭ in its P profile | none | 7.2.58 versus 7.2.10 |
| nī | none | none | 7.2.10, no admitted exception |
| kṛ | iṭ in P and A | none | 7.2.70 versus 7.2.10 |
| hṛ | iṭ in P and A | none | 7.2.70 versus 7.2.10 |
| pac | none | none | 7.2.10 |
| labh | none | none | 7.2.10 |
| edh | iṭ | iṭ | seṭ; 7.2.35 |

Each cell is determined after pada and exact affix identity are known.
A raw “aniṭ root → no i anywhere” rule would incorrectly remove i from
gamiṣya/kariṣya/hariṣya. A raw “future gam → i” rule would incorrectly
produce a luṭ stem with i.

## A4. Root guṇa and augment protection

Before the owned ārdhadhātuka future affix, 7.3.84 with 1.1.3 and
1.1.51 gives bhū → bho, nī → ne, kṛ → kar and hṛ → har. The ṛ
substitution includes r; no surface-only vowel table may discard it.
Where bho is followed by augment i, 6.1.78 gives bhav-i. With no iṭ,
ne is followed by s/t and remains ne at this junction.

The i of iṭ is protected against guṇa/vṛddhi by **1.1.6**. This is
explicit in Bālamanoramā's edhitā procedure stored under 7.4.70; it rules
out a later blind short-upadhā-guṇa query on the added i. Root guṇa and
augment protection are different operations on different owned sounds.
The a-upadhā of gam/pac/labh is not an ik upadhā for 7.3.86. Edh's
initial e is not a short ik upadhā either.

## A5. lṛṭ root/sya junctions

The following is a **normal-form summary**. Pre-Tripādī morphology acts
first on owned occurrences; the sound changes are read in their lawful
later views. The end is a thematic a-stem. Write it as B·a.

| Root | Owned junction after augment/guṇa | Later junction operations | B·a |
|---|---|---|---|
| bhū | bhav · i · sya | 8.3.59 s → ṣ after i | bhaviṣya |
| gam | gam · i · sya | 8.3.59 | gamiṣya |
| nī | ne · sya | 8.3.59 after e | neṣya |
| kṛ | kar · i · sya | 8.3.59 after i | kariṣya |
| hṛ | har · i · sya | 8.3.59 after i | hariṣya |
| pac | pac · sya | 8.2.30 c → k; 8.3.59 s → ṣ after k | pakṣya |
| labh | labh · sya | 8.4.55 bh → p before khar s | lapsya |
| edh | edh · i · sya | 8.3.59 after i | edhiṣya |

Labh's s is not after iṇ/ku in the relevant view; 8.3.59 does not
produce a retroflex s there. The later p from 8.4.55 supplies no such
license either. Pac's c→k is earlier than 8.3.59 and can supply its
velar trigger. These are distinct histories, not one “future cluster” map.

## A6. luṭ interface and the order restriction

Before ending operations the junctions are bhav·i·tās, gam·tās,
ne·tās, kar·tās, har·tās, pac·tās, labh·tās, edh·i·tās. The table
names H for a convenient **final readout factor**:

| Root | H | Eventual root/t junction |
|---|---|---|
| bhū | bhavit | guṇa, 6.1.78 and protected augment i |
| gam | gant | m → ṃ, 8.3.24; ṃ → n, 8.4.58 |
| nī | net | guṇa; no augment |
| kṛ | kart | guṇa with r; no augment |
| hṛ | hart | guṇa with r; no augment |
| pac | pakt | c → k, 8.2.30 |
| labh | labdh | t → dh, 8.2.40; bh → b, 8.4.53 |
| edh | edhit | protected augment i |

H is **not** a license to compute all Tripādī changes, materialize a
string Hās, then feed it backward into 6.4.143. For example the actual
third-singular schedule is

\[
 gam+tās+ḍā\ \to\ gam+t+ā\ \to\ gaṃtā\ \to\ gantā,
\]

not early n followed by a new 6.4.24 query. Similarly labh+tās+ḍā
first loses the owned ṭi ās, then changes root-junction t to dh and bh
to b, giving labdhā. Marker designation/lopa and the whole-ending
substitution are explained in E3.

**Local interface lemma A6.** For each of these eight roots and each
ending path E3–E5, lawful morphology followed by the declared phonetic
readout yields the corresponding H-form in E6.

**Proof.** Every ending path changes only the ās part of tās and/or the
following ending; it retains the root-facing t and its predecessor. The
root/t rules in the last table therefore encounter the same junction in
all nine slots. For labh, the later t→dh→root b sequence is local at that
junction; it cannot affect a later ending's dh. The ending's s-deletion
before dh is a distinct 8.2.25 event. For gam, the relevant m remains
nonfinal before a jhal consonant in every slot, so the same two later
events produce n. Pac's c has the same jhal follower. The other five
junctions already have the stated guṇa/augment output. Substituting these
eight cases into the lawful ending derivations proves the displayed
factorization. □

This is equality of selected final readings. It does not equate histories,
commute arbitrary Sanskrit rules, or establish general confluence.
