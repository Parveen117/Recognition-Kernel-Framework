# PG16 — Future meaning, pada and the admitted input

Author: Monty. 2 November 2026.

## F1. Native state before tense realization

Use the native history module, cuts and pairing of [N1](NATIVE_EXTENSION.md).
Its grammar labels extend PG14's state with

\[
 f=(\ell,\sigma,a,\beta,\nu,\tau,\chi,p,n,\mathcal E).
\]

Here ℓ is the exact lexical entry; σ its selected sense; a the agent;
β the principal-result evidence used for pada; ν the **word-level future
vivakṣā**; τ an external temporal interpretation; χ the construction and
source-reading profile; p,n the controller's person and number; and
\(\mathcal E\) the supporting evidence and explicitly excluded contexts.
Original teaching forms, accent/marker evidence, constituent owners and
all later actions stay in the history. None is reconstructed from a word.

This frame is supplied as an interpreted input. PG16 does not infer it
from unrestricted prose. All asserted events are future relative to the
utterance; the lakāra expresses kartṛ under the inherited 3.4.69 contract.
The agent therefore controls the 1.4.101–108 person/number selection.
This packet admits one resolved controller and one of the nine ordinary
person-number positions. Coordinated-controller conflict is not added.

## F2. Temporal interpretation is an adapter, not native time

Let a supplied calendar/utterance adapter give an utterance u, a future
domain Fut(u), and a current-day region D(u). A completed temporal input
states the intended event-time restriction and its evidence. The traditional
definition quoted by Tattvabodhinī on 3.3.15 includes adjoining portions of
the night; no identification with UTC midnight is imposed here.

No z-generator of N1 is a date. Ordered derivation events are grammatical
composition, not elapsed calendar intervals. Importing D and Fut supplies
a **linguistic semantic adapter**, not a physical clock derived from lambda.

Distinguish three things:

1. The event may in fact happen outside D(u).
2. A sentence constituent may communicate that fact.
3. The verb's own intended future meaning may specifically encode
   anadyatana, rather than future in general.

Only the third is the selection field ν. A scheduler that tests a factual
timestamp and automatically replaces every future verb by luṭ conflates
these levels.

## F3. Source-licensed selection relation

Kāśikā 3.3.13 gives lṛṭ for future in the residual domain and also, through
ca, in the purpose/upapada setting. Kāśikā 3.3.15 gives luṭ for future
anadyatana as an exception, excludes a mixed today/tomorrow meaning, and
separately discusses paridevana. Nyāsa 3.3.5 explicitly distinguishes
future-general **word meaning** from anadyatana supplied by another word
in the sentence. Its concluding distinction licenses the following
bounded selection policy:

| Completed ν | Additional condition | Result |
|---|---|---|
| `FutureGeneral` | Future asserted; no special construction in χ | lṛṭ, 3.3.13 |
| `FutureAnadyatana` | The verb's intended restriction excludes D(u) | luṭ, 3.3.15 |
| `FutureAnadyatana` | The intended restriction includes today | Excluded: inconsistent completed frame |
| ν or required temporal evidence unresolved | More than one completion remains | Open with conditional alternatives |

`FutureGeneral` is a positive semantic choice. It is **not** an unknown
date or an unknown ν. A deliberately mixed “today or tomorrow” future
cannot satisfy the anadyatana restriction; it can select lṛṭ. A future
event known to be tomorrow can still be spoken of with future-general
word meaning under the adopted Nyāsa distinction. We therefore make no
blanket assertion that every sentence containing “tomorrow” and lṛṭ is
ungrammatical. Conversely the two lakāras are not free optional endings
of one fixed completed ν in this construction.

The words and argumentative context of Nyāsa are preserved in
SOURCE_EXCERPTS; this is an attributed interpretation, not the literal
wording of 3.3.13 alone. Mahābhāṣya 3.3.15's yogavibhāga and jñāpaka
also retain lṛṭ in anadyatana in the discussion of sat substitutes. They
are recorded rather than erased by the finite-future policy. PG16 does
not construct that participial domain or claim to settle every interaction
of 3.3.14 with finite expression.

The special kṛt choices of 3.3.3–12, purpose-clause realization, kṛt/sat
replacement, paridevana and other exceptional uses are excluded from χ.
Using Nyāsa 3.3.5's semantic distinction does not import its entire kṛt
optionality into our finite-verb relation.

## F5. Pada and agreement

For bhū/gam use P in the stated ordinary frame; for edh/labh use A,
retaining the lexical evidence for 1.3.12 and the residual P rule 1.3.78.
For nī/kṛ/hṛ/pac use 1.3.72 and its Kāśikā interpretation:

\[
 \beta=\text{principal result accruing to agent}\ \Rightarrow A;
 \qquad \beta=\text{principal result not so accruing}\ \Rightarrow P.
\]

The result is the principal kriyāphala, not every incidental advantage.
Kāśikā's hired-worker discussion prevents wages alone from turning every
paid action into A. Thus a cook preparing the intended food for another
and a cook preparing it for the agent instantiate different frames. Unknown
β on these U roots is Open, not permission to choose either ending freely.
The 1.3.77 upapada option and other special pada environments are excluded.

There are six P profiles and six A profiles: 2 + 4 and 2 + 4 respectively.
Each has nine resolved controller positions. Applying both completed ν
choices gives 12 × 9 × 2 = 216 **positions across completed input frames**.
No single frame is asserted to license all 216 outcomes.

## F6. Stability under knowledge refinement

Interpret an incomplete f as a set of its admitted completions. The positive
cut for an operation contains only frames for which its guard and selected
source policy hold; its false and unknown complements are retained. A
stable branch is one whose guard holds on every completion.

**Proposition F6.** Consistent refinement preserves a stable selection;
it may remove a conditional alternative. Exact coefficient specialization
in N3 preserves all three guard channels.

**Proof.** Refinement shrinks the completion set, so every universally true
guard remains true. An existentially possible completion can disappear.
Specialization changes only residue coefficients and retains f, the
completion evidence and the selected interpretation. Guard membership is
therefore identical before and after it. □
