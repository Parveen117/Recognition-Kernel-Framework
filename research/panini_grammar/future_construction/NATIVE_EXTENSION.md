## N1. The full carrier and the conservative extension

Begin with the canonical native cut field C = C_Σ, its derived iota and
scalar dagger. Extend PG14/PG15's admitted event labels by the source-bearing
future events F1–E6. Construct the free, finite cut-path coefficient algebra

\[
 B_{16}=C\langle z_e,z_e^*:e\in E_{\leq16}\rangle.
\]

Products concatenate ordered residue words, with the rightmost operator
acting first. Dagger reverses words, swaps each generator with its starred
partner and conjugates cut scalars. No extra commutations, analytic
completion or relation z*z=1 is imposed. The whole family of typed
operators below, not one number or one residue letter, is the lambda
carrier used in this construction.

Let H_old be PG15's histories and H_future the histories beginning at the
tagged F1 future frames. They retain exact root IDs, source policies,
calendar-adapter evidence, principal-result/pada decisions, owned sounds,
original markers, intermediate states, ordered events and unresolved
obligations. Distinct paths remain different labels even when word readouts
are equal. Define finite-support right modules

\[
 M_{16,\lambda}=\bigoplus_{h\in H_{old}\sqcup H_{future}}e_h B_{16},
 \qquad
 M_{16,0}=\bigoplus_{h\in H_{old}\sqcup H_{future}}e_h C.
\]

The summands are graded by semantic-frame, selected-lakāra, selected-pada,
owned-affix, realized-ending, phonetic-word and outcome types. Only matching
types compose. The grammar labels are not collapsed merely because their
coefficients use a common native field.

The inclusion B_15 → B_16 takes old residue words to the same words.
Extend the old module's coefficients along this inclusion and embed it as
the old history sector. An old operator acts there exactly as before and
by zero on the new sector. Its old identity maps to P_old, **not** to the
identity on the entire new module. This is a corner extension, preserving
old products, source/target types, adjoints and pairing. No second shared
operator engine or changed historical theorem is introduced.

The declared pairing on the full new carrier is

\[
 \langle x,y\rangle_B=\sum_h x_h^*y_h.
\]

It is sesquilinear and algebraically nondegenerate: coordinate pairing
against e_h detects x_h. It is not a claimed Hilbert norm, probabilistic
inner product or inherited positivity theorem for some other pairing.

## N2. Cuts, events and the grammatical action

Every reviewed predicate gives cuts P_true, P_false, P_unknown on the
corresponding history type; they are mutually orthogonal, dagger-fixed
and sum to that type's identity. Their complements are retained. For
example, the s-initial/P cut in 7.2.58 differs from the sya cut in
7.2.70, and from the ārdhadhātuka/val cut of 7.2.35. Inspection of the
same string “i” cannot replace these predicates.

An admitted event e:h→h′ records its rule, commentary interpretation,
guard proof, read/write owners, old/new sounds and the actual appended
history. Choose U_e=1+z_e and set

\[
 T_{e,\lambda}(e_h a)=e_{h'}U_ea,
 \qquad T_{e,\lambda}(e_k a)=0\quad(k\ne h).
\]

This is right-B-linear; the updated h′ includes a real semantic or
morphological action. A non-applicable event is not a successful identity
derivation. Open and Excluded are separately tagged outcomes, and a
completed path has an explicit accept event.

The pairing gives
\(T_{e,\lambda}^*(e_{h'}a)=e_hU_e^*a\).
It is an algebraic adjoint, not a licensed reverse grammar rule. The
admitted operator category is generated by typed events, their adjoints,
the cuts, finite sums and lawful composition. Each fixed finite frame has
finitely many admitted next events; a retained history has its unique
immediate predecessor. These facts make the relevant operators and
adjoints well-defined on finite support.

F3's calendar information is a retained input adapter. Neither path length
nor z_e is elapsed time. E3's ḍā replacement, later marker designation
and ṭi-lopa are different events; no universal commuting rewrite is added
because an alternative commentary schedule prints the same word.

## N3. Exact typed specialization

Construct ε:B_16→C by taking the empty-word coefficient. All nonempty
residue words go to zero. Products of nonempty words remain nonempty,
so ε is a unital dagger-preserving algebra homomorphism. Define

\[
 \pi_{0,M}(\sum_h e_h a_h)=\sum_h e_h\epsilon(a_h),
 \qquad \Pi_{0,op}(A)=(\epsilon(A_{kh}))_{k,h}.
\]

Pairings specialize through ε, and input frames keep their full label.
These maps have distinct domains; neither is the word observer in N4.

**Theorem N3.** For every admitted finite typed construction A and
finite-support state x,

\[
 \pi_{0,M}(Ax)=\Pi_{0,op}(A)\pi_{0,M}(x),
 \qquad
 \epsilon(\langle x,y\rangle_B)
   =\langle\pi_{0,M}x,\pi_{0,M}y\rangle_C.
\]

Specialization preserves typed sums, products, adjoints, cuts, complementary
channels and the selected grammatical query results. On unit-start accepted
derivations it recovers **exactly** the labelled classical relation R of
D10, including the same Open/Excluded outcomes for partial inputs.

**Proof.** On one event, ε(U_e a)=ε(a). A cut keeps the same history
membership; the predicates inspect retained grammar data and source
policy, not invisible coefficient residues. The adjoint identity follows
from ε(U_e*)=1. For products, typed matrix multiplication has finite
coefficient sums on the admitted support, and ε respects each sum and
product. Induction proves the operator identity; the pairing statement
uses ε(a*b)=overline(ε(a))ε(b) coordinatewise.

A unit-start path e_1,…,e_m has coefficient
\((1+z_{e_m})\cdots(1+z_{e_1})\), whose constant coefficient is 1.
It therefore survives with its entire history. Conversely R and its
native lift have exactly the same guarded labelled edges by construction,
so every finite accepted classical path has that lift. Distinct branches
occupy distinct history summands and cannot cancel each other. The same
argument applies to the separately tagged negative/open outcomes. □

The support assertion is for unit-start branches, not arbitrary signed
linear combinations of inputs: those can cancel after ε. The algebraic
identity still holds for them. This theorem does not prove source facts
that were left outside the adopted domain or automatically identify an
old Python function with the new native operators.

## N4. Twelve luṭ fibres that lose pada under word observation

Fix canonical completed contexts, one P and one A for each of
nī/kṛ/hṛ/pac, and a canonical derivation for each of their three luṭ
third-person numbers. Let

\[
 I=\{nī,kṛ,hṛ,pac\}\times\{sg,du,pl\},\qquad |I|=12.
\]

For i∈I, E3 maps the two different histories to the same word, respectively
netā/netārau/netāraḥ, kartā/kartārau/kartāraḥ,
hartā/hartārau/hartāraḥ or paktā/paktārau/paktāraḥ.
These twelve words are distinct from one another in the selected reading.
Restrict to the free submodule

\[
 V_\lambda=\bigoplus_{i\in I}(e_{i,P}B\oplus e_{i,A}B).
\]

The word-only observer O and retained A-pada cut P_A are

\[
 O((a_i,b_i)_i)=(a_i+b_i)_i,
 \qquad P_A((a_i,b_i)_i)=(0,b_i)_i.
\]

**Theorem N4a.** No linear operator D on the twelve word coordinates
satisfies OP_A=DO on V_λ or its zero reading. The exact kernel of O is
the free right module with twelve independent generators
\(e_{i,P}-e_{i,A}\).

**Proof.** The equation O(a,b)=0 is coordinatewise b_i=−a_i,
which gives the displayed free kernel basis. On e_{i,P}−e_{i,A},
O gives zero but OP_A gives minus the nonzero i-th word basis vector.
Hence OP_A cannot factor through O. The coefficients are ±1, so the
same obstruction survives ε. □

In grammatical terms, the common third-person word cannot recover
whether its agent frame licensed P or A. Later paradigm questions do
distinguish them: nī gives netāsi versus netāse in second singular,
and netāsmi versus netāhe in first singular. Selecting a new controller
from the retained formation is an explicitly declared **paradigm query**,
not a claim that Sanskrit has a reverse rule from netā to an earlier state.

This is a block-sum application of PG15's observer obstruction to the
new luṭ derivations. It is not claimed as a new general no-descent theorem.

## N5. Exact repair, pairing and the restricted size bound

Retain the A coefficient in each of these twelve collision fibres:

\[
 R((a_i,b_i)_i)=((s_i,b_i)_i),\quad s_i=a_i+b_i,
 \qquad R^{-1}((s_i,b_i)_i)=((s_i-b_i,b_i)_i).
\]

Then R is an explicit right-B-module isomorphism. The repaired pada cut is

\[
 RP_AR^{-1}(s,b)=(b,b),
\]

not the naive coordinate cut (0,b). The transported pairing is

\[
 \langle(s,b),(t,d)\rangle_R
   =\sum_{i\in I}\big((s_i-b_i)^*(t_i-d_i)+b_i^*d_i\big).
\]

Substituting R^{-1} proves the pairing formula, nondegeneracy and the
adjoint properties of the transported cut. All entries use native 0,1,−1;
therefore R, R^{-1}, the cut and this pairing commute exactly with ε.
This repair preserves the original pairing through transport instead of
silently declaring the two output coordinates orthogonal.

**Theorem N5.** Among finite linear repairs (O,K) that have an injective
zero reading on this fixed 24-coordinate sector, at least twelve extra
cut-scalar coordinates are necessary. The repair R attains that bound.

**Proof.** At zero, ker O is a twelve-dimensional C-vector space. If
(O,K) is injective, K restricted to ker O is injective. A map from that
space into C^q requires q≥12. R adds twelve coordinates and has the
displayed inverse, so it attains the lower bound. □

The qualification “injective zero reading” is essential: no arbitrary
rank argument for free modules over a noncommutative algebra is being
smuggled in. Nor is this a twelve-bit bound for classifying one word, a
minimum for every grammar history, or a decoder from unrestricted Sanskrit
text. A single canonical word token and a supplied P/A tag are a different
problem from recovering all linear combinations in V. Outside the fixed
canonical sector, lexical, semantic and history information can create
many more collisions, and the present repair makes no claim to recover it.
