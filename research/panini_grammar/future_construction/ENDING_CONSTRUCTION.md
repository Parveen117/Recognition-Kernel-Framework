# PG16 — Nine-slot ending constructions

Author: Monty. 2 November 2026.

The following operations act on N1's owned constituent states. Every final
word retains its lexical ID, future vivakṣā, pada decision and original
tiṅ member. Conventional 3/2/1 below correspond to prathama/madhyama/uttama.
The displayed words are at pause. Final s therefore uses 8.2.66 and
8.3.15; sentence-external sandhi is not part of the table.

## E1. lṛṭ P

Let the selected sya aṅga of A5 be B·a. Under 3.4.78 the P endings are
tip, tas, jhi; sip, thas, tha; mip, vas, mas. Marker removal retains
their identities. Final s in the appropriate vibhakti members is protected
from it designation by 1.3.4; it is not deleted merely because it is a
final consonant.

| Slot | Owned ending path and junction | Final readout |
|---|---|---|
| 3sg | tip → ti, marker lopa | Bati |
| 3du | tas retained; final s → ru → ḥ at pause | Bataḥ |
| 3pl | jhi → anti, 7.1.3; a+a → a, 6.1.97 | Banti |
| 2sg | sip → si, marker lopa | Basi |
| 2du | thas retained; final s → ru → ḥ | Bathaḥ |
| 2pl | tha retained | Batha |
| 1sg | mip → mi; a → ā, 7.3.101 | Bāmi |
| 1du | vas; a → ā, 7.3.101; final s → ru → ḥ | Bāvaḥ |
| 1pl | mas; a → ā, 7.3.101; final s → ru → ḥ | Bāmaḥ |

Rule 7.3.101 has an a-final aṅga, a yañ-initial ending and the
sārvadhātuka condition here. Kāśikā literally illustrates pakṣyāmi,
pakṣyāvaḥ, pakṣyāmaḥ. It is not a general instruction to lengthen every
future's vowel before first-person endings.

## E2. lṛṭ A

The source endings are ta, ātām, jha; thās, āthām, dhvam; iṭ, vahi,
mahiṅ. Lṛṭ supplies the ṭit condition for 3.4.79. The ṭi selector is
1.1.64: the span from the last vowel onward, not simply the last letter.
The relevant non-pit sārvadhātuka endings receive the ṅit-like status
under 1.2.4. PG14 D1's thematic construction applies here because sya
leaves the same a-final interface, with its **future ownership retained**.

| Slot | Owned ending path and junction | Final readout |
|---|---|---|
| 3sg | ta → te, 3.4.79 | Bate |
| 3du | ātām → āte; initial ā → iy, 7.2.81; y-lopa, 6.1.66; a+i → e, 6.1.87 | Bete |
| 3pl | jha → jhe; jh → ant, 7.1.3; a+a → a, 6.1.97 | Bante |
| 2sg | thās → se, specific 3.4.80 | Base |
| 2du | āthām → āthe; initial ā → iy; y-lopa; a+i → e, as in 3du | Bethe |
| 2pl | dhvam → dhve, 3.4.79 | Badhve |
| 1sg | ending iṭ → i; i → e, 3.4.79; a+e → e, 6.1.97 | Be |
| 1du | vahi → vahe; a → ā, 7.3.101 | Bāvahe |
| 1pl | mahiṅ → mahi → mahe; a → ā, 7.3.101 | Bāmahe |

In 3pl of both padas, the ending's internal n undergoes 8.3.24 to
anusvāra and 8.4.58 back to dental n before t. A later n cannot then
be sent back to an earlier ṇatva query; 8.2.1 retains the ordered view.
Kāśikā 8.4.58 explicitly explains this phenomenon. Thus kariṣyanti and
kariṣyante have a retained phonetic history even where the spelling
before/after that pair of events is identical.

The A 1sg surface e does not by itself attest the 3.4.79 event: a+i
could also give e, but by **6.1.87**, not by 6.1.97. The actual licensed
construction here has ending i→e and then a+e under 6.1.97. Source
history, not a rule-ablation spelling difference, supplies that distinction.

## E3. luṭ third person in both padas

Rule **2.4.85** replaces the third-person members by ḍā, rau, ras.
There are six input members across P/A but three substitutes. Nyāsa
explains the correspondence by number: each singular member receives
ḍā, each dual rau, each plural ras. It is not a zip of a six-element list
with a three-element list, and it does not change madhyama/uttama members.

Write the pre-phonetic aṅga as J·tās. J can include a root-owned change
and an affix-owned augment. The third-person derivations are:

| Slot, either pada | Morphology before the late root/t junction | Readout using A6's H |
|---|---|---|
| 3sg | Original ending → whole ḍā; then ḍ designation/lopa; 6.4.143 removes the aṅga's ṭi ās | Hā |
| 3du | Original ending → rau; 7.4.51 removes tās's s before r | Hārau |
| 3pl | Original ending → ras; 7.4.51 removes tās's s; final ras-s → ru → ḥ | Hāraḥ |

**Replacement order matters.** In Nyāsa's account, before substitution
ḍā is not yet the relevant pratyaya, so its ḍ has not already undergone
1.3.7 and lopa. Its anekāl shape therefore replaces the **whole ending**
under 1.1.55. Only then is its ḍ retained as an unpronounced marker.
The resulting ḍit trigger gives 6.4.143 ṭi-lopa, explicitly extended
here beyond the ordinary bha restriction by the marker's purpose.
Deleting ḍ before determining replacement extent loses this argument.

**A provenance is retained without repeating every A operation.** The
selected Nyāsa schedule lets 2.4.85 act before 3.4.79. The same commentary
uses the thāsaḥ se jñāpaka to prevent ṭi-e from applying afresh to
tiṅ substitutes. Thus an A 3sg still remembers ta/A, but its substitute
ḍā is not rewritten to e. Kāśikā 3.4.79 restricts the operation to the
specified tiṅ members; a bare retained “A=true” flag is too broad.
Padamañjarī discusses another scheduling account. We preserve it as
source evidence, not a second mandatory pass through ṭi-e.

This is a query-relative use of original identity: number/pada history
survives, while the direct target predicate of a different operation can
be false. Global erasure and global reapplication both give wrong models.

## E4. luṭ P second and first persons

For these positions the original P ending survives 2.4.85's domain cut.

| Slot | Path from J·tās plus the ending | Final readout |
|---|---|---|
| 2sg | sip → si; 7.4.50 deletes the **tās-owned** s before ending s | Hāsi |
| 2du | thas retained; final s → ru → ḥ | Hāsthaḥ |
| 2pl | tha retained | Hāstha |
| 1sg | mip → mi | Hāsmi |
| 1du | vas retained; final s → ru → ḥ | Hāsvaḥ |
| 1pl | mas retained; final s → ru → ḥ | Hāsmaḥ |

The aṅga is s-final before first-person endings, so 7.3.101's a-final
guard fails. The long ā already belongs to tās. It is not newly
lengthened by a thematic-present operation. Kāśikā 7.2.58's negative
examples give gantāsmi/gantāsvaḥ/gantāsmaḥ; Siddhāntakaumudī 7.4.51
supplies the corresponding bhavitā- series.

## E5. luṭ A second and first persons

| Slot | Path from J·tās plus the ending | Final readout |
|---|---|---|
| 2sg | thās → se, 3.4.80; tās-s lopa before s, 7.4.50 | Hāse |
| 2du | āthām → āthe, 3.4.79; its initial ā remains | Hāsāthe |
| 2pl | dhvam → dhve, 3.4.79; tās-s lopa before dh, 8.2.25 under the selected Kaumudī reading | Hādhve |
| 1sg | ending iṭ → i → e, 3.4.79; tās-s → h before e, 7.4.52 | Hāhe |
| 1du | vahi → vahe, 3.4.79 | Hāsvahe |
| 1pl | mahiṅ → mahi → mahe, 3.4.79 | Hāsmahe |

Three scope points close the missing cells without guessing from a table:

1. **2du:** 7.2.81 inherits *ataḥ*, requiring a preceding short a-final
   aṅga. The immediately preceding aṅga ends in tās-s. The condition
   fails, so āthe remains āthe, giving edhitāsāthe, not a thematic
   *edhitāsethe*. Bālamanoramā's procedure stored under 7.4.70 explicitly
   derives edhitāsāthe from āthām and ṭi-e.
2. **2pl:** Siddhāntakaumudī 8.2.25 directly gives edhitādhve. That
   commentary admits the relevant tās-s lopa before the dh-initial
   ending. Kāśikā records a restriction dispute involving sic; we adopt
   and name Kaumudī's future procedure rather than silently treating all
   commentary interpretations as identical. The publisher's Bālamanoramā
   entry at 8.2.25 is misattached and is not evidence for this step.
3. **1du:** 7.3.101 again sees a consonant-final aṅga; vahi→vahe
   suffices. The result is edhitāsvahe, not an inserted thematic ā
   before v. The existing tās ā is unchanged.

For 1sg, h is a **substitute for s**, not an arbitrary connecting sound
or s-lopa followed by unexplained h insertion. Ending iṭ and augment
iṭ, when both occur in edhitāhe, have distinct owners and roles.

## E6. Finite ending exhaustion

E1/E2 partition lṛṭ by pada and the nine original tiṅ members. For luṭ,
E3 covers exactly the third-person number triples and E4/E5 cover their
complements. The original member, number and pada jointly determine the
selected case; each case has all of its admitted ending operations above.

**Proposition E6.** Given a completed F-domain frame and the selected
source/phonetic policy, these tables determine one ending path and one
word readout for each admitted root/tense/pada/slot combination.

**Proof.** F3 fixes a future identity and F5 fixes pada and agreement.
The 3.4.78 member is therefore fixed. If lṛṭ, it appears in exactly
one row of E1/E2. If luṭ third person, 2.4.85 and number select exactly
one row of E3; otherwise E4/E5 gives exactly one row. Each listed guard
is either established or explicitly false, including the contrasting
7.2.81 and 7.3.101 guards. A3 fixes the augment branch. A5 and lemma
A6 give the root-dependent phonetic readout. No remaining alternative
is claimed inside the chosen non-doubling, unaccented policy. □

This is completeness for the **presented bounded construction**. It does
not assert that no other source interpretation, Vedic use, special frame
or phonetic variant exists outside it.
