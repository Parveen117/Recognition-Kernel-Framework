# Written derivations

## H6. The two future stems of tapas

Write tapasyiṣyati/tapasiṣyati and tapasyitā/tapasitā. A parallel romantha request gives romanthāyiṣyate and romanthāyitā, one form per slot on this schedule. Distinguish the 3sg luṭ ending shared across padas from the derivational entitlement on which other slots depend.

## H7. Scope of the two past changes

On ūṣma and abhra, āṭ with 6.1.90 gives auṣmāyiṣyata and ābhrāyiṣyata in lṛṅ 3sg. On tapas, aṭ precedes the whole base: atapasyīt/atapasīt in P luṅ 3sg. On bāṣpa the inherited A aorist gives abāṣpāyiṣṭa.

## H8. The single y of tapasyāt has two preimages

The P blessing permits tapasyyāt and tapasyāt. The shorter word follows either morphological loss of the ky initial or later 8.4.64 reduction after s. Keep three histories for each pair. The A blessing of the experiential stem instead gives sukhāyiṣīṣṭa, with a determinate underlying y.

## H9. Two-word perfect and its retained role

Tapas yields tapasyām or tapasām with the selected P auxiliary set. Sukhāyām with A-kṛ or P bhū/as remains subject to the original experience-owner guard. The auxiliary cannot assign a different person as the experiencer merely because a different verb bears the visible ending.
