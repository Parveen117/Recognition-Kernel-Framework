# PG23 — Whole-root hi exceptions and actual modal seams

Author: Monty. 2 November 2026.

## E1. Han: four distinct root-facing situations

Retain PG11's śap-luk, exact unaccented han identity and separate
6.4.37/98 targets. All original sounds remain in history.

| Situation | Source operation | Modal consequence |
|---|---|---|
| strong tu or first-person āṭ | no weak deletion | hantu; hanāni, hanāva, hanāma |
| weak direct jhal tām/tam/ta or new tāt | 6.4.37 deletes the root n | hatām, hatam, hata, hatāt |
| weak direct vowel-initial antu | 6.4.98 deletes upadhā a; 7.3.54 reads actual h∣n | ghnantu |
| O yā/yus | y is neither jhal nor vowel; neither deletion applies | hanyāt, hanyuḥ |

Kāśikā 7.3.54 explicitly prints ghnantu and permits the actual new
h/n adjacency despite a's retained lopa history. That permission is local
to the addressed query. O 3pl has y between han and us after the
6.1.96 seam, so its following root sound is not the bare u of jus.
Copying the loṭ 3pl weak stem would produce the wrong O interface.

Ordinary hi has the separate **6.4.36 whole han→ja** substitution,
giving ja∣hi. It is not an unqualified initial h→j sound rule, and
does not require inventing copied material. Kaumudī/Bālamanoramā
6.4.36 and Kāśikā 6.4.22 explicitly keep the new a invisible to
the 6.4.105 hi-luk query, giving **jahi**, not *ja. The rule's
own hi condition is absent on tāt: that branch gives hatāt by the
weak jhal route, not *jatāt.

The source-qualified original query window for jahi thus differs from
a thematic a∣hi. N4 proves why a current-a-only observation loses the
hi-luk cut. The record retains both the actual new ja and the old han;
asiddha does not mean undoing the substitution everywhere.

## E2. Śās: i before weak hal, but whole śā before hi

On the exact अनुशिष्टौ entry, **6.4.34** gives short i for upadhā ā
before the admitted hal-initial kit/ṅit ending. The resulting original
s takes **8.3.60** ṣ. Before t this gives śiṣṭ-, and before O y
it gives śiṣy-. The other named āṅ-śās entry is excluded by the
source itself; spelling alone is not enough to select this operation.

The ordinary hi path has the specific **6.4.35 whole śās→śā**
substitute. Bālamanoramā identifies it as the exception to the i rule.
Kāśikā/Kaumudī 6.4.22/35 preserve the earlier jhal final s for
6.4.101, giving **śādhi**. Merely applying a late s-lopa to an
already completed śiṣ route would not justify the retained long ā.

Śās is named abhyasta by **6.1.6**, so ordinary P 3pl takes 7.1.4
at: **śāsatu**. Its vowel first sound blocks 6.4.34. Strong first
person retains śāsā-, with dental ending n in śāsāni; the original
s has no iṇ-triggered ṣ here. O has y first, so its factor is śiṣ.
Tātaṅ has weak t first and no actual hi, giving **śiṣṭāt**.

## E3. The five rudādi roots

M2 supplies all five iṭ guards. Rud alone among these five has an
ik vowel needing the new strong light-upadhā guṇa: roditu versus
ruditām/rudihi. Svap/śvas/an/jakṣ retain their stated root vowels;
ordinary strong first person gives svapāni, śvasāni, anāni and
jakṣāṇi. The last ṇ is an ending n reached from original ṣ across
ā by 8.4.1–2, not an alteration of the lexical root.

Jakṣ, like śās, is **named** abhyasta under 6.1.6. Its ordinary
3pl is jakṣatu, while rud/svap/śvas/an have antu. The designation
changes the ending query without creating an abhyāsa owner or allowing
a copied-span lopa. In O, 3.4.108 jus takes priority over ant/at.

Neither the bare class name nor “strong” alone selects iṭ. It occurs
before weak hi/tām but is absent before strong āṭ, and absent before
O y. All five new tāt branches are consonant-initial and take iṭ;
rud remains weak, giving ruditāt rather than *roditāt.

## E4. Brū: root ū, augment ī and the modal y boundary

Ordinary strong P 3sg has brū∣ī∣tu. Root 7.3.84 gives bro,
then 6.1.78 gives brav before the owned augment ī: **bravītu**.
Strong first-person āṭ similarly gives bravā-, with ending ṇ in
bravāṇi through the admitted r…v…ā interval, and **bravai** in A.

Weak consonant contexts keep ū: brūtām, brūhi, brūtam, brūta,
brūṣva, brūdhvam. The ṣ in brūṣva belongs to the following
affix's s under 8.3.59; the root vowel is unchanged.

Before weak vowel-initial material **6.4.77** supplies uvaṅ on the
root ū, giving bruvantu, bruvātām and bruvatām. It also gives the
A O factor **bruv** before resolved īy. P O has y first and uses
**brū**. The different factors are not optional variants of one slot.

M1/M3 prove why brūyāt and brūtāt have no īṭ. They do not become
vac- or āh- forms, whose sources belong to other moods/conditions.
No 6.4.106 hi-luk is licensed by this root-owned ū.

## E5. Śī: lexical guṇa and two different r owners

M4 gives śe independently of ending weakness. Before consonants it
remains śe: śetām, śeṣva and śedhvam. Before vowels it takes ay,
giving śayātām/śayāthām and the first-person śayai/śayāvahai/
śayāmahai. The ordinary 3pl follows the distinct ruṭ route to śeratām.

In O the resolved augment begins with ī; e→ay gives the common factor
**śay**, yielding śayīta through śayīmahi by PG22's A constructor.
In śayīran the r is owned by ran. No ruṭ is inserted there.

The retained vidhiliṅ schedule has dental dh in śayīdhvam/bruvīdhvam.
PG20's āśīrliṅ ṣīdhvam sound options are not transferred to these
sārvadhātuka forms. Bālamanoramā 7.1.6 separately explains why the
ordinary śedhve/śedhvam path does not invoke that ḍh operation.

## E6. Finite sound and option boundary

The new profiles have no eligible 8.4.65 cluster: the rudādi iṭ
separates root and t/dh contexts; brū/śī supply vowel-final junctions;
han has n deletion or a nasal followed by one stop; śās has ṣ∣ṭ,
which is not a savarṇa jhar pair. The selected non-doubling policy remains.

B retains every ordinary I word under its own meaning record and adds
one tāt word at each P 3sg and 2sg. No A or other slot has that target.
The root-specific tāt procedures above are evaluated before final readout;
one cannot replace the last syllable of jahi/śādhi/rudihi by tāt.

**Proposition E6.** M/E exhaust the new ordinary and B paths on D2,
and each path has one sound reading under the selected policy.

**Proof.** The nine lexical identities are exhausted by E1–E5. Their
following contexts are the prescribed strong/vowel, weak consonant,
ordinary hi and O augment boundaries. The addressed source exceptions
resolve the competing deletions/substitutions; E6's cluster classification
excludes an unlisted collapse branch. Only the explicitly admitted B ending
choice adds a second reading at its sixteen positions. □
