## S3. Effective pit and the two brū contexts

Tattvabodhinī 3.4.103 explicitly states that specific ṅit defeats
pitness. M1 uses this as the positive source for the P-optative effective
feature. The root's unchanged strong/weak history is retained separately.

The Kaumudī 2.4.53 entry prints brūyāt twice, including an anomalous
first occurrence between bravītu and the apit/no-īṭ explanation.
Bālamanoramā's expansion explicitly names **brūtāt** in that immediate
argument and later separately introduces vidhiliṅ brūyāt. Therefore
the first Kaumudī string is not used to claim that its no-īṭ sentence
unambiguously discusses the optative. The optative conclusion uses the
explicit 3.4.103 marker rule together with 7.3.93's pit guard.
The tātaṅ no-īṭ discussion supplies the analogous independent case.

This keeps both literal bytes and the inference visible. Neither the
host 2.4.53 rule nor its out-of-domain perfect/āśīrliṅ forms becomes
new PG23 operational coverage.

## S4. Words, derivations and literal anomalies

Kaumudī/Bālamanoramā 6.4.35–36 supply śādhi/jahi and modal anchors.
Bālamanoramā 7.2.76 prints the roditu/rudihi/ruditāt/rodāni paths.
Bālamanoramā 7.1.6 gives the śī modal paradigm and śayīta; its
printed second singular is damaged as **सेष्व**. Retain it literally
and derive śeṣva from śe plus the affix-owned s under 8.3.59 rather
than presenting that damaged token as an exact positive witness.

Bālamanoramā 2.4.53 has multiple **ब्राउ / ब्राऊ / ब्राव** spellings.
They remain raw in the archive; the undamaged lexical/rule evidence
and stated vowel operations supply brū/brav/bruv. Its explicit
grammatical explanations can still support their attributed claims.

P's complete tuples/factors are written consequences, not 270 separately
quoted word attestations. The 286 readings use the selected spelling
policy; other accents, external sandhi or unrestricted doubling are not
silently counted. Independent edition comparison and Sanskrit review
remain open.
