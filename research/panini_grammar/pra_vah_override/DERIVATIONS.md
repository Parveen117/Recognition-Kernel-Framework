# PG110 — Vah grades, samprasāraṇa and ḍh-junctions

Monty · 10 November 2026

## H06. Śap present system and pra+aṭ

The four present-system labels use śap and vah-a. Retain the ordinary
P thematic endings, loṭ 2sg hi-lopa and vidhi-liṅ nonfinal s-lopa.
Pra's r licenses ending-owned n→ṇ in pravahāṇi through permitted
interveners. Third-plural dental n is restored after 8.3.24/8.4.58 and
does not re-enter earlier ṇatva; laṅ/lṛṅ final n is separately protected.
Pra+aṭ→prā by 6.1.101, with augment history still present.

## H07. Two future interfaces, two final-consonant outcomes

Sya is aniṭ: vah+sya, h→ḍh (8.2.31), ḍh→k before s (8.2.41),
then s→ṣ, gives vakṣya-. Thus pravakṣyati and conditional prāvakṣyat
share this affix but have distinct contexts and aṭ histories.

Tās instead takes h→ḍh, t→dh (8.2.40), retroflex contact and ḍh-lopa
(8.3.13). **6.3.112** then replaces root a by o: voḍhā, not vāḍhā.
Use the inherited finite luṭ ḍā/rau/ras and remaining ending contracts.
Its finite voḍhā is not counted as an agent noun. The source's lexical
o examples and the sūtra's ot govern; the archived Kāśikā phrase
इत्येतयोरवर्णस्यौकार is not normalized or used to create an au variant.

## H08. Blessing root samprasāraṇa versus perfect copy work

P āśīrliṅ kit under 3.4.104 triggers yajādi 6.1.15: v→u, then
6.1.108 absorbs the following a, giving **uh+yāsuṭ**, hence uhyāt.
No reduplication or long ū is introduced. Pra+u gives pro by 6.1.87:
prohyāt and its eight companions.

In strong liṭ, 6.1.17 acts on the copy: u+vah. Ṇal's strong third
singular gives uvāha; thal keeps short root a. 6.4.126 blocks the
otherwise available thal-seṭ e/contraction pathway. Weak kit liṭ
instead applies root samprasāraṇa before reduplication; u+uh coalesces
to **ūh**, giving ūhatuḥ/ūhuḥ/ūhathuḥ/ūha/ūhiva/ūhima.

First-singular ṇal ON/OFF work gives uvāha/uvaha. Thal-iṭ gives
uvahitha; its aniṭ branch uses the owned ḍh-junction and 6.3.112 to
give uvoḍha. Prefix pra stays outside all copy work: provāha/provaha,
provahitha/provoḍha, and prohatuḥ/prohuḥ etc. Prefix guṇa conceals
whether its input was short u or long ū; the underlying owners survive.

## H09. Sic vṛddhi and contact-sensitive o replacement

P luṅ uses aniṭ sic and 7.2.3 halanta a→ā. With ending īṭ in 3sg/2sg,
the h-s sequence gives kṣ: avākṣīt/avākṣīḥ. The same retained sic
channel gives avākṣuḥ/avākṣam/avākṣva/avākṣma in the other non-t slots.

Before t in 3du/2du/2pl, 8.2.26 first deletes sic s. The h-t contact
then follows the ḍh-junction route, and 6.3.112 replaces even the
vṛddhi ā by o: avoḍhām/avoḍham/avoḍha. Kāśikā expressly explains
avarna-grahaṇa as retaining this long-vowel case. No false ā survives
in those three root grades. Prefix+aṭ remains prā throughout.

The apparent later retroflex input to 8.3.13 follows its archived
local visibility explanation. It is not permission to rerun every
earlier phonetic rule. Neither causative caṅ nor an A sic path is counted.

## H10. Complete bounded accounting

One qualified profile has **90 requests, 92 readings/paths and ten
complete paradigms**. Liṭ 2sg and 1sg each have two readings. Root iṭ:
three positions/three paths; initial aṭ: 27; ending īṭ: two; sic s-lopa:
