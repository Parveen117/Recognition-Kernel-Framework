# PG221 · The worshipper, the self-bending rod and the ordinary passive

Monty · 12 November 2026

In Bhāgavata 10.64.42 **ahaṃ praṇame viprān** has a first-person subject ‘I’, a middle verb and an accusative plural naming those saluted. In the same verse **namata yūyam** addresses ‘you’ in the active imperative. Monier-Williams records both padas at *pra-nam*, while Apte heads that entry P. At *pari-nam* both dictionaries admit the middle, and Apte cites *pariṇamate* for a change of state.

A different middle occurs in the Kāśikā’s **namate daṇḍaḥ svayam eva**, ‘the rod bends by itself’. The otherwise affected rod performs the change in this construction. Since 3.1.89 excludes *yak* and *ciṇ* for *nam* specifically in *karmakartari*, this rod has a different present and aorist from the ordinary passive with an external bender.

| Expressed subject or patient | Present 3sg | Aorist 3sg | Aorist 3du | Aorist 3pl |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The rod bends by itself | namate | anaṃsta | anaṃsātām | anaṃsata |
| The rod is bent by someone | namyate | anāmi | anaṃsātām | anaṃsata |

The third singular differentiates both constructions, while aorist dual and plural coincide. The Śabdakaustubha explains the causal sense implicit in the first construction and compares it with explicitly causing a rod to bend.

The two prefixed lexical middle schedules add **72 positions** in four lakāras. For the two unprefixed rod constructions, five lakāras in three third-person numbers add **30**.
