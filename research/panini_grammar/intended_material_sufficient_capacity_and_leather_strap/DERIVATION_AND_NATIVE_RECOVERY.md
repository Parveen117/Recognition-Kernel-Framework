# PG750 · Two readings of the same wall bricks

## The product and its intended material

At 5.1.12 the base denotes what is to be made. The derivative qualifies the material set apart to make it. The wood is to become charcoal; a scabbard intended for a sword never becomes the sword. A well may be a source of water without becoming the water. An account of which grain something came from also fails unless the speaker means an intended use. Kāśikā derives the supporting dative from purpose and cites an alternative carrying forward the earlier dative construction. The cha body īya, after the base-final vowel treatment, yields aṅgārīyāṇi on neuter plural kāṣṭhāni and prākārīyāḥ on feminine plural iṣṭakāḥ. The Bhāṣāvṛtti adds yūpīyam for wood. The earlier stake and cotton forms are positive controls owned by PG748.

At 5.1.13 use the three named bases with ḍhañ, giving initial vṛddhi by 7.2.117 and eya by 7.1.2. The grass for a roof has chādiṣeya, with the source ṣ in the derivative and ṇ in plural -āṇi under 8.4.1–2. The wood for the wheeled vehicle has aupadheya. Bāla at 13 identifies the inner wheel component as something mounted on the axle end. Prefix upa and the placing root dhā (03.0011) enter 3.3.92 ki, with k retained as an it condition; 6.4.64 removes ā before the admitted vowel-initial kit affix. Thus upa-dh-i. The material construction and the supplementary own-sense construction converge on aupadheyam. The former denotes wood destined for the component, the latter the wooden component itself. The similarly prefixed PG745 placement noun has a different inner suffix.

Bali gives bāleya. The grammar's rice has M NOMpl bāleyāḥ. In Raghuvaṃśa 14.77 the girls bring grain suitable for an offering. Bījaṃ is one object of āharantyaḥ; its adjective is N ACCsg bāleyam. Mallinātha himself assigns 5.1.13 and describes the grain as suitable for worship. Neither the grammatical gender of taṇḍula nor an initial letter of the English transcription determines this case.

## Calf, shoe and two r-histories

Rule 14 selects ñya; the same marker conditions vṛddhi. Ṛṣabha gives ārṣabhya (ṛ → ār) of a calf actually reared to become a bull. Upānah → aupānahya qualifies either masculine muñja grass or neuter hide. Both are intended to become a shoe. The common leather condition would otherwise fit 15. Nyāsa uses the shoe made from grass as the unopposed 14 example, the strap hide as the unopposed 15 example, and the shoe made from hide as the decisive overlap. Here 14 wins. The PG748 sanaṅgu exception remains on its own route.

Carman in 15 is a genitive specifying the material from which the product is made. The affix is added to the name of that product, in the sense of leather for it. The varatrā thong yields vāratra under añ. The strap example has a documented alternative. Five-pāda Uṇādi 2.28 supplies ran on vṛdh (01.0863, growth) and vap. The old vapra rampart is a different root. On the leather side guṇa of ṛ yields ar; vṛdh + ra gives vardhra. The marker n is deleted. Tattva explicitly describes initial accent. Taking the leather product under 4.3.134 gives the aṇ derivative with initial vṛddhi; 4.1.15 ṅīp gives the feminine strap vārdhrī. Its dative singular is vārdhryai. Now an independently owned outer 5.1.15 añ selects leather intended for that strap, giving vārdhram. The outer affix does not supply the already present r. The Padamañjarī explicitly uses the leather-strap comparison in the named animal: this supplies the inner derivation left open by PG608 on that source policy. Its already written outer nas and compound retain zero new case credits.

Tattva separately reports vadhrī from vadh with Uṇādi ṣṭran. Its derivative for hide destined for that strap is vādhram, with ā and no r before dh. The two written spellings correspond to different base histories. The lexical assignment of the r-bearing variant is positive without normalizing all Sanskrit editions to it. This packet treats the shorter inner route as a reported source analysis; no new general rule for that Uṇādi suffix is inferred.

## What the bricks can supply

The next relation holds for resources adequate to realize a result, or for a site adequate to contain it. The input is the nominative of the possible structure, and the genitive or locative is the denotation of the derivative, selected separately in 5.1.16. A quantity of bricks can suffice for a wall even if nobody has earmarked them for it. The same prākārīyāḥ thus has two separately posed requests, the dative-supported intended-material case and the genitive adequacy case. The passage also supplies wood sufficient for a palace, a site for a wall and ground for a palace. Mere prospect of Devadatta acquiring a palace supplies neither of the admitted adequacy frames. Under 17 the projected excavation parikhā selects ḍhañ and, with the feminine ground, ṅīp by 4.1.15: pārikheyī bhūmiḥ. Both adequacy analyses reach the one cited predicate. The adjective in the old PG747 syllable example remains Open: Śatapatha 1.5.2.16–18 independently gives the formula series, uses vauṣaṭ in §16, and identifies the seventeen syllables with Prajāpati, without the claimed chandasyaḥ wording.
