# PG113 — Owned causal constituents and dependent r/l

Monty · 10 November 2026

## H06. Root portion and present-system maps

Use the pinned PG96 four present-system P/A maps after a single ṇic.
Root portions before ṇic-i are gār, āś, bhoj, cal, cop, kamp, ād.
Gṝ has final-vowel vṛddhi/r accompaniment; aś/ad have a-upadhā
vṛddhi; bhuj/cup have light-upadhā guṇa. Cal preserves mit-short a
under 6.4.92. Kamp's penultimate sound is m, so no a-upadhā
vṛddhi is inferred from its first vowel. The num history remains.

Ṇic-i becomes e/ay before śap. The resulting themes are nigāray,
āśay, bhojay, calay, copay, kampay, āday. All non-ad profiles
are P. Ad's A/P is selected by H04, before any ending is attached.
In laṅ, vowel-initial aś/ad receive āṭ 6.4.72; their ā is not a
prefix ā. Ni places the ordinary aṭ after itself, giving nya-gāray.
The remaining consonant-initial roots have ordinary aṭ.

## H07. Gṝ r/l is one dependent phonological fibre

8.2.21 permits l for this ingestion root's r before a vowel-initial
root affix; here that affix is ṇic. Kāśikā explicitly preserves
this choice through ṇic-lopa in nigāryate/nigālyate, either by the
specified sthānivat account or by the earlier internal ordering.
Adopt the same limited retained-ṇic witness for P blessing and caṅ.
These are r/l readings at one request, not two lexical positions.

Thus nigārayati/nigālayati, nigāryāt/nigālyāt and the other
continuations retain the same eating frame. No irrigation/praise
homonym is added. P loṭ 1sg is nigārayāṇi on the r branch and
nigālayāni on the l branch: late ṇatva reads the surviving r/l
choice, not a forgotten r. Other ending n occurrences keep the
pinned thematic construction. There is no independent ṇ/n toggle.

## H08. Outer futures and blessing do not inherit bare aniṭ

The derived dhātu receives ordinary outer iṭ before sya/tās,
retaining ṇic as ay: C_root·ayiṣya / C_root·ayit. Reuse the
full finite P/A luṭ, lṛṭ and lṛṅ maps from PG94/96. The common
third-person tā/tārau/tāraḥ outputs preserve original pada/tiṅ.
Conditional repeats H06's prefix-relative aṭ or vowel-initial āṭ.

P blessing has yāsuṭ with no outer iṭ; 6.4.51 lops ṇic,
retaining C_root and its history: nigāryāt/nigālyāt, āśyāt,
bhojyāt, calyāt, copyāt, kampyāt, ādyāt. A ad has ordinary
iṭ and retained ay: ādayiṣīṣṭa. Only its 2pl has the two
8.3.79 readouts ādayiṣīdhvam/ādayiṣīḍhvam. The A kartari
label does not license 6.4.62 ciṇvat. Root ad/bhuj aniṭ does
not remove derived-dhātu iṭ, and ad's basic gh as replacement is
not imported into this causal boundary.

## H09. Perfect auxiliaries and caṅ copies

For liṭ attach ām to retained ṇic-ay and use PG95's exact kṛ,
bhū and as tuples. Only kṛ inherits primary pada via 1.3.63.
Ad-A therefore has kṛ-A, bhū-P and as-P; the seven P profiles
have all P auxiliaries. Retain ṃ/ñ before kṛ, ṃ/m before bhū,
and m before as. Each root option yields 45 A or 47 P readings;
gṝ doubles its P tuple through the owned r/l fibre.

| Input | Before caṅ, before augment | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| gṝ | jīgar / jīgal | ā shortens; g copy becomes j; sanvat and light-copy length; dependent late l. |
| aś | aśiś | Vowel-initial second-part copying after shortening; later āṭ gives āśiś-. |
| bhuj | būbhuj | Root o→u; copied bh→b, u retained and lengthened. |
| cal | cīcal | Mit-short a, sanvat i, light-copy length. |

| kamp | cakamp | Root syllable heavy through mp; no sanvat i or copy length. |
| ad | adid | Second-part di copy from lopped ṇic's retained witness; āṭ gives ādid-. |

7.4.80 requires the specified following consonant with a-vowel;
it does not independently turn bhuj/cup's copied u into i. Gṝ is
originally final ṝ, not the ṛ-upadhā input of 7.4.7. Do not add
an independent ṛ-root branch. Ṇic-lopa before caṅ is common to
all rows; attach PG95's full thematic secondary P/A tuple. Caṅ
intervenes, so no direct-abhyasta third-plural shortcut is admitted.

Ni+aṭ gives nya-, hence nyajīgarat/nyajīgalat. Aś/ad have
āśiśat, ādidat/ādidata; the other roots begin abūbhuj-, acīcal-,
acūcup-, acakamp-. Vowel-initial copy i and outer iṭ are distinct:
caṅ has no outer iṭ. These are written rule inferences, not a claim
that each complete table occurs verbatim in the source edition.
