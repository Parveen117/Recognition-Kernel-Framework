# PG754 · The amount given and the person who receives it

In 5.1.47 the amount, expressed by a nominative support, is what is given. The derivative predicates a locus. Kāśikā assigns five possible roles to the payment: the increment over a loan principal, a ruler's income from a village, the surplus over the acquisition cost of merchandise, the ruler's toll associated with protection, and an inducement. The commentary also admits an individual recipient with a dative interpretation and gives another account in which the same individual is understood as a locus. Manu 7.85 independently supplies the specific locative human-recipient phrase cited by the grammarian. It supplies no particular interest rate. In Manu 8.141–142 the object of the taking is specified with unit śatam, a hundred, and the vṛddhi passage explicitly gives a month. Dvikam, trikam, catuṣkam and pañcakam are neuter accusatives in this instance. Nothing in the bare kan suffix by itself supplies the denominator or the period. The same case in 8.141 and 142 is counted once.

At 48 take an ordinal, or ardha in its established half-rūpaka usage, with ṭhan. Dvitīya and tṛtīya retain their ordinal inner history. Pañcama with ṭhan gives pañcamika, different from pañcan with kan. Compare PG752's half-payment article where ṭiṭhan is licensed by a different provision. At 49 bhāga in its monetary sense branches between yat and the retained ṭhan. In the grammatical examples agreement with śatam or viṃśatiḥ selects N NOMsg or F NOMsg. Bhāgya here carries no general assertion of fortune.

## Price is a different stem from clothing

At 50 distinguish moving or taking away (including stealing in one gloss), carrying aloft, and bringing about. The named lexical class includes bamboo, kuṭaja and balvaja. In one transmitted analysis the base must have bhāra after that member; in the other the named thing must itself be a load. Vāṃśabhārika and vāṃśika follow separately. Unloaded bamboo and a load of unlisted rice do not license unrestricted substitution. The alternatives are positively taught and are not damage to the text. At 51 the contracted stem vasna takes ṭhan and dravya takes kan. The earlier material noun vasana ‘garment’ at PG752 was a different base with aṇ. Uṇādi 3.6 supplies an already written vas + na lexeme in the price/wage sense; its prior source credit remains in its packet. Ṛgveda 4.24.9 independently supplies Pada vasnam, in the discussion of an exchange. The Vedic Index distinguishes value or ware in this passage. In 4.24.10 a buyer is invited to purchase Indra with ten cows: both the number and the noun are instrumental plural. This is evidence for the price role with a numeric phrase, a different argument from a bare plural unit. Neither hymn token supplies the derivative vasnika; no Vedic case is added for either control.

## A vessel, a cook and a four-way compound

At 52 the accusative names the quantity which something can contain, someone gathers or measures, or someone cooks. Bāla explains transitivity in the contain reading and construes gathering as bringing even a slightly short quantity up to its measure. A woman may cook a prastha without being a corresponding vessel. Only the cooking branch admits the extra droṇa → aṇ, beside ṭhañ. Bāla explicitly derives both feminine words with ṅīp; the same commentator on 45 explicitly gives ṭhak to the separate field derivative. Thus a single droṇa gloss and an -ika ending are insufficient to infer a single governing affix.

Single āḍhaka, ācita and pātra have kha or ṭhañ at 53. At 54 a causal dvigu gives positive ṣṭhan with ṅīṣ and positive kha with the feminine ā ending. The third branch has ordinary ṭhañ with luk, then the feminine of the compound. Dvyāḍhakī and dvipātrī have the dvigu feminine, whereas dvyācitā has ṭāp through the named ācita exception at 4.1.22. In pātrīṇā the pronounced retroflex ṇ belongs to the inner īna body after r. At 55 both retained and elided ordinary ṭhañ are admitted for the qualifying kulija compound, beside ṣṭhan and kha. The retained ṭhañ yields dvaikulijikī. Nyāsa expressly places kulija in the exception to measure-final second-member vṛddhi. The result therefore has the strengthened first member dvai-, whereas the other three retain dvi-. These are four readings on a single feminine case position.
