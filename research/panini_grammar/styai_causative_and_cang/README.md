# PG208 · Causative aorists recover the two styai teaching forms

Monty · 12 November 2026

The causatives of the same two roots agree in **162 of 180** matched *pada*, *lakāra* and agreement requests. Every difference is in **luṅ**, where the causative takes *caṅ* and reduplicates. Puruṣakāra on *Daiva* 7 gives both third-singular aorists in running text. At pause they read **atistyapat** and **atiṣṭyapat**.

| Third singular | Dental teaching form | Retroflex teaching form |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Causative present P | styāpayati | styāpayati |
| Causative present A | styāpayate | styāpayate |
| Causative aorist P | atistyapat | atiṣṭyapat |
| Causative aorist A | atistyapata | atiṣṭyapata |

The inserted *p* has an owner. Before ṇic, 6.1.45 gives *ā* and 7.3.36 attaches *puk* to the preceding stem. This gives *styāp* and the ordinary causative *styāpayati*. In the aorist, after ṇi-lopa, *ā* is penultimate before the surviving *p* and 7.4.1 shortens it. The reduplicative syllable is *ti*. Its *i* precedes the root-initial consonant cluster, so it is metrically heavy and stays short under 7.4.94. Its presence now supplies the environment for the substituted radical *s* of the second root to become *ṣ*. These conditions yield *a-ti-sty-ap-at* and *a-ti-ṣṭy-ap-at* as surface segmentations; the internal *a* belongs to the shortened root and *p* to the earlier augmentation.

The middle requests represent a causer to whom the result accrues under 1.3.74. In the corresponding present the long vowel is visible in *styāpayate*. In the active blessing *styāpyāt* the *p* survives ṇic-lopa. In the middle blessing the ordinary iṭ route gives *styāpayiṣīṣṭa*. These examples distinguish the root-side augment from the causal affix. The perfect identifies each auxiliary separately, with its own pada.
