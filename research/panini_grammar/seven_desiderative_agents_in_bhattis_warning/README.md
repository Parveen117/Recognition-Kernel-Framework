# PG869 · Seven desiderative agents in Bhaṭṭi’s warning

### The seven agents in context

Bhaṭṭi places the six roots named in 1.2.8 across two successive stanzas. In the next stanza he uses a seventh desiderative which obtains kit under 1.2.10. These are three actual constructions of the sentence: an appeal to questioners who must stay awake, an argument that fortune shuns certain men and an exhortation to those seeking news of Sītā. The corresponding stanzas are VII.98–100 in the 1887 Nirṇayasāgara edition (p. 164), and VII.100–102 in Trivedi with Mallinātha (pp. 251–252).

The 1.2.8 list is explicit about rud, vid, muṣ, grah, svap and pracch. Its Kāśikā examples include both intervening iṭ and direct s. The necessary lexical and morphological tests therefore precede the kit decision. Kit can coexist with iṭ in this named group. In contrast, 1.2.10 asks whether **actual san is jhal-initial**. Substituting the other test would lose the treatment of pracch.

For the nominal extension use an actual san-derived root and agent u under 3.2.168, with the inherited 3.2.134 agent dispositions. Rule 6.4.48 removes the san-final a before the ārdhadhātuka kṛt affix u. The final u in these adjectives belongs to that affix. Each application preserves its inner desired act, participants and preverb. The same rule has separate lexical branches for āśaṃs in the desire sense and for bhikṣ. Those two branches do not require san. For the poem the commentators supply an *icchu* paraphrase, while 3.2.168 supplies the morphological analysis of the selected stems. The stanza alone establishes no permanent disposition in each person.
