# PG190 · The six words of Uṇādi 4.105 and two histories of saraṭa

Monty · 11 November 2026

Ujjvaladatta gives six words under 4.105. All six now have a complete written declension on their selected sense, **144 nominal positions and 153 readings**. A second **24 positions and 25 readings** preserve the lizard sense of *saraṭa* from a different Uṇādi rule. The wind and lizard have identical spellings in every one of those 24 positions.

| Word | Selected meaning | Inflection |
| --- | --- | --- |
| petva | nectar | neuter a-stem |
| kaccha | a vegetable root or bulb | masculine a-stem |
| saraṭa | wind | masculine a-stem |
| dhyātva | thought | neuter a-stem |
| hautna | the sacrificer | masculine a-stem |
| lūni | rice | feminine short-i stem |

The short-i stem has two forms in four singular cases on the selected feminine schedule. For example, the dative gives *lūnaye* or *lūnyai*, and the locative gives *lūnau* or *lūnyām*. Śabdakalpadruma also records an action noun for cutting with a ktin derivation. That separate sense is excluded from the six-word table.

### The short vowel in saraṭa

In the printed 1859 edition the lizard is explicitly placed under **4.81 aṭan** (p. 108). Under **4.105**, p. 112 gives the wind. The glossary on p. 260 prints two distinct entries. The preceding rule 4.104 enumerates the affixes separately and prints **aṭac** with a short initial vowel as its ninth member. Its ninth example is *bhṛ → bharaṭa* ‘potter’. The same regular grade and suffix give *sṛ → sar + aṭa → saraṭa*, so the wind has a specific candidate in that printed tradition. The appendix, p. 269 no. 5, groups the surface *saraṭa* under *aṭa* without specifying a sense or a technical marker.

The machine edition’s ninth field at 4.104 and 4.105 explicitly spells **āṭac** with long ā. Applying that spelling with the same ordinary operations gives *sarāṭa*. The documents inspected do not prescribe a shortening for this instance. Thus the candidate belongs to the short-a witness. The primary 103-field count stays at 94 direct, four inferred and five unresolved. For the ninth field there is now a specific editorial difference and a positive explanation of one reading.

The 24 *kaccha* forms also agree with a separate paradigm entered under that spelling. Its lexical entry lists other senses. The Uṇādi gloss supplies the vegetable meaning used here.
