# PG908 · The Vedic singular obliques of *viśva*

Four Śākala passages use nominal-type oblique endings where the PG904 ordinary pronoun table selects smai, smāt or smin. Grassmann (entry 8151) indexes the singular dative, ablative and **two** singular locatives. Macdonell's *Vedic Grammar for Students* §120b, p. 115 n. 1, lists the same three spellings but says each occurs once. Both locative passages have a segmented *viśve* and singular agreement. Whatever counting convention underlies the footnote, its frequency cannot serve as a limit on this register. ## Ending history and the register boundary

Begin with free *viśva* ‘all, whole’ and its original ṅe, ṅasi or ṅi request. In the ordinary pronoun policy, 7.1.14–15 give the corresponding pronominal substitutions. For the four Vedic loci the nominal ending realization is independently witnessed. On that branch ṅe gives ya (7.1.13) with length before y (7.3.102), yielding *viśvāya*. The ṅasi substitution āt (7.1.12) with a + ā gives *viśvāt*. For ṅi, a + i gives e (6.1.87), hence *viśve*. The conditioned external junctions then act on the completed words. These traces identify the ending mechanics. The exact historical account authorizing the distribution of the nominal versus pronominal endings remains Open. The witness licenses the branch only at its attested locus. Lexical root, preverb, voice and verbal affix are absent from *viśva*; *dṛśe* retains its separate older infinitive owner.
