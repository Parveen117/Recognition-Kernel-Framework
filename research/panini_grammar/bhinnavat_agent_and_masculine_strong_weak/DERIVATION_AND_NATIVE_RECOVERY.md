## k and u disappear from readout but still govern later operations

teaching u-it (1.3.2) undergo 1.3.9 marker lopa, retaining kit and ugit
witnesses. The surviving suffix is tavat. As at the exact matching PG349

    bhid_root | ktavatu_affix

## Strong case requests and nominative-only long ā

Use PG216's pinned ordinary masculine ktavatu case interface, carrying its
actual marker/strength predicates onto the newly admitted stem. 1.1.43
assigns sarvanāmasthāna to the first five endings in this masculine domain:
nom su/au/jas and acc am/au. The ugit witness licenses 7.1.70 num; 1.1.47
places its surviving n after the last vowel, before suffix-final t. Hence
bhinnavant- occurs in nom dual/plural and acc singular/dual, giving the
written bhinnavantau, bhinnavantaḥ and bhinnavantam. This num n is neither
root n nor the niṣṭhā n.

At nominative singular, 6.4.14 lengthens the at(u) stem's upadhā before su,
excluding sambuddhi. The Kāśikā specifically requires this lengthening
**before num**, since premature num would change the relevant upadhā query.
Retain that source-qualified order. After num and ending lopa, final nt
undergoes 8.2.23 cluster-final t lopa: bhinnavānt → bhinnavān. This is
not a global long-vowel strong stem. Ordinary strong dual/plural retain a.

Vocative singular is technical sambuddhi (2.3.49); it has the su strength
history and num, but not 6.4.14 lengthening. After final-t cluster lopa,
8.2.8 preserves final n against 8.2.7 in this vocative context, yielding
short-a bhinnavan. Do not copy bhinnavān into this cell. Vocative
plural/dual follow their own ordinary endings; singular-only sambuddhi must
not be generalized to every vocative request.
