# PG922 · Futures, perfect, aorist, blessing and conditional of √rudh

The imperfect *aruṇad* belongs to the class-7 nasal present system. The same unprefixed root ‘obstruct, enclose’ has *rotsya-* in the future, *rurudh-/rurodh-* in the perfect and two formations in the parasmaipada aorist. Mādhava presents these under a single root entry and explicitly restricts the optional aṅ pathway by the inherited rule context. The accompanying [word table](FINITE_FORMS.tsv) records all nine person–number requests for each category and pada, in Devanāgarī as published. A table row has one root, one sense, one voice, one pada and one category. Its slash-separated words are distinct accepted readings at that key. Use PG7's ID 1070001, gana 7, *रुधिँर् आवरणे*. As in PG921, kartari A requires the actor-principal-result premise under 1.3.72. P admits the residual actor request under 1.3.78. An unspecified result leaves the semantic choice open. The aorist example *aruddha* in the middle grid therefore has an actor reading; the independently reported patient *arodhī* has a different construction. The same suffix shape or a shared third-person luṭ word does not merge the two requests. No preverb, san, ṇic, yak or present-system śnam participates in the six new constructions.

**Two futures and a conditional.** O-grade *rodh* meets aniṭ sya directly. Devoicing before the sibilant gives *rotsya-*. The thematic vowel and ordinary active or middle endings give *rotsyati* and *rotsyate*. The conditional takes this future base and initial aṭ; P 3sg has the pause alternatives *arotsyat/arotsyad*. The middle third dual *arotsyetām* retains thematic adjustment; its luṭ counterpart *roddhārau* has another source. At luṭ the dental of *tās* after dh and the treatment of the preceding stop give *roddh-*. The third-person replacements ḍā/rau/ras apply in both padas. At 3sg ḍit deletes suffix-owned ṭi; ending ā supplies the final vowel of *roddhā*. At dual/plural the suffix s disappears before r; in P *roddhāsi* suffix s disappears before ending s. In A *roddhāhe* suffix s becomes h before e. The contexts for these operations and the different ā owners are pinned in the two earlier luṭ derivations.

**Perfect.** Reduplicate the root as *ru-rudh*. The strong P singular takes guṇa of root u in *rurodha* and *rurodhitha*. The remaining P cells and the A perfect retain the root vowel in *rurudh-*. The copy vowel and the original root vowel remain separate. Mādhava explicitly invokes the krādi restriction governing the perfect’s iṭ distribution (7.2.13) with *rurodhitha, rurudhiva*. A 3pl *rurudhire* has ending-owned i in ire. A 2pl *rurudhidhve* has augment i before dhve. The consonantal stem boundary preserves the dental ending under the same visibility distinction used in PG343. P and A 1sg/3sg coincide within their own paradigms, with different persons.

**Two P aorist paths.** Here the lexical marker ir licenses 3.1.57, under the parasmaipada condition inherited from 3.1.55. Substituting aṅ for cli gives the short-u series *arudhat/arudhad, arudhatām, arudhan*. In the competing sic branch 7.2.1 strengthens the root vowel to au, giving *arautsīt/arautsīd* in 3sg. The 3du *arauddhām* and 1sg *arautsam* show different treatment of the s at a following consonant and vowel. Both source-supported series extend over all nine P cells. Two branch readings at each of nine positions and two additional pause spellings at 3sg account for twenty readings. The middle is outside the inherited P guard of 3.1.57. Mādhava cites 1.2.11: sic is kit in this middle environment, preventing the o/au grade. The written series starts *aruddha, arutsātām, arutsata*. Only an actor-result request admits this middle reading here. Causative aorists and the patient ciṇ form require their own markers.

**Blessing.** P takes the kit blessing path, short u and yāsuṭ; *rudhyāt/rudhyād* retains dh before y and the two terminal stop readings. The lack of śnam distinguishes this word from ordinary optative *rundhyāt*. For A the kit condition of 1.2.11 likewise retains u. The root directly meets the initial s of the marker sequence, giving *rutsīṣṭa, rutsīyāstām*. Keep the initial and later s in different event histories. Neither the presence of y nor the blessing sense turns an earlier blessing-loṭ row into an āśīrliṅ credit.
