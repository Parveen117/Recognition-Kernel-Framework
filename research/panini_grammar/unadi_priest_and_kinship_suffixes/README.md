# PG193 · The last of ten kinship and priest stems and the open tṛn field

Monty · 11 November 2026

Two printed commentaries supply individual suffixes missing from the earlier lexical register.

**Uṇādi 2.94, *bahulam anyatrāpi*.** In Nārāyaṇa’s numbering this is 2.93. On printed p. 50 he assigns tṛc to *udgātā* and *unnetā*, then tṛn to *pratihartā, pratiprasthātā, praśāstā* and *prastotā*. Both affixes in the primary rule now have a named lexical witness. The earlier *mantā* ‘learned person’ retains its tṛc evidence. Perusūri also gives *mantā* and *hantā* under the extension, without specifying which suffix produces the thief. That narrower lexical assignment stays open.

The difference at *pratihartā* matters for a compiler. The Kāśikā on 3.2.135 restricts the priest-name supplement there to unprefixed inputs and explicitly gives tṛc for this prefixed name. Nārāyaṇa explicitly gives it tṛn on the Uṇādi path. Both records retain the same unaccented nominative with their own source, rule and marker.

**Uṇādi 2.95, *jāmātṛ*.** Śvetavanavāsin’s 2.96, printed p. 89, explicitly names tṛc after the jā-initial construction with māṅ and glosses *jāmātā* as the daughter’s husband. Across the sources used by the project, **all ten named ṛ-stems now have at least one individual affix assignment**. The 24 case–number slots for each had already been supplied by PG188. The older alternate derivations for *bhrātṛ* and *hotṛ* remain visible.

Across the 103 multiple-affix fields this moves **one** field from open to direct, giving **95 direct, four inferred and four open**. All four open fields are at 4.105. The jāmātṛ decision completes the ten-word sublist inside an already supported field.

Zero new finite positions. Cumulative **41,333**.
