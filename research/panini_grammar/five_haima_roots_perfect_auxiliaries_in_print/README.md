# PG831 · Five Haima roots and their perfect auxiliaries in print

The second reprint (2006) of Lāvaṇyavijayasūri's *Dhāturatnākara*, volume 3, sets five consecutive desire-root entries in two columns per page. On printed pp. 230–233 the numbered tense row 6 gives **twelve series of three auxiliary spellings** across roots 955–959. [The collation](PRINT_ROWS.tsv) records the heading's root number, normalized primary, ending series and order of the three printed auxiliaries. L and R mean columns on the same printed page. The order matters when the first expression receives the abbreviated conjugation and the following two appear as whole headwords.

### How to read the columns

- Follow vṛt 955 (turning) from p. 230 R into p. 231 L. Its expanded desiderative has middle endings and *vivartiṣāñcakre*. The short series gives active *vivṛtsāñcakāra*. The same short primary occurs later under vṛdh 957 (growth). Preserve both lexical owners.
- On p. 231 L, syand 956 (flowing) has an expanded middle series and begins a short middle series at the foot of the column. The latter continues at the **top right**, with *sisyantsāñcakre*, then *sisyantsāmbabhūva* and *sisyantsāmāsa*. The **lower right** starts a third series with active *sisyantsati* and perfect *sisyantsāñcakāra*. Reading across the horizontal line combines different paradigms.
- Entry 958 śṛdh (śabdakutsā) begins at the foot of p. 232 L. Its expanded series continues at the top right, where row 6 has *śiśardhiṣāmāsa*, *śiśardhiṣāñcakre* and *śiśardhiṣāmbabhūva*. The next series gives short *śiśṛtsati* and *śiśṛtsāñcakāra*. The printed **आत्मनेपद** heading above that second block conflicts with its endings. The corresponding label also appears over vṛdh’s active short series on the left. Preserve the labels as printed, classify the actual finite endings, and leave the reason for the mismatch open.
- Entry 959 kṛp (capacity) begins at the foot of p. 232 R. On p. 233 there are expanded middle, short middle and short active rows. The book uses the written लृ representation in the short root. The register normalizes that vocalic liquid as ḷ, in agreement with PG786. The short middle begins in the left column. A second आत्मनेपद label on p. 233 L stands above active cikḷpsati. Its perfect continues at the top of the right column with the kṛ auxiliary cakāra. Entry 960 begins below it.

### Derivational consequence

All twelve entries here have san, no preverb and an agent reading. The iṭ choice belongs to the root-to-san primary. The perfect uses ām and a separately inflected auxiliary. In each A series the kṛ member shows *cakre*, while the alternatives with as and bhū show *āsa* and *babhūva*. In the P series kṛ shows *cakāra*. At the junction with c the printed nasal is ñ, before b it is m, and with *āsa* the resulting sequence ends in *-āmāsa*. Keeping the primary and the auxiliary as separate fields explains why *sisyantsāmāsa* can occur in two rows without being a new surface alternative.

There are seven A rows (one each for vṛt, vṛdh and śṛdh and two each for syand and kṛp) and five P rows. This establishes the three auxiliary choices even where PG830 had relied on one word in a transcript. The compared form is the printed headword of the perfect series. Elliptically printed companion endings have no whole-word transcription credit here. Historical derivation of the misleading headings is Open. For Pāṇini, PG189’s same-word reading and the competing projected-entitlement reading remain separately indexed. An edition using Haima san and auxiliary rules cannot decide what Pāṇini’s 7.2.59 licenses after 2.4.81.
