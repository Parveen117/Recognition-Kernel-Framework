## Mandatory kha has specified compound inputs

5.4.7 is required after the qualified inputs, despite inherited optionality
elsewhere. The explanatory vigraha presents a derivative's source; it does not
prove a freely usable unsuffixed word is available in every ordinary utterance.

## Direction AND feminine is a joint restriction

5.4.8 is optional and tests the current denotation/gender of the owned input,
not an untyped union of all direction and all feminine words. Supplied prāc,
pratyac, avāc, arvāc, udīc with the source añc/lupta-n history give prācīna,
pratīcīna, avācīna, arvācīna, udīcīna. Where present, 6.4.138 deletes añc's
internal a on the bha query and 6.3.138 lengthens the preceding member before
the resulting c; īn+a comes from actual kha. These changes are distinct from
6.4.148 final-vowel lopa. We do not derive a universal añc/kviP recognizer.

The optional non-kha branch retains the already supplied prāk/pratyak etc.
It adds no second word credits. A feminine direction prācī dik/udīcī dik is
excluded on this local request. A feminine person prācīnā brāhmaṇī is admitted;
new ṭāp gives prācīnā/arvācīnā from the selected derivative's final a.
The lopped astāti directional avyaya under 5.3.30 is not feminine merely
because its earlier direction source was feminine. Its nonfeminine status
allows kha; this is the source's counterexample to an overbroad direction ban.

Keep Bāla's two accounts separate. Under padāsaṃskāra the kha word denotes
a contextual thing in general and is naturally neuter singular. The reported
vākyasaṃskāra account supplies the contextual referent, retaining its gender
in prācīnā vāṭī/prācīna vana and similar expressions. We preserve both named
interfaces and the shared permission; no single global agreement policy is
inferred. The F-person exception is not used to rewrite a feminine direction.

## Jāti's bearer and an equivalent station are different semantic inputs

Rule9 cha is required on the qualified jāti-final **bearer**: brāhmaṇajātīya,
kṣatriyajātīya, vaiśyajātīya. Bandhu means a substrate through which the
source's class manifests, not a relative/kinsman. Tattva describes the bearer
input as bahuvrīhi; abstract brāhmaṇajātiḥ śobhanā has a different compound
and reference. Do not apply this suffix to an abstract jāti just because its
spelling agrees. This is a traditional grammatical meaning contract, not a
new social/biological classification of people.

Rule10 cha is optional after a source same-station bahuvrīhi: pitṛsthānīya /
pitṛsthāna, mātṛsthānīya / mātṛsthāna, rājasthānīya / rājasthāna. The
station is status/respect/relation, as for a father or other reference; it is
not just a physical location. Terminal i (rule9) or a (rule10) is removed by
6.4.148 before initial ch → īy 7.1.2 plus the original affix a. Absence of
cha preserves the same qualified bahuvrīhi history; it does not turn it into
a literal lodging. All six station readouts are stem interfaces, not cases.

Nyāsa10 explicitly supplies the comparison/uttarapada-lopa account and iti's
vivakṣā constraint. Gosthāna/aśvasthāna in the literal lodging sense is not
admitted; neither is arbitrary tulyasthāna made by string matching. The
same-station comparison and licensed compound must be provided. Rule9's
mandatory force between optional8/10 is local source evidence, not permission
to flatten all optionality into one Boolean.
