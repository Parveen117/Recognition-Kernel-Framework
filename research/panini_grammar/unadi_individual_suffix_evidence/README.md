For 2.95 the relation treats all ten named lexical stems individually. **Nine** now have an identified affix in at least one of the selected sources. *Bhrātṛ* has competing named derivations, with tṛn in Śabdakalpadruma and tṛc in Vācaspatyam. At *hotṛ* Śabdakalpadruma distinguishes the tṛn nipātana from a second tṛc derivation. *Jāmātṛ* has a secure stem, sense and nipātana, while the individual marker on that path remains open. The accented readings and case government of *hantṛ* in Monier-Williams do not by themselves assign the thief example of 2.94.

The frozen primary 4.98 has *abdādayaś ca* in its heading and **शब्दः** in the gloss. Printed p. 111 and Kośa 4.98 place *abda* ‘year’ here. The Kośa places *śabda* ‘sound’ under the preceding śap rule. The primary gloss is preserved verbatim and the lexical reading for this comparison is *abda*. Under 4.105 the wind and lizard readings of *saraṭa* also need separate treatment. A suffix given for the lizard would not identify the wind derivation.

Printed pp. 59–60 and 111–112 supply the cross-edition alignment. In Aufrecht, the primary edition’s 2.94 is printed 2.95 and its 2.95 is printed 2.96. The second pāda of the Kośa includes the extension’s examples in its 2.94. The digital dictionary entries retain their individual Cologne IDs, text hashes and short lookup spans.
