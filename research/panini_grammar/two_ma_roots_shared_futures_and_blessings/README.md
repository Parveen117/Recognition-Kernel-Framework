# PG248 · Where the two mā histories converge in future and blessing

Monty · 12 November 2026

The two verbs that give *mimīte* and *mayate* in the present have the same middle futures. The Mādhavīya gives **mātā, māsyate** for *māṅ*, and **mātāse, māsyate** for *meṅ*. In *meṅ* the non-śit environment licenses *ā* under 6.1.45. In *māṅ* the class-three reduplication belongs to the present system. The prefixed future therefore leaves the two root histories indistinguishable in its letters.

| Both root histories | *pra + ni* | *pari + ni* |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Periphrastic future 3sg | praṇimātā | pariṇimātā |
| Periphrastic future 2sg | praṇimātāse | pariṇimātāse |
| Simple future 3sg | praṇimāsyate | pariṇimāsyate |
| Conditional 3sg | praṇyamāsyata | pariṇyamāsyata |
| Middle benedictive 3sg | praṇimāsīṣṭa | pariṇimāsīṣṭa |

Kṣīrasvāmin explicitly gives *māsīṣṭa* and explains why the special change to *ī* does not occur there. The initial *ī* of the benedictive ending and the long *ā* of the root occupy different places. The conditional has the same prefix–augment boundary as the imperfect. Its use also requires an appropriate conditional context.

The four schedules add **144 positions**. At each of the 72 same-prefix, same-lakāra and same-person comparisons, the two roots now give exactly the same set of readings. Across PG247–248 the declared grid stands at **288 of 360**.
