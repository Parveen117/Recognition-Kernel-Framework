# PG853 · The nasal in two printed versions of the tooth-offering example

PG641 established the case of the cited offering under 5.4.142, but its primary Kāśikā text has patrada**t**am. That source lacks a written n before t. Two printed presentations of the same grammatical example supply a different reading.

**Derivation and limit of the new evidence.** The rule specifies the samāsānta datṛ substitution for danta in the Vedic bahuvrīhi. Preserve the replacement's ṛ marker after its disappearance. In the masculine accusative singular the original am is a sarvanāmasthāna ending under 1.1.43; the nonroot ugit stem receives nuṃ by 7.1.70. In the dat stem the augment after the last vowel gives dant, and with am one reads patradantam. Thus retention of the letters dant in the printed word is compatible with this replacement history. Merely seeing those letters would also be compatible with an ordinary danta stem. The position of the example under an explicit replacement rule and Vasu's preceding account of the marker motivate the former reading here. The same construction in neuter accusative singular has no sarvanāmasthāna trigger and can give patradatam. The sources name the governed object in the singular but supply no independently identified animal or explicit token gender. Accordingly the nasal-bearing PG641 grammatical branch now has textual support. The particular omission needed for a *masculine* interpretation of the digital no-n word remains Open. Its neighbouring ubhayādataḥ, the absence reported elsewhere by Bhaṭṭabhāskara, or the mere possibility of neuter cannot decide it. No new general option to insert or omit n.
