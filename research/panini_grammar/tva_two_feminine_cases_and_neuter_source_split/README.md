# PG907 · Two feminine cases of *tva* and two readings of neuter *tvat*

In RV 5.61.6 the nominative *tvā* accompanies *strī*, the subject of *bhavati*. The commentary glosses it with feminine *ekā* and explicitly discusses the low accent. In RV 10.54.1 the dative *tvasyai* qualifies *prajāyai*, the beneficiary of Indra's gift. The commentary gives feminine *ekasyai*. Earlier in the **same** stanza *tvā* is the object of the call addressed to Indra. That earlier word is second-person accusative. Concord and verbal government discriminate these inputs even after removing accent marks. ## Two written derivations

Select the vowel-final indefinite pronominal stem and a feminine referent. The āp-bearing stem is *tvā* (ṭāp 4.1.4 and 6.1.101). For NOM sg, su loses its marker, then 6.1.68 deletes the single consonant after long āp. The surviving ā belongs to the stem. For DAT sg, the ṅit ending ṅe licenses 7.3.114. It shortens stem ā to a and supplies syāṭ on the ending. The ṭit augment stands initially under 1.1.46. Ending-internal syā + e yields syai by 6.1.88, giving tva | syai. The pronoun-specific selection takes precedence over ordinary āp-yāṭ at 7.3.113. Root, preverb, voice and verbal affixes are absent. Both interpretations follow the previously written āp/syāṭ derivation with a newly witnessed recipient. ## Which stem owns the neuter?

Grassmann's entry 3982 assigns both feminine forms to indefinite *tva*. His consecutive hymn numbers 415 and 880 correspond to RV 5.61 and 10.54. In the same entry he treats repeated neuter *tvad* in hymn 617 (RV 7.101.3) as alternating circumstances, and the single expression in hymn 898 (10.72.9) as applying to the second member. Macdonell §396 lists N NOM sg *tvad* under vowel-final *tva*. In his later grammar he distinguishes the adverbial use ‘partly’ in Brāhmaṇa prose. The lexical listing is positive evidence for that author's assignment. [Grassmann, p. 561, entry 3982](https://github.com/sanskrit-lexicon/csl-orig/blob/cf53a4240ac83c8f7dfd275533ebd0a0639e6b57/v02/gra/gra.txt#L25727-L25739) · [Macdonell, Vedic Grammar §396](https://en.wikisource.org/w/index.php?oldid=13313972) · [Vedic Grammar for Students, p. 110](https://gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de/gretil_elib/Mac916__Macdonell_VedicGrammarForStundents.pdf)

Sāyaṇa on RV 7.101.3 explicitly calls the pronoun t-final, unaccented and synonymous with *anya*. He supplies two different forms of Parjanya, comparing barrenness and giving birth. The pada text gives *tvat* twice. The continuous text has *tvad* before voiced bh in *bhavati*. Word-final voicing therefore cannot settle the lexical stem. On Sāyaṇa's nominal interpretation the two expressions support PG902's existing t-final N NOM sg with the understood *rūpam*. Grassmann's adverbial interpretation has different syntax. In RV 10.72.9 the pada text places *tvat* after *mṛtyave* in the clause also containing *prajāyai*. It gives no second free nominative request. Preserve the three authored assignments and keep the choice of a single historical derivation Open. An independent ordinary Pāṇinian ending deriving neuter d for vowel-final *tva* still needs evidence. No neuter position enters the census here.
