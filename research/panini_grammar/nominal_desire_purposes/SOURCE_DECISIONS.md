# Source decisions

## H1. Three sense gates in order

7.4.34 pairs aśana with bubhukṣā, udaka with pipāsā and dhana with gardha. Kāśikā and Kaumudī explicitly give the special and ordinary laṭ 3sg of all three. These are conditioned choices inside the kyac self-wish domain. An otherwise unspecified purpose leaves the analysis underspecified.

## H2. Food now or for later use

Tattvabodhinī contrasts wishing to eat immediately with wishing to have food for later use. Bālamanoramā also illustrates collection. Encode immediate eating as a witness of hunger, and stockpiling for later use as an explicit witness for the ordinary frame. Do not turn these examples into a rule that a hungry person can never store food.

## H3. Water for drinking or for a different purpose

The specialized pipāsā frame means thirst; the source gives wanting to drink. Tattvabodhinī gives bathing as its contrasting example and Bālamanoramā gives irrigation. The literal object udaka is shared. A missing purpose differs from a supplied counterexample to thirst.

## H4. Wealth and intensity of desire

Read gardha as coveting, with the example of someone already possessing wealth and wanting more. The contrasting example is a person in poverty desiring the means of subsistence. These source scenarios instantiate grammatical senses; there is no inference that a particular person is greedy from a statement of ownership alone.

## H5. Three special stem histories

For aśana and dhana the selected special branches have ā before y, while the ordinary branches have ī under 7.4.33. In udanya Bālamanoramā explicitly includes the substitution udan on udaka and the exceptional absence of n-lopa. Preserve both the replacement provenance and final n. Kaumudī gives the whole kyac-final shapes. The Kāśikā electronic field prints vadaka in the explanatory phrase; its udanyati/udakīyati opposition and the independent commentary identify the source nominal as udaka.
