# PG758 · Three people-word profiles and a change of case in the derivational input

Rule 72 retains the accusative support and changes its relation. The student, sacrificer and ascetic carry out different things. The post in 73 is a locus of doubt. In 74 the accusative measures distance covered, whereas the second supplement describes the distance *from which* a person is worth approaching. That contrast changes the case of the source operand even where the derived masculine adjective is identical.

In 75 pathin supports an accusative relation of going. Carry ṣkan as the suffix. The established -ika surface does not display its two source markers. The final n carries the accent instruction; the ṣ history licenses feminine ṅīṣ by 4.1.41, giving pathikī. Compare the short-i vocative, the nominative ī, and the y before a vocalic ending. For 76 retain the pantha substitution before ṇ and initial vṛddhi. The qualifying condition is habitual going. In the Hitopadeśa, a single traveller appears as both pathikaḥ and pānthaḥ. The passage provides the words while the annotation of the derivational distinction comes from the grammarians. The verse in Meghadūta describes the travellers’ wives. Its first member need not carry a free ending. Mallinātha gives the explicit plural and a ṣkan derivation in his own prose. No female-traveller case is inferred from the wives.

Each of three separately addressed lexemes has eight written cases in three numbers. Equal dual forms in several cases have different case obligations; the two final-stop readings at each masculine ablative singular belong to one position. The attestation label identifies the edition that supplies a cell. All other cells are written derivations from the admitted stem and case contract.

Rule 77 resets the argument to an instrumental route for both a thing brought and a person going. Its northern route and six named extensions each have the separate readouts shown. The last supplement selects aṇ on the land route for madhuka and marica. The output is the same land-route adjective attached to either neuter commodity; it receives one case address. The general land route remains independently available for other moved goods. A claim that a particular historical trade actually took place would require an independent witness.
