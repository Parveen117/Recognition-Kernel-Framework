# PG162 · Seven consonant-host intensives lose ya before outer endings

Monty · 11 November 2026

The preceding yaṅ has an observable effect on the copy even after the consonant-following ya element disappears before an ārdhadhātuka continuation. Kāśikā 6.4.49 gives *bebhiditā* as a direct example. The Siddhāntakaumudī also gives the three contrasting named bases *sosūcitā*, *sosūtritā*, *momūtritā* and the kṛ auxiliary perfect of sosūc. This packet joins those witnesses to the four regular PG160 present formations in the same consonant-host class.

| Derivational contrast | Result |
| --- | --- |
| sosūc plus present | sosūcyate |
| sosūc plus finite tāsi | sosūcitā |
| sosūc plus ām and kṛ | sosūcāṃcakre / sosūcāñcakre |
| sosūc versus sosūtr in the 2pl aorist | asosūcidhvam versus asosūtridhvam / asosūtriḍhvam |

The archive's anusvāra convention in the perfect corresponds to the first written junction. The two alternative expositions of 6.4.49 describe deletion of ya as a group or deletion of initial y together with the final-a rule. They support the same words here. Keep yaṅ as the earlier grammatical affix; this operation is independent of whole-affix luk under 2.4.74. The suffix-specific 6.4.50 option for ky does not apply. The named curādi inputs have an earlier ṇi-lopa. Removing the later yaṅ syllable gives neither a new causative nor a new caṅ trigger.

The ordinary derived-dhātu iṭ and reduplicated word survive. In the 2pl blessing and aorist, 8.3.79 consults the sound before iṭ. The c of sosūc lies outside iṇ, while the r of sosūtr and momūtr and the l of jājval belong to it. This is why the two 2pl spelling schedules are independent of the apparent common suffix. Reuse middle sic, tāsi, sya and ām-auxiliary schedules at their licensed boundaries.

The zero reading maps the composite of the source-qualified intensive history and outer continuation to each complete word. The retained history distinguishes the primary reduplication from the auxiliary, and identifies the earlier ṇi in the three named exceptions. A bare y-less future cannot recover those distinctions without the lexical history.
