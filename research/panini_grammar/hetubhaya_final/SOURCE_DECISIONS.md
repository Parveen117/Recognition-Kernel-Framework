## H01. Exact admission and the two remaining contexts

The four profiles are unchanged: bhī and vi+smi, each with
causer-source A or positive instrument-source P. One current ṇic,
current controller c, experiencer/object q and the instrument k
where admitted are retained. Curādi smi remains excluded. The
mental-event source is not reclassified merely to fit a word.

Liṭ uses the inherited non-Mantra, non-today parokṣa certificate,
including the qualified first-person premise. Luṅ uses the earlier-
today completed event without māṅ. Unknown source/role premises
stay HOLD; false admission guards stay EXCLUDED. No new lexical,
semantic, prefix or nested-causation request is counted.

## H02. Ām and auxiliary pada keep separate owners

Apply 3.1.35 ām to the ṇyanta. Specific 6.4.55 preserves
ṇic as ay. Thus primary factors are bhāpayām/bhīṣayām,
vismāpayām, bhāyayām and vismāyayām. There is no val-iṭ,
initial aṭ or fresh primary copy. Primary liṭ-luk 2.4.81 and
1.1.63 retain their pinned scope.

Rule 3.1.40 introduces auxiliary kṛ/bhū/as and a new auxiliary
liṭ. Kṛ alone transfers the primary pada by 1.3.63. Bhū/as
remain P in these kartari frames. Their auxiliary iṭ, kṛ's
7.2.13 prohibition, P1sg ṇal option and both external nasal
readouts are PG97's exact contracts, not new root-owned operations.

## H03. Caṅ preparation preserves the earlier augment choice

Actual ṇic licenses cli→caṅ by 3.1.48 in both padas.
Pronounced caṅ a takes no val-iṭ; 6.4.51 removes ṇic.
The source's local order is ṇic-lopa, eligible upadhā shortening
7.4.1, then 6.1.11 doubling. PG98's already-qualified root
portions become bhap/bhiṣ, smap, bhay and smay.

Puk and ṣuk were root-final augments before shortening. Their
historical owners persist; shortening ī to i does not request a
second 7.3.40 decision or discard the existing ṣuk. Likewise
the retained root y of the P branch is not restored ṇic.
Current roles admit no karmavat/ciṇvat branch. No basic-root
perfect copy or different class vikaraṇa is substituted.

## H04. Long bī but short si: two weight queries

Kāśikā/Nyāsa 7.4.93 explicitly admit the comparable conjunct-
initial class through the acikṣaṇat argument. Smi's shortened
smap/smay has a light root a, so copy sa receives san-like i
under 7.4.79. However that copy i is followed by original sm,
a consonant cluster; it is guru under 1.4.11. Therefore 7.4.94
does not lengthen it. The copy stays **si**. Smi is not smṛ
in 7.4.95's separate named list.

Bhī's shortened bhap/bhiṣ/bhay instead has both a light root
syllable and a light copy syllable before the single bh consonant.
Copy i is supplied directly on bhiṣ or by san-like a→i on
bhap/bhay, then lengthened under 7.4.94. Rule 8.4.54 gives
copy bh→b while original root bh remains: **bī|bhap,
bī|bhiṣ, bī|bhay**. This is a labelled later readout of the
owned copy, not an unlicensed global return to earlier rules.

## H05. Root ṣ is not a desiderative-affix operation

The original ṣ of smi became s under root preprocessing. Inside
the caṅ copy/root constituent, that substituted root s now follows
copy i and receives ṣ by 8.3.59: **si|ṣmap / si|ṣmay**.
Only root s changes; copy s remains dental. The copy is the
padādi segment, whereas the following root s is now internal.

Rule 8.3.61's special restriction concerns actual ṣ-form san.
Actual caṅ is not san. The selected 7.4.93 transfer provides
the stated copy work, not a new san affix or a root-ṣ prohibition.
Kāśikā's non-san siṣeca control explains why the actual-affix
guard matters. PG98's dental vismāp-/vismāy- before an outer
affix remains a different configuration with no new internal copy.

The new words are source-qualified applications, not literal
whole-paradigm quotations or grammar-engine results. Source faults
and the uncounted literary/nested-ṇic analyses from PG98 remain
qualified rather than silently normalized.
