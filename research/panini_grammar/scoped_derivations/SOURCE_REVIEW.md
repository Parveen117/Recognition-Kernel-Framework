## Annotated scope is evidence, not an automatic semantic proof

PG2 parsed the publisher's `an` and `ad` fields. PG4 turns its six adjudicated
flag groups into explicit view policies. The `pc` field supplies the author's
padaccheda and case annotations. PG4 preserves all columns, including unknown
extra fields, instead of assigning them a guessed grammatical meaning.

The compiler binds hand-reviewed semantic roles to the resolved source terms.
It never reads a case number as sufficient instruction semantics. Kāśikā
`11047` calls अचः a partitive genitive. Kāśikā `31068` describes a following
agent-expressing sārvadhātuka affix; that is a semantic reading of कर्तरि.
The inherited धातोः at 3.1.68 comes from 3.1.22 and imports no yaṅ action.
In particular, the later 3.1.91 heading is not retroactively substituted for
that source.

No claim of semantic verification is made for all 23,589 raw inheritance
annotations or all 9,653 padaccheda tokens. The 19 bound records are an
explicitly delimited executable/review vocabulary.

## A seventh scope adjudication: 8.2.78

The publisher annotates पदस्य from 8.1.16 at 8.2.78. Kāśikā `82078` instead
explains lengthening before r/v occupying the root's penultimate position,
with a consonant following. Its examples include हूर्छिता and ऊर्विता.

[Tattvabodhinī](https://github.com/ashtadhyayi-com/data/blob/5744762f010d677cfb43f347a42d02796cf615d6/sutraani/tattvabodhini.txt)
at `82078` explicitly rejects carrying पदस्य into that reading: doing so
would obstruct कूर्दते and गूर्दते. PG4 therefore excludes that one inherited
term while retaining its raw annotation and source. This is a commentary
choice beyond PG2's structural interval flags; a structurally consistent
edge can still require semantic correction.

The adapter implements only original r/v in the declared root-upadhā domain,
with short i/u/ṛ preceding and a consonant following. Kāśikā distinguishes
yaṇ substitutions and bahiraṅga effects that are unavailable in the relevant
view. PG4 therefore refuses an unspecified yaṇ/bahiraṅga visibility judgment;
a separately supplied reviewed original-vowel view blocks the r/v test.
The special ḷ lengthening reading remains unresolved. No hypothetical long
ḹ is introduced to fill that gap.

This connects a real inherited-scope decision to an operation and its negative
controls. It does not supply the general sthānivad/antaraṅga/bahiraṅga theory.

## Conditioned teaching retroflexion

[Bālamanoramā](https://github.com/ashtadhyayi-com/data/blob/5744762f010d677cfb43f347a42d02796cf615d6/sutraani/balamanorama.txt)
at `64122` explicitly treats ṣṭabhi's dental restoration after ṣ → s and
the later num/anusvāra/parasavarṇa steps. At `64068` it describes the teaching
readings for ṣṇai and ṣṭhā; other passages describe ṣṭip, ṣṭep and ṣṭam.
The adopted finite family is listed by teaching form and lexical identity.
Its 34 records are checked against the pinned Dhātupāṭha.

[Prakriyāsarvasva](https://github.com/ashtadhyayi-com/data/blob/5744762f010d677cfb43f347a42d02796cf615d6/sutraani/prakriyasarvasvam.txt)
at `24079` explicitly gives षणु दाने, withdrawal of ṇatva and सनोति/सनुते.
That supports the two named ṣaṇ witnesses. The digitized passage prints
पकारजं, an apparent typo that is preserved and flagged; it is not silently
emended or used to infer a general phonetic cause.

The ṣṇai passage uses ṣṭutva terminology, whereas other readings distinguish
ṇatva. The adapter consequently labels the change as a source-identified
conditioned dental restoration, not as a universal reverse application of
8.4.41. It requires the actual retained 6.1.64 event on the initial ṣ UID.
It does not fire merely because the current visible string begins with s.

Most importantly,
[Siddhāntakaumudī](https://github.com/ashtadhyayi-com/data/blob/5744762f010d677cfb43f347a42d02796cf615d6/sutraani/kaumudi.txt)
at `72074` explicitly calls the nimittāpāya maxim non-universal. A generic
"delete a cause, roll back every consequence" algorithm would be unjustified.
PG4 only admits the named root convention and keeps the old source sounds.

## tuk recipients, priority and options

Kāśikā `61073` says the short vowel itself receives tuk, rather than the whole
string ending in it. The adapter attaches the fresh augment UID to that
vowel UID and uses the pinned T82 ordinary `tu~k` → t placement contract.
The subsequent c in cch is a distinct 8.4.40 event, not an inserted c silently
attributed to 6.1.73.

Kāśikā `61075` supplies the long-vowel cases ह्रीच्छति and म्लेच्छति. At
`61076`, a long pada-final vowel admits both branches, as in कुटीच्छाया /
कुटीछाया. Kāśikā `61074` gives mandatory tuk for the identified āṅ/māṅ
readings even where the option would otherwise apply. The records express
these particular precedence relations; selecting the latest numbered rule
would incorrectly make the identified-particle cases optional.

Particle identity and sense are supplied separately from the visible ā.
Missing identity/sense or boundary information remains unresolved. Vedic
extensions at 6.1.76 are not generalized. Kāśikā `61086` also explains
ekādeśa visibility for tuk; an unreviewed prior ekādeśa is therefore outside
this junction interface.

Kāśikā `71058` places idit num at the teaching-root stage. The root pipeline
retains that earlier event. In उछिँ, num yields unch and removes direct
vowel–ch contact; the later query gives uñch without tuk. A superficially
similar non-idit teaching form can license tuk. This distinction depends on
retained grammatical history, not an output spelling lookup.

## Contact rules and their prohibitions

Kāśikā `84040` explicitly allows contact on either side and distinguishes the
trigger relation from the ordered substitution mapping. The palatal record
therefore does not pair only s-with-ś and dental-with-palatal by a misplaced
yathāsaṅkhya restriction. The corresponding retroflex record inherits स्तोः
at `84041`.

The following prohibitions remain separate records with their inherited न:

- `84042`: a preceding pada-final retroflex stop blocks the relevant ṣṭutva,
  with nām and the separately attributed navati/nagarī supplement excepted.
- `84043`: dental targets before ṣ are blocked in the stated contact.
- `84044`: dental targets following ś are blocked; प्रश्नः is a control.

The adapter checks the trigger sound's UID and the actual boundary. Unknown
following-unit identity at the 8.4.42 boundary remains unresolved. A context
with mixed allowed and prohibited causes is flagged for review rather than
resolved by an invented universal priority ladder.

Kāśikā `84053` supports the later voiced-stop substitution before jhaś, with
दोग्धा among its examples. Thus the source-backed masj contact passes through
maśj before majj in this profile. This does not add every neighbouring
Tripādī operation or establish a full phonetic normal form.

## Root nasals and visibility

Kāśikā `83024` includes both n and m; PG3's restriction to newly inserted num
was a local implementation boundary. PG4 admits existing sounds too, with
explicit non-pada-final and jhal guards. A nasal or semivowel following is
not jhal. The later parasavarṇa record uses the admitted varga mapping.

Kāśikā `84058` explains why an n → anusvāra → n sequence, as in कुर्वन्ति,
does not reopen an earlier ṇatva operation. The adapter executes an ordered
query and never feeds its output into the earlier root state. Within a
contact record, each change exits that record's target class; its local
iteration terminates without revisiting a later stage. Query history records
both events even when the visible result is unchanged.

This is a bounded visibility discipline. General rule-relative asiddha,
8.4.59 pada-final alternatives, semivowel nasalization, unrestricted
sthānivad and full priority competition remain open. Primary attribution,
local examples and lexical agreements do not replace independent specialist
or cross-edition review.
