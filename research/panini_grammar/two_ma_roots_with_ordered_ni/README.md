# PG247 · The two mā teachings selected by the ordered preverbs

Monty · 12 November 2026

The Kāśikā explains the **mā** in 8.4.17 with two middle verbs. Class-three *māṅ* gives **praṇimimīte** and **pariṇimimīte**. Class-one *meṅ* gives **praṇimayate** and **pariṇimayate**. The Mādhavīya glosses the latter’s *praṇidāna* as exchange and reports ‘giving back’ as another interpretation. It explicitly preserves retroflexion in *mayate*, even though its present lacks an *ā*.

| With *pra + ni* | Present 3sg | Present 3du | Imperative 2sg | Imperfect 3sg |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| *māṅ*, class 3 | praṇimimīte | praṇimimāte | praṇimimīṣva | praṇyamimīta |
| *meṅ*, class 1 | praṇimayate | praṇimayete | praṇimayasva | praṇyamayata |

The Nyāsa takes the reduplication from *ślu* and the copy’s *i* from 7.4.76. The class-one present uses *śap*. The imperfect places the augment inside *ni*. The two present histories differ in all 36 person–number and lakāra comparisons for each ordered prefix pair.

The adādi **māti** falls outside the Nyāsa’s selection. A fourth-class **māyate** also occurs in the root data. The Mādhavīya expressly attributes that root entry to only some teachers. It cannot be substituted for the class-three **mimīte** cited in the rule commentary. With two root entries, two prefix pairs and four present-system lakāras, this packet adds **144 written positions**; all 72 distinct bare cells agree with the fixed morphological archive.
