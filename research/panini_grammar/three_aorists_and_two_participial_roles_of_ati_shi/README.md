# PG854 · Three aorist readings and the teacher and pupil with ati + śī

Mādhavīya, śīṅ svapne, pp. 339–340, uses ati + śī in two environments. In one the finite verb presents an affected participant as acting of itself (karmakartṛ). In the other a teacher and pupil take different cases according to which participant the participle expresses. The examples specify their syntax without naming a participant in the aorist. The teacher or pupil cannot be supplied as an attested subject of that aorist.

### One third singular, three finite readings

The request fixes ati, simple śīṅ, luṅ, middle third singular and the karmakartṛ construction. In 3.1.87 an agent showing the action of the object can receive object-conditioned operations. For a vowel-final root before the original ta, 3.1.62 admits a choice between ciṇ and the ordinary sic replacement of cli (3.1.43–44). The optional ciṇ-like treatment of sic in 6.4.62 creates the second fork. The printed order on p. 339 corresponds to the following paths.

| Path | Root grade and ending | Word |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Actual ciṇ | The ṇ marker licenses vṛddhi (7.2.115). Before the surviving i, ai gives āy (6.1.78). The following ta has luk by 6.4.104. | atyaśāyi |
| Sic with ciṇ-like treatment | Vṛddhi produces the same śāy before iṭ. Sic and its following ending survive. In the resulting i-s-ta sequence, the s and dental stop are realized as ṣṭ. | atyaśāyiṣṭa |
| Ordinary sic | The ordinary seṭ path of this root has guṇa (śe), then śay before iṭ, giving short a. The ending survives as on the preceding sic path. | atyaśayiṣṭa |

The a of the past augment belongs between preverb and root (6.4.71). At ati + a the prefix-final i becomes y (6.1.77). Thus the initial *atya-* is common to all three. The i at the end of the first word belongs to ciṇ; the i before ṣṭ in the other two belongs to the augment of sic. Kāśikā 3.1.66 separately exemplifies the bare impersonal aorist **aśāyi bhavatā**. PG850 already constructed bare agentive **aśayiṣṭa**. Neither licenses transferring the three-way choice to another person or to the bare agent grid.

### What kta expresses in the two clauses

| Source | Agent denoted by kta | Object denoted by kta |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mādhavīya, p. 340 | **atiśayito guruṃ śiṣyaḥ**. The pupil is nominative and the teacher accusative. | **atiśayito guruḥ śiṣyeṇa**. The teacher is nominative and the pupil instrumental. |
| Kāśikā, 3.4.72, distinct preverb upa | **upaśayito guruṃ bhavān**. The addressee is nominative. | **upaśayito gurur bhavatā**. The teacher is nominative. |

The Mādhavīya explicitly attributes transitivity to the prefix here. Kta in 3.4.70 can express the object. The named śīṅ entry in 3.4.72 also admits the agent. On the seṭ niṣṭhā path, 1.2.19 withdraws kit status, allowing guṇa and ay before iṭ. Add ta, then masculine nominative singular agreement to give atiśayitaḥ; in both printed phrases the following g yields the connected atiśayito. Its two identical surfaces leave the roles of guru and śiṣya recoverable from their cases. Kāśikā additionally gives upaśayitaṃ bhavatā as a bhāva example. Record that example with its own prefix. The selected ati passages supply two roles, and do not establish a third ati quotation. Likewise the following intensive with yaṅ-luk has its own marker history outside this niṣṭhā derivation.
