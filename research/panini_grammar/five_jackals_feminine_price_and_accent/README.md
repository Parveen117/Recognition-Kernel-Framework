# PG241 · The price of five female jackals and the accent of kroṣṭrī

Monty · 12 November 2026

The price and the purchased object have different grammatical roles in the Kāśikā’s **pañcakroṣṭṛbhī rathaiḥ**, ‘with chariots bought for five female jackals’. Stating the price separately gives *pañcabhiḥ kroṣṭrībhiḥ*. The derived adjective agrees with *rathaiḥ* in the masculine instrumental plural. At a pause its ending is **-bhiḥ**. Before the *r* of *rathaiḥ*, the running text has **-bhī**.

The Bhāṣya tests what licenses the *tṛj*-like stem after the subordinate feminine affix disappears. Making an overt ī both the prerequisite for that stem and the suffix that the stem must first license produces mutual dependence. Two traditional accounts handle the ordinary feminine and the price compound differently.

The main Bhāṣya and Kāśikā passages agree on **ṛ** in the price adjective. A later sentence of the pinned Nyāsa transcription instead has *pañcakroṣṭubhir* with **u**. The table credits **one new nominal position** to the shared primary ṛ reading. The feminine instrumental plural already belonged to PG230. The accent explanation adds a source observation.
