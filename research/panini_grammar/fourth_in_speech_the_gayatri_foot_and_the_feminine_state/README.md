# PG845 · The fourth part of speech, Gāyatrī’s fourth foot and the fourth state

At 5.2.51 PG791 registered two masculine nominative positions for the ordinal of four. Its first profile retains the numeral and inserts thuk before ḍaṭ. Its second has two supplementary paths after loss of the initial syllabic unit of catur. The cha path gives turīya, the yat path turya. The Kāśikā gives the same completing relation for both. An ordinal selects a member by its position in an ordered set of four. The lexicalized fraction ‘a quarter’ and the separate verb meaning ‘go’ have different input types. The [case forms](CASES.tsv) extend just these two PG791 profiles through seven cases in each gender and number.

The masculine and neuter use ordinary a-stem endings. For feminine agreement, the final ṭ marker of ḍaṭ licenses ṅīp under 4.1.15, producing caturthī. Neither supplementary suffix bears that final ṭ. Under 4.1.4 the ā-bearing feminine gives turīyā and turyā. Thus the feminine direct dual is caturthyau on the first history and turīye or turye on the second. The intervening dental stop th blocks transmission of retroflexion from r in caturthena, caturthāni and caturthānām. The permitted interveners in the two y stems transmit r under 8.4.1–2, hence turīyeṇa/turyeṇa and turīyāṇi/turyāṇi. The two masculine and neuter ablative pause outputs use the inherited final-stop policy. Each feminine ablative has its own vowel-stem realization.

Śabdakalpadruma 12076 explicitly supplies the ḍaṭ/thuk history, and 15132 attributes long turīya to the supplement. The neighbouring 15131 is a neuter nominal entry. Monier-Williams 71343, 86069 and 86079 distinguish the ī feminine from the ordinary feminine formation of the two other adjectives. Retain their distinction from MW 86055, the verb for going. A case label, the fourth vowel in a truncation, the quarter-fraction and a particular recited wording do not open general interchangeability.
