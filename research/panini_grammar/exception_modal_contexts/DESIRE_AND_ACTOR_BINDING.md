# PG28 — Desire verbs, same actors and different person frames

Author: Monty. 3 November 2026.

## C6. 3.3.157: an expressed desire verb and different actors

Retain a desire event w and desired event e with actors a_w and a_e,
plus their separate clause/discourse frames. On supplied kāmapravedana,
an expressed desire-verb upapada and **a_w≠a_e**, select **{O,I}**
for the desired-event verb. The grammatical choices keep the same e;
they do not create a second desire or replace its actor with the hearer.

Kāśikā/Nyāsa and Kaumudī use icchāmi bhuñjīta/bhuṅktām bhavān.
The outer subject is the speaker and the inner actor is the addressed
bhavān, whose finite agreement is third person. P2 supplies the two
inner forms on bhuj's eating/A profile. This is ordinary I, not B.

The disclosure premise matters. The cited icchan karoti does not by
itself supply this communicative frame. Tattvabodhinī disputes a broader
inference that infinitival expressions with icchan could never occur.
PG28 records that disagreement and claims only **no license from this
specific disclosure construction** when its premise is absent.

## C7. 3.3.158–159: same actor changes the expression family

For **a_w=a_e**, 3.3.158 prescribes tumun instead of C6's ordinary
liṅ/loṭ alternative. Nyāsa 3.3.159 explicitly says the tumun provision
blocks both finite choices; 3.3.159 then restores **liṅ alone**, leaving
ordinary loṭ unavailable from this same-actor construction.

The resulting addressed continuation family is

\[
 E(w,e)=\begin{cases}
 \{I,O\},&a_w\ne a_e,\\
 \{tumun,O\},&a_w=a_e.
 \end{cases}
\]

This is a replacement followed by a specific restoration, not the union
of all nearby suffix names. In particular {tumun,I,O} is not the
same-actor result. Unknown actor identity does not license that union;
it leaves a relation-valued obligation pending.

E4 derives **bhoktum** from the same exact eating root. The infinitive
does not introduce its own finite controller; its semantic actor link to
w is retained. The source's icchati bhoktum does not license a separate
unexpressed actor chosen independently for bhoktum.

### Same actor does not mean the same grammatical person

Kāśikā 3.3.159 gives **भुञ्जीयेतीच्छति**. Its segmented reading is
*bhuñjīya | iti | icchati*: a first-person desired-event expression
inside the reported frame and a third-person outer desire verb.
The shared actor can be first person in the inner self-expression and
third person relative to an external narrator. The outer verb is a
quoted source witness here; this packet does not add a new iṣ paradigm.

P2 supplies bhuñjīya. For this one joined example, 6.1.87 joins its
final a with iti's i to e; 6.1.101 joins iti's final i with icchati's
initial i to ī, giving **bhuñjīyetīcchati**. Retain both boundaries
and owners even when the displayed string has no space. This local
join is not a complete external-sandhi or reported-speech grammar.

Conversely, two distinct actors can both receive third-person endings
in one narrator's frame. Comparing only person/number labels therefore
cannot decide same-kartṛ status in either direction. The equality test
belongs on retained actor identities before those clause-relative readings.

## C8. 3.3.160 acts on the desire verb itself

When a desire-meaning root is the **addressed verb** and present time
is supplied, 3.3.160 licenses **{L,O}**. Kāśikā/Kaumudī give
icchati/icchet and kāmayate/kāmayeta. These examples witness the
selector; their complete lexical paradigms are not new PG28 positions.
Without desire meaning, this rule does not turn every present verb
into a liṅ option. Unknown present time is not a future or counterfactual
default.

This is a different owner from the complement verb in C6/C7.
In a frame where both sets of premises are supplied, the outer choice
{L,O} composes with the appropriate inner E(w,e). Changing the outer
mood does not change a_w=a_e or authorize loṭ in the same-actor inner
branch. A software flag meaning only “somewhere in a wish sentence”
would conflate these two operations.
