# PG28 — Śnam, im and source-addressed consonant choices

Author: Monty. 3 November 2026.

## E1. Bhuj retains its sense while constructing śnam

Apply 3.1.78 to the exact class-7 root, placing śnam's pronounced na
after u by 1.1.47. The inherited nitya śnam-before-guṇa account gives
strong bhunaj; effective kit/ṅit/ṅitvat 6.4.111 deletes only śnam's a
and gives weak bhunj. There is no lexical num here.

The actual-boundary readout gives weak bhuñj before vowels and y/ī,
bhuṅkt before t, bhuṅkṣ before sva and bhuṅgdh before dhi/dhvam.
Use 8.2.30, 8.3.24, 8.3.59 and 8.4.53/55/58 with their retained
owners. No savarṇa root/ending stop pair occurs, so there is no
8.4.65 extra reading. Strong P 3sg is bhunaktu; ordinary first
persons are bhunajāni/bhunajai and their dual/plural companions.

All ordinary laṭ A endings are weak on this inherited śnam interface.
The new upabhoga/A present profile therefore uses bhuñj, including
first singular bhuñje. Do not carry loṭ's first-person āṭ or strong
bhunaj into that present paradigm. Sense chooses the license and
retained event; it does not change this shared A stem construction.

## E2. Tṛh's im needs the composed host and effective pit

First place śnam after ṛ: tṛ-na-h. Strong śnam retains a; weak
śnam loses a by 6.4.111. Preserve the root and infix as different
owners. 7.3.92 prescribes im to the already śnam-bearing aṅga, not
to the isolated root. Its pronounced i is placed after that host's
last vowel, śnam's a, and 6.1.87 gives a+i→e with both inputs in
Smriti. The result is tṛṇeh before the relevant strong consonant ending.

The operative guard is exact tṛh-with-śnam, sārvadhātuka,
**effective pit and actual hal-initial continuation**. PG23 M1's
Tattvabodhinī 3.4.103 policy is essential: historical p does not stay
operative after the explicit O ṅit prescription. Hence no im is
manufactured in tṛṃhyāt merely because y is a hal sound. B tāt has
its own weak/ṅit override. Ordinary first-person āṭ is pit but starts
with a vowel, so it also has no im. Only ordinary I/B 3sg receives im.

Strong śnam n has ṛ-triggered ṇatva before surviving a/e. In weak
forms, nonfinal n before h/jhal first becomes anusvāra under 8.3.24.
Before surviving h, it remains ṃ: tṛṃhantu, tṛṃhyāt. Before later
retroflex stops, 8.4.58 restores ṇ. These two ṇ histories are not
identified. Strong first-person ni remains dental: intervening śnam ṇ
blocks a second r-to-ending-n window, giving tṛṇahāni.

## E3. Two explicitly different contact contracts

**T-family.** At tu/tām/tam/ta/tāt, h→ḍh by 8.2.31 and
t→dh by 8.2.40. Reuse PG8's exact source permission: following
dh→ḍh under 8.4.41 is visible to the addressed 8.3.13 query.
Delete the root-owned ḍh, retaining the ending-owned consonant.
Thus strong tṛṇeḍhu contrasts with weak tṛṇḍhām/tṛṇḍham/tṛṇḍha.
For tāt the complete result is **tṛṇḍhāt**. No short a/i/u directly
precedes the deleted root ḍh in these weak forms, so 6.3.111 does
not create a long ṛ. The complete modal endings are listed in P.

**Ordinary hi.** 6.4.101 supplies dhi. Prakriyāsarvasva 7.3.92
explicitly follows its hi discussion with 8.4.65; Bālamanoramā prints
both tṛṇḍhi and tṛṇḍḍhi. Adopt the following finite schedule:

1. Assemble weak tṛ-n-h | dhi with original ending/marker owners.
2. At h→ḍh, the earlier 8.3.13 query retains following **dental dh**.
   No tṛh-hi forward-visibility certificate is supplied in this policy.
   Its adjacent-ḍh predicate is false on that query view.
3. Apply later 8.4.41 ṣṭutva and 8.4.53 jaś; restore the homorganic
   nasal under 8.4.58. The actual word is **tṛṇḍḍhi**.
4. The first ḍ is now a jhar after hal ṇ and before savarṇa ḍh.
   8.4.65 optionally deletes it, yielding **tṛṇḍhi**.

There is one collapse site and therefore two hi readings. Optional
pronunciation doubling is not involved. No blanket replay of 8.3.13
after the late events is licensed. The contracted output alone cannot
distinguish this late-collapse history from a hypothetical early-lopa
history, but the uncontracted branch and deleted-owner record can.

This local selection does not rewrite PG24's lih route, which has its
own explicit Bālamanoramā līḍhi witness and deletion/lengthening account.
Nor does citing Prakriyāsarvasva's hi clause silently adopt every adjacent
weak-tas remark as a new global schedule. Their wider reconciliation
remains a source-adjudication obligation. P's exhaustion is relative to
the exact contracts just stated.

## E4. Nonfinite bhoktum keeps a different boundary

C7's same-actor desire frame licenses tumun by 3.3.158. Retain its
teaching markers and pronounced tum. It is a kṛt ārdhadhātuka
continuation under 3.4.114, not a sārvadhātuka finite ending; therefore
class-7 śnam is not introduced. The exact bhuj is aniṭ under the
inherited 7.2.10 lexical account; Kāśikā's aniṭ list includes it.

The owned derivation is bhuj | tum → bhoj | tum by 7.3.86;
j→g by 8.2.30, then g→k before t by 8.4.55, yielding **bhoktum**.
No finite A ending is appended. Eating sense and same-actor dependency
remain in the frame even though the infinitive has no finite person
exponent. The spurious bhuñj-tum would incorrectly carry a śnam from
a different aṅga/affix boundary.

Kāśikā 3.3.158 expressly supplies icchati bhoktum. This packet
constructs that one nonfinite continuation and its actor link; it does
not add all tumun roots or a complete infinitival syntax to the count.
