# PG28 — Affirmation, disclosed wishes, capacity and future dependence

Author: Monty. 3 November 2026.

These selectors start on D/N's retained semantic frame. O denotes
non-āśīs liṅ; I ordinary loṭ; F lṛṭ; L present laṭ. A selector
returns addressed grammatical requests, not generated words or a judgment
that the described event will happen. Counterfactual kriyātipatti is
outside this new domain. Source-qualified meaning is supplied evidence.

## C1. 3.3.152: uta/api must have the admitted sense

When uta **or** api is used in the source's bāḍham/affirmative sense,
select O at the addressed verb. Kāśikā/Nyāsa explain samarthayoḥ as
their equal sense in these alternative uses; both particles need not
occur together. Their interrogative/covering uses do not satisfy this
rule. The source contrasts **uta kuryāt** with questioning and
api as part of covering a doorway. There is no instruction to identify all printed api
tokens with this modal meaning.

Formally, on the supplied context family,

\[
\{particle\in\{uta,api\},\ sense=bāḍham\}\longmapsto\{O\}.
\]

A known non-affirmative sense gives NoLicenseFrom3.3.152; unknown sense
gives Open. This is not a global prohibition of O from another rule.

## C2. 3.3.153: disclosure without a desire-verb upapada

Kāśikā defines kāmapravedana as disclosure of one's intention to
another. On the selected Bālamanoramā/Tattvabodhinī division, contextual
or nominal disclosure without an expressed desire-verb upapada belongs
here; desire-verb embedding belongs to C6–C7.

If the disclosure is supplied and kaccit is absent, select O. The
literal example kāmo me bhuñjīta bhavān uses bhuj's eating/A profile.
Bhavān has third-person agreement; it is not changed to a second-person
ending merely because the hearer is addressed. If kaccit is present,
this rule supplies no license. Its cited jīvati example is a boundary
witness, not a new jīv paradigm or a universal rule forcing laṭ in
every kaccit construction.

The ordinary O selector neither implies blessing nor introduces B's
tāt. The two semantic fields remain distinct even when English glosses
use the same word “wish”.

## C3. 3.3.154: capacity, an unspoken alam and semantic evidence

Require a supplied assessment of sufficient capacity, together with the
source's **understood but unspoken** alam meaning. On that frame select
O. Kāśikā/Nyāsa distinguish this from a mere likelihood assertion;
Bālamanoramā allows the mountain-breaking example as rhetorical excess.
The grammar therefore does not certify a person's physical ability or
assign a probability to the asserted event.

Keep these independent fields:

- the assessed capacity/adequacy meaning is established;
- the alam sense is understood in context;
- the alam word is not overtly used in the addressed construction.

Overt alam or a mere “probably” reading fails this particular guard.
Missing evidence for the understood meaning remains Open. In particular,
absence of the printed word alone is not proof of its intended sense.

## C4. 3.3.155: optionality has its own lexical context

Retain all C3 premises. If an expressed **capacity-assessment verb** is
the upapada and the relevant **yad** is absent, 3.3.155 relaxes the
otherwise obligatory O choice. On the admitted future frame the relation
is **{O,F}**. Kāśikā gives sambhāvayāmi bhuñjīta bhavān alongside
sambhāvayāmi bhokṣyate bhavān. The latter is a source example; PG28
does not construct a new bhuj future paradigm.

If the relevant yad is present, the relaxation does not apply and
C3's O remains. Without the capacity verb, a nominal mention of
possibility does not activate 3.3.155. The source specifically says
yacchabda; this field must not be aliased with the conditional **yadi**
in C5 or with every relative/conditional expression.

| Completed C3 frame | Expressed assessment verb | Relevant yad | Selection |
|---|---|---|---|
| no special lexical relaxation | no | either | O |
| lexical relaxation | yes | absent | O or F |
| relaxation blocked | yes | present | O |

These are source-declared override cases. They are not obtained by
choosing whichever numbered rule happens to be later in a global queue.

## C5. 3.3.156: future cause and consequence retain two event owners

Require a supplied future cause/consequence relation H(e_c,e_f).
Kāśikā and Bālamanoramā make the optional O/F prescription available
at a verb expressing the cause **or** its result, and restrict this
construction to future reference. Present hanti iti palāyate is their
negative control for this future restriction, not a new conjugation.

At each addressed licensed event,

\[
 H(e_c,e_f)\land Future(e_c,e_f)\ \Longrightarrow\
 L(e_c)=L(e_f)=\{O,F\}.
\]

For the admitted two-finite-clause subdomain, apply the optional rule
independently at its two verb owners, giving {O,F}×{O,F}. The source
prints the uniform (O,O) and (F,F) examples; the two mixed choices
are consequences of this explicitly stated sitewise composition, not
quoted examples or a claim that a single global mood toggle controls
the sentence. No additional discourse constraint is silently assumed
absent outside this declared subdomain.

Neither the two actors nor their person/number controllers need be
identical. The causal link is supplied semantic structure, not an
inference that grammatical subordination proves real-world causation.
Unknown future reference or causal relation leaves a corresponding
obligation rather than turning both truth values into licensed words.

The earlier lṛṅ rules 3.3.139/140 address the separate kriyātipatti
case; C5 does not replace them. A selector can be complete on this
non-counterfactual domain while a particular F morphology adapter is
still open. Contextual reach and realized word positions stay distinct.
