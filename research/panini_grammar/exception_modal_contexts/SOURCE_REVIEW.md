## Bhuj admission and the additional sense

Keep the literal 1070017 lexical record with पालनाभ्यवहारयोः and
its supplied P label. PG8 already separates protection/P from eating/A.
Kāśikā 1.3.66 excludes protection and the distinct tudādi crookedness
homonym. Kaumudī/Bālamanoramā explicitly explain upabhoga through realm
and suffering examples. PG28 therefore adds one upabhoga/A profile on
the same root; it does not silently rewrite the raw row or treat every
object as another sense. Tattvabodhinī's ātmasātkaraṇa discussion is
retained without creating a fourth bhuj profile.

The new profile receives laṭ and I/O/B only. Quoted perfect/past
examples in the source do not become constructed positions here. Bhuj
7.2.10 aniṭ evidence and Kāśikā 3.3.158's icchati bhoktum support
E4's separate nonfinite continuation; no śnam crosses that affix boundary.

## Tṛh: what was resolved and what remains source-relative

Kāśikā/Kaumudī 7.3.92 make im depend on śnam-bearing tṛh and the
pit hal-initial sārvadhātuka context. E2 preserves the composed host;
PG23's Tattvabodhinī 3.4.103 policy supplies effective, rather than merely
historical, pit. This prevents a spurious im in tṛṃhyāt.

PG8's source-addressed early ṣṭutva view is reused for the T-family.
The new hi branch has additional direct evidence: Prakriyāsarvasva
7.3.92 explicitly puts 8.4.65 after its hi/ḍhatva/ṣṭutva account.
Bālamanoramā prints both तृण्ढि and तृण्ड्ढि. E3 selects the
corresponding hi schedule without early 8.3.13 forward visibility, then
retains or collapses the late root-owned ḍ. This is an optional lopa
branch, not optional pronunciation doubling.

The selection is expressly local. Prakriyāsarvasva also has a nearby
weak-tas collapse remark; PG28 does not adopt all adjacent scheduling
claims as one universal algorithm. Kāśikā/Nyāsa/Padamañjarī 8.3.13
and the inherited early-visibility account are retained for comparison.
Their wider equivalence is open. PG24's separately witnessed līḍhi
route remains intact; a universal “no early view before hi” rule would
be unjustified. The modern sutrārtha 8.3.13 object is labelled explanatory
context, not a traditional authority settling that conflict.

## Contextual liṅ and clause-addressed evidence

Complete selected Kāśikā, Kaumudī, Bālamanoramā, Tattvabodhinī and
Nyāsa fields for 3.3.152–160 were consulted, including absent fields.
Kāśikā 3.3.151 and 3.3.139/140 provide contextual boundaries only.

| Rules | Admitted use and limit |
|---|---|
| 3.3.152 | uta or api in the specified affirmative sense; not a mere token match |
| 3.3.153 | disclosed wish without desire-verb upapada on the selected commentary division; kaccit blocks this license |
| 3.3.154–155 | supplied sufficient-capacity sense with unspoken alam; expressed assessment verb and absence of relevant yad permit the optional future alternative |
| 3.3.156 | optional O/F at each addressed future cause/result verb; mixed pairs are the declared independent-site inference, not quoted examples |
| 3.3.157 | expressed desire verb, disclosure and different actors permit inner I/O |
| 3.3.158–159 | same actor first replaces both finite choices with tumun, then restores O alone |
| 3.3.160 | present desire-meaning verb itself permits L/O; this is a different clause owner |

Nyāsa 3.3.159 expressly explains restoration after the tumun override.
The joined bhuñjīyetīcchati source witness supports retaining actor
identity across inner first-person and outer third-person frames; the
local two-boundary sandhi is constructed explicitly. Outer iṣ/kam forms
are source witnesses, not new complete paradigms.

The contexts exclude counterfactual kriyātipatti, whose lṛṅ treatment
belongs to 3.3.139/140. C's negative guards mean no license **from the
addressed rule**, not universal ungrammaticality. Unknown equality or
semantic evidence is not optionality. Tattvabodhinī's objection concerning
icchan kartum is preserved; Kāśikā's non-disclosure example does not
establish a blanket ban on such infinitives.

## Literal anomalies retained

- Bālamanoramā 7.3.92 mixes tas/ti in its strong-form explanation and
  contains damaged तृणेहिऋ and तृंह्रात् /तृह्रात् readings. The
  complete field is archived; P's endings follow explicit owned rules.
- Nyāsa 7.3.92 prints 7.2.31 for ho ḍhaḥ, 6.1.66 for halṅyādi
  and 8.4.55 for vāvasāne. Canonical sūtra IDs are independently taken
  from the pinned sūtra table, not silently substituted in that field.
- Padamañjarī 7.3.92 contains तृणेढै and तृणाढ; Prakriyāsarvasva
  includes तृम्हन्ति. Literal source spellings remain untouched.
- Kāśikā 3.3.157 prints भुङ्क्ता भवान्. Nyāsa/Kaumudī give the
  complete bhavān construction with final m/anusvāra. P2 derives bhuṅktām
  from the complete ending instead of copying a truncated display.
- Bālamanoramā and Tattvabodhinī fields keyed to 3.3.159 actually begin
  with लिङ् यदि and discuss 3.3.168. They are archived as displaced,
  **not** used as 3.3.159 evidence. Nyāsa's field keyed to 3.3.155
  discusses 3.3.156 and is likewise not used for 3.3.155.
- Bālamanoramā 3.3.153 prints नित्यं लिङ् in its kriyātipatti
  sentence. The conflicting lṛṅ context in Kāśikā 3.3.139/140 and
  Tattvabodhinī is recorded; that counterfactual domain is not admitted.

## Closing T148 source adapter

T148's causative extension was reviewed after its new commit appeared.
Complete Kāśikā/Kaumudī/Bālamanoramā 1.3.74 fields were freshly
consulted and archived for its pada adapter. They retain kartṛ-directed
fruit; two requested morphology columns do not alone establish two
licenses for one fixed meaning. This review does not add a new PG28
causative paradigm or another independent 1.3.74 rule ID. The four
new combined IDs come from the attributed T148 ledger, with their
separate evidence grade in coverage.
Kāśikā 2.4.46 was also read completely: its exclusion of bodhana
is a meaning condition for the iṇ→gam substitution, to be retained
in the companion's admission adapter rather than inferred from NP/NA.

Inherited ending, marker, śnam, blessing and phonological interfaces
retain their earlier source policies. Written completeness is relative
to this exact admission and scheduling contract. No runtime success,
whole-sentence parser or resolution of every textual disagreement is
inferred from matching a quoted form.
