# PG86 — Dāṇ/yam lexical, case and sense boundaries

Monty · 9 November 2026

## H01. Two roots behind the present yacch interface

Select **1011079 दाण् दाने**, bhvādi, lexical P, anudātta/aniṭ.
Final ṇ is an it marker under 1.3.3/9; it is not ñ and does not give the
root general U status. Kāśikā 1.1.20 explicitly includes this dāṇ among
ghu roots. Exclude the juhotyādi **1030010 डुदाञ्**, although some
nonpresent forms coincide, and **1020054 दाप्**, which is not ghu.

Select **1011139 यमँ उपरमे**, bhvādi, lexical P, anudātta/aniṭ.
Its final nasalized teaching a disappears under 1.3.2/9; internal a and
root-final m remain. The publisher explicitly lists upa with marriage
meaning. Exclude the seṭ **1010930** and curādi **1100119** homographs,
and the separately catalogued **1011031** textual variant. Its mit status
does not insert ṇic or create causal forms in this basic domain.

The two selected roots share a present **yacch** readout for different
reasons. That equality does not collapse lexical identities, ā/m ownership,
ghu membership, pada conditions or their nonpresent continuations.

## H02. Sam+dāṇ: case form, recipient meaning and usage certificate

Under **1.3.55**, sam+dāṇ takes A when the expressed, connected tṛtīyā
phrase has **caturthyartha**, here recipient meaning, in the source's
specified **aśiṣṭavyavahāra** usage. This is the historical source's
usage classification, not a new unrestricted instrumental-to-dative rule.
Kaumudī gives dāsyā saṃyacchate; Kāśikā gives the intervening-pra case.
The counted role carrier is agent ⊗ gift/theme ⊗ recipient, with separate
case-expression, intended relation and usage witnesses. Nominal inflection
is supplied as input, not newly derived or counted.

An ordinary instrument does not meet the meaning guard: Kāśikā's
pāṇinā saṃprayacchati is a local P control. Nor may the fourth-case
condition be dropped because PG85's sam+car accepted an instrument.
Missing case/meaning/usage evidence yields HOLD rather than a guessed A.

Nyāsa explains the conventional speaker's role construal and repeats parts
of its electronic text; it also prints **सम्प्रच्छते** in one example.
Retain those bytes without adopting that truncated verb as a competing form.
Mahābhāṣya records discussion of an alternative analysis through sahayoga
and karmavyatihāra. The counted contract follows the explicit Kāśikā/
Kaumudī 1.3.55 construction, retaining that discussion as source history.
These are not independent synonym paths or additional positions.

## H03. Sam is relational here; an intervening pra is admitted

Kāśikā, Kaumudī and Bālamanoramā explain inherited **samaḥ** as a
qualifying **ṣaṣṭhī**, not a pañcamī imposing immediate adjacency.
Accordingly SAM_RECIPIENT_DAN_A uses [sam], while
SAM_PRA_RECIPIENT_DAN_A uses ordered [sam,pra]. The latter is directly
supported by **dāsyā saṃprayacchate**. Retain prefix order and the
original sam relation after pra and after the separate past augment.

This interpretation also explains their sam+car examples with intervening
prefixes. Those would be new car requests, not retroactively credited to
PG85 or counted in PG86. No claim that every arbitrary prefix insertion
preserves every grammatical licence follows from this local rule.

## H04. Upa+yam: record the source disagreement instead of merging it

Kāśikā and Nyāsa restrict **1.3.56** to **pāṇigrahaṇa-qualified
svakaraṇa**, with bhāryām upayacchate as witness. This is the selected
UPA_MARRIAGE_YAM_A domain; the supplied participant relation is agent ⊗
spouse/theme. No tṛtīyā certificate from 1.3.55 is inherited here.

Mahābhāṣya and Bālamanoramā explain making what was not one's own one's
own; Padamañjarī explicitly distinguishes that broader reading from
Kāśikā and cites Bhatti's non-marriage acquisition usages. The broader
domain remains an **untallied source policy**, not an error silently erased
or a second free reading of every selected slot. In particular Kāśikā's
other-person's-wife control is not a universal negative across both policies.
Producing or modifying an already-owned garment is a separate rejected
control in the broader account too.

The chosen marriage domain also meets **1.2.16** for optional sic-kit.
It does not carry 1.2.15's distinct gandhana disclosure premise; Kaumudī's
priority discussion when such premises overlap is retained outside this
selected event domain. Do not transport the marriage-specific option into
all broader acquisition readings merely by sharing the word svakaraṇa.

## H05. Context and iṭ interfaces

Use PG85's exact ten context certificates: present, non-today past, ordinary
loṭ/vidhiliṅ, future/non-today future, qualified nonfulfilled future
conditional, unattained blessing, non-Mantra parokṣa perfect with its
first-person premise, and witnessed earlier-today aorist without māṅ.
Each completed agreement frame receives the same profile-specific sense,
roles and, for dāṇ, expressed case/usage witnesses.

Both roots are aniṭ under **7.2.10**. There is no root-facing iṭ at the
sya/tās, A-sīyuṭ or A-sic interface. In particular **7.2.73** is
P-specific: its sak and iṭ do not enter these A aorists. PG85 car's seṭ
policy and 7.2.58's gam policy cannot be transferred by present similarity.

For **liṭ**, 7.2.13's krādi restriction leaves these roots seṭ at the
actual consonant-initial A endings, namely 2sg/2pl/1du/1pl. The P-thal
rules do not create a choice at A-thās. Thus exactly **12 positions/paths**
have short root-facing iṭ. Neither long iṭ nor a dh/ḍh option is licensed.
Original ending i, irec's i and dāṇ's aorist root-i remain distinct.
