## H06. Present śap and two differently owned yacch results

Both bhvādi roots take pit śap, **3.1.68**, throughout the four selected
present-system labels. Dāṇ receives the **whole-root yacch substitution**
of **7.3.78**, with 1.1.55's multi-sound replacement scope. Yam instead
receives final **m→ch** under **7.3.77**, then short a takes tuk under
**6.1.73** and the t/ch contact becomes c/ch under **8.4.40**.
These are not the same operation despite identical yacch readouts.

The śit condition is the specified initial indicatory ś environment;
Bālamanoramā 7.3.78 warns against extending it to perfect eś merely
because eś has a final ś marker. There is no yacch in the later sya,
tās, sīyuṭ, liṭ or sic interfaces. Neither present equality nor an
unqualified global replacement is a lawful nonpresent stem constructor.

Use the thematic A maps L/J/I/O pinned at PG85 H05 on yacch, preserving
śap's thematic a and the endings' own changes. In particular loṭ 1sg
is yacchai and vidhiliṅ 3du is yaccheyātām. Before applying J,
insert initial aṭ independently and perform H10's prefix seam.

## H07. Aniṭ futures and finite luṭ

Select sya/tās under 3.1.33; H05 blocks ordinary iṭ. Dāṇ retains ā,
giving **dāsy** and **dāt**. Yam retains m through the non-kit interface:
yam+sya gives **yaṃsy** through 8.3.24, while yam+tās gives **yant**
through anusvāra followed by 8.4.58's dental parasavarṇa before t.
The latter n is late; it is not the input of an earlier fresh ṇatva pass.

PG85 H06's L/J and finite T_A maps give the three future labels. Luṭ
retains the original A ending, the 2.4.85 number substitution at third

after root ā or the m/anusvāra interface; do not import car's iṭ-driven ṣ.

## H08. Blessing and the two perfect root schedules

A blessing uses PG20's B_A map with **Z=dās** or **Z=yaṃs**, always
dental dh at 2pl. It is not P's kit liṅ. Neither root satisfies the
local A liṅ kit conditions, so dā stays ā and yam keeps m until the
late anusvāra operation. Sīyuṭ's initial s stays dental, while singular
suṭ-s after ī becomes ṣ and gives ṣṭ/ṣṭh. Dual suṭ remains st/sth.
Do not apply dā's P-only de-yāt route to these A dāsīṣṭa words.

All selected A liṭ endings are apit and both roots are non-saṃyogānta,
so **1.2.5** supplies kit. Root-facing iṭ is only H05's four slots.

For dāṇ, 6.1.8 with **7.4.59** gives **da|dā**; the copied ā is
shortened while original root ā keeps its own owner. **6.4.64** deletes
the original ā before the actual vowel-initial kit ending or iṭ. Thus

\[
(dade,dadāte,dadire,dadiṣe,dadāthe,dadidhve,dade,dadivahe,dadimahe).
\]

The copy survives; there is no present ślu/reduplicated vikaraṇa here.
For yam, retain **ya|yam** after 6.1.8/7.4.60. **6.4.120** replaces
internal short a by e and separately deletes the copy, giving **yem**:

\[
(yeme,yemāte,yemire,yemiṣe,yemāthe,yemidhve,yeme,yemivahe,yemimahe).
\]

Kāśikā explicitly illustrates yem in this contraction. The actual vowel-
initial ending/iṭ blocks 6.4.37's jhal requirement, so m is not deleted.
At both perfect 2pl interfaces iṭ follows d or m, neither in iṇ; hence
8.3.79 gives no retroflex alternative. Irec's i/r and the equal 3sg/1sg
e readouts retain their distinct ending provenance.

## H09. A sic: ghu root-i versus yam's optional nasal lopa

Cli→sic under 3.1.43–44 uses PG32's owned enunciative-i/c-marker
account. No other cli substitution premise is selected. P-only 2.4.77,
7.2.1 and 7.2.73 cannot supply their luk/vṛddhi/sak/iṭ branches here.

For dāṇ, ghu membership activates **1.2.17**: original ā→**short root i**
and sic becomes kit. This i is not iṭ. Kāśikā/Kaumudī/Bālamanoramā
give adita and its continuation; the explicit short-i prescription is not
replaced by 6.4.66's generic ghu ī. Padamañjarī explains why the coupled
prescription permits the later sic deletion without invalidating root-i.

Before ta/thās, **8.2.27** deletes sic-s after the short root. At 2pl,
8.2.25 deletes s and **8.3.78** gives mandatory ḍh after root i. At
the other six slots sic remains; i conditions ṣ, and no A ending īṭ
is supplied. The unaugmented tuple is

\[
(dita,diṣātām,diṣata,dithāḥ,diṣāthām,diḍhvam,diṣi,diṣvahi,diṣmahi).
\]

For yam in the selected marriage domain, **1.2.16** gives exactly two
sic histories. Kit permits **6.4.37** to delete the original anudātta
root's final m before jhal-initial sic; the non-kit history retains m.
These branches are selected before the following Tripādī operations:

\[
Y_K=(yata,yasātām,yasata,yathāḥ,yasāthām,yadhvam,yasi,yasvahi,yasmahi),
\]
\[
Y_N=(yaṃsta,yaṃsātām,yaṃsata,yaṃsthāḥ,yaṃsāthām,
yandhvam,yaṃsi,yaṃsvahi,yaṃsmahi).
\]

In K, 8.2.27 deletes s before ta/thās; in N the root is not short-
vowel-final, so it remains. Both have s-lopa before dhvam under 8.2.25.
N's surviving root m then becomes anusvāra and obligatorily dental n
before dh under 8.4.58: **yandhvam**, not an optional nasal spelling.
K has ya+dhvam. Neither a nor m/n supplies 8.3.78's iṇ condition;
both dh readouts stay dental. Each yam slot has two distinct words,
including 2pl, with no further sound-choice multiplication.
