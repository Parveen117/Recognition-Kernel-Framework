# PG872 · Hanumān recognises Sītā and begins Rāma’s story

### Recognising the hearer before speaking

The two commentators join Bhaṭṭi VIII.102 and 103 as one narrative unit. Jayamaṅgalā explicitly carries the reader forward from VIII.102 to *prāstāvīt*. Mallinātha calls the pair a *yugma*. Hanumān is the son of the wind, Sītā the woman recognised, and the ten-headed king whose coming made Hanumān stay hidden is Rāvaṇa. When the king is absent, Hanumān begins a story of Rāma so that Sītā may understand. Sītā then considers why this figure has approached her. The grammatical subject of understanding changes between the two acts of cognition.

At VIII.103 Mallinātha identifies the Rāma narrative by citing the opening of Hanumān’s speech about King Daśaratha. The printed verse has accusative *rāmasaṃkathām*. Trivedi additionally reports *kathāḥ* for T., G., Com. as the variant at the latter member, without changing the identity of the speaker. Neither apparatus variant supplies a new nominal paradigm.

### Which augment survives in *prāstāvīd rāmasaṃkathām*?

Both editions print a voiced stop before the next word. The lexical base is adādi **02.0038 ṣṭuñ stutau**, ubhayapada, transitive and lexically aniṭ. With pra the commentaries explain the beginning of the narration by *prārabhata* or equivalent. This is the ordinary augmented kartari luṅ, parasmaipada, on the witnessed reading. In the nine addresses below the speaker may vary in person and number while retaining that reading and pada. The word in the poem supplies third singular alone.

1. On the finite base select cli and replace it by sic (3.1.43–44). The exceptional 7.2.72 supplies **iṭ to sic** for this root when the following endings are parasmaipada. Kāśikā contrasts the middle *astoṣṭa*. The same lexical base cannot propagate the active iṭ decision across padas.
2. Before this sic, 7.2.1 gives vṛddhi of root-final u. The resulting au before i gives āv by 6.1.78. Prefix pra and the stem’s initial past augment aṭ (6.4.71) coalesce to prā under 6.1.101. The two long ā vowels in *prāstāviṣṭām* thus have different derivational owners.
3. In the third and second singular the one-consonant ending receives **īṭ** by 7.3.96. Sic s between iṭ and īṭ falls by 8.2.28, and the like vowels coalesce. The other seven addresses retain sic with ṣ after i and the appropriate dental-to-retroflex contact before t. The three short i/long ī decisions here distinguish the root-side iṭ, ending-side īṭ, and realized vowel after deletion.
4. At the third singular 8.2.39 and optional devoicing at pause under 8.4.56 supply the retained voiced and the unvoiced citation readings. Before the voiced r of the next word the poem gives *prāstāvīd*. In the selected citation policy that joined token corroborates the voiced branch. It is not a third reading.
