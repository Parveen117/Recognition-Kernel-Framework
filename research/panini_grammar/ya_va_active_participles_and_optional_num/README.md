# PG264 · The traveller and the wind, with and without the participial nasal

Monty · 12 November 2026

Kṣīrasvāmin gives both *yātī, yāntī* for the traveller and *vātī, vāntī* for the root of moving or bearing scent. Kāśikā 7.1.80 illustrates the two environments with *yātī/yāntī kule* (two families, neuter dual) and *yātī/yāntī brāhmaṇī* (one woman, feminine singular). The Mādhavīya independently records the pair in the opposite order.

| Environment | From *yā* | From *vā* |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Masculine nominative singular / dual | yān / yāntau | vān / vāntau |
| Feminine nominative singular | yātī or yāntī | vātī or vāntī |
| Feminine genitive singular | yātyāḥ or yāntyāḥ | vātyāḥ or vāntyāḥ |
| Neuter nominative or accusative dual | yātī or yāntī | vātī or vāntī |
| Neuter nominative or accusative plural | yānti | vānti |
| Masculine or neuter locative singular | yāti | vāti |

One optional nasal gives two complete feminine stem histories. In the neuter it gives a choice in the nominative, accusative and vocative dual. The nominative/accusative/vocative singular has the archive’s final *t/d* readings; the plural has obligatory *n*. Each root contributes 72 gendered positions and 102 readings. The class-two finite present *yāti/yānti* or *vāti/vānti* can therefore share its spelling with an inflected participle. Both spellings also occur in feminine vocative singular, one for each stem. Agreement and the role of the word in its clause decide between them.
