## Affix and relation distinctions

**3.2.56:** च्व्यर्थ requires the prior state and transformation to be intended,
not simply the occurrence of a new quality. ख्युन् denotes the instrument;
आढ्यादि expressions are the object उपपद, not the instrument. In the excluded
च्वि construction, Kāśikā and Nyāsa also exclude fallback ल्युट् by the force
of the prohibition. Kaumudī explicitly reports a Bhāṣya policy that allows
ल्युट् and takes अच्वौ as serving the following rule. Keep these policies
distinct. A request lacking the decisive policy remains Open on that branch.

**3.2.58–61:** nominal उपपद does not always mean an object. मन्त्रस्पृक् and
जलस्पृक् show instrument relations; ऋत्विज् and the other 59 words have
their own stipulated relations and operations. क्विन्, क्विप्, ण्वि and
विट् must retain their original marker identities even when their visible
suffixes disappear. The selected सू and विद् root entries at 61 are narrower
than an untyped spelling match.

**3.2.66:** Nyāsa explicitly defines अन्तः as मध्य, “within/middle.” The
expression is **not within the ṛk-pāda**, rather than “not at its end.”
Kaumudī and Vasu confirm the distinction: अग्निश्च हव्यवाहनः illustrates
ञ्युट्, whereas the internal हव्यवाट् in हव्यवाडग्निरजरः… follows 64's
ण्वि. A scheduler needs the metrical location, not just the word हव्य.

**3.2.69:** the extra rule blocks अण् for the raw-flesh क्रव्य–अद् relation.
Kāśikā and Kaumudī separately explain क्रव्यादः from a prepared/cooked-meat
expression with a lexical replacement. Their exact wording differs; the
two routes are not merged solely because the surface strings are similar.

**3.2.75–76:** दृश्यते makes usage evidence relevant to the extended affix
family. Kaumudī explicitly carries that qualification into क्विप्. An archive
of examples is not permission to produce every affix from every root.

## Meaning and restrictive clauses

The control 3.2.78 concerns habitual disposition. The next clauses separately
admit actor-simile, scripturally stipulated vow and repeated engagement.
3.2.82 selects मन्यते, not मनुते; 83's खश् branch additionally requires
self-appraisal. Unknown discourse or actor identity does not satisfy those cuts.

**3.2.87:** Kāśikā and Nyāsa describe four directions of restriction;
Kaumudī explicitly reports the Bhāṣya's twofold restriction as another policy.
Nyāsa calls the restriction प्रायिक, gives exceptions and explicitly preserves
the later निष्ठा construction ब्रह्माणं हतवान्. Do not interpret क्विबेव
as deletion of every alternative expression across the grammar.

**3.2.89:** the technical names of the three niyama directions are easy to
misread. Nyāsa spells out the consequences: the relevant past कृ construction
takes क्विप्, another root such as अधी does not get that branch, and the
present/future reading does not get it. **Other उपपद are still admitted** by
the general क्विप् route, as shown by शास्त्रकृत्/भाष्यकृत्. Kaumudī confirms
those consequences. सु is not treated as a substantive object.

**3.2.92:** the conventional altar formation denotes the piled object/site,
unlike 91's actor. **3.2.95:** युध् becomes transitive here through the
included causative meaning; **96** does not force सह into an object slot.

## Time and affix ownership

**3.2.108:** Nyāsa resolves the apparent optional-substitution ambiguity.
The special inferred लिट् is admitted together with **mandatory क्वसु**;
when that branch is not selected, independently licensed लुङ्/लङ्/लिट्
remain. It does not manufacture an unrestricted finite perfect in every past
environment. Kaumudī states this coupled interpretation explicitly.

**3.2.109:** each stipulation has its own operations. उप is not indispensable
to the इण् construction, but the exceptional इट् is a restoration of the
proper blocked augment, not permission to insert it in उपेयुषः/उपेयुषा.
नञ् + अश् has its own इट् absence; अनु + वच् has an actor कानच् clause.

**3.2.112–114:** लृट् can express remembered non-today past. 113's trigger
is **यत्**, not the conditional यदि. The linked लक्ष्य/लक्षण expectancy in
114 admits the option with or without यत्. Ordinary memory alone does not
supply that relation. **120–122** distinguish the single ननु, separate न/नु,
and पुरा without स्म. Temporal reference and morphological tense remain separate.

The Kāśikā control **3.2.111** contains a local `लुङ्` in its explanatory
sentence despite the लङ् heading and अकरोत्/अहरत् examples. The control is
read with its declared लङ् subject; no global numbering or tense substitution
is inferred from that damaged occurrence.

**3.2.127** is a designation, not a rewriting operation. It applies to शतृ
and शानच् themselves, including their future uses. **128–130** use the
direct-affix analysis stated by Kāśikā and defended by Nyāsa: शानन् and
चानश् are not automatically replacements of लट्. This matters for actor
meaning, accent and pada classification. 2.3.69 nevertheless reaches them
through its तृन् range; independent origin does not remove that case restriction.
