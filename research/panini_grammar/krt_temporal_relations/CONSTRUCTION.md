Author: Monty. 4 November 2026.

## C1. Addressed admission and affix origin

For input q, let J(q) contain only clauses whose positive conditions and
decisive exclusions are resolved under its selected source policy. Output
records (q, clause, owned operations, obligations), retaining original token
addresses. Absence of evidence yields Open, not a default lexical membership.

At 3.2.108 the admitted alternative selects लिट् together with its required
क्वसु replacement in that clause. It does not add an unrelated finite लिट्
option. At 3.2.128–130 direct affixes must retain that origin rather than
borrowing every property of a लट् replacement. At 3.2.139 marker policy is
consumer specific: the adopted गित् treatment and a reported कित् analysis
cannot be merged merely because they display the same visible suffix.

The 3.2.186 clauses map by role: ऋषि admits the instrumental analysis;
देवता admits the agent analysis. The selector must not take the Cartesian
product of names and roles. Likewise, every prefix/root/sense list in the
tables denotes the stated relation, not a bag of freely recombinable tokens.

## C2. Habit, purpose and ordinary competition

3.2.134 includes the endpoint क्विप् at 177. Its three meanings are not
synonyms: spontaneous disposition, an adopted practice and skillful performance
provide separate admission witnesses. Their lexical suffix clauses retain local
restrictions. The source's qualified/prāyika exclusion of vāsarūpa does not
justify either a universal ban or unconditional restoration of every suffix.

For purposeful future action, retain two actions A and B and evidence that
B is undertaken for A. 3.3.10 admits its specified तुमुन्/ण्वुल् choices;
mere simultaneity, incidental consequence or a non-action purpose correlate
does not establish that frame. The following restorations have their own gates:

|Clause|Owned result|Boundary|
|---|---|---|
|3.3.11|Appropriate action-denoting affix|Keep its original root/sense restrictions; not every भाव suffix on every root|
|3.3.12|अण्|Requires both कर्मन् upapada and purposeful-action context; local precedence is retained|
|3.3.13 control|लृट्|Its admitted future reading differs from recollective-past लृट्|
|3.3.14|सत् replacement|Obligatory in the specified non-nominative/vocative/sign-cause contexts, optional in the admitted nominative frame|

Thus करिष्यन्तं देवदत्तं पश्य and करिष्यन् देवदत्तः do not demonstrate the
same kind of optionality. Direct and replacement histories remain distinct
even if their unaccented visible projections coincide.

## C3. Action nouns and feminine outputs

Select the intended action or non-agent relation before resolving the lexical
घञ्/अप्/अच् and other exceptions. A concrete named meaning is an additional
condition; the occurrence of the same prefix and root outside that meaning
does not automatically inherit the exceptional affix. Preserve accent,
prohibition and optionality with the responsible clause.

The feminine frame requires its own authority and exception relation. Under
3.1.94, the specific feminine affix can block the general one; it is unsafe
to restore the general suffix just because its marker or surface differs.
Examples in the tables are source witnesses. Gender and intended meaning
remain input evidence; no complete nominal declension is counted here.

## C4. Relational time and accompanying predicates

3.4.1 interprets a dependent action relative to its governing action. In
कृतः कटः श्वो भविता, the retained completion relation and future governing
frame coexist. The construction preserves both annotations. It does not turn
all tense labels into freely exchangeable choices. Supplied तद्धित contexts
such as गोमान् आसीत् are included in the source's extension, not generated
by assuming every noun is a verb.

|Context|Owned dependency|
|---|---|
|3.4.2 repetition/intensity|लोट् with its हि/स्व realization and qualified त/ध्वम् alternative; retain pada and finite identity|
|3.4.4|The accompanying predicate uses the same root; लुनीहि does not license replacing लुनाति with छिनत्ति|
|3.4.3 aggregation|Optional aggregate construction, preserving each constituent action|
|3.4.5|The accompanying predicate expresses the common action, e.g. अभ्यवहरति for eating/drinking constituents|
|3.4.6 Vedic relation|Qualified optional लुङ्/लङ्/लिट् across temporal readings; ordinary alternatives remain contextual|
|3.4.7 versus 8, Vedic|Optional लेट् for the general liṅ meaning; mandatory local लेट् for the stated agreement/apprehension contexts|

हि/स्व here does not infer ordinary second-person singular reference: the
source illustrates different persons and numbers. Repetition and aggregation
have different accompanying-predicate conditions. These clauses also differ
from 3.1.149's skilled-performance meaning of समभिहार.

At 3.4.21, earlier action and the same actor are independently required.
भुक्त्वा व्रजति supplies both; two unrelated actors do not. The dual wording
does not cap a sequence at two actions. The source's व्यादाय स्वपिति and
संमील्य हसति extension remains a named exception, not general simultaneity.
The subsequent bounded ktvā/ṇamul clauses preserve the intended manner,
repetition, comparison or other exact relation in their own table rows.

3.4.24 drops the repetition condition inherited by 22 and retains the
specified finite alternatives. Its shared co-prescription remains outside
the 2.2.20 compound route. Conversely, 22's repetition requires the source's
doubled expression; selecting a suffix alone does not provide it. The local
extensions at 18–20 do not erase the ordinary boundary at 21.
