# PG870 · Sītā’s slender waist and the departure of the rākṣasīs

### What Trijaṭā saw and who went to sleep

The 1887 Jayamaṅgalā edition (p. 192) and Trivedi’s Sarvapathīnā edition (p. 295) print the same stanza numbers VIII.100–101. In Trijaṭā’s dream, Sītā is the person seen. A feminine participle describes her touching the sun in the company of the moon. Both commentators explain the sun and moon in terms of Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa. The agent instrumental *mayā* identifies Trijaṭā as the one who saw, and *candramasā* marks the companion with *saha* (2.3.18–19). Neither is the instrumental which qualifies Sītā’s waist. The verse itself supplies the waist and contact; it names no hand as the instrument of touching.

Both commentaries identify the women as the rākṣasīs rebuked by Trijaṭā and explain *suṣupsavaḥ* by the desire to sleep. In the next stanza *gatāsu tāsu* restates their departure in the feminine locative plural. Mallinātha expands this as a circumstance already obtaining when Hanumān acts. That independent agreement controls the gender assigned at PG869.

### A quality adjective with two feminine formations

On the explicitly attested quality ‘slender’, 4.1.44 optionally selects ṅīṣ on short-u *tanu*. With the feminine affix, u before ī gives v under 6.1.77. Without it, feminine gender is already supplied in the sense, and the short-u stem takes sup directly. Jayamaṅgalā prints one branch in the verse and the other as its explanation. Compare *paṭuḥ/paṭvī* in Kāśikā. Its further controls distinguish a species term and bar the option for kharu and a conjunct-upadhā base. This is no rule for replacing every feminine *u* by *vī*. The noun *tanū* ‘body’ has a different lexical meaning and quantity. For the sleepers, retain PG869’s agent u after svap-san; the poem supplies the short-u feminine, and its ps consonant group before u independently obstructs the optional quality-word operation under the stated exception.
