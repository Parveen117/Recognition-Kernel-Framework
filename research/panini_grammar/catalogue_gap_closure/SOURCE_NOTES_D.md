## Meaning, origin and dependent options

**4.1.130–134:** northern attribution at 130 does not erase the previously
licensed गोधा form. 131 carries ढ्रक् from 129, not intervening आरक्.
Kaumudī explicitly locates 133's deletion at the end of पितृष्वसृ before ढक्.
134 transfers both छण् and the separate ढक्-plus-deletion branch to मातृष्वसृ.
The deletion cannot be detached and applied to every branch.

**4.1.140–142:** absence of a preceding *compound member* is the condition in
140. बहुच् is not such a member; the source's बहुकुल्य branch is therefore
allowed. Its three branches differ from the three महाकुल branches of 141 and
the two दुष्कुल branches of 142. Equal-looking suffixes retain their own markers,
grade/accent effects and entry point.

**4.1.144–145 and 161:** the descendant भ्रातृव्य uses व्यत्; the enemy word
uses व्यन् and has no अपत्य meaning. Likewise मानुष/मनुष्य in 161 are
class-denoting words with their own अञ्/यत्-plus-षुक् construction, not an
ordinary descent relation. Surface identity does not authorize inherited
plural गोत्र deletion or genealogical conclusions.

**4.1.147–150:** Kāśikā explicitly describes a four-rule scope pattern:
147 requires the supplied कुत्सन context; 148 and 149 require both that
context and Sauvīra qualification; 150 retains Sauvīra but drops कुत्सन.
वृद्ध in 148 is the grammatical word classification controlled by 1.1.73.
Historical labels and evaluative utterance meanings are supplied textual
inputs, never classifications inferred or endorsed about living people.

**4.1.150 has a real policy difference.** Kāśikā, Nyāsa and Vasu explicitly
allow each of the two affixes after each base: four branches. Kaumudī records
that this is the vṛttikāra's treatment, but reports that the Bhāṣya maintains
यथासंख्यम्, the ordered pairing. Its field nevertheless prints the same four
surface examples. Adopt the explicit Kāśikā/Nyāsa policy as the table default;
retain the reported ordered-pair policy separately. This packet has not read
an independent Bhāṣya manuscript or silently resolved that discrepancy.
Unknown policy remains an open input when branch selection depends on it.

**4.1.151–160:** distinct affix origins matter. The ण्य from कुर्वादि 151 is
not the तद्राज ण्य from 172. Consequently homographic कौरव्य words have
different later फिञ्, इञ् and deletion behavior, as 154 and the reread 2.4.58
explain. वामरथ receives the stated कण्वादि-like operations *except accent*.
153's तक्षन् control retains ण्य although the other list's अण् blocks इञ्.
155 derives from original कोसल/कर्मार with a coupled base shape; it does not
insert an unlicensed extra descent generation. 159's optional कुक् is nested
inside an already optional फिञ् branch, giving three histories. It is not a
free augment on the ordinary इञ् branch. फिन् in 160 remains distinct from
फिञ् despite similar output.

stated same-named ruler extension. Neither the suffix nor the ancient word

**176/177:** 176 addresses specific ञ्यङ्/ण्य affixes with feminine referents.

## 8.3.119: complete local ṣatva review

Read the whole target and the preceding 111–118 prohibition sequence, together
with 55's non-word-final domain and 65's prefix-sensitive positive prescription.
Kāśikā explicitly drops 118's सद्/स्वञ्ज् restriction at 119 and adds स्तु
examples. The clause requires actual नि/वि/अभि prefix relation, intervening
अट् and Vedic register. Its optional prohibition retains dental स or allows
the otherwise licensed ष. Kaumudī/Vasu phrase the resulting optional ष
positively; this does not create retroflexion where no ordinary trigger exists.

The operation is source- and token-addressed: deleting a prefix connection,
ignoring the augment or extending Vedic optionality to ordinary prose would
change the contract. 116's distinction between prefix-origin and abhyāsa-origin
retroflexion demonstrates why unrelated operations must not be globally erased.
