## Suffix identity survives identical spelling

**4.1.26:** The literal Kāśikā body says ङीष् in one sentence, but its own
heading and opening prescribe ङीप् over ङीष्. Kaumudī identifies the exception,
and Nyāsa explicitly gives ङीप् in its explanation. Adopt **ङीप्**, preserving
the conflicting literal label rather than silently repairing the archive.
आदि also permits intervening material in द्विविधोध्नी; it is not a demand
that the numeral immediately precede ऊधस्.

**4.1.60–61:** Direction-first formations receive **ङीप् only where the
preceding body-part rules permit ङीष्**. Both optionality and prohibitions
survive. Nyāsa explains why declining ङीप् does not reopen the competing
ङीष् branch; the result is प्राङ्मुखी or प्राङ्मुखा. The next rule 61
expressly returns to **ङीष्**, confirmed by Kāśikā and Kaumudī.

The pinned Nyāsa 60 field has a continuation discussing वाह/ऊठ् after its
own explanation and includes a misaligned numerical reference. Nyāsa 61 is
empty. Preserve this field arrangement literally; no missing 61 field is
invented, and the adopted 61 contract relies on Kāśikā/Kaumudī. Its placement
does not turn वाह into part of the direction-first rule.

**4.1.66:** ऊङ् identity matters even when an already-long-ऊ base has the
same surface vowel. Kaumudī/Nyāsa explain the consequence through the
6.1.175 accent consumer in अलाब्वा etc. Neither visible length nor identical
nominative spelling identifies whether ऊङ् was prescribed.
