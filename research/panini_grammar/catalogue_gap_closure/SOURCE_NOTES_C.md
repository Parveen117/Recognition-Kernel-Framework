## Affix identities and source correction

even when all produce an unaccented final ई. At 78, Kāśikā gives the
अञ्-derived आर्तभागी as a negative control for replacing अण्/इञ् with ष्यङ्,

The contract adopts that correction with attribution. The literal Kāśikā
field remains archived unchanged; its main rule is not discarded because
one explanatory suffix label is defective.

Nyāsa also confirms that 78 replaces the **specified अण्/इञ् token**, not the
whole base and not a new independent suffix appended after it. The base's
heavy-penultimate condition and the affix's non-ऋषि/गोत्र provenance are
different addressed predicates. Its discussion of उडुलोम requires the
proper earlier टि deletion before the resulting heavy-penultimate check;
the compact table is not a claim to execute that entire sequence. 74 owns
the subsequent चाप्. The rules for अकङ् and इनङ् at 97 and 126–127 likewise
retain final-element addressing under 1.1.53 rather than overwriting an

## Deletion cause, prospective domain and source policies

**4.1.88.** The deleted taddhita must be the semantic reason the द्विगु was
formed. A taddhita subsequently added to an already completed द्विगु does
not qualify. Thus पञ्चकपाल and पाञ्चकपाल retain different histories.
Kāśikā and Nyāsa use the inherited वा as a **व्यवस्थितविभाषा**, with deletion
in the stated पञ्चकपाल class and non-deletion for रूप्य/मयट्. Kaumudī's
compressed formulation instead states an **अजादि** condition. The packet
records the formulations separately and adopts the K/N qualification for
the witnessed branches; it does not infer an unrestricted global option.
Nyāsa's inconsistent embedded 2.1.50/51 references remain literal source
data; no global renumbering is performed.

**4.1.89–91.** A retained गोत्र affix and a deleted युव affix are not the same
target. At 90, अचि describes an **intended, not yet realized continuation**.
The युव suffix is removed first; the next suffix is then selected from the
exposed base. For example, a वृद्ध status acquired only through the removed
suffix cannot remain an unqualified premise for छ selection. At 91 the
फक्/फिञ् branches can retain or delete the addressed युव affix, and each
branch owns its subsequent formation. This is a source-qualified derivation
plan, not a promise of a general termination or look-ahead algorithm.

## Descent labels and genealogy evidence

**4.1.93–94.** The one-suffix restriction for गोत्र prevents iterating a
new descent affix for each generation. Kāśikā offers equivalent suffix-
and base-restriction explanations at 93. At 94, Kāśikā and Nyāsa explicitly
split the rule so that feminine reference blocks the **युव designation**.
It does not merely remove the restriction on which base may receive a युव
suffix; that reading would admit precisely the unwanted derivations.
Kaumudī states the same feminine boundary succinctly.

The terminology is relational. 162 defines गोत्र relative to the ancestor;
163's living-वंश्य condition and its source interpretation of generations
must be supplied. A person's apparent age or modern family name is not a
substitute for this evidence. The special contextual annotations ऋषि,
आङ्गिरस, कौशिक, वत्स and other historical classes are properties of a
supplied grammatical reading, not inferred identities of living people.

**4.1.100–101 and 110.** An already descent-affixed listed input cannot
receive a second गोत्र suffix merely because it appears under the गोत्र
authority. Kāśikā admits a युव interpretation where required by 93. At 101,
the यञ्/इञ् must themselves have been prescribed for गोत्र; phonologically
identical non-descent affixes from द्वीप and सुतङ्गमादि do not qualify.

**4.1.108.** Kāśikā initially gives वातण्ड outside the आङ्गिरस gate, then
explicitly admits **both वातण्ड्य and वातण्ड** outside that gate because of
dual gaṇa membership. Kaumudī confirms this. The negative example is not
silently promoted into an exclusive prohibition of यञ्.

## Names, morphology and limited alternatives

**4.1.113 and 120–122.** Name status, technical वृद्ध designation, feminine
affix provenance, vowel count and इञ् provenance are separate predicates.
120 requires a qualifying **feminine-affix-ended base**, not semantic
female reference alone. Nyāsa explicitly uses विमातृ's separate gaṇa entry
as evidence for that distinction. At 122 the feminine restriction has ended,
while the two-vowel and non-इञ् restrictions remain.

**4.1.117.** Kāśikā accepts both शुङ्ग and शुङ्गा textual/base readings;
Kaumudī reports the same. The matching भरद्वाज clause selects अण्, but the
outside-case control differs: शौङ्गि for one base policy and शौङ्गेय for
the feminine-affixed base policy. Preserve the base reading before resolving
the suffix. Three ordered ancestry pairs are not a Cartesian product.

**4.1.119 and 127.** मण्डूक has three recorded alternatives, ढक्/अण्/इञ्.
At 127 only **इनङ् substitution** is optional; ढक् is already available
from 120. Kaumudī explicitly distinguishes the non-pejorative itinerant
meaning of कुलटा from the derogatory sense considered by 131. Those are
source lexical labels with supplied evidence, not a judgment about a person.

**4.1.128.** Parent-base gender and the offspring's feminine reference are
different coordinates. The reported चटक extension and feminine-offspring
लुक् preserve that distinction. The ensuing feminine realization has its
own inherited owner and is not counted as a new paradigm.
