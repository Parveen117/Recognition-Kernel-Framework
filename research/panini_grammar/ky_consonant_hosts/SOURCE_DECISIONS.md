# Source and admission conditions

## H01. Self-directed acquisition of the nominal object

3.1.8 licenses optional kyac when the nominal is the object wished for and the recipient is the wisher. The option is between a derived word and an analytic expression. A desire for someone else to receive kindling fails this certificate. The desiderative of an action uses a different input. Kāśikā 6.4.50 names the desire to obtain samidh and supplies both samidh and dṛṣad pairs. The same desiderative nominal role for dṛṣad is an explicitly qualified application.

## H02. Imitation by the subject

3.1.11 inherits the behavioral comparison and requires the standard to occupy the agent role. The finite subject behaves like the named standard. The source's samid iva is the comparison phrase. Its external word-boundary alternation does not change the stored nominal samidh. Predicate, standard, actor and outer agreement are separate fields.

## H03. The behavior of an object is a different request

3.1.10 addresses treating an object like the nominal standard, as in treating a pupil like a son. A bare English gloss “act like” cannot select between the two rules. This packet admits the 3.1.8 and 3.1.11 relations only. It gives zero credit for relabeling forms under 3.1.10.

## H04. Markers and the embedded nominal boundary

In the selected kartari domains the ṅ marker of kyaṅ selects A under 1.3.12. Kyac goes to residual P under 1.3.78. Under the restriction 1.4.15 it is the n-final nominal that receives the special padatva before ky. Both witnesses end in a stop. Do not substitute either the isolated nominative or the surface comparison phrase for the underlying nominal input.

## H05. Explicit y-option and the guṇa blocker

Kāśikā 6.4.50 puts both samidhyitā/samidhitā and dṛṣadyitā/dṛṣaditā on record. The Kaumudī and Bālamanoramā read the conditioned operation as initial loss on the following ky formation, with 6.4.48 removing its a and the local sthānivad condition protecting the former upadhā against guṇa in samidhita-type derivation. Store this bridge rather than inferring a free deletion wherever the spelling contains y. The agent-noun-shaped witness supports the stem boundary used by luṭ; the entire conjugation is a written rule inference.
