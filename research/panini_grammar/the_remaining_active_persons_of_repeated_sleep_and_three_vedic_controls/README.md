# PG861 · The active persons of repeated sleep and the three Vedic controls for īṭ

Mādhavīya p. 339 fixes the unprefixed agentive present of repeated sleeping with yaṅ deleted. It prints all three third-person positions and says that ordinary guṇa applies when the ending has p. This statement and the markers of the active endings determine which of the remaining persons can receive long īṭ.

| Unprefixed kartari laṭ | Singular | Dual | Plural |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Third, printed and closed at PG857 | śeśayīti / śeśeti | śeśītaḥ | śeśyati |
| Second, derived here | śeśayīṣi / śeśeṣi | śeśīthaḥ | śeśītha |
| First, derived here | śeśayīmi / śeśemi | śeśīvaḥ | śeśīmaḥ |

**Strong singulars.** Tip, sip and mip retain their p marker as a grammatical condition after its disappearance. All are consonant-initial sārvadhātuka endings. In the derived stem śeśī the final ī receives guṇa by 7.3.84. On the 7.3.94 branch long īṭ intervenes, and 6.1.78 changes e to ay before that vowel. Without īṭ the e remains before the consonant. The s of sip becomes ṣ after either ī or e under 8.3.59. Mip has no sibilant. Thus the two paths distinguish śeśayīṣi from śeśeṣi and śeśayīmi from śeśemi. There is no short iṭ alternative in these pairs.

**Weak duals and plurals.** Thas, tha, vas and mas lack the p condition. Under 1.2.4 their sārvadhātuka grade behaves as ṅit; root-final ī therefore remains before th, v or m. The final s of the second and first dual and of the first plural yields the citation-form visarga. In the already printed third plural only, at replaces the jh of jhi after the reduplicated stem. Before that vowel 6.4.82 gives śeśyati. The ordinary agent of this unprefixed history takes active endings. The prefixed vyatiśeśyate in Kāśikā 7.1.6 is a separate middle third plural, already a PG40 example. The wording there tests the absence of ruṭ when the marked simple root has been replaced by a derived input. It does not identify the participants or a scene for a literary quotation. Mādhavīya separately prints the unprefixed impersonal third singular śeśīyate with an instrumental. Preserve all three endings and their own roles.

### The Kāśikā's positive and negative controls

The three Ṛgvedic loci actually cited at 7.3.94 are recoverable in the Śākala word text. The witness inherited by the grammar and the continuous verse have separate source addresses. Each example is a third singular and belongs to its own lexical root.

| Locus | Grammatical word | Subject and scope in the verse |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 6.47.31 | vāvadīti, augmented | The drum, dundubhiḥ, sounds. The second-singular imperative pratyāvartaya elsewhere in the stanza addresses a participant and has a different ending and root. |
| 4.58.3 | roravīti, augmented | The thrice-bound bull, vṛṣabhaḥ, roars. The other finite predicate about entering mortals is distinct. The morphology field labels the roar root rū. |
| 1.164.11 | várvarti, unaugmented | The twelve-spoked wheel, cakram, keeps turning. Its neuter nominative singular owns the singular agreement. Sāyaṇa explicitly reads repeated turning. The many paired children mentioned in the next line do not license a plural ending on várvarti. |

This evidence verifies the commentary's use of the long augment and its absence in other roots. For śī specifically the source of both third-singular words is Mādhavīya. The six other cells in the table are a derivation from the named conditions; they are not quoted as Vedic forms. Since the three Vedic words already appear as controls in the earlier 7.3.94 source register, their finite credit is zero.

**Credit and register.** The full selected active present has nine positions and twelve readings. PG857 accounts for three and four, leaving **six new finite positions and eight readings**. Two new source-to-clause connections with īṭ and one without it give three interfaces of one syntax comparison. The morphological completion has six new interfaces. No nominal positions or readings are added. The unprefixed agentive present of śī under san, retained yaṅ and yaṅ-luk is now closed for the selected senses, in addition to the one attested yaṅ-luk bhāva cell and the two selected past participles. Other eligible lakāras and prefixed/role conditions, further C2–C5 domains, and [the specific S1–S7 source obligations](../december_2018_api_exports_at_dhi_ca_and_anadyatana/README.md#current-obligations) remain open. PG114's conscious causative and the PG335, PG340/346, PG349–352 and PG355–363 results retain their earlier credits. Claude retains execution.

**Typed zero-specialization.** Lift each step to the PG14 paired modules over its involutive operator-history algebra. Label input h by root, source, role, marker and deletion history of yaṅ, person, number and original ending. Optional insertion has two distinct targets if the surviving test sees pit, a consonant onset and a sārvadhātuka affix. For each admitted edge h → k define e_h b ↦ e_k(1+z_j)b. Augmenting the output gives e_k ε(b), the result of the classical edge on the augmented input. On a weak grade, the augmenting channel is zero and the unaugmented path remains. This commutation holds under ordered composition and the involution-compatible pairing. The constant section lifts both strong readings and the unique weak reading on their typed termini.
