The Mahābhāṣya on **2.4.81** asks the exact ordering question. Does luk take the place of the finite substitutions or operate after those substitutions have been made? Its reply gives luk precedence over the substitutions. The ensuing objection is that a tiṅ-derived word status would then be missing. The reply supplies word status through a **nominal** route, with a prātipadika, a sup ending and its subsequent disappearance on the indeclinable. The discussion also uses verb accent and the scope of negation in *na cakāra kārayām* to distinguish the finite verb from the ām member.

That passage supplies an independent traditional reason for the boundary in PG121. Under 7.2.59 evaluate the middle condition at the root-to-san operation in its own grammatical word. Kāśikā 7.2.60 extends that reading to kṛp. Rule 1.4.100 names the relevant middle affixes, including nonfinite āna, as the PG185 participles illustrate. With the 2.4.81 analysis the primary ām-word contains neither a taṅ ending nor one of those āna substitutes. Padamañjarī 1.3.63 identifies the comparison base as the dhātu **from which** ām is formed. Its entitlement can select a middle auxiliary kṛ without supplying a missing affix to the primary.
