# PG33 — Owned sya and conditional endings in bhāva/karma

Author: Monty. 4 November 2026.

## C1. The actual affix and the partial ciṇvat transport

Rule 3.1.33 supplies sya for lṛṅ as for lṛṭ: Kāśikā explicitly
illustrates kariṣyati/akariṣyat. Sya is ārdhadhātuka (3.4.114),
while the following tiṅ is separately sārvadhātuka (3.4.113).
No passive-present yak is inserted before sya. A finite ending's
ṅit-like status does not make the intervening sya kit.

Extend PG18 V2's actual-affix domain from future sya to conditional
sya. Rule 6.4.62 names sya, and its Kāśikā explicitly gives conditional
ci/dā/han/grah/dṛś and causal śam examples. Eligibility still requires
Bhāva/Karma and either the original vowel-final witness or named
han/grah/dṛś. For a causal formation, owned ṇic supplies that vowel
witness; a deleted realization does not erase its teaching identity.

When eligible, choose ordinary O or ciṇvat C. C couples its transported
aṅga operations with exactly one i_cv; it does not independently offer
ciṇvat without that augment. Its priority excludes a duplicate ordinary
i_val. When ineligible, only the ordinary relation is available. Unknown
eligibility stays unresolved rather than becoming grammatical variation.

Only PG18 V4's justified aṅga effects transport: final-vowel/a-upadhā
strengthening, eligible yuk, han's h→gh and causal mit length choice.
The actual affix stays sya. Neither chapter-2 aorist root replacement
nor following-ending ciṇ-luk (6.4.104) transports. Conditional han is
therefore not converted into an unlicensed vadh aorist pipeline.

## C2. Ordinary basic branches and the expression-dependent gam guard

Retain PG18 R's guards on the new sya occurrence:

| Roots | Ordinary iṭ | Root-facing readout before endings |
|---|---|---|
|bhū, edh|7.2.35|bhaviṣya, edhiṣya|
|kṛ, hṛ, han|7.2.70, independent of P/A here|kariṣya, hariṣya, haniṣya|
|grah|7.2.35 then 7.2.37|grahīṣya|
|nṛt|7.2.57, two admitted choices|nartiṣya / nartsya|
|sthā, dā, pā|none|sthāsya, dāsya, pāsya|
|nī, ci|none; root guṇa|neṣya, ceṣya|
|dṛś|none; am and late consonant changes|drakṣya|
|pac, labh|none; inherited late junctions|pakṣya, lapsya|
|gam|none on this A branch|gaṃsya|

Basic gam's P-conditioned 7.2.58 is unavailable in Karma A. Hence
**agaṃsyata**, not an automatic copy of kartari **agamiṣyat** with
an A ending. The root m takes 8.3.24 anusvāra before s; it remains
ṃ there. This basic root is neither vowel-final nor one of the three
named 6.4.62 exceptions, so it has no C branch.

Nṛt's 7.2.57 is not restricted to P. Its two Bhāva conditionals are
anartiṣyata/anartsyata. The source/meaning restrictions of the exact
entries remain those of PG18, including one-object ci and drinking pā.

## C3. Ciṇvat basic branches retain their own owners

The C readouts are bhāviṣya, sthāyiṣya, kāriṣya, hāriṣya,
nāyiṣya, dāyiṣya, pāyiṣya, cāyiṣya, ghāniṣya, grāhiṣya,
darśiṣya on the eligible basic entries. They retain the PG18 derivations:

- Vṛddhi and ayādi give bhāv/kār/hār/nāy/cāy. The y in nāy/cāy
  belongs to āy, while original ā-final sthā/dā/pā receive yuk (7.3.33).
- Han has h→gh (7.3.54) and a-upadhā vṛddhi (7.2.116), retaining n
  under the ciṇ-model restriction. It gives aghāniṣyata, not aghātiṣyata.
- Grah's O augment is long by 7.2.37; C's augment remains short and
  the root has ā: **agrahīṣyata / agrāhiṣyata**.
- Dṛś O has am (6.1.58), ṛ→r and the 8.2.36/41 junction, giving
  adrakṣyata. C's intervening i_cv defeats the jhal-initial am guard;
  ordinary upadhā-guṇa gives **adarśiṣyata**, not an invented vṛddhi.

Edh/nṛt and pac/labh/gam lack C eligibility on these basic frames.
Bhāva restriction is imposed after branch selection without erasing
its provenance: abhāviṣyata has a basic Bhāva history distinct from
the same spelling in causal Karma.

## C4. Causal ṇic and its source-relative lopa query

Start at the inherited owned state R_root|i_ṇic, not at a printed
active word with its ending removed. All thirteen causal formations
are eligible for 6.4.62. Their two ordinary construction paths are

\[
O:\ R_{root}|i_{ṇic}|i_{val}|sya
 \longrightarrow R_{root}|ay_{ṇic}|i_{val}|sya,
\]
\[
C:\ R_{root}|i_{ṇic}|i_{cv}|sya
 \longrightarrow R_{root}|\varnothing_{ṇic}|i_{cv}|sya.
\]

On O, visible i_val defeats 6.4.51's aniṭ guard; 7.3.84 and 6.1.78
produce ṇic-owned ay. On C, the source-qualified 6.4.22 view hides
the ābhīya i_cv from that lopa query, and ṇic is deleted. The augment
remains realized and later licenses ṣatva. Its invisibility to one query
is not deletion from the word. The root's prior causal strengthening
and puk/yuk remain; do not start another unrestricted vṛddhi cycle.

Thus causal sthā has asthāpayiṣyata/asthāpiṣyata, retaining puk-p;
basic sthā Bhāva has asthāsyata/asthāyiṣyata with its different yuk.
Causal nī has anāyayiṣyata/anāyiṣyata. Causal pā's inherited yuk
likewise survives in apāyayiṣyata/apāyiṣyata.

For causal gam and śam, C additionally admits short/long under 6.4.93:
agamayiṣyata/agamiṣyata/agāmiṣyata and
aśamayiṣyata/aśamiṣyata/aśāmiṣyata. O does not get a fourth long
branch. These are dependent options, not {O,C} × {short,long}.
The causal role/controller choice remains independent of these surface
alternatives under the fixed input domain.

## C5. Initial augmentation is a separate event

All new inputs except basic edh are consonant-initial. They receive
aṭ under 6.4.71, with initial placement under 1.1.46. Edh receives
āṭ under 6.4.72, and 6.1.90 gives aidh: **aidhiṣyata**.
This initial augmentation does not change original-final-vowel evidence,
iṭ origin or the earlier ṇic-lopa query. The retained source-relative
schedule constructs inner morphology, records augmentation and then
realizes its vowel contact; it claims no universal commutation theorem.

## C6. Reconstruct the nine A endings

Let C be the augmented sya factor with its final thematic a omitted.
Original A members are ta, ātām, jha; thās, āthām, dhvam; iṭ, vahi,
mahiṅ (3.4.78). Lṛṅ is not ṭit, so present/future substitutions
3.4.79–80 are unavailable. PG31 E3 supplies the actual paths:

|Slot|Operation|Word|
|---|---|---|
|3sg|ta retained|Cata|
|3du|ā→iy (7.2.81), y-lopa (6.1.66), a+i→e (6.1.87)|Cetām|
|3pl|jh→ant (7.1.3), a+a→a (6.1.97)|Canta|
|2sg|thās retained; final s→ru→ḥ at pause|Cathāḥ|
|2du|same owned ā→iy and junction before thām|Cethām|
|2pl|dhvam retained|Cadhvam|
|1sg|ending iṭ→i; a+i→e|Ce|
|1du|a→ā before vahi (7.3.101)|Cāvahi|
|1pl|a→ā before mahi; marker removal|Cāmahi|

Final m is retained by 1.3.4. The ending-facing short a establishes
the dual/jha environment; no root-final spelling substitutes for that
query. In 3pl the inherited nasal readout restores dental n before t.
The 8.3.78–79 dh/ḍh rule does not include lṛṅ; all these 2pl words
therefore retain dh. A1sg's final e comes from the original i ending,
not ṭit substitution. None of these cases imports sic or sīyuṭ.

## C7. Bounded exhaustion

For a completed admitted D input, C2–C4 exhaust its inner branches.
C5 supplies one initial augment route, and C6 supplies one word per
admitted slot. D restricts four Bhāva profiles to 3sg; the other 25
profiles each admit nine slots across frames. The explicit register
therefore supplies 229 positions and 448 readings, with no extra
role-witness or contextual duplicate counted. This is written relative
exhaustion under the declared source policy, not an executed validation.
