# PG33 — Expression and agreement before conditional morphology

Author: Monty. 4 November 2026.

## D1. Retained input

Begin on N1's full history carrier. Each input retains the exact root and
sense, formation, intended event and participants, source-labelled kāraka
assignments, expression type, discourse controller, PG31 conditional-admission
certificate, commentary policy and phonetic policy. A causal input retains
its owned ṇic and the distinct base event, causee and causer.

The new domain is classical, unprefixed and unaccented, with the inherited
non-doubling and devoiced-pause reading. It excludes karmakartṛ, reciprocal
and nested causal constructions, san/yaṅ, coordinated mixed-person controllers,
special person exchange, and unrestricted sentence sandhi. Nominal case
realizations remain supplied interfaces; this packet constructs the verb.

## D2. Admit lṛṅ on the interpreted event

Require a resolved R-admission certificate under PG31's 3.3.139–141 and
its explicitly admitted contextual cases. Preserve the effective liṅ-nimitta,
the kriyātipatti witness, intended time relative to utterance, and every
override/source decision. Absence of an observed occurrence is not that
witness. PG31's past optionality and selected exclusive boundaries remain
unchanged; selecting R here does not delete their other permitted requests.

The certificate is evaluated on this input's actual event and expression
frame. Do not copy a certificate from an unrelated active sentence merely
because its verb has the same root. A change of expression/controller can
transport a contextual predicate only where its addressed event, participants
and all relevant semantic premises remain unchanged. Unreviewed intersections
remain Open. This is a morphology construction for supplied admitted R
requests, not a parser or a proof that every PG31 context freely changes voice.

The temporal meaning remains counterfactual/conditional as admitted. Initial
aṭ/āṭ is required by lṛṅ morphology even for future reference. Māṅ and its
augment suppression are outside this domain. An inferred ordinary past
reading is not substituted for the supplied certificate.

## D4. Causal expression is a dependent relation

Carry PG15 K1–K5 and PG18 D5's thirteen causal profiles unchanged in
their role premises: bhū, kṛ, nī, hṛ, pac, gam, sthā, dā, pā, vid
(1020059), budh (1040068), kṛṣ (1060006), śam (1040098, upaśama).
The bhū/sthā/śam causees are conscious under the inherited domain.

First select the source-qualified causee status χ, then an eligible
expressed karman q. In the two-karman cognition/ingestion cases the
inherited policy permits either; gam and the kṛ/hṛ causee-karman branch
express the causee. The kṛ/hṛ retained-agent branch expresses the base
karman. These choices cannot be multiplied as an unrestricted product.

Every admitted derived causal event is transitive, including causal bhū,
sthā and śam. Thus it has Karma(q), A and slot(q), not a basic-root
Bhāva default. Retain the instrumental causer, other instrumental actors
and any unexpressed accusative karman separately. Basic-root transitivity
does not decide the derived event's expression type.

## D5. Counting and domain exhaustion

There are 20 distinct lexical IDs, 16 basic and 13 causal formation
profiles. Four basic Bhāva profiles contribute four positions; twelve
basic Karma profiles contribute 108; thirteen causal Karma profiles
contribute 117. Total: **229**. Branch options and alternative role
witnesses do not multiply these formation/expression/slot positions.

PG31's kartari R profiles are different expression positions even where
the printed word coincides. PG18's lṛṭ/luṭ positions have different
lakāras. None is subtracted or counted again as an identical position.
The new register closes only this explicitly bounded composition.
