# PG782 · Where the case attaches, and which verb carries the history

At 5.2.28 the two grammatical examples predicate a quality of a pair of horns. A bull may then be described by association. Kāśikā explicitly recognizes how conventional such quality terms are. In Bhaṭṭi 2.50 the masculine nominative describes the son of Jamadagni. Vakṣasi supplies the bodily respect. The Jayamaṅgalā glosses his broad chest and explicitly supplies śaṅkaṭac. A different epithet in the verse concerns height relative to a palm tree and has 5.2.37 as its own address. In the Rāmāyaṇa the king and city are names: the masculine founder and the feminine city that he founded. The two cited sarga numberings point to the same city-founding clause.

At 5.2.29 ca inherits vi as the fourth operand alongside sam, pra and ud. Final d before k gives t in utkaṭa. The supplements require more than a prefix or a surface substring. Dust must come from a listed substance. A cattle or buffalo enclosure has an animal as its occupant, whereas a flock and its spread have different suffixes. Goyugac counts two camels or horses, and ṣaḍgavac counts six of the selected animals. Neither changes these animals into cows. Tailac takes the source of an oily product; śākaṭa and śākina require the field in which the crop grows. The selected neuter grammar examples in the panel are positive citation readings. Their -am is syncretic with the accusative, but no second governed slot has been inferred.

In Śakuntalā IV.14 the tree name stands in the genitive plural. The oil is the singular patient of a passive verb, while tvayā supplies its agent and mukhe the location. A genitive outside tailam is positive evidence of that material relation. It leaves the uninterrupted Kāśikā compound and its first-member spelling with the grammar source. In the next prose speech jananyā belongs to a separation relation with vinā. The two instrumentals have different governors and grammatical functions.

For nyaṣicyata start with ni and lexical ṣica (06.0170), in the pouring sense. Initial root ṣ is represented by s under 6.1.64. The passive takes yak under 3.1.67, so its visible stem is sicya. The active-class nasal is not introduced before passive ya. Laṅ supplies aṭ under 6.4.71 and the third singular passive has ta. Before a, the final i of ni gives y by 6.1.77. Rules 8.3.63 and 8.3.65 authorize the retroflex s across the augment on this prefix/root history. This yields ny-a-ṣic-ya-ta. The separate narrative aiti has ā plus strong eti of iṇ 02.0040. Jayamaṅgalā expressly assigns laṭ and the vṛddhi at the prefix boundary: 6.1.89 gives ai. The particle sma and the past gloss belong to the narration. They supply no past augment to aiti.
