# PG36 — Typed selection, measurement and retained deletion

Author: Monty. 4 November 2026.

## C1. Construction order

1. Read the full evidence-bearing [input](DOMAIN.md). Identify the intended
   relation and support case before collecting suffixes.
2. Enter exactly the relevant current scope. Retain earlier scopes in
   history, but do not keep their selectors active across a reset.
3. Collect positively witnessed lexical/structural/register clauses.
   Check every potentially decisive exception. Unknown is Open.
4. Apply the table's source-backed priority, additive, optional and
   restriction relations. An unresolved overlap remains Open; a larger
   sūtra number alone is not a priority proof.
5. Append an owned token for the selected **original affix**. Apply coupled
   base/augment instructions and addressed elision in their stated domain.
6. Return the distinct labelled histories and unresolved obligations.
   Surface realization follows only in its separately completed layer.

The [303-row register](RULE_REGISTER.json) records these written contracts.
`Select`, `Branch`, `Luk`, `Transfer` and `Stipulate` are specification
operations; this packet adds no runtime implementation of them.

## C2. Scope and earlier-rule priority

The pre-kṛta P family is not governed by 4.4's default यत् merely because
4.4.75 says प्राग्घितात्. Rule 5.1.1 supplies छ for the subsequent
benefit/material/sufficiency meanings, with 2–17's exceptions.

In P, 5.1.2 explicitly protects the उ-final सनङ्गु against the leather
अञ् at 15, and चरु/सक्तु against optional यत्/छ at 4. Rule 14 protects
उपानह्→ञ्य even with leather as material. Conversely 4.4.99 expressly
retains its खञ् where its साधु meaning coincides with हित. These are
source-backed earlier priorities; they cannot be reproduced by keeping
the last numbered applicable rule.

Within A, 19 selects ठक् except for गोपुच्छ, number and technical
परिमाण. Rule 20's uncompounded निष्कादि exceptions refine that decision.
A compound of निष्क is not licensed by the uncompounded branch. The
scope of these selectors ends after 71, where Kāśikā explicitly closes
the entire ārhīya family. At 72, ठञ् is the default again.

## C3. Quantity and causal compound structure

Four records are different even if a renderer produces a similar numeral:

|Input|Selection/deletion contract|Reason|

|शत price for buying cloth|5.1.21 ठन् or यत्|The bought object is not essentially the hundred|
|शत as size of a group|22/58 कन्|The target itself consists of the stated hundred|
|Two शूर्प measures buy something; compound is formed for this derivation|26's qualified affix, then 28 लुक्|This affix occasions the द्विगु|
|Already-derived द्विशूर्प is the price in a new purchase|20's tertiary boundary; ordinary ठञ्|The new affix did not occasion the inherited compound; neither automatic अञ् nor 28 लुक्|

The third and fourth rows require a derivation graph with token addresses.
A `starts_with_numeral` flag cannot distinguish them. The first two require
the semantic role and essential-count test, not a regex on शत.

Rule 57 re-enacts its measure construction, preventing ordinary 28-लुक्
in the relevant compound-measure case. The word परिमाण is broader there
than its technical use in 19. Store the relation-qualified measure type,
not one global tag for both senses.

## C4. Dependent alternatives and elision

`Branch(a,b)` creates two labelled selections on an admitted input; it
does not multiply every previous optional operation. Explicit selectors
32–34 survive 28. For 35, यत् survives, while the ordinary alternative
is elided. For 36, the additional अण् also survives.

For 5.1.54's causal द्विगु ending आढक/आचित/पात्र, the permitted
bundle is:

\[
\{(ष्ठन्,\mathrm{retain}),\ (ख,\mathrm{retain}),\
  (ठञ्,\mathrm{luk}_{28})\}.
\]

For कुलिज at 55, add `(ठञ्, retain)`. Do **not** independently toggle
deletion on ष्ठन् and ख. The four histories have their own feminine and
vṛddhi obligations; source words illustrate them but do not discharge those
obligations globally.

For वर्ष at 88 the source instead permits optional लुक् on **both**
ख and ठञ्. There are four affix/elision histories but only three ordinary
unaccented readouts. Under Nyāsa's refinement, rule 89 restricts a sentient
referent **in the भूत relation** to the two deleted histories. A hired
human is not automatically subject to that restriction. This distinction
explains why histories, surface words,
branch readings and sūtra IDs are separate counts.

## C5. Two nested मतुप् tokens

At 4.4.125 the input is a मतुप्-derived descriptor of a mantra used in
placing **bricks**. Select outer यत् and delete the **inner मतुप्** by
लुक्. A mantra for worship rather than placing, a hand used for placing,
or potsherds instead of bricks fails a different gate. The whole mantra
string is not the base selected by तद्वान्.

At 126, अश्विमत् selects outer अण्, retaining the same inner deletion
and its own base-restoration obligation. At 127, the mantra must contain
both वयस् and मूर्धन्; the selected outer affix is मतुप्. Thus the
inner deleted मतुप् and outer retained मतुप् have different addresses.
Deleting every token with the same suffix name destroys the construction.

## C7. Transfers, comparisons and abstract restrictions

Rule 5.1.96 uses a supplied PG35 ExistingAt witness for the same time
base. Copy the qualified affix and authorized associated treatment to
the new GivenAt/DoneAt relation. मास, प्रावृष्, वसन्त and हेमन्त do
not all take one default suffix. The source witness and destination
semantic proof remain separate; no unknown predecessor is invented.

The four comparison/fittingness frames at 115–117 preserve their distinct
cases. The action-only requirement at 115 blocks comparison merely in
colour, size or possession. It does not mechanically constrain 116's
locative/possessive comparisons to an instrumental action frame.

Rule 120 explicitly retains त्व/तल् alongside special abstract affixes.
Rule 121 blocks the following specials for negative तत्पुरुष, subject
to its finite exceptions; it does not block a negative बहुव्रीहि just
because both begin with अ-. At 136, priestly ब्रह्मन् takes त्व and
excludes both छ and तल्. The class-denoting ब्रह्मन् input retains
त्व/तल्. Thus a general “all abstract alternatives survive” rule is also
insufficient.

## C8. Finite stipulations and unsettled evidence

The ten 5.1.59 numeral clauses and the eight 4.4.91 base/sense pairs
are printed in the rule tables. They are dependent finite constructions,
not a numerical cross-product and not extra IDs. Named constructions
such as धेनुष्या, ऐकागारिक, आगवीन and हैयङ्गवीन retain their target
denotations and coupled operations; no arbitrary analogue is admitted.

The cross-rule घ/घच् reading at 4.4.117, the marker-sensitive extra
reading in 4.4.132, the archived य/यत् divergence at 5.1.66, the damaged
exception segmentation at 5.1.121, and unreviewed
gaṇa/priority intersections remain explicit source obligations. A bounded
positive contract for a rule does not imply those open cases are settled.

The written result is a source-relative finite selection relation for
admitted inputs. [Native continuation](NATIVE_CONTINUATION.md) recovers
this exact level under zero-reading; it does not upgrade it to complete
word generation or verified execution.
