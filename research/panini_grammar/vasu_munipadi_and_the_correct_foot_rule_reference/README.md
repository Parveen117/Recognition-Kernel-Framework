# PG879 · Vasu’s *munipadī* list slip and the correct foot-rule reference

### Two literal strings do not create two grammatical alternatives

The pinned Vasu-English field at 5.4.139 describes the feminine affix as **ī**, says that it is the obligatory **ṅīp** in this named list, and gives *kumbhapadī* and *śatapadī* as its examples. In the numbered inventory, however, item 5 alone is printed **munipadi**, with short final *i*. The raw string remains preserved as Vasu’s diplomatic source spelling. It is not selected as a second stem.

Three independent controls make that decision positive rather than conjectural normalization. Kāśikā 5.4.139 has **munipadī** in the same named feminine inventory. Monier-Williams entry 165629 has the head and citation form **munipadī**, marks it feminine, and assigns it to *kumbhapadyādi*. Most importantly, Vasu’s own governing prose requires long *ī* and supplies no short-*i* affix or inflectional environment for this citation-list item. The short-*i* token is therefore a source-local list slip. It is **Accepted** in the diplomatic Vasu layer and **Excluded** only as an independently licensed grammatical stem or an extra case reading. The lexical meaning of K’s *munipadī* remains Open; a corrected citation form is not a guessed gloss.

The same Vasu field says that *pad* replaces *pāt* by **4.4.130**. That address cannot own the stated operation: the pinned sūtrapāṭha and both Kāśikā and Vasu at 4.4.130 prescribe *yat/kha* after *ojas* when a day is meant. The rule whose pinned text actually says that **pad** replaces **pād** in the *bha* environment is **6.4.130, pādaḥ pat**. Vasu’s own field 6.4.130 repeats that whole-member replacement and gives *dvipadaḥ, dvipadā, dvipade* and *dvipadikām*. The 5.4.139 pointer is therefore an internal miscitation to 4.4.130; its selected target is 6.4.130. The literal `[[4.4.130]]` remains retained in source history and is not silently rewritten.
