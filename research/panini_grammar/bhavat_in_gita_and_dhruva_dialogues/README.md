# PG911 · How honorific *bhavat* addresses a listener

PG897 assigns third-person agreement to honorific *bhavat*. PG903 supplies its written cases. Five passages now show how a speaker alternates that honorific with second-person forms while addressing the same listener. Three masculine singular case keys acquire direct textual evidence. The six tokens and their local partners are individually indexed. [Passage register](PASSAGES.tsv)

### Kṛṣṇa as possessor, subject of a question and object

In Gītā 4.4, Arjuna contrasts Kṛṣṇa’s birth with Vivasvat’s. The two possessors depend on *janma*. This parallel selects GEN sg *bhavataḥ*. Before j the continuous text has *bhavato janma*. An isolated ablative could share the pause form, but the two births fix the genitive relation in this passage. The second half resumes Kṛṣṇa with *tvam*. Rāmānuja’s commentary also compares the dates of the two births. [Gītā 4.4 with Rāmānuja, revision 403068](https://sa.wikisource.org/w/index.php?oldid=403068)

Gītā 11.31 has two different case requests inside one speech turn. *Ko bhavān* asks who the addressee is. *Bhavān* is nominative in a question with no expressed copula. In the next clause the speaker wishes to know that same being. *Bhavantam* is accusative, modified by *ādyam*, and governed by the infinitive *vijñātum*. In the surrounding clauses the imperative *ākhyāhi* addresses that being directly, while *tava* marks possession. Neither the adjacent imperative nor the first-person *icchāmi* establishes finite agreement with the nominative *bhavān*. [Gītā 11.31 with Śaṅkara, revision 92507](https://sa.wikisource.org/w/index.php?oldid=92507)

### Dhruva as addressee and grammatical subject

In Bhāgavata 4.8.11 Suruci addresses Dhruva as *vatsa* and denies that he deserves to ascend the seat. *Bhavān* is the subject of third-singular *arhati*. The infinitive *āroḍhum* expresses the action at issue. The *tvam* of the following clause refers to the same child. This verse distinguishes three roles in a single utterance: the overt vocative, the nominative controller and the second-person pronoun. The presence of an honorific here does not imply approval or affection.

In 4.8.12 Suruci continues with second-person *asi* and third-person *veda* with overt subject *bhavān*. The ending of *veda* in isolation can also serve a first person. The explicit subject makes the third-person analysis determinate here. In 4.8.32 Nārada addresses Dhruva with *yatiṣyati bhavān*. The third-singular future supplies a second speaker and a different finite ending for the same agreement relation. [Bhāgavata 4.8.11–12, 32, revision 353504](https://sa.wikisource.org/w/index.php?oldid=353504)

### Scope and recovery

All six words belong to masculine singular honorific *bhavat*. Their original nominative su, accusative am or genitive ṅas remains attached to the PG903 case history. The three finite verbs are observed partners. This stage introduces no new verbal derivation, root-sense licence, preverb, voice, verbal affix or inflectional reading. It contributes one written discourse construction linking a shared addressee across clause boundaries to five verse interfaces. Three source decisions distinguish possessive genitive, nominative versus accusative government, and the clause-local scope of agreement. The five verse observations do not duplicate PG897’s grammatical policy. Feminine, neuter, non-singular, other registers and critical transmission have open evidence obligations.
