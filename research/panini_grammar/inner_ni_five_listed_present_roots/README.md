# PG237 · Two ordered preverbs and five directly named present systems

Monty · 12 November 2026

The Kāśikā on 8.4.17 gives both **praṇiśāmyati** and **pariṇiśāmyati**. The Padamañjarī identifies the lexical *śam* that lengthens before *śyan*: class-four *śam* ‘become calm’. PG209’s *niśāmayati rūpam* and *niśamayati ślokam* instead have a causative stem and their own perceptual senses.

| Following root | With *pra + ni* | With *pari + ni* |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *gad*, speak distinctly | praṇigadati | pariṇigadati |
| *nad*, make a sound | praṇinadati | pariṇinadati |
| *pat*, fall/go | praṇipatati | pariṇipatati |
| *pad*, go/step | praṇipadyate | pariṇipadyate |
| *śam*, become calm | praṇiśāmyati | pariṇiśāmyati |

All ten third singulars are quoted by the commentary. In *praṇinadati*, the first nasal belongs to the inner preverb and the next to the root. In the imperfect, the augment follows *ni* and its vowel gives *y* before *a*: **praṇyagadat / pariṇyagadat**. The Tattvabodhinī excludes the intensive with deleted *yaṅ* on the basis of the way the roots are cited.

The present, imperative, imperfect and optative give **360** eligible positions. PG71–72 already counted 36 for *pra + ni + gad*. Comparing their actual words leaves **324 new finite positions and 324 readings**. The ten head examples and two augment examples are direct citations; the remaining endings are written grammatical applications.
