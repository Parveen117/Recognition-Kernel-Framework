# PG251 · Three short-a extension words in the commentary and the Purāṇas

Monty · 12 November 2026

Śvetavanavāsin’s alternative commentary at §541 writes short **aṭac** three times. With *tan* it gives **taṭaḥ**, a bank; with *van*, **vaṭaḥ**, a banyan; with *av*, **avaṭaḥ**, a well. It invokes loss of the nasal in the first derivation and expressly states it in the second. The three masculine paradigms add **72 nominal positions and 75 readings**.

| Head | Nominative singular | Accusative singular | Locative singular | Source use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bank | taṭaḥ | taṭam | taṭe | The Bhāgavata places an āśrama on the western bank of the Sarasvatī, *paścime taṭe* (1.7.2). |
| Banyan | vaṭaḥ | vaṭam | vaṭe | At Viṣṇupurāṇa 5.9.2 the tree closes *bhāṇḍīravaṭam*. At 5.9.15 *vaṭam* is a separate word. |
| Well | avaṭaḥ | avaṭam | avaṭe | The commentator glosses the word *kūpaḥ*. The archive’s noun entry describes a hole in the ground. |

The corpus table locates six examples of the bank and banyan in two Purāṇas. *Taṭe* after a genitive in Viṣṇupurāṇa 2.15.6 and *-taṭam* as the final member of the Yamunā compound at 1.12.2 instantiate different constructions. At Bhāgavata 10.18.22 the banyan itself is named *Bhāṇḍīraka*. Neither the accusative nor the locative alone distinguishes masculine from neuter in these *a*-stems. The masculine selection here follows the root commentary’s nominatives.

The ordered list in the project’s primary 4.105 has long **āṭac**. A genuinely attested *avaṭa* therefore cannot, on its own, settle the technical vowel of that separate list. The register stays at **99/103**.
