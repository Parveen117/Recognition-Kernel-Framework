## D1. Input and evidence

An input contains the full inherited carrier coordinate and a finite
grammatical history, with these additional annotations:

|Field|Required distinctions|
|---|---|
|Lexical origin|Root entry, sense, original accent/markers, nominal identity, source-listed class and attested extension|
|Morphological structure|Root/copy/affix addresses, original lakāra and sup, retained or deleted यङ्/णि/सन्/चङ्, augment owner and insertion point|
|Boundaries|Current and historical पद, compound tree and member order, sentence/clause, metrical pāda, utterance and pause|
|Syntax and meaning|Direct/indirect particle relation, preceding nominative's own predecessor, anaphoric reuse, speaker attitude, question scope, praise/censure, ritual formula|
|Sound|Segment origin and active value, vowel duration, accent and its source, nasal consonant versus nasalized vowel versus anusvāra, रु versus plain र्|
|Register and policy|Ordinary/Vedic, recension, teacher, lexical nipātana, commentary interpretation and chosen duration convention|
|Visibility|Observer rule, observed event, addressed trigger/target, prior and current view, named exception and conflict history|
|Evidence|Affirmative admission, resolved exclusions, citation or unresolved obligation for each decisive gate|

Historical social categories in 8.2.83 are annotations of the cited greeting
construction. This packet supplies no procedure for assigning those categories
to a living person. Speech-act or ritual labels likewise require supplied
context; they are not inferred from a string alone.

Each decisive gate has T/F/? cuts inherited from PG37. Missing same-sentence
evidence, a lexical entry, source policy or pada boundary remains ?. It does
not license the residual/default branch. A false gate rejects that candidate;
an unresolved gate leaves the requested decision Open.

## D2. Three different kinds of copying

1. Root abhyāsa retains the selected 6.1 copy unit and morphological trigger.
   7.4.75–97 processes that copy. Root and copy vowel changes have different owners.
2. Whole-expression repetition under 8.1.1–15 copies a licensed expression.
   The second member receives आम्रेडित identity. Matched phonetic alternatives
   can need explicit visibility before this repetition.
3. Consonant duplication under 8.4.46–52 is a phonetic event at a segment
   address, with teacher-specific restrictions. It does not create a root copy
   or an आम्रेडित member.

An identical printed sequence does not identify these histories.
