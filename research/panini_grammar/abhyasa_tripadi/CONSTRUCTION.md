# PG41 — Construction from copied units and sentence-scoped sound histories

Author: Monty. 4 November 2026.

Use [Domain](DOMAIN.md), the eleven rule tables and the
[register](RULE_REGISTER.json). The [native adapter](NATIVE_CONTINUATION.md)
instantiates the existing full-carrier construction; the steps below specify
its new event labels and observation queries.

## C1. Selection and event ownership

An admitted clause supplies a finite list of addressed events. A replacement
changes an active value while retaining the original token; deletion marks a
token inactive; copying creates a new address linked to its selected source
view. Augmentation records whether it belongs to the preceding word, following
word, root, copy or affix. No operation is selected from spelling equality alone.

Resolve the decisive semantic, lexical, boundary and policy gates before
selecting an optional branch. Choices belonging to one clause stay coupled.
Teacher alternatives and incompatible commentary interpretations are separate
policies; they are not additional free choices inside every derivation.

## C2. Qualified copy work

The causative-aorist route inherits PG40's root-upadhā shortening and its
exclusions. Copy creation is then licensed by its own morphological context.
At 7.4.93, सन्वत् transfers the admitted copy operations; it does not transfer
7.4.58's complete copy deletion. 7.4.94 requires both the qualifying following
light syllable and a light copy. The seven-root 95 exception blocks both
transferred इ and lengthening. 96/97 retain their own paired alternatives.

|Admitted source path|Addressed continuation|Excluded shortcut|
|---|---|---|
|कृ causative चङ्, qualified short-root/copy setup|सन्वत् copy इ and copy length yield ची in witnessed अचीकरत्|Deleting the copy because सन् has a copy-loss rule|
|गण् causative चङ्|7.4.97 chooses copy ई or short अ, with inherited consonant treatment: अजीगणत् / अजगणत्|Inventing an independent long-आ option|
|नृत् intensive with यङ्-luk|7.4.90–91 retain रुक्/रिक्/रीक् histories: नर्नर्ति / नरिनर्ति / नरीनर्ति|Treating the letters of a printed augment as its pronounced output|
|चर् intensive|Copy augmentation at 87; root अ→उ at 88; applicable length separately: चञ्चूर्यते|Changing the copy vowel under the root-vowel rule|

These are written source paths using inherited prerequisites, not new complete
nine-slot paradigms. Where a prerequisite remains unconstructed, it is an input
obligation, not an implicitly generated form.

## C3. Repetition, sentence relation and accent

For whole-expression repetition, select a legal 8.1.4–15 meaning first.
Assign the second member आम्रेडित and its accent, unless a later specific
construction supplies another accent. 8.1.9 transfers selected बहुव्रीहि
effects without transferring every designation or compound-final rule.

The commentary requires matched द्रोग्धाद्रोग्धा or द्रोढाद्रोढा choices.
Selecting independent घ्/ढ् outcomes after copying would manufacture mixed
members. Record the permitted pre-copy source view. This does not make every
tripādī event visible to morphological reduplication: 8.2.1's औजढत् discussion
and 8.4.21's explicit two-copy treatment retain their narrower domains.

Enclitic वाम्/नौ/वस्/नस्/ते/मे/त्वा/मा replace the qualified inflected pronoun
word, with original person, number and case retained. The preceding पद must
belong to the same sentence, and the position cannot be metrical-pāda initial.
Direct चादि connection and nonvisual cognition have different blocking scopes.
8.1.26's anaphoric alternatives require an explicit policy.

In देवदत्त पचसि, the preceding vocative is absent for the later suppression
query under 8.1.72, while its own vocative accent survives. In यावद् देवदत्त
पचसि, the same visibility can restore the adjacency required by the praise
exception. Neither operation deletes the vocative token.

## C4. Tripādī visibility is indexed by observer

Store an observation request as (history, observer rule, observed event,
trigger address, target address, exception policy). 8.2.1 blocks a later
tripādī result for an earlier observer, including earlier tripādī rules.
It does not erase the later event. A specific अपवाद can have a different
admission relation; 1.4.2 is not an unrestricted “highest ID wins” selector.

|Boundary|Admitted visibility|Practical consequence|
|---|---|---|
|8.2.2|नलोप hidden for सुप्, accent, designation and तुक् in कृत्; visible outside named classes|राजभिः does not acquire ऐस्, while राजीयति can use the shortened base|
|8.2.3 core|मु visible for qualified ना behavior|अमुना is not rejected by blindly hiding all tripādī changes|
|8.2.3 commentary|Internal single-replacement accent; सिच् loss for vowel coalescence; selected निष्ठा, copy and repetition operations|Each exception has a named observer and source clause|
|8.3.13|Required ष्टुत्व visible to ढ् deletion|लीढम् can proceed; final जश् case श्वलिड् ढौकते does not gain an absent ढ्|
|8.4.58|Restored nasal remains later than णत्व|कुर्वन्ति: internal न्→anusvāra before त्, then restored न्; no retroactive णत्व|
|8.4.66|Dependent स्वरित hidden from earlier 6.1.158|Earlier उदात्त is not erased merely because a later dependent स्वरित appears|

The 8.2.3 archive also lists cluster-final loss for रु→उ, pluti modification
for तुक् before छ्, श्चुत्व for धुट् and homorganic nasal for consonant
duplication. They remain named transfers; this packet does not supply a
general solver for all traditional visibility disputes.

## C5. Addressed consonant and affix examples

For नह् + निष्ठा, 8.2.34 gives root-final ध्, 8.2.40 gives suffix-initial ध्,
and 8.4.53 supplies the first dental's जश् result: नद्ध. The suffix and root
consonants remain distinct. For आह् + थ, 8.2.35 gives थ्, then appropriate
चर् treatment gives आत्थ. Adopting the damaged ध् reading would create a
wrong 8.2.40 environment.

For सम् + राज् + क्विप्, 8.3.25 retains prefix म् against the ordinary
anusvāra rule; सम्राट् is a witness of this lexical-affix gate. This does not
retain म् before every राज् formation: संराजिता has another suffix.

At 8.2.80, ordinary अदस् case forms preserve short/long distinction under
मु replacement. Kaumudi also explicitly maps a consonantal target to short
उ. The अद्रि-derived double-द् cases have three source analyses; select one
before addressing either द्. A parser cannot replace every द् in the string.

## C6. Nasal and sibilant branches

At 8.3.2–4 the nasalized-vowel branch and the unnasalized-vowel-plus-anusvāra
branch are complementary. Long आ before अट् can force the former. The
सम्/पुम्/कान् commentary also supplies a direct-स् analysis to prevent
unwanted visarga alternatives. Preserve its history separately from रु.

8.3.58 permits the named षत्व interveners individually, not their arbitrary
combination. In contrast, 8.4.2 permits combinations of its listed णत्व
interveners, and नुम् there denotes the relevant audible anusvāra. This
difference cannot be encoded by one global “ignore nasal marks” function.

The target's cause matters. 8.3.116 blocks prefix-triggered षत्व in
स्तम्भ्/सिव्/सह् + चङ् but retains separately licensed copy-triggered षत्व.
8.3.118 blocks the later/root स् in निषसाद and परिषस्वजे without erasing
the earlier copy's ष्. 8.4.33 can vary the initial न् in प्रणिक्षणम् /
प्रनिक्षणम् while the later ण् has its own trigger.

## C7. Phonetic readout

Pluti keeps the selected vowel, speech act, accent, and duration convention.
8.2.106 explicitly preserves the three-mora-total and four-mora analyses.
8.2.107's अ३इ/अ३उ decomposition has enumerated contexts, word-final and
प्रगृह्य restrictions; 108's य्/व् event is distinct from ordinary 6.1.77.

Consonant duplication and optional deletion use retained segment addresses.
They do not license an unbounded duplication loop, arbitrary deduplication or
reopening of earlier rules after every change. Teacher policies at 8.4.50–52
are applied within their domain. 8.4.68 changes the articulation of short अ
at readout while its grammatical open-vowel representation remains available
to earlier observers. Long and pluta vowels are excluded.

The resulting object remains a bounded written relation. Full realization,
automatic semantic annotation, executable predicates, exhaustive lexical
extension and independent phonetic verification are open.
