repair or silent normalization is applied to any commentary file.

Literal fidelity is not a proof of linguistic correctness. A damaged field
can remain exact in the archive while its row adopts a separately justified
reading. Other minor transcription errors and example spellings remain
visible; the archive is not a normalized dictionary.

## S3. Policies retained instead of flattened

6.4.52's set-निष्ठा restriction and ordering interpretation, 6.4.93's
positive lengthening branch, 6.4.120–126's joint एत्व/copy-loss conditions,
and 6.4.171's split-rule interpretation remain explicit. At 7.1.36 some
sources inherit optionality for विद्→वसु. At 7.1.74 masculine treatment
requires the *same sense*, not a homonymous masculine word. At 7.1.90 the
ओत् textual reading and गोत् interpretation are distinct source routes.

7.2.10's long lexical anit list contains a मृज्/विज् dispute; marker/class
data must resolve a concrete root before use. 7.2.45 records competing
perfect-iṭ priorities. 7.2.58 has a same-word middle account and a
root-class account. 7.2.61–66 preserve the named Bhāradvāja restriction and
later exceptions. 7.2.101's neuter जरा routes are school-dependent.

7.3.17 has an alternate कुलिज exclusion. 7.3.30 discusses formation order
and suffix eligibility; this is not permission to ignore अङ्ग admission.
7.3.46–49 label northern/other-school diminutive outcomes. 7.3.80 records
two प्वादि list boundaries. 7.3.95 records Āpiśala's narrower Vedic scope.
7.4.12's alternate lexical-root explanation does not establish all क्वसु
forms. 7.4.46–47 retain competing analyses of the substitute's size/final
consonant rather than pretending one surface proves every derivation.

Fixed Vedic rules and आकृतिगण extensions are bounded by their source
evidence. Complete normalized lexical lists, every optional branch,
independent philological review and executed forms remain open.
