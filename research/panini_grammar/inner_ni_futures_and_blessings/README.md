# PG238 · Futures and benedictives after pra or pari plus ni

Monty · 12 November 2026

With either ordered prefix pair, the roots *pat* and *pad* remain distinguishable in the futures. *Pat* ‘fall’ takes *iṭ* here; middle *pad* ‘go’ does not. The two periphrastic futures therefore differ even though no personal ending is heard in their third singulars.

| Root after *pra + ni* | Periphrastic future | Ordinary future | Benedictive |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| *gad* | praṇigaditā | praṇigadiṣyati | praṇigadyāt |
| *nad* | praṇinaditā | praṇinadiṣyati | praṇinadyāt |
| *pat* | praṇipatitā | praṇipatiṣyati | praṇipatyāt |
| *pad* | praṇipattā | praṇipatsyate | praṇipatsīṣṭa |
| *śam* ‘become calm’ | praṇiśamitā | praṇiśamiṣyati | praṇiśamyāt |

Maitreya supplies *pattā* and *śamitā*. For *śam*, the long vowel in his *śāmyati rogaḥ* belongs to the present stem and the short vowel reappears in the future. His *praṇadati* and *praṇinadati* show how introducing an actual second preverb changes which nasal is retroflex. Kṣīrasvāmin gives both the two-preverb middle present *praṇipadyate* and the independently prefixed future *prapattā*.

The two futures, conditional and benedictive cover **360** positions. PG71–72 already contain the 36 *pra + ni + gad* requests. The other **324 positions and 324 readings** are new. These eight lakāras supply 720 positions. PG71–73 already supplied eighteen further perfect and aorist positions for *pra + ni + gad*. The ten-lakāra total so far is **738/900**, leaving 162 new positions.
