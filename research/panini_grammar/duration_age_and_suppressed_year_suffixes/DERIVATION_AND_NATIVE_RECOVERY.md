# PG760 · A month of work, a month of age, or a recurring monthly payment

5.1.78 supplies the time-base restriction; the Kāśikā carries it through 97. It produces no word by itself. Rule 79 takes an instrumental for the interval of completion. Rule 80 measures the span of the resulting work or state by an accusative. Even a momentary engagement can have a month's teaching as its effect. In the drama a regular gift is payable monthly; in the epic a daily rite is owed at dawn. The period and the form are evidenced, but those passages by themselves cannot select the accomplishment relation of 79. The two chapter numbers for the dawn verse refer to the same word.

For a single month as age, carry yat and khañ. In a month-final numeral compound retain yap; for six months the rules at 83–84 distinguish two-branch non-age and three-branch age readings. The stronger ṣāṇmāsya can cross the boundary, while ṣaṇmāsika in the illness example has a short initial a. The letter sequence or number six alone does not recover which meaning was supplied.

Samā has a separate kha history; a fifth interpretation with an instrumental support is expressly attributed to some. With a numeral compound kha may alternate with ṭhañ. For ahan the kha branch has ṭi loss by 6.4.145, while the visible -ahnika branch has its own augment and vṛddhi rule under 7.3.3. In saṃvatsara-final dvigus the second member strengthens on the ṭhañ path. For varṣa-final dvigus the condition includes time orientation: in the explicit future example the strengthened vowel is in trai-, whereas with past duration the word has tri-vārṣika. When kha or ṭhañ undergoes luk, keep the individual affix in the history. The son mentioned by the grammar is a sentient referent and requires luk. Manu and Yājñavalkya describe incomplete age using longer compounds. Mitākṣarā supplies an explicit ‘whose two years are incomplete’ analysis. The named texts support different whole words and do not licence an unconditioned -ika option on the boy.

At 90 ṣaṣṭi and rātri supply the temporal measure; the latter disappears in the stipulated rice name. The plural in the sūtra does not prescribe plurality of the resulting noun. Caraka's masculine nominative singular chooses a named grain type, independently verifying that distinction. No agronomic claim is required to carry the grammatical name.
