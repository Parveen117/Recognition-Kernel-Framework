# PG863 · Two printed readings of Haradatta and time as agent or patient

### The two commentators at 3.3.39

At PG862 the addressed digital Nyāsa preserved *parimāṇam api*. Printed Shastri–Shukla vol. III p. 39 and Tripathi vol. IV p. 37 have the same word under the Nyāsa heading. On the **same pages** the Padamañjarī passage has *param api inuṇam*, printed together as परमपीनुणं, with the subsequent inuṇ as the rival of ghañ. In Padamañjarī, the turn to lie beside the king licenses ghañ even when the turn-taking pervades the group. In Nyāsa, the redundant specification of a turn is explained through overcoming an obstacle. Its printed noun is *parimāṇam*. Recognizing the argument in context gives no authority to replace the noun with the name of a suffix. The agreement of these two printed witnesses settles what these editions say. Their independence or the autograph reading remains unestablished.

### An attributed difference about which time words govern tumun

In the 3.3.167 Padamañjarī of both editions the first account reads **पर्यायान्तरनिवृत्त्यर्थम्**. Shastri–Shukla prints it on p. 134, Tripathi on p. 136. The enumerated synonyms serve to restrict additional synonyms. The pinned digital text reads **पर्यायान्तरविवृत्यर्थम्**, with *vi-* in place of *ni-*. Retain both readings with their addresses. For the restrictive interpretation PG29 now has a direct printed ground. Both prints then introduce the other interpretation with **अन्ये त्वाहुः**. It treats the enumeration as indicating the meaning of time or opportunity. The examples include *avasaro bhoktum* and the Kāśikā's own *rājānam upaśayituṃ paryāyaḥ* under 3.3.39. At 3.3.39 Haradatta himself uses *avakāśo bhoktum* alongside *avasaro bhoktum*. At 3.3.167 the wider argument is reported in the voice of unnamed others. These distinct passages can inform the same construction without identifying the unnamed speakers. PG29's chosen lexicon and its already constructed infinitive and lyuṭ clauses retain their readings.

For the narrower printed reading, kāla, samaya and velā in a directive or appropriate-opportunity sense license the selected infinitive. For the wider reported reading a synonym must denote that opportunity and govern the relevant action. The word *kāla* in a sentence with its own finite predicate supplies neither premise by itself. In the presence of the yad word 3.3.168 has a separately governed liṅ, as PG29 shows. No new inflectional credit is assigned to these grammatical examples.
