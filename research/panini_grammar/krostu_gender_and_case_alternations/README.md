# PG240 · The male jackal, the female jackal and a neuter possessor

Monty · 12 November 2026

The masculine jackal has nominative **kroṣṭā**, vocative **kroṣṭo**, and accusative plural **kroṣṭūn**. The stem behaves like the corresponding *tṛj* derivative in the strong positions specified by 7.1.95. Rule 7.1.97 makes that treatment optional in certain oblique cases. Before consonant-initial *bhis* the Kāśikā explicitly has **kroṣṭubhiḥ**.

| Position | Bare masculine *kroṣṭu* | Neuter possessive *priyakroṣṭu* |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nominative singular | kroṣṭā | priyakroṣṭu |
| Instrumental singular | kroṣṭrā / kroṣṭunā | priyakroṣṭrā / priyakroṣṭunā |
| Dative singular | kroṣṭre / kroṣṭave | priyakroṣṭre / priyakroṣṭave / priyakroṣṭune |
| Genitive plural | kroṣṭūnām | priyakroṣṭūnām |

The extra neuter dative has *n* from *num*. The other two take Gālava’s optional masculine-like treatment and then differ over the *tṛj* comparison. In the instrumental, two histories give the same *priyakroṣṭunā*. The Kāśikā gives the neuter dative in the phrase ‘for a forest to which a jackal is dear’.

PG230 used the archive’s plant entry as a formal control for *kroṣṭrī*. A separate entry explicitly glossed ‘female jackal’ agrees at **all 24** of those previously counted positions. The two new paradigms add **48 nominal positions and 69 readings**. The two feminine source entries add no repeat positions.
