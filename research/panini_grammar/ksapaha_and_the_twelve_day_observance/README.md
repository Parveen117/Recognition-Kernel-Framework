# PG919 · Twelve days of observance and the nights and days within them

In Mahābhārata 3.118.17 the king subsists on water and air for twelve days. His ablutions recur during those nights and days. The verse puts a singular duration accusative and a locative plural next to the same observance. The plural *kṣapāhaḥsu* supplies evidence beyond Manu’s genitive *kṣapāhasya* (PG915). An ordinary a-stem locative plural would end in *-eṣu*. [Mahābhārata 3.118.17](https://gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de/gretil/1_sanskr/2_epic/mbh/sas/b03/b03c118.htm#b03c118v017)

**The twelve-day period.** Böhtlingk’s shorter dictionary distinguishes the masculine period (head 53720) from the adjective and a named ceremony (53721). In this verse the duration of the ascetic observance admits the period sense under 2.3.5. Select numeral *dvādaśan* and *ahan*, with the numeral in the combining form *dvādaśa-*. Kāśikā on 5.4.89 blocks the 5.4.88 *ahna* replacement in an aggregate. In its analysis 5.4.91 supplies ṭac, 6.4.145 permits deletion of the final *an* of *ahan*, and the sounded affix a gives *aha*. At the member junction a and a coalesce by 6.1.101. This yields *dvādaśāha*. PG594 separately preserves Tattvabodhinī’s reported analysis in which 5.4.89 blocks exceptional ac. Both analyses retain ordinary ṭac here. Add am and apply 6.1.107 for the masculine accusative *dvādaśāham* (ṃ in the transmitted phrase). The ritual locative quoted in PG594's source file supplies no prior position for this duration accusative. **The repeated occasions.** The epic’s consonantal branch admits *kṣapā + ahan* in the sense of nights and days, with last-member neuter under 2.4.26. The surface does not independently mark gender. The internal junction gives *kṣapāhan*. With the actual locative plural sup (su), the stem receives pada designation by 1.4.17. Under 8.2.68 final n of this *ahan* gives ru. The following suP precludes the *asupi* edge of 8.2.69. Before the sibilant, 8.3.15 gives visarga and 8.3.36 licenses retention, yielding the transmitted *kṣapāhaḥsu*. Whitney §430a,c describes ahar/ahas in middle cases and the several available final compound stems. That descriptive analysis can account for the same boundary without identifying a unique original stem from this single inflection. The competing derivations yield one attested reading. Other visarga spellings have no independent verse credit. The semantic locative gives the occasions of the action. The object of the participle *kurvan* is the separate noun for the ablution. **The received short-a stem.** Wilson, entry 12240, gives the neuter head *kṣapāha*, ending *-ham*, and the two lexical members *kṣapā* and *ahan*. MW 59022 assigns Manu 1.68 to this neuter. Medhātithi’s gloss confirms a single whole period. Kāśikā 5.4.91 explicitly conditions its ṭac on tatpuruṣa. The mere presence of final *ahan* in a coordinate expression cannot establish that operation. PG914’s opposite-order dual with *rātri* has its own ending history. Wilson closes the lexical member decision. The historical analysis licensing the a-stem in this precise coordinate compound stays Open.
