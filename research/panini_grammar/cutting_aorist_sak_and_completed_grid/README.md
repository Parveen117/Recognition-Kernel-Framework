# PG281 · The cutting aorist has a root-owned sak as well as sic

Monty · 13 November 2026

The retained-*sic* aorist of an ā-final root has an additional root-owned **sak** under **7.2.73**. The Kāśikā explicitly prescribes that augment and iṭ for ā-final stems. Consequently *praṇyaṣāsiṣṭam* has three different s-histories: the root initial changed by the qualifying preverb, dental *s* from root-owned sak, and retroflex *ṣ* from the iṭ-following sic. The adjacent ending *t* becomes *ṭ* under 8.4.41.

| Cell, pra + ni | Sic-luk path | Retained-sic path |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2sg | praṇyaṣāḥ | praṇyaṣāsīḥ |
| 2du | praṇyaṣātam | praṇyaṣāsiṣṭam |
| 1sg | praṇyaṣām | praṇyaṣāsiṣam |

The second singular's long ī is not copied to the dual. Its apṛkta ending takes īṭ under 7.3.96, and 8.2.28 deletes the s after iṭ before that īṭ. The retained vowels then combine. The dual has no such apṛkta īṭ and keeps its *-iṣṭam* sequence. These operations retain their distinct hosts.

The other branch is the optional sic-luk of 2.4.78, independently witnessed in PG274's *abhyaṣāt/abhyaṣāsīt*. A luk-deleted affix does not recreate aṅga work under 1.1.63. The shorter aorist is therefore not produced by taking the complete *sak + iṭ + sic* word and erasing an arbitrary surface *s*.

These **12 new positions and 24 readings** finish the two cutting-root profiles. Their exact key grid is now **180/180**: ten selected Classical lakāras, nine persons/numbers, and two ordered preverb pairs. This does not claim every derived stem, voice, preverb or Vedic use of the root. Striking remains **126/180**, so the pair has **306/360** positions.

12 positions, 24 readings and 24 retained paths. Cumulative **50,336**.
