# Derivation and scope

## Role, designation and case

In the positive rows, 1.4.32–37 or 1.4.39–41 assign sampradāna. 2.3.13 realizes dative under the inherited 2.3.1 condition. A transfer recipient is only one of the semantic roles here. The desired flowers, pleased experiencer, creditor, intended knower and original speaker have different typed positions. The grammar does not imply an exchange of objects on all of them.

## A prefix can change the case while preserving the event role

Compare devadattāya krudhyati with devadattam abhikrudhyati, and the parallel druh pair. Source 1.4.38 overrides the preceding sampradāna designation for prefixed krudh/druh. The directed anger or hostility target is the same participant, but its designation becomes karman. 2.3.2 yields the accusative. Record the morphological prefix and its attachment before choosing the case.

## The previous actor is an argument of the current clause

Under 1.4.40 the promise follows another person’s prompting; that earlier actor is dative. Under 1.4.41 the hotṛ first recites and another person responds in encouragement. The two roles are linked by participant identity across ordered events. An isolated gloss “to the priest” would discard this temporal ordering and the difference between the two actors. These rules assert order, without supplying elapsed duration.

## Case does not reveal number of events

The form devadattāya occurs in the pleasure, praise, debt, anger, prognostication and promise constructions. Inverse recognition first recovers its dative morphology and then retains the compatible verb/prefix/participant readings. A bare -bhyaḥ ending can also belong to an ablative, exemplified in [PG134](../apadana_role_relation/README.md).
