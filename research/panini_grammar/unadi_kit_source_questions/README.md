# PG181 · Two traditional marker questions and the two senses of kīnāśa

Monty · 11 November 2026

Two Uṇādi comments explicitly defer the effect of a *k* marker. The printed Uṇādisūtravṛtti of Ujjvaladatta reproduces both difficulties. Its treatment also identifies a specific homonymous root and a verse that lets the lexical interpretation be decided independently.

| Record | Printed evidence | What can be decided |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **4.80 · kuṭaru** | Aufrecht’s 1859 edition, printed p. 108, PDF p. 132. Ujjvaladatta assigns kit to *aru* and gives the tent sense. He leaves the marker’s purpose open. | The gloss *vastragṛha*, a cloth dwelling, identifies the tent sense. Vājasaneyi Saṃhitā 24.23 has **kuṭarūn** in its bird list. Apte and Monier-Williams give the cock and tent senses separately. The Vedic occurrence supports the animal reading. |
| **5.56 · kīnāśa** | Printed p. 145, PDF p. 169. Ujjvaladatta identifies *kliś* in the **vibādhane** sense. He gives the cultivator and Yama readings, quotes Māgha, and says he sees no effect of the *k* marker. | The sense identifies Dhātupāṭha **09.0058** for this commentator. The homonym **04.0057** is marked *upatāpe*. Ṛgveda 4.57.8 places the word in farming. In Śiśupālavadha 1.73, Mallinātha expressly glosses **kīnāśa** as Yama. |

### A limited answer for the guṇa question

The Kāśikā on 1.2.1 starts its kuṭādi range at *kuṭa kauṭilye*. The matching Dhātupāṭha entry is 06.0093. Take that root as the candidate input for *kuṭaru*. The affix *aru* has neither a ñ nor a ṇ marker. Rule 1.2.1 therefore treats it as ṅit for the relevant operation. The ordinary guṇa of the short penultimate *u* under 7.3.86 is blocked by 1.1.5.

Now compare this single operation with and without the additional explicit kit marking of Uṇādi 4.80. For **this root and this affix**, the outcome is the same in the two cases. Thus blocking ordinary *u → o* is insufficient to demonstrate a distinct purpose for the extra marker on that history. A different root entry or an operation with different marker requirements requires separate analysis. The printed source does not settle that wider question.

### A positive verse decision

Māgha 1.73 has the complete accusative compound *kīnāśaniketanātithim* with *vidhehi*, an imperative. Mallinātha understands the words as a request to make Śiśupāla a guest in Yama’s house. The verse supplies the Yama reading and a syntactic role. The Ṛgvedic plural supplies the agricultural reading in its own context. In neither case does an attested word ending expose what the unpronounced marker contributed internally.
