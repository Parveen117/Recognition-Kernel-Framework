# PG85 — Car: transitivity and an expressed case relation

Monty · 9 November 2026

## H01. Counted lexical identity and selected meanings

The counted root is **1010640, चरँ**, bhvādi, udātta/seṭ, lexical P,
with the publisher's meaning **गतौ भक्षणे च**. The teaching's final
nasalized a is it under 1.3.2 and disappears under 1.3.9; internal short
a remains in **car**. Final r is a consonant, not PG83/84's long ṝ.
Consequently gṝ-specific iṭ, guṇa and r/l options are not transferred.

The counted meanings are the motion/departure/transgression and vehicle-
assisted motion constructions identified below. Kaumudī's 7.2.2 field
includes **559 car** and reports its eating use. The publisher's lexical
note separately reports a Mādhavīya/Puruṣakāra disagreement about that
meaning; this packet preserves the note but does not adjudicate a reading
of those full works. No eating table is counted.

The same-spelled **1100274**, curādi, nityaṇic, U, **संशये**, is a
separate lexical control. Its cārayati-family entries do not license
changing these basic car tables to causative or class-10 forms.

## H02. Ud requires a transitive construction, not a lexical karma tag

Kāśikā **1.3.53** prescribes A for ud+car when the action is sakarmaka.
Its geham/kuṭumbam/guruvacanam examples and Kaumudī's dharmam example
use a supplied object with departure or transgression: utkrāmya/ullaṅghya
gacchati. UD_TRANSITIVE_A selects that domain, with agent ⊗ object and
the intended action relation, at all nine agreement slots.

The local **bāṣpam uccarati** control has no intended object in Nyāsa's
explanation: it rises/goes above. Its printed -am does not itself certify
a karma relation. The publisher's lexical karma=S tag likewise does not
make every utterance transitive. Preserve the source's role analysis
instead of inferring A from a spelling or dictionary-wide transitivity.

The independent sources spell the control vāṣpam/bāṣpam differently.
Nyāsa's positive-example line also contains the malformed **गेहेमुच्वरते**.
These literal fields remain archived; Kāśikā/Kaumudī and the explicit
rule control the admitted uccarate construction. No spelling fault becomes
an extra root, pada option or counted position.

## H03. Sam requires an expressed, connected tṛtīyā phrase

Kāśikā **1.3.54** takes tṛtīyā as the **case expression**, with a semantic
connection to car: **aśvena saṃcarate**. Kaumudī gives **rathena**.
Mahābhāṣya's question is decisive: an instrument can be understood for
every action, so the stronger condition is the place where tṛtīyā is
actually expressed. Nyāsa and Padamañjarī preserve the same distinction.

SAM_EXPRESSED_TRITIYA_A therefore receives a real tṛtīyā phrase and its
relation certificate, not merely an abstract instrument inferred by a
model. An unrelated third-case word elsewhere in a sentence is insufficient.
The selected table frame is motion with agent ⊗ instrument and no object;
the phrase is supplied anew with each completed event/agreement frame.
Nominal case inflection is an input contract, not newly counted morphology.

Bālamanoramā explicitly ends the preceding **sakarmakāt** condition.
It also permits transitive car under 1.3.54; the table's chosen no-object
frame is not a rule-wide intransitivity guard. That additional semantic
frame receives zero new table credit here. Its **rathikāḥ saṃcaranti**
control has an understood chariot but no expressed rathena: inference alone
does not select A. The Mahābhāṣya's two-worlds control has the same boundary.
These are local P controls with other A licences absent, not a theorem
that every failed special guard universally forces P.

## H04. Context, short iṭ and source-qualified exclusions

Both profiles require actual prefix/kriyāyoga under 1.4.58–59, basic
kartari and their H02/H03 certificates. The ten context labels are the
inherited present, non-today past, ordinary loṭ/vidhiliṅ, future/non-today
future, PG31 nonfulfilled future conditional, qualified blessing,
non-Mantra parokṣa perfect and witnessed earlier-today aorist. First-person
perfect has its own indirect-knowledge premise; no māṅ is supplied.
Missing event, role, prefix or case-expression premises yield HOLD.

At every actual val-initial ārdhadhātuka interface, **7.2.35** supplies
short iṭ. This includes sya/tās, A sīyuṭ/sic and the four val-initial A
perfect endings. The anudātta, snu/kram and vṛ/ṝ/cluster-initial-ṛ
conditions of 7.2.10/36/38/42/43 are absent. Final consonant r does not
satisfy a final ṛ-vowel condition. No long-iṭ or aniṭ branch is inferred
from the preceding gṝ packet.

For perfect, 1.2.5 kit and 6.4.120's e-contraction are addressed separately
in H07. For A sic, **7.2.2 inherits P**, so its ar-final vṛddhi does not
lengthen this A root. The source's mandatory P prescription and its running
lexical field are retained without adding a P or cār alternative here.
