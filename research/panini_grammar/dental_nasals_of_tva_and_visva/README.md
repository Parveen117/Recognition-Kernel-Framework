# PG906 · The dental nasals of *tva* and *viśva*

PG893, PG904 and PG905 copied the last phonological step of *sarva* along with its case pattern. The pattern licenses an ending. Retroflexion requires a trigger in the resulting word. [Six corrected rows](CORRECTIONS.tsv) supersede the identified readings and their retroflexion histories. Each key still owns one reading at this ordinary endpoint.

## Where the nasal comes from

* **Instrumental singular.** Free vowel-final *tva* ‘one/another’ (M) and *viśva* ‘all’ (M/N) select ṭā. Aṣṭādhyāyī 7.1.12 substitutes ina. Stem-final a and i yield e by 6.1.87. The remaining n belongs to ina. The resulting sequences tv-e-na and viśv-e-na have no preceding r or ṣ in the word. Rule 8.4.1 therefore leaves dental n. In *sarv-e-ṇa* the r is present and 8.4.2 permits the intervening v and vowel.
* **Neuter plural.** Keep separate jas for nominative, śas for accusative and address use of the first case for vocative. The short śi substitution (7.1.20) carries the sarvanāmasthāna designation (1.1.42). The a-final neuter takes num (7.1.72) and length before it (6.4.8), giving viśvā-n-i. The ś of viśva is palatal. Neither it nor the it-marker of śi supplies a sounded ṣ. The same operations on sarva give sarvāṇi through the retained r. The ordinary three-case syncretism preserves all three histories.

These are nominal sup requests with free lexical stems. Root, preverb, voice and verbal affix have no input here. Yuṣmad ‘you’, abstract -tva and the t-final *tvat* analysis retain their separate sources. The PG905 ablative pause alternatives stay attached to the ablative. Only the six recorded retroflex outputs lose acceptance in these ordinary domains.

## What the verses establish

* RV 4.18.2 repeats the instrumental *tvena*. The commentary identifies two counterparts, an opponent and an inquirer, and glosses each instrumental with *ekena*. This directly supports the masculine indefinite and its dental n. [Text, pada and commentary](https://sa.wikisource.org/w/index.php?oldid=321007)
* RV 1.50.13 associates *viśvena* with the neuter *sahasā* ‘might’. The construction supports N INS sg. Its masculine homograph in the correction table has independent grammatical support from the pattern. [Text and padapāṭha](https://sa.wikisource.org/w/index.php?oldid=401983)
* RV 5.82.5 gives *viśvāni* with *duritāni*, the object of the request to drive evils away. The addressee is Savitṛ. In the next verse *viśvā* qualifies the plural *vāmāni*, with the commentator supplying *sarvāṇi*. Thus the same neuter accusative plural has a shorter Vedic realization. It stays indexed to its verse. The earlier √cakṣ source note already quotes *viśvā* with *rūpā* as object. We record the new locus with zero new reading credit. The exact internal affix analysis of this shorter reading stays Open. [Text, pada and commentary](https://sa.wikisource.org/w/index.php?oldid=199814)
* RV 10.71.4 contrasts two masculine nominative subjects and a dative recipient. Verse 7 compares groups among masculine plural friends. Verse 8 places *tvam* as the object of a third-person plural verb and *tve* with the plural verb of going about. The pada text isolates *tvam*, a genuine homograph of second-person nominative *tvam*. Agreement, verbal government and the verse's contrast identify the indefinite reading. These five inflectional keys of vowel-final *tva*, including the instrumental in 4.18.2, now have direct witnesses. Kāśikā's competing accent and stem traditions at 1.1.27 still require independent historical adjudication.
