# PG966 · The Ṛbhus give and Agni bears or gives

Mādhavīya 741 reports that some teachers admit giving alongside holding and nourishing for juhotyādi ḍudhāñ (1030011). [PG935](../dha_remaining_actor_categories/README.md) admitted the latter two meanings in the ordinary actor paradigms. Two early-verse contexts now give independent evidence for what a giving interpretation actually selects.

**The command in Śākala RV 1.20.7.** The speakers address the Ṛbhus. The object is the wealth and Sāyaṇa identifies the Soma-presser in the dative as the beneficiary. He paraphrases the imperative *dhattana* by *prayacchata* and explains the Vedic replacement of ta by tana under 7.1.45. The Vedaweb annotation identifies √dhā, second plural present-system imperative, active. The verse has no preverb on this predicate. Supply loṭ on the request, the original P second-plural ending, the weak reduplicated root as in the inherited *dhatta* and the special Vedic ending. The final -na belongs to that ending. The pada text marks the finite word unaccented in this clause. Keep that observation distinct from lexical root accent and from accent on the neighbouring numeral. Dayānanda's paraphrase uses a third-plural imperative and explains the shift by *vyatyaya*. This is a separate interpretation of person in the paraphrase. The recited form is identical. One finite request and one reading are credited. PG755/768 retain the nominal, group and accent work already attached to this verse. **The epithet in Śākala RV 1.1.1.** *Ratnadhā́tamam* agrees with Agni, the object of praise, in the masculine accusative singular. Yāska's Nirukta 7.15 glosses it as the foremost giver of desirable wealth. The immediately preceding naming of Aurṇavābha concerns the derivation of *hotṛ*. Keep the wealth gloss at its own textual address. Sāyaṇa cites the giving gloss and also offers bearing or nourishing the treasures that are ritual rewards. His explicit analysis uses *ratnāni dadhāti*, placing the root of the inner member under the object relation. The outer superlative under 5.3.55 takes **tamap**, yielding *tama* after loss of the marker. The case ending belongs to the entire adjective. Sāyaṇa gives two accounts of the accent on the compound base, a final compound accent or retention of the kṛt final member's accent. He assigns no independent udātta to the p-marked degree suffix. Both paths give the observed accent on dhā. One inflected epithet and one accented reading are credited. The alternative meanings and accent explanations stay labelled within it. **Boundary and recovery.** The two verses establish a giving imperative and an explicitly interpreted giver epithet. They do not determine which teachers Mādhavīya meant or the distribution of a giving sense over other voices, preverbs, persons and categories.
