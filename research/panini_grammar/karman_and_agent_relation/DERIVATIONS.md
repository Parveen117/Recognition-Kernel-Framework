# Derivation and scope

## Recover the whole milking clause

Pāṇinā kāṃsyapātryāṃ gāṃ dogdhi payaḥ supplies a hand (instrument), a bronze vessel (locus), a cow (secondary karman on the akathita reading) and milk (principal karman). The current finite word has its own understood actor. Kāśikā explicitly excludes the already designated hand and vessel from the residual secondary-karman rule. A missing noun in the text and absence of a competing grammatical designation are different conditions. The neuter payaḥ is accusative in this construction even though its spelling coincides with a nominative.

## Carry both old and new events across ṇic

In pāṭhayati māṇavakaṃ vedam the causee is the reader of the Veda. Keep those two accusatives in their underlying roles. In the specified cooking counterexample the base cook instead appears in the instrumental. In causative kṛ the source allows either accusative or instrumental causee. Kāśikā prints the alternate instrumental as a continuation; the normalized record restores its common phrase. The direct basic/causative passive-selection policy remains in the previously reviewed [PG causative domain](../causative_passive/DOMAIN_AND_KARMAN.md).

## Simultaneous titles versus alternatives

The causer in 1.4.55 acquires both hetu and kartṛ. This is one participant, not a choice of two cases. Hetu supports the formation with ṇic and outer kartṛ supports finite expression in the admitted kartari construction. Compare 1.4.53's genuine alternatives on a different participant. In 1.4.54 sthālī pacati the pot is itself construed as independent; PG136's sthālyāṃ pacati instead supplies the pot as locus. Store the construed perspective explicitly.
