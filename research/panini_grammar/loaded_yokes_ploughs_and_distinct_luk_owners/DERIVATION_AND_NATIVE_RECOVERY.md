# PG733 · Yokes, carts and ploughs

## Source situation and the accent of dhaureya

The frozen PG732 bare noun begins at **01.0654 dhurvī hiṃsārtha** with **kvip**, no preverb, and the documented lopa of the lexical v. Its long free citation **dhūḥ** and short internal **dhur** keep their earlier histories. Here the carrying predicate is bare **01.1159 vah prāpaṇe**, kartari in the cited literal ACC relation. No finite form is credited for explanatory vahati.

The 1910 Madurai Siddhāntakaumudī with Bālamanoramā prints **धुरो यड्ढञौ** on p.807 under 4.4.77. The facing continuation gives **यड्ढकाविति पाठान्तरम्**. It also names P **8.2.79** for the short u where the accent-independent yat construction has bha. This is direct print evidence for both addresses. It adds a specific basis for the **ḍhañ** also present in the Padamañjarī explanation. PG732's exact Vasu and Śabdakaustubha **ḍhak** remain their own branch. The digital Bāla **ḍhakañ** report and the wider manuscript relations remain Open.

In the new positive **ḍhañ** arm the marker ñ is retained in history after its physical lopa. The later marker-sensitive operations consult that history. At P **7.2.117** the initial-vowel vṛddhi changes u to au. The ḍh-to-eya realization comes from **7.1.2** and yields **dháureya**, with the ñit initial udātta under **6.1.197**. The older **ḍhak** selects grade by **7.2.118** instead. The two histories map to the same segmental stem and case slots. No new dhaureya case count follows from newly fixing either source label or tone. Haima's differently owned yaṭ gives a genuinely new female reading below.

## The head noun and the attaching kha

The actual semantic inputs of 4.4.78–79 are feminine, even when the sūtra uses the shorter cited base. For **sarvā ca sā dhūś ca** and **ekā ca sā dhūś ca**, **P 2.1.49** forms the co-referential tatpuruṣa, **5.4.74** adds a after the relevant non-axle compound-final dhur, and **2.4.26** carries the head's gender into the whole. The exposed a takes **ṭāp** at **4.1.4**. This supplies **sarvadhurā** and **ekadhurā** at the separate cases of the objects themselves. Nyāsa and Padamañjarī recognize the short canonical mention as a śabdarūpa in this context. Padamañjarī's textual discussion quotes the beginning pūrvakāla. The project numbering is **2.1.49**. The non-axle limit belongs to the samāsānta operation, so the named yokes need no invented analysis as an akṣa.

At **4.4.78** start with the actual accusative **sarvadhurām**. The semantic input removes the case ending independently of the stem's provenance. Apply **kha → īna** under **7.1.2** and delete the base-final a/ā in its **6.4.148** environment. Now **sarvadhur + īna** gives **sarvadhurīṇa** by **8.4.1–2**, since r reaches the n through ī. Kāśikā specifically licenses **dakṣiṇadhurīṇa** and **uttaradhurīṇa** by separately taking kha at the stated split. Their directions enter the internal taddhitārtha compound discussed by Padamañjarī and Bhaṭṭoji. Haima **7.1.4** has the named **vāma** group and explicitly lists the four derivatives. It also reports **sarvadhurya** via ya on the same referent. Other members depend on actual usage.

The original ṭāp base has genitive plural **sarvadhurāṇām** or **ekadhurāṇām**, with r reaching to the ordinary ending. In an **īṇa** derivative, the nearer stem-final n/ṇ interrupts the route to the later n in the case suffix. The written instrumental is **sarvadhurīṇena** and the genitive plural **sarvadhurīṇānām**. In **sarvadhurya** the intervening y is in aṭ. Therefore **sarvadhuryeṇa** and **sarvadhuryāṇām** keep the expected case ṇatva. The same witness-backed classifier applies to the analogous named stems. Independently attested **dhurīṇa** is the bare-dhur extension attributed to another Haima teacher at 7.1.4. It supplies 25 new readings at already counted masculine keys.

## Two outer owners at ekadhurā and one inner effect

The shared input in P **4.4.79** is **ekadhurām**. The unelided arm adds and sounds kha, giving **ekadhurīṇa**. There are two textual accounts of the second arm.

| Policy | Selected outer event | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Kāśikā, Bāla and Vasu | Select kha and apply its optional luk. | No sounded īna. The masculine result is ekadhura. |
| Tattvabodhinī | The positive ca has kha; luk belongs to the separate inherited yat. | No sounded ya. The masculine result is ekadhura. |

Nyāsa allows inference of yat from an otherwise unowned luk in his argument for why the kha provision has work. Padamañjarī distinguishes making kha available through ca from what could be read as inherited yat in a hypothetical wording. Bhaṭṭoji's printed argument recognizes a sounded kha arm from ca and says that a wording with no ca could instead prescribe yat-luk. These are recorded scope explanations. In either actual silent arm the resulting taddhita-luk licenses **P 1.2.49** to delete the inner **ṭāp**, expressly as explained by Amarasudhā for the named masculine form. The surviving **a** belongs to samāsānta. **1.1.60–61** give the disappearance and its type. Keep the record of the selected outer affix through the event, while **1.1.62–63** distinguish surviving affix properties from the prohibited aṅga effect of a lumat suffix. In particular no 6.4.148 contact with a sounded initial ī or y is supplied by either silent affix. The explicit condition of 1.2.49 consults occurrence of a taddhita-luk event. The case surface is **ekadhureṇa** in the instrumental by the ordinarily unobstructed route from r.

Haima **7.1.5** directly licenses audible **a** and **īna** on both named object genders. Keep **ekā dhūḥ**, **ekasya dhūḥ** and **ekā dhūr asmin** as three inputs, the last with a bahuvrīhi referring to a one-yoke object. The word-level accusative may be **ekadhurām** for either of the yoke bases or **ekadhuram** for the outside referent. They yield the same two stem surfaces under three role keys. The shared one-yoke role can also retain the previously specified P histories. The independently owned a in H stays distinct from P's two lopa operations.

At H **7.1.3** the reported **yaṭ** (voiced at the following vowel in the cited affix pair) carries the ṭ marker. The actual print gives **dhurī** with a feminine ṅī. Use H **2.4.20** to select ṅī after the ṭit nominal derivative, **2.4.86** for the loss of the preceding affix a, and **2.4.88** for loss of the consonant-following taddhita y before the same female ending. The same report's **eyakaṇ** is already positive in PG732 and receives no repeat credit.

## Cart and plough carriers under two case relations

The named category in **4.4.80** is carrying the actual cart, **śakaṭam**, with **aṇ**. The owned ṇ licenses **7.2.117** vṛddhi and the terminal a drops under **6.4.148**, giving **śākaṭ + a → śākaṭa**. The nominal result applies when one carries the two carts too. Construct **dvi + śakaṭa** as the declared numeral compound with the cart head, select aṇ on that complete input, change the initial i to ai, and preserve the second member's vowel: **dvaiśakaṭa**. The extension from the named stem to the dependent compound is expressly justified by the existence of the special rule, according to Tattvabodhinī and Padamañjarī. The same qualification has a companion at **4.4.81**.

For **hala** and **sīra** the new accusative prescription is **ṭhak**. Let **7.2.118** continue the taddhita-grade operation with its k marker and **7.3.50** read ṭh as ika. Combine initial grade and terminal a-loss to get **hālika** and **sairika**. On **dvi + hala**, **dvi + sīra** and **tri + sīra** that yields **dvaihalika**, **dvaisīrika** and **traisīrika**. The P rule addresses the first vowel of the whole input. A second long vowel is not supplied by this operation. H **7.1.6 ikaṇ** and **7.1.7 aṇ** are the independently owned parallel selections. The two rules and the named compound construction supply their exact stem and quantity domain. In the shared classical case table the r of **sairika, dvaisīrika, traisīrika** reaches past the k, permitted by ku, and intervening vowels at the instrumental singular and genitive plural. Each of the seven retained P aṇ/ṭhak stems gives a feminine by **4.1.15** and final a-loss. The corresponding Haima licensed affixes use **2.4.20** with **2.4.86**. These are separately traced constructions even where a case shape coincides.

The older **4.3.120** aṇ and **4.3.124** ṭhak select the stated genitive relation. The prāgdīvyatīya **4.1.88** makes a non-descendant dvigu carrier on this route lose the taddhita, yielding **dviśakaṭa**, **dvihala** and **dvisīra** without a surviving grade effect. This GEN reading concerns the term related to the two things. The current pragghitīya ACC derivation can describe the same bearer and keeps the affix. Haima preserves the two explicit clause frames and the old H **6.1.24** conditioned lopa behind the GEN route. Another Haima report permits the genitive frame and declines the independent literal carrying prescription in these names, with the additional restriction on extending hala or sīra as the head of a compound. This latter view stays tied to the report. A digging predicate with an instrument continues to belong to **4.4.2** and the earlier documented implement words.

The direct 1896 Vasu and the Śabdakaustubha p.220 prints have **द्वैहालिकः**, with a long second vowel. Padamañjarī's available text agrees. Nyāsa and Tattvabodhinī give **द्वैहलिकः**. Preserve both exact readings in the source register. For the long word the evidence still calls for a supported second grade or independently licensed alternate stem. The same Śabdakaustubha print has **द्वैशकटिकः** under its local rule 79, corresponding to P 4.4.80. Its actual extra ika is a separate source observation, with its derivation still Open. Vasu, Tattvabodhinī and the independent Haima example give the derived **dvaiśakaṭa**. Neither open textual shape is silently applied across the written case panels.

## Full PG14 carrier and zero specialization

Use PG14's free `*`-algebra **B=ℂ⟨zₑ,zₑ*⟩** and typed free right modules **Mλ(G)=⊕ₕ∈G eₕB** on full source-qualified histories. Inputs **I** carry root identity, preverb, voice, named base, gender, argument relation and source policy. Intermediate types include original compound parts and flexible numerical composition, pre-affix lexical acceptance, a positively selected affix grade **K** or **Y**, a distinct post-luk grade **L_K** or **L_Y**, and optional sounded paths. The appropriate target **Z** is the completed case and independent tone history. Genitive possessives, licensed directions and the Haima audible a have separate legs. One case output can have several histories.

Let **D_K** and **D_Y** be the constant diagonal 0/1 guards at the typed selection stage for kha and yat. The source guards lead to disjoint history bases. **U_K:Mλ(K)→Mλ(L_K)** and **U_Y:Mλ(Y)→Mλ(L_Y)** are edges storing the identity of the lost suffix and its lopa event. Each edge **e:h→h′** is the PG14 operator **Tₑ(eₕa)=eₕ′(1+zₑ)a**, zero on other basis coordinates. The reverse edge in the pairing adjoint uses **1+zₑ***. **R_K** and **R_Y** map their own post-luk grade to the appropriate ṭāp-luk history. They test the actual preceding event, keep its coordinate, and use a separate edge and generator for each target. The older GEN condition and its lopa have their own typed maps. Taking a union of admissible branches uses a direct sum of target histories with source labels and complementary masks, leaving all witnesses intact. Forget the branch identity only in the later segmental readout.

For each forward edge and adjoint **ε((1+zₑ)a)=ε(a)**, with the same identity for the star.
