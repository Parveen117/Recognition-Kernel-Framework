### A dated printed witness for the separate command

The British Library Endangered Archives Programme identifies *Paṭṭikāviyam*, EAP918/10/91, as a Grantha-script Sanskrit print of 1874 held by the Roja Muthiah Research Library, Chennai. On canvas **237**, the verse beginning *samagadhvaṃ puraḥ śatroḥ* contains *modayadhvaṃ raghūttamam*. Its second half mentions fear, Sītā and Daśānana. These four controls identify Trivedi VII.103 and GRETIL VII.101. Below the verse the commentary presents the joined prohibition *modāhadhvam* with attribution to the Subodhinī commentator. Near the foot of the same page it separately quotes **āhadhvaṃ mā raghūttamam** and labels it **iti kvacit pāṭhaḥ**. The immediately following discussion turns on the intransitive restriction and the difficulty of the middle with an expressed object. The continuation on canvas **238** passes to the reading about disclosing fear and then to the marriage clause about Sītā. The regional images in STATE make the last lines and page turn inspectable.

This places the separate alternative in an identifiable print 24 years before Trivedi’s 1898 volume. ### Readings assigned to the Paṭṭaṇa manuscript

Trivedi identifies **P** as an undated manuscript from Paṭṭaṇa extending into canto X. Haridhara R. Desai collated it and sent the manuscript to Trivedi for a second collation. This is the editor’s report of P. The following decisions have apparatus-level authority only.

| Location in Trivedi | Reading and grammatical consequence |
| --- | --- |
| VII.102, line 2 | P reads *sma* for *ca*. The prohibition now has an explicitly reported *mā sma* particle sequence. PG868 already treats the budh cell. The next appendix entry is the canto end. Its silence at VII.103 supplies no reading for P there. |
| VIII.101, line 2 | P reads singular *talpam* for plural *talpān*. On either reading the departing rākṣasīs can be plural subjects. The number of their destination is separately specified. PG870 already registers the singular and plural alternatives. |
| After VIII.103 | P appends *yugmakam*, marking a pair of stanzas. This supplies witness-level evidence for joining Hanumān’s circumstances in VIII.102 to the finite predicate in VIII.103. It does not resolve the separate *maithilyā/maithilyāḥ* variant. PG872 owns the construction. |
| VIII.109, both lines | P reads **vāriṇā** for **vā vinā** and **neha** for **naiva**. In the base, PG871 relates instrumental *vainateyena* to the overt privative *vinā*, beside *pṛthak* and the wind. P’s first replacement is a single instrumental form for water. The text no longer supplies *vinā* as a governor of *vainateyena* on that branch. The two instrumental forms and *pṛthak* require a fresh analysis. The second replacement permits *na + iha*, giving the question a local deictic element. Its change of polarity is zero, since both negatives remain negatives. |
| VIII.110, lines 1 and 2 | The base has feminine accusatives **cintāvatīm** and **muktām**, with Hanumān as the elephant-like monkey who approaches Sītā and speaks. P reads **cintāvatī** and **muktā**, both feminine nominative singular. Their agreement with one another survives. The accusative-object attachment of the two words to *samāsādya* no longer follows from their endings. The apparent nominative does not license changing the masculine subject of *proce* into Sītā. The manuscript syntax remains open, including the possibility of a change of construction or an imperfect transmission. |
