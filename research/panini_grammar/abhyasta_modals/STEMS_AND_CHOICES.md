# PG25 — Owned copies, vowel queries and dependent choices

Author: Monty. 2 November 2026.

## M1. Copy construction before root operations

For the twelve class-3 roots, 2.4.75 supplies ślu and 6.1.10 the
owned repetition. Retain the PG10/11 copy procedures, source links,
shortening, halādiśeṣa, palatalization and copy deaspiration.
7.4.75 gives copy guṇa only to nij/vij/viṣ; 7.4.76 gives copy i
to bhṛ/māṅ/hāṅ; 7.4.77 gives the reviewed i replacement to
pṝ/pṛ/ṛ. Hāk remains outside 7.4.76's three-root list.

| Root(s) | Pronounced copy before its later vowel seam |
|---|---|
| bhī, bhṛ | bi |
| hrī, hāṅ | ji |
| pṝ, pṛ | pi |
| māṅ | mi |
| hāk | ja |
| nij | ne |
| vij, viṣ | ve |
| ṛ | i, subsequently iy before the unlike root vowel |

For ṛ, retain i+r followed by deletion of generated r in the copy;
do not replace the whole history with an unexplained i. Then 6.4.78
supplies copy iy before the unlike root vowel. The root's guṇa/yaṇ
is a distinct operation. For hrī, loss of r from the copy does not
delete the root-internal hr cluster.

Jāgṛ, daridrā and cakās receive 6.1.6 designation only. They have
no manufactured repeated owner and do not undergo the copy-table rules.

## M2. Final-ik guṇa and light-upadhā guṇa have different guards

At strong ordinary endings, 7.3.84 gives the final-root changes
bhī→bhe, hrī→hre, pṝ/pṛ→par, bhṛ→bhar and ṛ→ar.
7.3.85 supplies jāgṛ's addressed guṇa; its explicit ṅit exclusion
retains jāgṛ in the weak fields. At a vowel seam e→ay by 6.1.78.

Nij/vij/viṣ instead query light-upadhā guṇa under 7.3.86. Their
strong consonant-initial tu gives nej/vej/veṣ. But **7.3.87 retains
the inherited light-upadhā target**: before vowel-initial pit āṭ
the root i stays i, while the earlier copy e stays e. Thus the first
persons are nenijāni/nenijai, vevijāni/vevijai and veviṣāṇi/veviṣai.

This restriction does not prohibit all abhyasta guṇa. Final-ik bhṛ
still gives **bibharāṇi**, bhī **bibhayāni**, hrī **jihrayāṇi**,
pṝ/pṛ **piparāṇi**, ṛ **iyarāṇi**, and jāgṛ **jāgarāṇi**.
Kāśikā/Bālamanoramā expressly retain the light-upadhā restriction;
their examples separate nenijāni from the final-vowel family.

In weak contexts, final ik and the three light upadhās retain their
unstrengthened values, subject to the specific M3/M4 operations below.
Strong/weak does not alter the already owned lexical copy map.

## M3. Three non-ghu ā contracts

Apply 6.4.112's weak ā-lopa except where its hal-conditioned
specialization or lexical exception governs the query.

| Root | Strong ordinary | Weak before actual vowel | Weak before ordinary hal |
|---|---|---|---|
| māṅ | mi∣mā | mi∣m | mi∣mī by 6.4.113 |
| hāṅ | ji∣hā | ji∣h | ji∣hī by 6.4.113 |
| daridrā | daridrā | daridr | daridri by 6.4.114 |
| hāk | ja∣hā | ja∣h | ja∣{hi,hī}, with the refinements below |

Daridrā's i is short and mandatory in this field. Therefore O P is
**daridriyāt**, while its ordinary 3pl is **daridratu**. The
ārdhadhātuka account producing āśīrliṅ daridryāt is outside D.

Māṅ/hāṅ O A uses the resolved ī-facing source view inherited from
PG19: delete root ā under 6.4.112, retain the modal ī and its owner.
This gives mimīta/jihīta. A different schedule retaining root ī and
then coalescing ī+ī would have different history; equal words do not
prove that adapter. First-person ordinary strong ā is instead retained
before its seam, giving mimai/jihai and their āvahai/āmahai forms.

Hāk's 6.4.116 gives i optionally beside the ī of 6.4.113 before
ordinary weak hal. **At actual hi**, new 6.4.117 adds the ā outcome:

\[
 ja\mid hā\mid hi\quad\leadsto\quad
 \{jahāhi,jahihi,jahīhi\}.
\]

At **actual y-initial sārvadhātuka**, 6.4.118 preempts that i/ī
choice and deletes ā: **jahyāt/jahyātām/jahyuḥ**. Its source view
queries the hāk ā before a generic ī choice can conceal the target.
There is no jahi-yāt/jahī-yāt option on this selected domain.

At B's **tāt**, hi is no longer the actual ending. Therefore hāk
has exactly **jahitāt/jahītāt**, with no jahātāt from 6.4.117.
The three hi options and the two tāt options are separate fibres.

## M4. Bhī, hrī and the two pṛ entries

6.4.115 gives **bhī→{bhi,bhī}** before weak hal-initial
sārvadhātuka. This includes ordinary tām/hi/tam/ta, O's actual y,
and B's tāt. It excludes actual vowels and all strong contexts.
Thus O has both bibhiyāt/bibhīyāt throughout its nine slots.
Hrī has no such lexical short-i option: weak hrī remains long before
hal, including **jihrīyāt**. The contrary Laghu literal is retained
and discussed in R; no extra operation is inferred from it.

Before weak vowels, bhī's root i-vowel has no root-internal preceding
cluster, and the whole aṅga is anekāc: 6.4.82 gives y, hence
bibhyatu. Hrī's hr cluster excludes that rule; 6.4.77 gives iy,
hence jihriyatu. The deleted copy r does not change this root test.

For pṝ, 7.1.102 with r-accompaniment gives root ur where the
strong guṇa does not preempt it. 8.2.77 lengthens u before an actual
following hal, including y: **pipūrtām, pipūrhi, pipūryāt**.
Before the actual a of ordinary 3pl retain **pipuratu**.
The short-pṛ entry does not meet the ṝ target: it gives **pipṛtām,
pipṛhi, pipṛyāt**, and yaṇ gives **pipratu** before a.

Bhṛ and ṛ retain weak ṛ before consonants; before vowels 6.1.77
gives r. Thus bibhṛhi/iyṛhi coexist with bibhratu/iyratu and
O A bibhrīta. These are actual vowel seams, not a root-wide ṛ→r.

## M5. Exhaustion of stem choices

**Proposition M5.** The only stem-choice multiplicities on D are
bhī's two weak-hal readings, hāk's two weak-hal readings, and hāk's
three actual-hi readings. Hāk before y has one continuation.

**Proof.** M1 fixes the copy or noncopy history. M2 partitions the
guṇa targets and ending contexts. M3 partitions the four ā roots,
with hi/y/tāt predicates resolved before the corresponding choice.
M4 partitions bhī/hrī and final ṛ/ṝ by the exact lexical and next-
sound conditions. All other entries retain one stem at each query. □
