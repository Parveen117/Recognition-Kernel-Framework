## R1. Shared source interfaces, not fresh unrestricted rules

PG10/11 supply the copy procedures, lexical abhyasta designation,
qualified padas, pṛ/pṝ distinction and ṛ's classical license.
PG19–24 supply modal meanings, effective markers, endings, tātaṅ and
the retained sound policy. PG25 adds the complete I/O/B contexts of D2.
Full source entries include discussion outside that domain; copying an
entry does not admit every example or host rule in it.

## R2. Hāk: hi, y and tāt are different queries

Kāśikā 6.4.116 gives optional i beside ī at weak hal. Its 6.4.117
explicitly lists jahāhi/jahihi/jahīhi. Kaumudī agrees, and
Tattvabodhinī cites the three forms together from Bhaṭṭi. Kāśikā
6.4.118 explicitly gives jahyāt/jahyātām/jahyuḥ. Bālamanoramā
identifies the y-lopa as the exception to 6.4.116; Tattvabodhinī
retains the sārvadhātuka condition, contrasting āśīrliṅ heyāt.

Thus the generic i/ī choice must not conceal ā before the addressed
y query. Nor does 6.4.117's actual hi field apply to B tāt. The
latter retains the two weak-consonant choices. This supplies the new
operative 6.4.117 clause and the dependent counts in N4/P.

Hāṅ is a different lexical entry. 7.4.76 explicitly includes it
beside bhṛ/māṅ and excludes the following hāk. PG25 retains those
owners and exact identities rather than generalizing from the spelling hā.

## R3. Daridrā and the selected A modal-augment view

Kāśikā 6.4.114 gives short-i weak forms such as daridritaḥ.
The Kaumudī field there prints दरिद्रतः; PG11 already recorded
that mismatch and selected the rule-supported short i. PG25 retains
that decision. Bālamanoramā 6.4.112 explicitly supplies the modal
daridrātu/daridritāt/daridritām/daridratu and daridrāṇi series,
and explains **daridriyāt** by its sārvadhātuka i prescription.
Its separate āśīrliṅ discussion is not a second O option.

For māṅ/hāṅ O A retain PG19's resolved-augment query view:
root ā-lopa before modal ī. Kāśikā 6.4.112/113 and the lexical
Kaumudī/Bālamanoramā 6.4.113 discussion supply the ā-lopa/hal-ī
partition and mimīta-family forms. A schedule that retains ī in
the root and then merges it with modal ī may print the same result;
the present packet does not prove that its history is equivalent.

## R4. Only light-upadhā guṇa is prohibited by 7.3.87

Kāśikā and Bālamanoramā 7.3.87 explicitly carry the light-upadhā
target. The former gives nenijāni/vevijāni and distinguishes final-
vowel juhavāni; the latter supplies nenijai/nenijāvahai and the
parallel vij/viṣ account. Their final-root examples elsewhere include
bibharāṇi, bibhayāni, piparāṇi and iyarāṇi. The guṇa prohibition
cannot be broadened merely from the word abhyasta.

7.4.75 fixes the copy e of nij/vij/viṣ independently of their root
query. Kāśikā 7.3.85 and the addressed Bālamanoramā entry give
jāgṛ's ṅit exclusion, jāgṛhi/jāgratu and jāgarāṇi. Their liṭ
and other guṇa discussion does not import those moods into PG25.

## R5. Bhī/hrī and ṛ/ṝ

Kāśikā 6.4.115 explicitly limits short i to bhī before weak hal,
excluding bibhyati and strong bibheti. Bālamanoramā at that rule
gives **bibhiyāt/bibhīyāt** as well as both ordinary hi and tāt
readings. Its duplicate बिभीतम् — बिभीतम् is retained as a
transcription issue; the rule's i/ī option supplies the two distinct
constructed tam forms.

Hrī has the root-internal hr cluster. Kāśikā 6.4.82 supplies the
root-owned cluster condition; 6.4.77 supplies iy when that exception
does not apply. This gives jihriyatu before a vowel, while hal-facing
hrī remains long in jihrīhi/jihrīyāt. The Laghu 6.4.115 literal
**जिह्रियात्** conflicts with the latter construction. It is retained
without silently repairing it or adding an unsupported short-i option.
Bālamanoramā's nearby hrī spellings contain repeated ह्य for hr,
including **जिह्यीयात्**; that literal is not a clean exact-word
witness for the normalized constructed output. The source-relative
rule derivation, rather than either anomalous token alone, determines P.

Kāśikā/Kaumudī 7.1.102 supply labial-preceded ṝ→ur and give
strong guṇa precedence. 8.2.77 supplies u-length before actual hal.
The lexical Kaumudī/Bālamanoramā discussion at **7.4.12** gives
pipūryāt versus short-pṛ pipṛyāt and their modal contrasts.
The host liṭ shortening rule is not applied in D. The commentary
also preserves the qualification concerning the short-pṛ entry;
PG25 continues PG10's explicit two-entry source contract, without
claiming an unrestricted adjudication of every Dhātupāṭha tradition.

Ṛ retains the PG11 classical-use license explicitly explained at
7.3.87. Kaumudī gives iyṛtaḥ/iyrati/iyarāṇi/iyṛyāt; the
larger Bālamanoramā passage explains copy i+r, its retained r-lopa,
copy iy and the separate root guṇa/yaṇ. Its additional Vedic roots
are not added to the domain.

## R6. Cakās source choice and other damaged tokens

The 6.4.112 lexical commentary explicitly distinguishes **cakāddhi**
under a sic-restricted s-lopa account from the Bhāṣya-supported
**cakādhi**. Kāśikā 8.2.25 records both sides of that discussion.
S4 continues PG11/24's chosen broad root-s deletion before a dh-initial
affix. The alternative account remains visible, but is not counted as
a free pronunciation option within the chosen policy.

Other raw tokens include Bālamanoramā 6.4.113 **मिमतात्** in its
ordinary A plural list and 6.4.112 **चिकास्ताम्**, **चटकास्तम्**.
PG25 constructs mimatām, cakāstām and cakāstam from the retained
root and prescribed ending; it does not claim exact matches to those
tokens. Kaumudī 7.4.75's **नेनिग्ध** omits the expected final i
of the constructed hi→dhi form; its literal is preserved. Full entries
can contain other out-of-domain transcription problems not adjudicated here.

## R7. Evidence boundary

The completed statements are written, source-relative constructions
and the native theorems on their declared domain. Literal extracts,
source hashes and metadata arithmetic are not independent Sanskrit
review, runtime execution or proof-assistant validation. PG25's new
written clause adds 6.4.117. Separately, the T143 ledger adds 7.3.88,
7.3.91, 8.2.73 and 8.2.74 to the prior combined union, giving 574
distinct IDs. These companion additions retain their declared levels;
they do not import their laṅ or other contexts into D. Mere commentary
locations and outside-domain mentions do not add written operations.
