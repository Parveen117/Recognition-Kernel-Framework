# PG851 · A month spent sleeping and the village as grammatical object

In the 1983 Mādhavīya p. 339, entry 26 śīṅ svapne, the author places three non-agentive readings beside the causative discussion. The Sanskrit text first gives grāmo ’dhiśayyate and invokes adhiśīṅsthāsām. Then it gives the bare bhāva śayyate devadattena, followed by śayyate māsaṃ devadattena and śayyate māso devadattena. The duration alternation represents an actual change in what the verb expresses. The instrumental sleeper is Devadatta throughout the last two examples. The named village is the support of the posture. The passage does not license treating any unrelated object as a patient of bare sleep.

Dual and plural are written agreement extensions. The speaker may supply the instrumental devadattena to either duration row. Read the dual and plural analytically at word boundaries (in connected recitation grāmau + adhi… has an external vowel junction); nominative plural grāmāḥ has the shown prevocalic readout. The printed nominative singular month becomes māso before devadattena; before ś the table uses its ordinary visarga spelling. Māsau is syncretic between nominative and accusative. Changing only the verbal number separates the two complete dual clauses. A speaker describing two months can therefore choose between singular event predication and dual duration predication *on the stated duration-object analysis*. Two sleepers in an instrumental dual would still leave the event singular.

**Source decisions.** 2.3.5 gives accusative extent when the time is occupied throughout. It does not itself assign object status. For the other duration reading the Mādhavīya invokes the treatment of time, activity, path and destination as objects with verbs like ās; admit its exemplified time member here. For the village the decisive rule is 1.4.46 and the preverb adhi. Kāśikā's active grāmam adhiśete is already constructed in the instrument-and-locus register. Under 3.4.69 the marked place is expressed by the passive ending, leaving grāma in the nominative. A bare locative of place receives no object entitlement from the prefixed rule.

**One sound junction, different owners.** Middle under 1.3.13, yak under 3.1.67 in bhāva or karman, present endings under 3.4.78–79. Before this y-initial kit affix 7.4.22 replaces the root-final vowel with marker-bearing ayaṅ. The surviving root contribution is śay; yak contributes the next y and its a. Thus śay + ya yields śayya-, giving singular śayyate, dual śayyete and plural śayyante. The digital Kāśikā at 7.4.22 has the anomalous first example श्य्यते; its prose names the substitute, its 3.1.67 field gives शय्यते भवता, and the printed Mādhavīya gives the same full stem. Preserve the short digital string as its own transcription without creating a grammar alternative. The same chapter uses śāyayati of a conscious person caused to sleep. Its ṇic and the causer's agreement do not occur here.
