# PG973 · The penalty that is to fall and the two passive stem families

In the Sundarakāṇḍa, Vibhīṣaṇa asks that the penalty fall on those who sent the messenger. The GRETIL text numbers the verse 5.50.10. The Sanskrit text with Marathi translation at Satsangdhara gives the same clause at 5.52.20. In *teṣv ayaṃ pātyatāṃ daṇḍo yair ayaṃ preṣitaḥ kapiḥ*, the singular *daṇḍaḥ* governs passive *pātyatām*. The people on whom the penalty should fall are locative plural. In the relative clause those senders are the instrumental agents of the dispatch, and the monkey is nominative singular. Neither the plural locative nor the relative instrumental selects a plural ending on *pātyatām*. The final m of the independent verb appears as anusvāra before d in the verse. The penalty construction is figurative. The matching command form supplies evidence for inflection while the physical patient reading of PG970 keeps its own semantic guard. In laṅ the initial a is aṭ before the verbal formation. In the imperative no such augment is present. The similar dual and second-plural endings in those categories have different histories. In ordinary liṅ the middle ending receives sīyuṭ. Following deletion of the applicable s, its y survives before a vowel-initial ending, as in *pātyeyātām*. Before a consonant the derived e appears without that y, as in *pātyeran*. The other y immediately after pāt belongs to yaK throughout. Compare *aśoṣyata*, a finite past predicate with initial augment, to Gītā's nominal *aśoṣyaḥ* with initial privative a.
