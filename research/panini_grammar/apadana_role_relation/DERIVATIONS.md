# Derivation and scope

## Derive the case from the relation

First choose the intended event and the particular participant to classify. On a positive row, one of 1.4.24–31 assigns apādāna to that participant. Then 2.3.28 assigns the ablative in the 2.3.1 unexpressed domain. Kāśikā 2.3.28 independently gives grāmād āgacchati and adhyayanāt parājayate. Its further vārttikas record additional uses of the fifth case. Completeness here is confined to the eight selected designations and the specified examples.

## Preserve intention and perspective

For 1.4.28 the desire to avoid being seen is decisive. Kāśikā explicitly permits actual sight in the presence of that intention. For 1.4.29, disciplined learning is the guard. The performer’s song exemplifies the contrast; the text gives a genitive. For 1.4.30–31 the material from which something arises and the source of first manifestation have separate labels. The biological or historical truth of an ancient example is irrelevant to the grammar of the example.

## Read backward with a finite fiber

Araṇye bibheti and caurebhyo bibheti share the verb: ask whether the participant is the scene or the threat. In upādhyāyād adhīte and upādhyāyād nilīyate the very same separated ablative can occupy two different semantic slots. Source text may show final sandhi (grāmād, śṛṅgāc); store grāmāt and śṛṅgāt for the lexical-case interface. A bare plural -bhyaḥ is compatible with the case syncretism in 4.1.2. A source-assigned ablative is one resolved reading in this packet.
