# PG770 · Read the governor of each occurrence

Under 5.1.124, karman means the activity characteristic of the base. The alternative bhāva is its state. A supplement separately permits designated words to stand for their own referents. Śaṅkara on Bhagavadgītā 4.13 identifies the four classes themselves as the referent of cāturvarṇyam. In Manu 8.332 the occurrence of steyam as a predicate of bhavet illustrates the name for a kind of taking. Here 125 joins yat to loss of the n in stena. In the alternative division attributed by Kāśikā to some grammarians, ṣyañ licenses stainyam and the n remains. Its occurrence in that grammatical account does not give it a second textual occurrence in Manu.

In RV 4.23.5 the two identical forms sakhyam stand on opposite sides of the verse. In the first question Indra enjoys the mortal's friendship. In the second Sāyaṇa supplies bhavet: the conduct of Indra as friend would be there for the friends. The word is accusative in the first clause and nominative on this reading of the second. In 6 it is again an object under the act of declaring. The same commentator expressly cites 5.1.126 at RV 1.11.2 and identifies affix accent on locative sakhye. Keep his explicit suffix identification independent of the interpretation of the relationship. At 126 plain ya on dūta or vaṇij is supplementary. The earlier Ṛgvedic and Yajurvedic accusatives of the envoy word keep their original case owners. The new dūtyam is the grammatical citation nominative. The strengthened trade noun instead has the brāhmaṇādi history. At 127 the two possible senses apply to both listed bases.

The purposes in RV 10.85.36 attach to different acts. The speaker takes the hand for saubhagatva; the gods have given the bride for gārhapatya. PG736 already owns the household dative and separately owns the dative with a fire referent. Haradattamiśra interprets the latter wedding purpose as householder status. In his account of the parallel Pāraskara rite, Gadādhara adds service of the future household fire as a possibility. For the first dative, he makes the inner noun the collection of the fortunate, then applies an outer abstract to it. His final purpose gloss does not make the internal collective the rule's compulsory analysis everywhere. RV 3.8.2 and 11 have the shorter saubhagāya. RV 6.13.1 directly juxtaposes the vocative adjective subhaga with saubhagāni, the things that come from Agni. Sāyaṇa glosses them as wealth. These contexts give one bare dative, one plural subject and one dative of a tva-stem. None gives a new gārhapatya case.

The two youthful stages in Bhagavadgītā 2.13 illustrate the limits of a purely semantic grouping. Kaumāra uses the age term with añ under 129. Yauvana uses yuvan with aṇ under the more specific 130. Jarā, in the same coordination, makes the nominative role visible. The officiant terms in the udgātṛ list have their own class, and the śrotriya supplement's y-loss must be kept on its own stem.
