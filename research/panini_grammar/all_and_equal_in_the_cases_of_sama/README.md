# PG843 · All and equal in the cases of sama

The sense of the free adjective *sama* decides whether the sarvādi substitutions apply. Kāśikā on 1.1.27 permits them when the word means ‘all’. The same discussion invokes 1.3.10 for entities corresponding in number and the example of sacrificing on level ground. Both controls keep ordinary adjectival inflection. Equal, even and level here share the ordinary ending policy while retaining their individual interpretation. The [literal panels](CASES.tsv) contrast the two policies through seven cases, three numbers and three agreement genders. Compounds, the noun *samā* ‘year’, and the homophonous finite first singular from root DP 01.0963 have separate inputs.

**The discriminating cases.** In the all-inclusive masculine nominative plural, śī substitutes for jas by 7.1.17. The ordinary equality branch retains jas and yields *samāḥ*. The dative and locative singulars differ as *samasmai/samāya* and *samasmin/same*. The genitive plural preserves pronoun suṭ versus the ordinary nuṭ. Compare *sameṣām/samānām* for M or N and *samāsām/samānām* for F. For feminine DATsg compare the pronominal syāṭ history of *samasyai* with the āp history of *samāyai*. Feminine NOM/ACC dual and neuter NOM/ACC dual converge on *same*, independently of the lexical designation. The neuter plural *samāni* has dental n. No r or ṣ triggers its retroflexion. In both M/N ablative singular branches the existing PG420 pause convention admits the separate final t and d readouts. These switches require the actual original sup and the positively supplied sense. They never generate a free choice between *samāya* and *samasmai* on the same fixed sense.
