## Inherit PG250 and distinguish the source policies

Use the PG250 four-way clause register and
the two finished Classical neuter case
schedules. Its first means input is

praṇidāne in the reciprocal exchange

the source tradition. 7.1.37 selects
actual lyap after the prefix, 6.4.69

for the named 4.4.21 member needs
its own evidence. The other
measuring root designated *māṅ*
keeps its lexical address.

The inherited 4.4.21 ordering
keeps kak for *apamitya* and kan
for the distinct kta nominal
*yācita* of bare yāc in asking.

7.2.118. The sounded ka belongs to
each respective suffix. Outer debt

introduce tiṅ padas to either

is lexical, with no kit rule from

The exact sources present more than

## Śaunaka and the corresponding Paippalāda addresses

In Śaunaka 6.117.1 the pada line
spells *apa-mityam* with final m
before *apratīttam*. Keep its
unrepaid-debt context. The actual
pada has *asmi*. Whitney explicitly
proposes to read *admi* in his
translation and also discusses a
possible reading of the asmi
clause. The local Trivedi edition
takes an *as* root meaning to take
or receive. Each interpretation
has its own source address. The
transmitted *asmi* is retained,
and its definitive lexical identity
and governance remain Open there.

In Śaunaka 6.117.2 the pada
separates *apa-mitya*, *dhānyam*,
*yat*, *jaghasa* and *aham*.
Whitney accepts the absolutive
having borrowed. The borrowed
grain is also what the reciter
ate. The two events share their
agent and borrowing precedes
eating. This gives the ordinary
prior-action reading independently
of the older 3.4.19 later-action
conditions in PG250. The first
half-verse has plural repayers
and living recipients. Their roles
stay separate from the later
first singular. The ordinary
contact *jaghasa + aham* gives
*jaghasāham* by 6.1.101. Whitney
flags his own printed *jaghāsa*
as a misprint at this address.
His suggested alternative
*apamityam* and Griffith's
throwing-away interpretation
remain source-labelled. The
new construction selects the
marked original pada and the
borrowing reading.

The available Paippalāda textual
parallels are **16.49.10** and
**16.50.1**. The first shares the
opening *apamityam apratītam
yad asmi*, with the differently
printed vowel length in the unpaid
term and with an explicit first-
person return at the end. The
second has *apamitya dhānyaṃ*,
different borrowing/eating
boundary spelling *yaj jaghāsa*,
and an actual prayer that Agni
make the reciter debt-free.
In *agnir mā tasmād anṛṇaṃ
kṛṇotu*, Agni is the agent, *mā*
the object, *anṛṇam* its resulting
predicate and *tasmāt* the
separation basis. This locally
attests the independent pronominal
role. It gives no decision on
the different Bhaṭṭi fragment in
S1. Whitney's Kashmir report
has *apamṛtyu* in both parallel
contexts. The differing published
witnesses are retained. In
particular a source for the
modern normalized Paippalāda
line supplies no ground to replace
Whitney's manuscript report.
