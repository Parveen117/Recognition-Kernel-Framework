# PG226 · Patient agreement and impersonal third singular across twelve roots

Monty · 12 November 2026

Who supplies agreement changes the size of the table. At the twelve PG206 addresses the Dhātupāṭha marks six selected senses transitive and six intransitive. For the transitive readings an expressed patient can be any person and number. For the intransitive readings the verb expresses the activity itself (*bhāve*), with a third-singular ending. An instrumental agent does not determine the number of that ending.

| Selected action | Construction | Present 3sg | Why the form matters |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Measure, *māh* | patient | māhyate | The radical long vowel survives. |
| Honour, class-one *mah* and curādi *maha* | patient | mahyate | Both give the same word. In the curādi history the vowel of ṇic is lost before *yak*. |
| Change or ripen, class-four *samī* | impersonal | samyate | It coincides with the impersonal of class-one *ṣam*. Their agentive presents have different stems. |
| Sound or gather, the two *styai* roots | impersonal | styāyate | *Yak* has the marker k; 6.1.45 supplies ā. Neither root belongs to the compulsory ī group of 6.4.66. |
| Dance, two class-one *naṭ* entries; perform, curādi *naṭ* | impersonal; patient | naṭyate; nāṭyate | Both the vowel and the allowed agreement requests depend on the lexical selection. |

The four present-system lakāras give **240 licensed positions** on this domain. Twelve ordinary passive positions of *nam* already have a written owner in PG221. This packet adds **228**, comprising 204 new patient-agreement positions and 24 impersonals. All 240 licensed forms occur in the archive. Its other 192 entries for these intransitive readings have no matching patient to license their agreement.

The Bālamanoramā makes a further distinction. An ordinarily transitive verb can express the activity when the object is genuinely outside the speaker’s intention, as in *devadattena bhujyate*. That is another reading with its own semantic condition.
