## What licenses a dual

The Śabdakaustubha ad 1.1.27 distinguishes the two colours of one gem from a request to describe two such gems together. Ekaśeṣa can motivate dual agreement for the latter. The author invokes actual expression to restrict that inference. In reporting the dispute ad 1.1.37 he attributes nonproduction of the dual to Kaiyaṭa and acceptance to Haradatta. Consequently the fourteen M/N nonvocative dual keys in PG821 survive **only as constructions on the permissive number policy**. Their spellings are supplied by the PG821 ending table. Attribution to Haradatta extends to admission of the dual, not to fourteen quotations or proof of attestation. The restrictive source branch blocks this number explicitly. Substituting a form of *ubha* on that branch changes the lexical key. Feminine dual word forms and the three dual vocatives remain Open on the permissive branch. Seven PG821 feminine dual credits are withdrawn because they used ṭāp.

## Formation, feminine ending and word realization

Keep the two PG789 readings of the numeral premise on the *tayap → ayac* analysis. At 1.1.27 the Śabdakaustubha also reports the Bhāṣya's analysis at 1.1.44 in which 5.2.44 supplies a separate suffix. On that analysis the history records the direct affix, without a fictive deleted *tayap*. In the replacement analysis the inherited *tayap* class licenses ṅīp at 4.1.15. The reported independent-affix account instead extends the affix range covered there. Both account for *ubhayī*. Their historical priority and direct collation of the underlying passages remain Open. The no-dual/dual distinction belongs to a different question. The register asserts no historical pairing of one author's number decision with another author's affix analysis.

For F singular and plural attach the feminine ī before the case realization. Śabdamañjarī §101 explicitly compares the stem to *nadī*. Thus an oblique with a vowel-initial realization can show the stem-final glide after the stem's existing y, as in instrumental singular *ubhayyā*. The plural instrumental retains ī before *bhiḥ*. Ablative and genitive singular have distinct sup histories and the same *ubhayyāḥ*. The vocative singular shortens ī to i, giving *he ubhayi*. The comparison and the explicit forms in §37 of the declension table support the fourteen corrected seven-case readings and both feminine address forms.

For M singular direct address, removal of the singular address ending after the a-stem gives *he ubhaya*. RKM §81 supplies this word. The Śabdamañjarī transcription §100 displays a visarga here, which is retained as a source discrepancy. The M plural address has *ubhaye* in both tables. For N, Śabdamañjarī §102 explicitly assigns NOM, VOC and ACC to the *phala* pattern. Its §24 calls one fruit with the bare stem and more than two with *-āni*. Application gives N *he ubhaya*, *he ubhayāni*. RKM §81 instead prints M-like *ubhaye* in the N plural vocative column of its electronic table. Its F forms also include single-y or ā spellings in several cells, including the locative singular. These are source observations pending print collation. They acquire no general alternate-form credit. Strip the address particle *he* only from the word field, retaining address as its own grammatical key.
