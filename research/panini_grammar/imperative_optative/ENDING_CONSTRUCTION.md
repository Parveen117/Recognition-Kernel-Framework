# PG19 — Owned loṭ/vidhiliṅ endings and marker-sensitive stems

Author: Monty. 2 November 2026.

## E1. Original endings and the two boundaries

Use 3.4.78's original P and A series in the order
(3sg,3du,3pl,2sg,2du,2pl,1sg,1du,1pl). Retain the original tiṅ owner,
teaching markers, lakāra owner, substitutions and augments separately.
The basic and causal event selects its ending before the final stem query.
Deleting a marker from pronunciation never deletes its source evidence.

Both loṭ and the present **non-āśīs liṅ** use sārvadhātuka tiṅ by
3.4.113. The 3.4.116 exception is specifically liṅ in āśīs; it is false
on D's domain. Consequently the kartari vikaraṇa and bhāva/karma yak
routes apply. Future sya/tās and a past aṭ/āṭ do not enter this domain.

Śap/śa/śyan/śnā or u and tiṅ address different aṅgas. A later ṅit
ending cannot cancel guṇa already licensed at a distinct śap boundary.
Likewise an ending's visible first letter is not its complete marker state.
This is needed for both bhavet and kuryāt.

## E2. What loṭ transports from laṅ

3.4.85 is a **restricted atideśa**. Kāśikā names the tām substitutions
and s-lopa and expressly excludes past aṭ/āṭ and the cited laṅ jus
effect, explaining its adopted vyavasthitavibhāṣā reading. Thus it does
not turn loṭ into laṅ, make its meaning past, or copy every laṅ rule.

| Loṭ target | Adopted operation |
|---|---|
| P tas, thas, tha | tām, tam, ta via 3.4.101 under 3.4.85 |
| P uttama vas/mas final s | lopa via 3.4.99 under 3.4.85 |
| P tip/jhi final i | u by 3.4.86, giving tu/antu after jh substitution |
| P sip | hi, expressly apit, by 3.4.87 |
| P mip | ni by 3.4.89, replacing the otherwise transported am route |
| all uttama endings, P and A | initial āṭ and pit status by 3.4.92 |

The hi and ni vowels are not changed to u or deleted by a blanket
laṅ-i-lopa pass: the Kāśikā/Bālamanoramā discussion explicitly preserves
them. After a short-a aṅga, 6.4.105 gives hi-luk; after the eligible
affix-u, 6.4.106 gives hi-luk. Otherwise hi remains, as in krīṇīhi.
The Vedic pit option of 3.4.88 and blessing tātaṅ of 7.1.35 are excluded.

The first-person **āṭ is an ending augment**, not a past augment attached
at the left edge of the verbal word. Its ṭ supplies initial placement
under 1.1.46. It remains a distinct owner at the stem/ending seam.

## E3. Loṭ A changes its own ṭit endings

Loṭ is ṭit, so 3.4.79 gives the A ṭi-e and 3.4.80 replaces thās
by se. The loṭ-specific rules then act on those e occurrences:

| Slot | Ending route before the stem seam |
|---|---|
| 3sg | ta → te → tām, 3.4.90 |
| 3du | ātām → āte → ātām, 3.4.90; the two displayed ātām states have different histories |
| 3pl | jha → anta/ata → ante/ate → antām/atām, 7.1.3/5 and 3.4.90 |
| 2sg | thās → se → sva, 3.4.80/91 |
| 2du | āthām → āthe → āthām, 3.4.90 |
| 2pl | dhvam → dhve → dhvam, 3.4.91 |
| 1sg | iṭ → e → ai, 3.4.79/93, with initial āṭ |
| 1du | vahi → vahe → vahai, 3.4.79/93, with initial āṭ |
| 1pl | mahi → mahe → mahai, 3.4.79/93, with initial āṭ |

3.4.91's v/am and 3.4.93's ai are the stated exceptions to generic
3.4.90 ām. They are not three free alternatives for one e.

After a thematic short a, 7.2.81 replaces the initial ā of the weak
dual endings by iy. Before t/th, 6.1.66 removes y and 6.1.87 gives e:
a|ātām → a|iytām → a|itām → etām, and likewise ethām.
The first-person āṭ is **pit** by 3.4.92, so it does not satisfy that
ṅit condition; there is no erroneous first-person *-evahai*.
Kāśikā 7.2.81 gives pacāvahai/pacāmahai as its explicit counterexamples.

For thematic first singular, āṭ and ai join by 6.1.90 and the preceding
a/ai seam by 6.1.88. First dual/plural a|āṭ coalesce by 6.1.101.
For kṛ/krī the same ending augments trigger the distinct stem routes in A.

## E4. Vidhiliṅ augment and marker owners

The liṅ owner supplies the named ṅit-lakāra ending substitutions of
3.4.99–101. That syntactic reference must be distinguished from asserting
that every substitute inherits a phonological ṅit effect from a lakāra.
Kāśikā/Nyāsa 3.4.103 expressly reject that automatic inheritance and
use the explicit prescription to secure the required effect here.

3.4.102 prescribes **sīyuṭ**. In P, 3.4.103 substitutes the yāsuṭ
prescription and gives the liṅ ending **ṅit status**; the status is not
merely attached to a free-floating augment. In A, the apit sārvadhātuka
ending already has the 1.2.4 ṅitvat condition. The P pit endings can
therefore still have an explicit ṅit prohibition: pit and ṅit are not
one Boolean with opposite values.

The ṭ-marked augments occupy the beginning of their own recipients by
1.1.46. By 3.4.107, suṭ additionally augments the specified t/th
occurrences; it does not replace yāsuṭ/sīyuṭ, whose recipient is different.
The source explains why the two prescriptions are not competitors.

Under 7.2.79, delete the **non-final** s belonging to yāsuṭ, sīyuṭ
and suṭ on this sārvadhātuka domain. Keep final s of the P 2sg and
jus ending. Thus yās becomes yā, sīy becomes īy, and the t/th suṭ
realization disappears while its source and addressed consonant remain.
The āśīrliṅ counterexamples in the source do not license that deletion.

There is no generic 7.2.35 iṭ before these sārvadhātuka endings.
Sīyuṭ is also not PG18's ciṇvat augment: the 6.4.62 commentary limits
that sīyuṭ application to the relevant ārdhadhātuka liṅ. Vidhiliṅ
does not inherit the future ciṇvat branch merely because “sīyuṭ” is present.

## E5. Vidhiliṅ P: all nine paths

The ordinary ending consequences are

\[
 (t,tām,us,s,tam,ta,am,va,ma).
\]

Here tip/sip lose i by 3.4.100; 3.4.101 supplies tām/tam/ta/am;
3.4.99 removes uttama vas/mas s; 3.4.108 supplies jus for jhi.
Jus's initial j is it by 1.3.7; final s is retained under 1.3.4.
Its later pause form is uḥ. The lakāra, ending and jus owners stay distinct.

After a thematic short a, 7.2.80 replaces yā by **iy**, with its
specific exception relationship to the ordinary seam rules as discussed
in Kāśikā. Before consonants 6.1.66 removes y. Before vowels it remains.
The a+i seam takes 6.1.87 guṇa. Hence:

| Slots | Owned junction | Readout |
|---|---|---|
| 3sg/3du/2sg/2du/2pl/1du/1pl | B+a ∣ iy ∣ consonant-ending → B+a ∣ i ∣ ending | Bet, Betām, Beḥ, Betam, Beta, Beva, Bema |
| 3pl | B+a ∣ iy ∣ us | Beyuḥ |
| 1sg | B+a ∣ iy ∣ am | Beyam |

For a non-short-a stem, 7.2.80 is false. In kuryā|us and krīṇīyā|us,
6.1.96 gives the specified a/ā+us pararūpa: kuryuḥ, krīṇīyuḥ.
It must not replace the thematic pacey- path: the 7.2.80 commentary
explicitly supplies paceyuḥ and discusses that interaction.

## E6. Vidhiliṅ A: all nine paths

Liṅ is not ṭit. The A thās/ātām/āthām therefore do not first become
laṭ's se/āte/āthe. Rule 3.4.105 replaces jha by ran; 3.4.106 replaces
the **first-person singular ending iṭ** by a. It does not target an
iṭ augment elsewhere. Kāśikā treats the t in its “at” prescription as
a pronunciation aid, not a new pronounced final consonant in paceya.
Final n of ran is retained as an ending sound; it is not an erased it.

The ending series is

\[
 (ta,ātām,ran,thās,āthām,dhvam,a,vahi,mahi).
\]

Prepend sīyuṭ and the applicable t/th suṭ, then apply E4's s-lopa.
The remaining īy loses y before consonants and keeps it before vowels.
Thematic a+ī gives e by 6.1.87. The result is

\[
 (Beta,Beyātām,Beran,Bethāḥ,Beyāthām,Bedhvam,Beya,Bevahi,Bemahi).
\]

For example a|sīy|s|thās → a|īy|thās → a|ī|thās → ethāḥ.
In a|īy|ātām the intervening īy prevents a direct short-a/ā seam;
one cannot also apply 7.2.81 to manufacture *-etām* on this path.
Kāśikā and Kaumudī give the pace-/edhe- families with these contrasts.

## E7. Four thematic constructors, with a typed phonetic readout

Let B be the realized stem **without its final thematic a**, together
with its owned derivation. In slot order define

\[
 I_P(B)=(Batu,Batām,Bantu,Ba,Batam,Bata,\nu(Bāni),Bāva,Bāma),
\]
\[
 I_A(B)=(Batām,Betām,Bantām,Basva,Bethām,Badhvam,Bai,Bāvahai,Bāmahai),
\]
\[
 O_P(B)=(Bet,Betām,Beyuḥ,Beḥ,Betam,Beta,Beyam,Beva,Bema),
\]
\[
 O_A(B)=(Beta,Beyātām,Beran,Bethāḥ,Beyāthām,Bedhvam,Beya,Bevahi,Bemahi).
\]

ν is the explicitly bounded n→ṇ readout of T3. Other late junctions
and pause rules are already included in these factorizations; they remain
ordered owned operations, not arbitrary string replacements. These formulas
do not accept a non-thematic stem merely because it has a printable ending.

**Theorem E7.** On each declared owned thematic interface, the four
constructors specify exactly its admitted loṭ/vidhiliṅ ending paths.

**Proof.** E2–E3 enumerate all nine loṭ targets and their specific
exceptions; E4–E6 enumerate all nine liṅ targets and augment/marker
conditions. Every resulting seam is either the displayed consonant case,
vowel case, ṅit dual case or pit first-person case. T3 supplies the late
readout. These cases exhaust the 3×3 slot product in each pada. D4 supplies
the independent controller restriction. N3 proves compatibility of their
composition with the same history-bearing zero-reading. □

The theorem is relative to the selected source/sound policy. It does not
assert a universal scheduling theorem for all grammatical rule conflicts.
