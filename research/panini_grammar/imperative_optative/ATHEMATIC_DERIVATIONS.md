# PG19 — Kṛ and krī: ending features choose the stem

Author: Monty. 2 November 2026.

## A1. Strong/weak is a query result

PG6's kṛ/krī source procedures act at owned root/vikaraṇa/tiṅ seams.
Loṭ changes sip to apit hi and makes **every first-person ending pit**.
Vidhiliṅ explicitly supplies the P ending's ṅit condition; A is apit.
One cannot import laṭ's strong-slot list unchanged.

| Mood/pada | Strong continuation | Weak continuation |
|---|---|---|
| loṭ P | 3sg; all three first-person slots | 3du/3pl; all three second-person slots |
| loṭ A | all three first-person slots | all third/second-person slots |
| vidhiliṅ P | none on this domain | all slots, by 3.4.103 |
| vidhiliṅ A | none on this domain | all slots, by 1.2.4 |

Here “strong” summarizes the relevant absence of the kit/ṅit inhibition;
it does not say every vowel must undergo guṇa. In particular long ā of
śnā has its own rules. The local marker sources remain authoritative.

## A2. Kṛ loṭ

3.1.79 supplies the u vikaraṇa. Its root-facing condition permits
kṛ→kar by guṇa, while 6.4.110 changes the a of kar to u before
the weak sārvadhātuka continuation. Its t-qualified short-u output is
not immediately strengthened again. This is the retained PG6 source policy.

The strong 3sg has kar|u|tu → kar|o|tu → **karotu**.
Weak consonant cases have kur|u, e.g. kur|u|tām → **kurutām**.
Before the realized vowel of ant/at, the vikaraṇa u has the 6.1.77
v substitute: **kurvantu/kurvatām**. Jhi/jha is resolved before the
vowel/consonant query, preserving the PG6 ordering witness.

For 2sg P, hi is apit and 6.4.106 removes it after the eligible affix-u.
Kāśikā 6.4.110 expressly includes the formerly present sārvadhātuka
condition in **kuru**. Kaumudī also uses the ābhīya asiddha view of
hi-luk. Thus the printed absence of hi does not license a strong *karo*.
This is the adopted local view, not unrestricted resurrection after luk.

For first person, 3.4.92 gives pit āṭ at the ending boundary. Hence
kar|u|āṭ|ni → kar|o|ā|ni → kar|av|ā|ni → **karavāṇi**.
The initial āṭ separates u from later v/m, so 6.4.108's direct m/v
environment does not apply. The same strong path gives karavāva/karavāma
and **karavai/karavāvahai/karavāmahai**. Kāśikā 3.4.92–93 quotes
the six first-person forms. Copying the weak laṭ A stem would fail here.

The complete two loṭ tuples are:

\[
 I_P(kṛ)=(karotu,kurutām,kurvantu,kuru,kurutam,kuruta,
          karavāṇi,karavāva,karavāma),
\]
\[
 I_A(kṛ)=(kurutām,kurvātām,kurvatām,kuruṣva,kurvāthām,kurudhvam,
          karavai,karavāvahai,karavāmahai).
\]

Kuruṣva uses the affix-s ṣatva after u; the dual u|ā seam gives v.
No thematic a is invented to turn kurvātām into a thematic -etām.

## A3. Kṛ vidhiliṅ

The P ending is ṅit by the explicit 3.4.103 prescription, even in
the originally pit slots. Its yāsuṭ realizes yā after the non-final
s-lopa. With kar|u→kur|u, 6.4.109 deletes the vikaraṇa u **before y**:

\[
 kur|u|yā|t\to kur|\varnothing|yā|t\to kuryāt.
\]

Kāśikā/Nyāsa supply kuryāt/kuryātām/kuryuḥ and the actual u-lopa
witness. The whole affix's earlier identity survives the sound lopa.
The 8.2.79 kur prohibition inherited from PG6 prevents a spurious
late lengthening of the root u. In 3pl, yā|us takes 6.1.96.

In A the remaining augment is **īy**, not yā. The vikaraṇa u sees
a vowel, so 6.4.109's direct y condition is false and 6.1.77 gives v.
Then īy's y disappears before consonants and remains before vowels:
kur|u|īy|ta → kur|v|ī|ta → **kurvīta**;
kur|u|īy|ātām → **kurvīyātām**. No 6.4.108 deletion can skip
that intervening īy to target a later v/m in first dual/plural.

\[
 O_P(kṛ)=(kuryāt,kuryātām,kuryuḥ,kuryāḥ,kuryātam,kuryāta,
          kuryām,kuryāva,kuryāma),
\]
\[
 O_A(kṛ)=(kurvīta,kurvīyātām,kurvīran,kurvīthāḥ,kurvīyāthām,kurvīdhvam,
          kurvīya,kurvīvahi,kurvīmahi).
\]

The contrast is not an arbitrary kṛ exception table: yā versus īy
changes the addressed u's right context, selecting lopa versus yaṇ.

## A4. Krī loṭ

3.1.81 supplies śnā. Its apit/ṅitvat condition blocks root ī-guṇa;
the retained ṇatva yields the krīṇ- constituent. For weak outer endings,
6.4.113 makes śnā's ā into ī before consonants, while 6.4.112 removes
it before vowels. Before strong outer endings ā remains.

Kaumudī/Bālamanoramā 3.1.81 expressly place jh→ant/at before that
query, and Bālamanoramā gives krīṇātu and krīṇīhi literally.
Thus P 3pl is krīṇ|ant|u → **krīṇantu**, and A 3pl has the
non-short-a jha→at condition, giving **krīṇatām**. They do not
undergo an earlier ī substitution based on teaching jh's consonant.

Hi remains in krīṇīhi. Even where the earlier ā is visible to the
6.4.105 query, it is long ā, not that rule's specified short a;
Kāśikā illustrates this distinction with lunīhi/punīhi.
In first person the pit āṭ condition preserves śnā's ā; ā|ā coalesces,
and A's ā|ai gives ai. These are written applications of the general
ending rules, not separately quoted full krī first-person paradigms.

\[
 I_P(krī)=(krīṇātu,krīṇītām,krīṇantu,krīṇīhi,krīṇītam,krīṇīta,
           krīṇāni,krīṇāva,krīṇāma),
\]
\[
 I_A(krī)=(krīṇītām,krīṇātām,krīṇatām,krīṇīṣva,krīṇāthām,krīṇīdhvam,
           krīṇai,krīṇāvahai,krīṇāmahai).
\]

## A5. Krī vidhiliṅ

All endings are weak. P's yā-initial continuation is consonant-initial,
so śnā gives ī and the stem becomes krīṇī|yā. It is not short-a-final:
7.2.80 is false. The 3pl yā|us seam again uses 6.1.96.

In A, removal of sīyuṭ's non-final s reveals vowel-initial īy.
Using that resolved augment view, śnā's ā is removed under 6.4.112,
giving krīṇ|īy. The preceding root and surviving augment vowels are
not silently assigned to the same owner. E6's y retention/lopa yields
the displayed forms. Bālamanoramā 3.1.81 quotes krīṇīyāt/krīṇīta;
the remaining slots are the source-relative ending composition.

\[
 O_P(krī)=(krīṇīyāt,krīṇīyātām,krīṇīyuḥ,krīṇīyāḥ,krīṇīyātam,krīṇīyāta,
           krīṇīyām,krīṇīyāva,krīṇīyāma),
\]
\[
 O_A(krī)=(krīṇīta,krīṇīyātām,krīṇīran,krīṇīthāḥ,krīṇīyāthām,krīṇīdhvam,
           krīṇīya,krīṇīvahi,krīṇīmahi).
\]

This selects a resolved-augment schedule. It does not claim that all
alternative ābhīya schedules have identical owned histories; a different
history could print the same ī. SOURCE_REVIEW records that proof boundary.

## A6. Exhaustion

**Theorem A6.** The two exact roots have four mood/pada paths each,
with all nine slots constructed above: 2·2·2·9=72 positions.

**Proof.** A1 partitions the ending-feature conditions. Kṛ then has
the strong, weak-consonant, weak-vowel, hi-luk and y-lopa cases of A2–A3.
Krī has the preserved-ā, consonant-ī and vowel-lopa cases of A4–A5.
E2–E6 enumerate every remaining ending, and T3 fixes the late readout.
The displayed eight tuples exhaust the slot products. N3 preserves
their owned guards and compositions under typed zero-specialization. □
