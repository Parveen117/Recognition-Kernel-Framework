# PG19 — Intended modality, voice and the finite domain

Author: Monty. 2 November 2026.

## D1. A frame before a mood or an ending

Start with N1's native history module. Each input retains exact lexical
identity/sense, formation, interpreted event and role graph, expression
type, discourse participants, person/number, intended modal sense σ,
relevant upapada, temporal-context evidence, source policy and obligations.
The residue is ordered grammar memory, not a probability of obeying a
command. An event need not have happened for its intended participants
and grammatical relations to be specified.

All inputs are classical, unprefixed and unaccented. Exclude āśīs,
prohibitive māṅ, Vedic leṭ, karmakartṛ, reciprocal use, mixed-person
coordination, 1.4.106 person shifts, nested ṇic, san/yaṅ and unreviewed
idioms. The causal benefit-conveying upapada of PG14 remains admitted;
the sma context below is the additional stated modal exception.

## D2. Six shared meanings, not one English label per mood

Kāśikā 3.3.161 distinguishes these intended meanings:

| σ | Adopted sense distinction |
|---|---|
| vidhi | directing/prompting an action |
| nimantraṇa | enjoining participation as an obligation |
| āmantraṇa | allowing participation at the recipient's discretion |
| adhīṣṭa | respectfully requesting an undertaking |
| sampraśna | deliberating/inquiring what is to be undertaken |
| prārthana | asking for a desired undertaking |

The source gives liṅ for these and 3.3.162 gives loṭ as well. Its
nimantraṇa/āmantraṇa examples even share the same verbal forms. Thus
neither an English “should/command” distinction nor one Sanskrit ending
recovers σ. The frame must supply it. The readings are an adopted primary
commentary account, not an assertion that everyday English glosses have
identical pragmatic boundaries.

For one of these six senses with no admitted overriding context, define

\[
 \mathcal L(F)=\{\mathrm{loṭ},\mathrm{vidhiliṅ}\}.
\]

Both are positive grammatical choices. Unknown intended meaning is an
Open input, not a seventh sense or an additional grammatical option.

## D3. Three further contexts and two source restrictions

Kāśikā 3.3.163 distinguishes praiṣa (dispatch/direction), atisarga
(permission) and prāptakāla (the occasion for the action has arrived).
The domain takes these as supplied, source-qualified categories; it does
not classify them by searching for an English imperative. Its chosen
vidhi/praiṣa distinction is the commentary's stated account, not a claim
of unanimous interpretation.

Let U mean that the intended event is ūrdhvamauhūrtika in the sense of
3.3.164, and S mean that the relevant sma is present. U is evidence from
an explicit temporal-context adapter. No count of grammar events is a
muhūrta, and no numerical clock calibration is inferred here.

The following table defines the admitted semantic profiles. A dash is
irrelevance within that row, not a missing value.

| Sense/context | U | S | Finite mood relation | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| any of D2's six senses, ordinary context | — | false | loṭ, vidhiliṅ | 3.3.161–162 |
| adhīṣṭa with the specified sma | — | true | loṭ | 3.3.166 |
| praiṣa / atisarga / prāptakāla, ordinary immediate context | false | false | loṭ | 3.3.163 |
| those three, later context without sma | true | false | loṭ, vidhiliṅ | 3.3.164 |
| those three, later context with sma | true | true | loṭ | 3.3.165 |

The last two rules are source-declared restrictions, not a numerical
“later sūtra always wins” policy. Kṛtyas are also licensed by 3.3.163
and retained by 3.3.164; this packet constructs only the displayed finite
verbs. It therefore claims completeness for its **finite-mood projection**,
not for every possible Sanskrit expression of the intended meaning.

Unlisted combinations and the other liṅ environments remain outside.
If U or S is unresolved where relevant, retain the conditional liṅ
obligation; do not pronounce its two possible truth values optional words.
Where a completed profile excludes liṅ, its excluded channel is retained.

## D4. Expression and agreement survive the change of mood

Use PG14 V2–V3 and PG15 K's source-qualified role construction.
Kartari expresses the basic actor or causal causer. Karma(q) expresses
its selected karman. Bhāva expresses the intransitive process and has
default 3sg, regardless of the instrumental agent's number/person.
1.3.13 prescribes A for Karma/Bhāva; active pada has its own evidence.

An addressee need not be second-person tiṅ: the source's bhavān examples
are third-person agreement. Conversely, an instruction concerning an
expressed first-person participant can have first-person tiṅ. No modal
rule in D2–D3 replaces the controller by the hearer. The source explicitly
uses first-person middle forms in deliberation/request.

The register ranges over completed admissible frames. It does not assert
that every sense and every person slot are interchangeable inside one
fixed discourse. Repeated semantic witnesses are retained in history but
factored out of the morphological counts.

## D6. Causatives and voice domains

Use the twelve PG14/PG15 causal frames, plus PG18's class-4 śam with a
conscious causee distinct from the causer. Their root constituents and
role witnesses are preserved. In kartari, PG14's complete F/U/E relation
selects pada. Here its U is the **benefit-conveying upapada**; it is a
separate field from D3's temporal U. No implementation may alias them.

Bhū, sthā and budh retain their P-only cases. The added conscious,
intransitive śam satisfies 1.3.88 and is not in 1.3.89's exception list,
so its causal kartari profile is P-only as well. The other nine causal
entries admit the inherited P/A profiles across the qualified frames:
13 roots, 22 pada profiles. Causal Karma(q) has A for all thirteen,
with PG15's dependent causee-status/karman-selection relation.

The basic voice sector is intentionally smaller: Karma(q) for kṛ, hṛ
1011046, pac and goal-taking gam; Bhāva for bhū and standing sthā
1011077. These six yak routes already have PG14/PG15 source witnesses.
The causal śam passive route is constructed in C3, not obtained by
copying its basic śāmyati stem.

## D7. Composition theorem for completed frames

**Proposition D7.** On the declared completed profiles, the modal relation
is finite and nonempty. Each selected voice/controller/pada/mood branch
has one slot and one of the stem routes named above. Incomplete evidence
remains an obligation without enlarging the accepted relation.

**Proof.** D2–D3 give the exhaustive finite modal cases, with their
overrides attributed. PG14/PG15 fix expression/controllers; the new śam
case follows the stated 1.3.88 and 1.4.52 conditions. D5 and D6 enumerate
the morphological inputs. Modal choices change no event role or lexical
identity. Their composition on matching native grades therefore has the
claimed properties. □
