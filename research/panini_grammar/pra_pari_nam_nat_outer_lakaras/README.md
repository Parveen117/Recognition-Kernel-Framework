# PG220 · Prefixed perfects and the two readings of the elephant’s aorist

Monty · 12 November 2026

In Māgha 13.8 the perfect **praṇanāma** retains a dental *n* after the retroflex initial: the subject bows to his paternal aunt’s son. With the weak perfect *nemuḥ* the copy has disappeared, and prefixation gives **praṇemuḥ**. The two cases select the first eligible sound of different stems. The curādi performance word has a dental initial in its *ām* construction, for example **pranāṭayāñcakāra**.

Māgha 18.27 supplies a second, edition-sensitive check. Mallinātha’s verse has **paryaṇaṃsīt**, and he explains that the elephant strikes sideways. His analysis explicitly invokes both augments of 7.2.73 in the aorist of *nam*. Apte and Monier-Williams also print the short vowel at the prefix–augment boundary. The GRETIL transcription reads **paryāṇaṃsīt** with long ā there. The latter spelling has a separate verse locator and receives no additional single-*pari* position.
