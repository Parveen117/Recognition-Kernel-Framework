## H02. Ācāryakaraṇa concerns the agent and a qualified teaching relation

Kāśikā 1.3.36 gives **māṇavakam upanayate**: the one performing the initiation becomes ācārya. It does not mean that the pupil is being turned into a teacher. Siddhāntakaumudī/Bālamanoramā retain initiation followed by teaching as the relevant qualification; generic physical approach or leading a pupil does not establish it.

Padamañjarī explains why 1.3.36 is useful despite this eventual agent-related status: the direct result of initiation is the pupil's saṃskāra; teacher status belongs to the subsequent qualified teaching. Therefore do not replace the actual source relation by the shortcut “all results accrue to the agent, so 1.3.72 alone.” The adopted request retains **DIRECT_RESULT=INITIAND** and **TEACHER_STATUS=AGENT_THROUGH_QUALIFIED_TEACHING** as different fields.

The same sūtra lists respect, lifting, knowledge, wages, settlement and expenditure. Nyāsa/Siddhāntakaumudī distinguish verbal meanings from contextual qualifications; they are not seven interchangeable labels for ordinary leading. Those six other senses receive no additional finite-table credit here. Their literal explanations remain in the archive, including differing analyses of the respect example.

## H03. Internal object is not sufficient without the niyama premises

Kāśikā 1.3.37 gives **krodhaṃ vinayate**, **manyuṃ vinayate**, and contrasts another person's anger, bodily gadu/ghāṭā, and instrumental buddhyā/prajñayā. The object must be a karman kāraka, not an instrument merely located in the agent. The counted profile fixes anger belonging to the grammatical agent and excludes a bodily object or body part.

Nyāsa, Siddhāntakaumudī and Bālamanoramā explicitly read this rule as **niyama**: the A already available through ñ and intended agent-directed result under 1.3.72 is restricted in the agent-internal-object domain. The body-part case is excluded even when its benefit accrues to the agent. Bālamanoramā also distinguishes an objectively agent-related result from its being intended in the utterance; the former does not automatically supply the latter. The selected VI_OWN_ANGER profile therefore carries a positive result-vivakṣā certificate.

This refines the operational reading of PG42's compact 1.3.37 row without changing that frozen file. It does not turn the earlier row into a new set of words. Kāśikā's surface “A occurs” wording and the explicit niyama explanation remain separately attributed. The counted profile is chosen inside their shared positive example; no second source-policy copy of all ninety words is counted.

## H04. Perfect iṭ and anekāc yaṇ are new obligations

Nī's ordinary aniṭ classification is not a blanket ban on liṭ iṭ. Kāśikā 7.2.13 restricts the liṭ aniṭ set to its named roots; nī is outside it. Thus 7.2.35 supplies iṭ before the A val-initial se/dhve/vahe/mahe endings. No P thal option is imported.

Kāśikā 6.4.82 explicitly supplies ninyatuḥ/ninyuḥ: after doubling, the non-cluster-preceded root ī belongs to an anekāc aṅga and becomes y before the actual following vowel. This includes the inserted iṭ at the specified A endings. Bālamanoramā 8.2.27 directly gives **ninyiṣe, ninyāthe, ninyiḍhve/ninyidhve, ninye, ninyivahe, ninyimahe** in its nī discussion. These are not guessed forms such as *ninīṣe or *ninivahe.

The two 2pl perfect readings implement 8.3.79 with the qualifying iṇ/iṭ history. Keep that pair distinct from mandatory 8.3.78 in aniṭ luṅ/blessing. No primary ām, auxiliary role or causative stem enters this basic perfect.

Literal source cautions remain separate from adopted interpretation. The pinned Nyāsa 1.3.37 field has **कर्त्तृस्थाभावो** in its gadu discussion; do not use that potentially damaged sequence to relabel a body-part object as necessarily outside the agent. Kāśikā and the explicit SK/Bālamanoramā restriction supply the adopted control. Bālamanoramā 1.3.36 also retains electronic damage such as **तदर्थ#ं**. No source field is silently repaired and no new scan collation is claimed.

## H05. Contexts, prefix placement and scope

Reuse PG74's exact ten event certificates: present laṭ; ordinary vidhyādi loṭ/vidhiliṅ; non-today past laṅ without māṅ; general future lṛṭ; expressly non-today future luṭ; the selected future 3.3.147 attitude/upapada plus 3.3.139 kriyātipatti lṛṅ; unattained-event blessing; qualified parokṣa liṭ with its first-person premise; and witnessed completed earlier-today luṅ without māṅ. Each must apply to this prefixed event. An English “would” alone does not license lṛṅ.

Actual upasarga/gati relations use 1.4.59/60 and ordinary preposition uses 1.4.80. These nī sūtras themselves do not prescribe upa or vi as a textual prefix condition; the chosen meanings and source examples fix the present frames. PG74's special prefix-conditioned pada intervention issue is therefore not mechanically copied as another requirement.

At laṅ/luṅ/lṛṅ, 6.4.71 aṭ belongs at the verbal aṅga's beginning, after the prefix. **Upa+a → upā** by 6.1.101 preserves two short-a owners. **Vi+a → vya** by 6.1.77 changes prefix i, not root ī. Neither long upā nor its written ā proves 6.4.72 āṭ, which is not licensed by consonant-initial nī. Prefixes are outside root reduplication in liṭ. This exact domain has no prefix-r/ṣ trigger for retroflexing the root n.

Unchanged endings are pinned from PG16/19/20/31/32. New liṭ and luṅ root/affix decisions are proved in H07/H08. Other nī senses, additional prefixes, causatives/desideratives, passive, accent and Vedic variants remain outside the counted scope. Optional consonant doubling is not enumerated.
