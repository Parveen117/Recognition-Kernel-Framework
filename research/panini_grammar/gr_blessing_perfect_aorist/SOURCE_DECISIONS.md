## H02. Optional iṭ changes the actual kit interface

Kāśikā/Kaumudī **7.2.42**, qualified by Bālamanoramā, carry forward
vṛṅ/vṛñ and original **ṝ-final** roots from 7.2.38. The ordinary seṭ
iṭ of 7.2.35 becomes optional before A liṅ/sic. Thus both omission and
insertion are admitted here; PG83's mandatory future iṭ is not transferable.
Nyāsa explicitly identifies the rule as a qualification of expected iṭ.

In the **aniṭ** branch, sīyuṭ/sic is actually jhal-initial. **1.2.12**
then makes it kit for this ṛ-vowel-final root, so 1.1.5 blocks ordinary
guṇa. Apply 7.1.100 with 1.1.51: gṝ→gir. The inherited long ṝ is
not the short-ṛ root vṛ or the kryādi homograph.

In the **seṭ** branch, the root-facing sound is i, not s. The jhal
condition of 1.2.12 fails, so ordinary 7.3.84 guṇa gives **gar**. Retain
PG83's explicit i-substitution versus guṇa precedence policy. This is
not obtained by giving all A endings one kit flag and adding i afterwards.
Neither P's 3.4.104 kit nor the outer luṅ ṅ supplies a substitute rule.

The related **7.2.43** is not a second independent source of options: it
has its own short-ṛ/initial-cluster domain. Prefix letters do not turn this
teaching into such a root. **7.2.75** makes kirādi iṭ compulsory specifically
in san and withholds its optional length there in Kāśikā's account. No san
exists here, so that prescription does not cancel the 7.2.42 branches.

## H03. Iṭ length and l-option have different guards

For blessing, **7.2.39** blocks 7.2.38's optional iṭ length: its seṭ
branch has **i only**. For A sic, 7.2.40's P restriction is false,
so both **i and ī** remain available. For liṭ, inherited **aliti**
already excludes 7.2.38: all eligible perfect iṭ is short.

PG83's **8.2.21** addresses root r before an ajādi verbal affix. A seṭ
branch has a vowel-initial i/ī interface and therefore admits r/l. Aniṭ
blessing/sic instead has consonantal s, so it has **no l branch**. The
following outer tiṅ vowel cannot be substituted for the intervening s.
At luṅ 2pl, later sic-s lopa exposes dh, still a consonant.

In aniṭ gir, **8.2.77** lengthens root i before the following consonant,
yielding **gīr**. This is root length, not an invisible long iṭ. Seṭ gar
has root a and its r/l is before an affix vowel; neither gives root ī.
The yaṅ l-option of 8.2.20 is outside this target. It cannot be imported
to create *gīl* before s or to override this 8.2.77 decision.

Perfect's vowel-initial endings also admit r/l **without iṭ**. The
generalization is actual ajādi verbal-affix adjacency, not “l requires iṭ”.
The 3pl irec r belongs to the ending: **avajagalire** retains that r.

## H04. Perfect's guṇa exception, literal witnesses and source boundaries

All A liṭ endings are apit; this root is not saṃyogānta. They are kit
under **1.2.5**, but **7.4.11** expressly prescribes guṇa for long-ṝ
roots even where ordinary guṇa is prohibited. Kāśikā gives **nijagaratuḥ /
nijagaruḥ**; Nyāsa explicitly names gṝ nigaraṇe and explains the 1.1.5
prohibition that this rule overcomes. Thus the counted A root is **gar**,
not gir/gīr and not a root strengthened by ordinary guṇa despite kit.

Rule **7.4.12** names śṝ/dṝ/pṝ, not gṝ; it supplies no optional
shortening branch here. The source's strong P jagāra/jagāla examples
have a different ending/grade domain and receive zero new credit.
Kāśikā's prior-vṛddhi account and Bālamanoramā's guṇa-then-upadhā-vṛddhi
discussion differ in their strong-P explanation; the counted A domain has
no ṇal/ṇit-vṛddhi trigger, so no choice between those schedules is needed.

The same source edition is pinned throughout. Nyāsa 7.2.42 prints
**यासुडादित्वाभावात्** in its P-liṅ discussion, which conflicts with
the surrounding val argument; retain the field literally. Use Kāśikā's
explicit yāsuṭ non-val explanation rather than silently repairing that
clause or treating a text fault as a new branch. Its **उश्च** citation
separately refers to the correctly pinned 1.2.12 rule.

An additional online traditional Dhātuvṛtti transcription corroborates
avajagare, avagīrṣīṣṭa/avagariṣīṣṭa and the three r-retaining aorist
grades. Its web transcription has unrelated apparent spelling faults;
it is an unpinned cross-check, not controlling evidence or a whole-paradigm
attestation. The written tables depend on the pinned rules and contracts.

Kāśikā 1.3.53–54 is archived only as the next frontier: car's ud
transitivity and sam's expressed third-case condition. Those records give
no PG84 position credit and do not alter gṝ's inherited domain.
