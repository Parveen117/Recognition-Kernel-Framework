## H05. Blessing's three branches and the second-plural refinement

Use PG20's complete A map in slot order
3sg,3du,3pl,2sg,2du,2pl,1sg,1du,1pl:

\[
B_A(Z,\delta)=(Zīṣṭa,Zīyāstām,Zīran,Zīṣṭhāḥ,Zīyāsthām,
Zī\delta vam,Zīya,Zīvahi,Zīmahi).
\]

Concatenation at the sixth coordinate contains no word break. H02–H03
give **Z=gīrṣ, gariṣ, galiṣ**. Initial sīyuṭ-s becomes ṣ after root r
in the first branch and after affix i in the latter two. Sīyuṭ's ī remains
its own vowel. Singular t/th-directed suṭ-s follows ī and takes ṣṭ/ṣṭh;
dual suṭ follows ā and remains st/sth. This is not one global s replacement.

At 2pl, aniṭ gīr is iṇ-final, giving mandatory **ḍh** by 8.3.78.
In each seṭ branch, iṭ follows root r or l, both in iṇ. **8.3.79**
gives dh/ḍh. Thus gīrṣīḍhvam has one history, while gariṣīdhvam /
gariṣīḍhvam and galiṣīdhvam / galiṣīḍhvam retain two each. The result
is three readings at eight slots and five at 2pl: **29 per profile**.

No initial past augment, present śa, long-iṭ branch or P yāsuṭ occurs.
Final n of ran retains 8.4.37 protection. The first-person a is the
3.4.106 ending substitute, not root a or an initial augment.

## H06. Owned perfect copy, special root guṇa and irec r

Apply 6.1.8 to the one-vowel root, keeping prefix outside the copy.
The abhyāsa's ṝ takes the **7.4.66 a** operation with r accompaniment;
7.4.60 removes the copied noninitial r, and 7.4.62 changes copied g to
j. The copy is **ja**, while the original root independently takes
H04's special **gar** under 7.4.11. Retain both sound identities even
where the final word merely shows jagar.

Do not contract this to *ger*. The copy's substituted initial j fails
6.4.120's anādeśādi condition; Kāśikā's jagana-family control explicitly
uses this restriction. Neither a guṇa-created a nor kit alone licenses
e-contraction. No variant is borrowed from tṝ's different copy conditions.

For all A slots use PG82's exact 3.4.79–82 ending provenance. Root-facing
iṭ occurs only at **2sg,2pl,1du,1pl**, eight positions across the two
profiles. The root is outside 7.2.13's named aniṭ restriction. Remaining
endings are already vowel-initial; irec's i is not an inserted iṭ.
All eligible iṭ is short as H03 establishes.

Before prefix and late root-r choice the tuple is

\[
(jagare,jagarāte,jagarire,jagariṣe,jagarāthe,
jagari\delta ve,jagare,jagarivahe,jagarimahe).
\]

Every slot has an ajādi verbal interface and therefore r/l. Replace only
the **root r**: jagarire → **jagalire**, not *jagalile*. At 2pl iṭ
follows r/l and 8.3.79 gives both dh and ḍh. Hence eight slots have two
readings and 2pl has four: **20 per profile**. Equal 3sg/1sg e still
has eś versus original-ending-i→e provenance. There is no initial aṭ.

## H07. A sic: five branches, retained s and the dhvam deletion

Select cli→sic by 3.1.43–44 under the inherited PG32 enunciative-i,
c-marker policy. No named alternative cli-substitution premise is present
for this unmarked basic gṝ request. In particular, root ṝ is neither a
ḷit marker nor an aniṭ śal-final input for ksa. P-only 7.2.1 vṛddhi is
inapplicable. H02 gives **gīr** without iṭ, or **gar|i/ī** with iṭ;
H03 permits late root l only in the latter branches.

Let T be the pre-sic readout factor in
**{gīr, gari, garī, gali, galī}**. The complete unaugmented A map is

\[
L_A(T,\delta)=(Tṣṭa,Tṣātām,Tṣata,Tṣṭhāḥ,Tṣāthām,
T\delta vam,Tṣi,Tṣvahi,Tṣmahi).
\]

This is a factorization of already qualified histories, not permission
to move a tripādī operation earlier. Third plural uses jha→ata under
7.1.5. Final 1sg i is the original ending. No A slot has the apṛkta-hal
condition for 7.3.96 īṭ, so 8.2.28 cannot delete sic-s here.

In aniṭ, 7.1.100/1.1.51 already make **gir** consonant-final before the
tripādī. Root r is not jhal, and this is not a short-vowel-final aṅga:
neither 8.2.26 nor 8.2.27 deletes its s. Later 8.2.77 gives gīr.
This differs from short-root kṛ/hṛ's akṛta/ahṛta. In seṭ branches,
the i/ī is affix-owned; 8.2.27 likewise cannot treat it as root-final.
Retained s takes ṣ after r or i/ī, with adjacent t/th retroflexion.

At **2pl only**, 8.2.25 deletes sic-s before dhvam. Aniṭ then gives
**gīrḍhvam**, mandatory 8.3.78. The four seṭ factors each retain
8.3.79's dh/ḍh pair: gari-, garī-, gali-, galī-. Thus eight slots have
five readings and 2pl has nine: **49 per profile**. Sic-s lopa does not
erase sic's earlier kit/iṭ history or make it a new perfect-like interface.

## H08. Prefixes, ordered options and no cross-branch leakage

Reuse PG83's aṭ-intervention adapter for the contextual A licences.
Luṅ has initial aṭ before consonant-initial gṝ. Ava+aṭ gives **avā**
by 6.1.101; sam retains **m** before the augment vowel. Blessing has no
initial augment; perfect's **ja** copy follows the prefix directly.

| Profile | Blessing start | Perfect start | Luṅ start |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| AVA_NIGARANA_A | avagīr-/avagar-/avagal- | avajagar-/avajagal- | avāgīr-/avāgar-/avāgal- |
| SAM_PRATIJNANA_A | saṃgīr-/saṃgar-/saṃgal- | saṃjagar-/saṃjagal- | samagīr-/samagar-/samagal- |

The selected padānta-anusvāra policy retains ṃ before both g and copied j;
homorganic-nasal variants are not extra readings. Prefix, original root,
copy, iṭ, sīyuṭ/sic and ending each retain their own recipient addresses.
Apply iṭ choice and permitted length before their kit/grade and later
r/l/dh queries. Display order groups alternatives for reading convenience;
it does not reorder the derivation. Later output never restarts earlier
guṇa, iṭ or ṇatva decisions on a rewritten string.
