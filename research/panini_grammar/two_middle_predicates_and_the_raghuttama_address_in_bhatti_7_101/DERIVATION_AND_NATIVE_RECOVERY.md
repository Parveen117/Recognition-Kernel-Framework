# PG775 · Why the phrase at 7.101 identifies a different verb

At 7.100 an addressee receives a prohibition with mā. In the next verse the second predicate takes raghūttamam directly. Jayamaṅgalā paraphrases this predicate by a causal command to gladden Rāma by performing his task. Importing the preceding mā into this clause would change both the text and the argument structure. The Lāvaṇya commentary already owned by PG361 instead supplies Daśaratha as first-person target with an elliptic prāpya or bhettum. The two sources agree on a written epithet and diverge in speaker reference, governing verb and role. The narrative at 3.19–23 reports the king’s grief and death without establishing the phrase as an actual verse of his speech. The exclusion of 7.101 applies to the editions checked.

The first clause of 7.101 has sam-gam with participants gathering. Puraḥ with śatroḥ locates their meeting. Jayamaṅgalā supplies a wish with 3.3.132, luṅ and aṭ. In the middle selected by 1.3.29 the witnessed branch of 1.2.13 treats sic as kit. Root-final m drops under 6.4.37. Sic drops after the short vowel before dh under 8.2.27. The prefix retains its m before augment a. Thus sam + a + ga + dhvam gives samagadhvam. The command-like translation and the past-shaped verbal morphology describe different properties of the same word.

In the second clause the causee experiences joy. Bhvādi mud adds ṇic in causation. The short light penultimate u takes guṇa before the suffix, giving mod-i. In the present-system causal the following śap permits the i to grade and surface as ay before a. The second-person plural middle loṭ ending yields modaya-dhvam. Before the following consonant, final m appears as anusvāra. The curādi contact root shares letters but the commentary’s joy word selects the bhvādi causative. The paraphrase harṣayata spells an active ending. It provides sense evidence while the poem itself supplies the middle.

In the third clause Jayamaṅgalā reads signalling fear and cites 1.2.15. The transcribed middle-selection reference to 1.3.28 raises a separate question about the prefix and the expressed fear. We preserve the commentary and defer a new finite derivation of this member. In the fourth clause the commentator explicitly connects marriage, 1.3.56 and the optional sic-kit rule 1.2.16. Its non-kit upāyaṃsta retains the nasal and s. PG86 has already written that third-singular form and both readings of its marriage second plural. The two occurrences of no- are continuous na + u- in the text. Neither creates an additional surface reading or turns the first two clauses into prohibitions.
