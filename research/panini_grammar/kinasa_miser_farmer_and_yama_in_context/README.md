# PG256 · Three literary senses of kīnāśa and the old ox

Monty · 12 November 2026

Both literal occurrences of **kīnāśa** in the GRETIL Bhāgavata have an identifiable role. At **3.22.13** someone begs from a miser. The Sanskrit has accusative **kīnāśam** for the person asked. The Kāśikā illustrates this use of *yāc* with *pauravaṃ gāṃ yācate*. In the Bhāgavata, the offering *udyatam* is attached to *anādṛtya*, ‘having disregarded’. The verse supplies no separate expressed thing requested by *abhiyācate*.

At **3.30.13**, **kīnāśā iva go-jaram** means ‘as farmers [treat] an old ox’. *Kīnāśāḥ* is nominative plural, with the visarga absent before *iva*. *Gojaram* is the accusative singular object. Apte gives this verse as 3.30.13. Monier-Williams and Vācaspatyam cite 3.30.14. The GRETIL line and the Bhaktivedanta word gloss both support .13 in their editions. Vācaspatyam also attributes the old-ox gloss to Śrīdhara.

| Passage | Role of *kīnāśa* | Where the ending belongs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bhāgavata 3.22.13 | miser | *kīnāśam*, accusative singular |
| Bhāgavata 3.30.13 | farmers | *kīnāśāḥ*, nominative plural |
| Naiṣadhīyacarita 6.75 | Yama, named with Fire and the Lord of Waters | *dūtīḥ*, the female messengers in the accusative plural |

Śrīharṣa’s compound supplies a third textual sense. The word for Yama is internal to the compound. The 24 new nominal positions here belong to **gojara**, the old ox. The three *kīnāśa* contexts add no new inflectional positions and supply no independent function for the initial *k* at five-pāda 5.56.
