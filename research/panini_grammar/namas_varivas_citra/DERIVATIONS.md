# Written derivations

## H6. Three visible present words

Write namasyati, varivasyati and citrīyate. The same endings map in the other present-series labels; A 1sg citrīye keeps the marker-selected entitlement. A recipient-like English gloss of worship alone cannot override the source accusative in namasyati devān.

## H7. Futures with original s

Namas produces namasyiṣyati/namasiṣyati and namasyitā/namasitā; varivas gives varivasyiṣyati/varivasiṣyati. Citraṅ instead has vowel ī before y, protecting that y: citrīyiṣyate, citrīyitā. Conditional and aorist add aṭ before these consonant-initial nominal-derived roots.

## H8. P blessings with a two-preimage single y

For namas P 3sg the retained ky-y and yāsuṭ give namasyyāt. The traditional post-consonant yam reduction produces namasyāt; independently one can delete the ky-y before the blessing to get namasyāt. Both histories survive in the normalized spelling relation. The same distinction holds for varivas.

## H9. Aorist and periphrastic junctions

P 3sg gives anamasyīt/anamasīt and avarivasyīt/avarivasīt. Their perfect primaries are namasyām/namasām and varivasyām/varivasām; they take the inherited P auxiliaries. Citraṅ has the single primary citrīyām with A-kṛ or P bhū/as. Its two readings of wonder survive on each auxiliary history.

## H10. Count grammar and semantics independently

The two consonant-host P profiles each contribute 90 keys, 220 readings and 229 histories. The one vowel-host A profile contributes 90 keys, 128 readings and 256 histories when the two source meanings are retained. Total 270 positions, 568 readings and 714 paths. Independent namas with dative is a case control and adds no finite position.
