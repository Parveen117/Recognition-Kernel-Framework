# PG246 · Ritual work and the tree in the accented Saṃhitās

Monty · 12 November 2026

In Ṛgveda 5.42.10c and 8.75.14b **śámīm** is accusative singular of ritual work or service. In Atharvaveda Śaunaka 6.11.1a **śamī́m** is the tree that the *aśvattha* has climbed. The two words have the same letters and case. Their acute falls on different vowels. Monier-Williams likewise distinguishes initial-accented ‘effort’ and final-accented ‘tree’.

| Ṛgvedic position | Attested accented readings | Tokens |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Accusative singular | śámīm | 2 |
| Instrumental singular | śámi, śámī, śámyā | 10 |
| Instrumental plural | śámībhiḥ | 8 |
| Genitive singular | śámyāḥ | 1 |

The three *śámī* instrumentals are at 1.110.4a, 4.22.8b and 9.74.7c. Their case comes from the Zürich analysis. Assigning nominative from the final *ī* would lose the syntax of those verses. The ten singular instrumentals comprise five short-*i* forms, three long-*ī* forms and two *-yā* forms.

All 21 tokens tagged with this Ṛgvedic lemma have initial accent and the ritual-work gloss. The one Atharvan comparison supplies a tree accusative already present in the written tree table. This packet adds **four attested sense-qualified nominal positions with six readings**, and records **22 source tokens**.
