# PG119 — When the middle ending counts

Monty · 11 November 2026

## H01. Carry the same desire and actors into three new contexts

Reuse the ten PG118 profiles and their five exact lexical entries. The
two-event condition of 3.1.7 and the same actor, unprefixed kartari and
selected sense remain input coordinates. Lṛṭ dates the desire as future
(3.3.13); luṭ selects the non-today future (3.3.15). Lṛṅ additionally
requires the inherited PG31 construction with the relevant liṅ environment
and frustrated causal relation (3.3.139/147). The mere fact that a desired
action is in the future supplies none of those counterfactual witnesses.

## H02. Decide on the original root and its actual inner recipient

Here san, not outer sya or tāsi, is next to vṛt, vṛdh, śṛdh, syand or
kṛp. The P entitlement under 1.3.92 thus still applies when the requested
outer lakāra is luṭ. In particular **vṛt-san, luṭ, P** is admitted even
though a bare vṛt luṭ P request is outside PG17. Kṛp also retains san
through 1.3.93. The A cause is the PG118 cause under 1.3.12/62.

Retain PG118's twelve stem-branch IDs. For this packet the san and the
finite P/A ending are constituents of the same grammatical word.
Under 7.2.59 (first four) or 7.2.60 (kṛp) P forces inner aniṭ. In A,
the first three retain short iṭ; syand and kṛp permit both choices.
These conditions do not apply a second time across the intervening san.

## H03. Classify a substituted ending by the query being asked

Kāśikā 2.4.85 expressly derives the three substitutes from the original
third-person P **and** A members. Nyāsa resolves six sources to three
substitutes by number. The licenser at 7.2.59–60 asks about the presence
of A in the same word, a grammatical ending-class query. At 1.1.56 the
Kāśikā permits a tiṅ substitute to serve as the original for applicable
non-sound-based operations. This justifies retaining the taṅ class in the
selected finite A luṭ histories after ḍā/rau/ras substitution. The
phonemes of ta, ātām or jha do not have to survive.

Nyāsa 2.4.85 separately explains why the substitute does not receive a
fresh 3.4.79 vowel operation. An ending-class query and a direct
phonological target query read different properties. Primary liṭ-luk
before a separate auxiliary, and a kṛt word lacking a finite A ending,
fall outside this argument.

## H04. An identical -itā readout can have different constituents

Kāśikā 7.2.59 uses vivṛtsitā as a **kṛt** example while explaining
the inner prohibition. The finite luṭ P 3sg construction can also
give vivṛtsitā. Its t and long ā belong to tāsi and the ḍā history.
The commentary's kṛt example does not by itself attest a finite
A form, nor does it entitle the kṛt input to a finite position.
Use the actual constituent type in the request and the predecessor's
finite tāsi derivation.

## H05. Count only continued san requests

The selected extension has three labels per PG118 profile and nine
slots per label. Its keys are disjoint from PG118's four present-system
labels. Basic futures in PG17 and bare-root conditionals in PG31
supply comparison cases only. Retain the source edition, undoubled
unaccented spelling and ordinary kartari selection. A missing source,
same-actor or conditional-context witness gives HOLD. A known failed
premise is excluded from the relevant finite construction.
