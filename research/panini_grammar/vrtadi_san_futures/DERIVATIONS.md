# PG119 — Two affix boundaries in the same future

Monty · 11 November 2026

## H06. Attach sya or tāsi to the derived dhātu

Let R be one of PG118's licensed a-final san-dhātus. Rule 3.1.32
establishes this outer recipient. Rule 3.1.33 selects sya for lṛṭ
and lṛṅ and tāsi for luṭ. Each is val-initial and ārdhadhātuka;
7.2.35 supplies **short outer iṭ** to R. Under 6.4.48 R loses its
final san-a, retaining the constituent and its original marker history.
The earlier vowel/kit query of the lexical root is closed. Write C for
the resulting readout in PG87's ordered derivation.

A P path therefore can have inner iṭ absent and outer iṭ present.
An A path can have both present. For syand and kṛp A, the inner iṭ
alternative makes two stems; outer iṭ remains obligatory in both.

## H07. Lṛṭ reads sya and its own tiṅ endings

After C, outer iṭ supplies i and the s of sya becomes ṣ by 8.3.59.
The factor Ciṣy takes the P or A laṭ-like nine-slot maps of PG87 H07–08.
The finite endings here belong to lṛṭ. With vṛdh P this gives
**vivṛtsiṣyati**; with vṛdh A it gives **vivardhiṣiṣyate**. The first ṣ
in the latter reflects inner san after its own iṭ; the second belongs
to sya. The machine representation keeps both recipient addresses.

## H08. Lṛṅ adds aṭ before the whole san formation

The same sya construction takes the inherited lṛṅ P/A ending maps,
and 6.4.71 places the initial aṭ before the completed san copy.
Kṛp A 3sg thus has **acikalpiṣiṣyata** and **acikḷpsiṣyata**.
The first a is the initial augment and the i after san-s or san-ṣ is
the outer iṭ. Preserve the supplied counterfactual witness at the
desire event while realizing either inner path.

## H09. Luṭ preserves the history of its ending

Use the PG16 E3–E5 and PG87 H08 construction for Cit plus the finite
tāsi/ending readouts. In third person, append ā, ārau or āraḥ according
to number; H03 specifies the licensed ending-class query. In the other
six slots retain the distinct P and A maps. A second plural gives
-ādhve following the selected deletion of tāsi-s; A first singular
gives -āhe with the addressed s→h. There is no future auxiliary here.

Compare **vivṛtsitā** (vṛt P) with **vivartiṣitā** (vṛt A).
For kṛp, **cikḷpsitā** can be either P or A aniṭ; A also permits
**cikalpiṣitā**. Thus knowing the final substitute or even the entire
word need not identify the requested pada. These are written finite
predictions on the stated reading of 1.1.56 and 2.4.85.
