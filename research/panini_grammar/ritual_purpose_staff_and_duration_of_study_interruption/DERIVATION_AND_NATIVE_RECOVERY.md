# PG766 · What is the purpose, and what bears the ending?

5.1.109 opens the purpose relation with nominative support. The two pairings at 110 fix both a stellar name and a referent. Kumārasambhava 5.30 compounds skin and staff with the bearer. The Nyāsa gives two senses for the first referent, and Māgha 11.8 uses a mountain as the churning stick. The staff alone in the second pair takes the aṇ derivation explicitly cited in the commentary. The completed masculine bearer is a new attested compound. Cūḍā and śraddhā occur in a supplementary list and have their own bases.

At 111 distinguish the ordinary cha list, the conditioned action nouns with a preceding member, the svarga list with yat and the recitation words with luk. Retain the frozen extra t as a source observation; Bhāṣāvṛtti independently attests the ordinary form used in the panel. Āśvalāyana places the food name in a request to give, and then with cooked rice. Gobhila uses the plural locative for the ceremonies. The word shape by itself does not decide what is eaten, what is done or the gender of an unexpressed head. Manu's two adjectives point to the desired outcome of eating. Medhātithi uses a dative benefit gloss. A resemblance of meaning to the grammatical purpose condition is insufficient to select the exact affix by this verse alone. Gobhila 3.2.1's mahānāmnikāḥ refers to years of study, independently of PG763's grammarian's citation for the person or vow.

At 112 a preceding lexical pada is required. Bahuc alone cannot supply it. At 113 the meaning 'thief' guards both the ordinarily analysed ṭhañ route with its stipulated marker behavior and the alternative ikaṭ with vṛddhi. The traditional counterexample is the mendicant whose aim is a single house. At 114 the primary prescription substitutes ākāla for samānakāla when the two temporal endpoints coincide. Its feminine thunderbolt example and the supplementary feminine keep the -ī and -ā readings. The Kāśikā reports ṭhan and ṭhañ in the supplement. Kumārasambhava 3.34 has a feminine accusative in agreement with the onset of spring. Mallinātha cites 114, relates its short duration to its untimely occurrence, and also reports a competing ṭhak formation for what occurs out of season. Manu uses the identical stem for elapsed time *until the same hour next day*, applied to an interruption of study after weather events. The time of the phenomenon and duration of the resulting interruption are different arguments. The later digest explicitly measures from the triggering time to next day's corresponding time.

## Typed zero specialization

Use PG14's complete C, derived iota and dagger, ordered coefficient algebra B=CΣ⟨zₑ,zₑ*⟩ and finite-support right modules with their orthogonal-history pairing. Grade I contains the source, base, lexical member and relation. Grade D adds entitlement, individual suffix and any subsequent luk, while grade W adds the actual word, case, speaker and referent. On individual admitted edges Tₑ(eₕa)=eₕ′(1+zₑ)a; on wrong pairings and the mendicant control Tₑ=0. The adjoint carries the reverse event with factor 1+zₑ*. The empty-word ε on the coefficients induces π₀,D Dλ=D₀π₀,I and π₀,W Wλ=W₀π₀,D, since ε((1+zₑ)a)=ε(a) on every source row. Both sides vanish on the excluded rows. Each admitted ordinary route lifts with the same labels and constant coefficient one. Multiplicativity and compatibility with dagger specialize the ordered composites, adjoints and pairing. A written luk or a homonymous time adjective therefore preserves its own interpretation at zero.
