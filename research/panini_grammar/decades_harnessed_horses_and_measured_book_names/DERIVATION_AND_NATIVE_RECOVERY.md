# PG756 · Nine numbers, one team of horses

## What 5.1.59 stipulates

Take the named numeral as a lexical input with its own grammatical gender and number. For the twenty–forty stems the tradition supplies vin, trin and catvārin and the respectively named suffixes. The pre-sibilant nasal is realized as the anusvāra in viṃśati, triṃśat and catvāriṃśat. Five tens uses pañcā + śat. Six tens has the specially prescribed ṣaḍ before ti, including the stipulation about its non-pada status; the resulting lexical stem is ṣaṣṭi. Seven, eight and nine tens each have their own specified replacement before ti. In particular the ī in aśīti is supplied in the aśī history. It does not license the first-member replacement rejected earlier for compounds ending in eighty. Ten tens has śa + ta, neuter śata. These ten received lexical clauses, including paṅkti, were classified in PG36. The present extension carries their outputs to independently witnessed inflections.

In the Śākala hymn Indra is invited to come with a team. Verse 4 uses a dual with two horses and instrumental plural endings for several small numerals. Verses 5–6 change construction: each conventional decade has its own singular instrumental while the accompanying noun haribhiḥ is plural. The i-stems have -yā here, the consonantal tens have -ā, and the neuter a-stem hundred has -ena. This gives a complete nine-term textual control for twenty to one hundred. The metrical edition resolves ṣaṣṭyā with an additional syllable; the received Pada gives ṣaṣṭyā. Both concern the same enumerated verse position. A different hymn offers a dative śatāya. A dative bargain price is syntactically distinct from both the team instrumentals and PG751’s outer priced-object adjective.

Paṅkti denotes the five-footed metre in Śatapatha 4.2.5.22. The text lists five offerings, then uses the instrumental compound haviṣpaṅktyā and refers back to the series in the genitive paṅkteḥ. The pronoun etasyāḥ settles the genitive with the linked clause. The grammatical example for a line of Brahmins or ants uses the conventionally extended row sense; it does not ask for evidence that exactly five individuals stand in each row.

## A class and a title

For a varga of five or ten, 60 gives a local ḍati route with the previously available kan as alternative. Bālamanoramā explains ṭi deletion and the pronunciation-only i. Loss of base-final an and retention of the suffixal t give the consonantal stems pañcat and daśat. The bare masculine nominative with the consonant-stem ending eliminated has its final stop, seen voiced immediately before vargaḥ in the traditional connected citation. The case panel uses a voiceless pause spelling and records the witnessed voiced contact. Kan instead produces the explicit pañcakaḥ and daśakaḥ. The prior case ‘a recitation of five repetitions’ and the case ‘a named group of five birds’ cannot be relabelled as these new class-size inputs.

With 62, the denoted thing is a named Brāhmaṇa work. The old Chandas restriction and the requirement for a group in 61 have ceased. The locative brāhmaṇe specifies the referent, so a statement *about* such a work may occur outside Brāhmaṇa prose. The marker ḍ in ḍaṇ licenses terminal ṭi deletion under 6.4.143 and the ṇ conditions initial vṛddhi. The result with thirty is traiṃśa, with forty cātvāriṃśa. Final neuter plural agrees with brāhmaṇāni. Retain the nasal in the archive and in the Bālamanoramā example; one online Kāśikā display writes the first with the nasal missing. Haug expressly proposes the Kauṣītaki and Aitareya identifications from their extant chapter counts. These counts are independently corroborated, while the sūtra itself names neither school. No manuscript title or lost date is inferred from the match.
