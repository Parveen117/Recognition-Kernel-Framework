# PG769 · When two abstract nouns are evidence for one another

At 5.1.119 the genitive base supplies the distinguishing ground on which its name applies. Horse and cow give a class property. Nyāsa also allows a quality or action to supply the ground on its own word. At 120, tva and tal can accompany the later specially selected affixes. At 122 a further option lets the independently licensed aṇ on pṛthu and mṛdu coexist with imanic. These are two separate decisions. Before imanic the grammar invokes 6.4.154–155 for the final segment and 6.4.161 for the light root vowel ṛ in the two named bases. The resulting masculine n-stem has nominative singular -ā and accusative singular -ānam. The neuter aṇ stem and the feminine tal stem each retain their own gender.

TS 4.7.2.1 places prathimā among the blessings wished for. Bhaṭṭi 4.17 gives the breadth of a bodily feature as the object of dadhānena. In Raghuvaṃśa 18.49 the subject is the family's admired qualities, and their breadth is what they attain. Mallinātha replaces that accusative with the tva abstract in the same clause. This yields a separately witnessed *accusative* pṛthutvam even though its letters coincide with the grammar's nominative. Likewise Śaṅkara's commentary on Bhagavadgītā 16.2 uses the feminine abstract mṛdutā to explain the neuter mārdavam. The nominatives in the list of qualities depend on bhavanti in the next verse.

For 121 the compound relation changes the admissible suffix. Negative a-pati loses the subsequent yak route. Negative a-paṭu loses aṇ and negative a-ramaṇīya loses vuñ. Both general abstracts survive on each. The same written apaṭu can describe one who has no skilful persons. This external possessor supplies the bahuvrīhi input for āpaṭavam. Among the listed exceptions the frozen Kāśikā gives a-budha, while the canonical sūtrapāṭha and Bālamanoramā give a-yudha. The different bases own their own words. Ālasyam in the Nītiśataka is confirmed as nominative by a predicate identifying laziness as an enemy.

At 123 colours are illustrated through particular qualities such as white and black. The rule also reaches the named dṛḍādi members. Bhāṣāvṛtti and Nyāsa identify a further consequence of the written ṣ of ṣyañ: ṅīṣ under 4.1.41 and y loss under 6.4.150. The four cited feminine words have a different history from the feminine -tā branch. The compounding limitations of the gaṇa and PG481's puṃvad condition retain their individual owners.
