# PG205 · Śaṇḍha, ṣaṇḍha and the two lotus beds

Monty · 12 November 2026

The two consonants in the middle of *śaṇḍha, ṣaṇḍha, śaṇḍa* and *ṣaṇḍa* distinguish an aspirated **ḍh** from **ḍ**. The initial palatal **ś** and retroflex **ṣ** vary separately. The sources assign the four words different roots, suffixes and meanings.

| Source | Word and documented account |
| --- | --- |
| Uṇādi 1.99 | *śam + ḍha → śaṇḍha*. The Kośa cites *śāmyati*. The palatal initial belongs to this analysis. |
| Uṇādi 4.104 | *ṣam + ḍha → ṣaṇḍha*. The Tattvabodhinī expressly preserves initial ṣ and treats the suffix consonant as audible. Compare the ordinary initial s in *samati*. |
| Śabdakalpadruma | Its retroflex headword *ṣaṇḍha* also cites *śam* and the incipit *śamer ḍhaḥ*. Its two entries give different rule numbers for that incipit. Each entry retains its spelling, rule reference and sense. |
| Uṇādi 1.111 | The unaspirated *ṣaṇḍa* ‘cluster’ has *ḍa*. The commentary records *śaṇḍa* as another reading. Śabdakalpadruma explicitly allows masculine and neuter for a cluster of lotuses. |

Five profiles contribute **120 nominal positions and 125 readings**. Three profiles have direct archived form tables, and two use the checked masculine a-stem endings. The bull gloss for *śaṇḍa* comes from its own dictionary headword.

### Two flower beds decide the case

In the two genders of the collective, **ṣaṇḍe has five readings in isolation**. It can be locative singular in either gender or nominative, accusative or vocative dual in the neuter. Māgha 11.15 has *kumudakamalaṣaṇḍe* as the subject of the dual verb *dhattaḥ*. Mallinātha distinguishes the two beds, one of night lotuses closing and the other of day lotuses opening. The shared adjective *upagīte* agrees in the dual. This gives the **neuter nominative dual**. The thing that both beds assume, *tulyarūpām avasthām*, is the accusative object.

Bhāgavata 1.7.3 also has a cluster, inside *badarīṣaṇḍamaṇḍite*. Here the final *-e* belongs to *maṇḍita*, the adjective describing the singular *āśrame*. It provides another lexical occurrence of the collective inside a compound. These two passages preserve different case owners.
