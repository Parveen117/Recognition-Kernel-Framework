# PG975 · When anger arises in Bhaṭṭi 6.34

Jayamaṅgalā takes the four predicates in this verse separately. Dejection seems to issue forth, prowess seems to have checked itself, anger arises, and Rāma sighs deeply. The commentary expressly assigns *samabhāvi* to bhāve under 3.1.66. The instrumental *kopena* supplies the participant in the arising. The verb expresses the occurrence. No accusative patient is supplied. The speaker describing Rāma assigns him the act of sighing in the fourth clause. Jayamaṅgalā extends final *muhuḥ* (“repeatedly”) to all four predicates. These decisions explain why adjacent aorists in the same verse require different analyses. ## A single finite request in each category

Fix 1010001 √bhū *sattāyām*, here the occurrence or arising of anger with *sam*. There is one preverb and no derivational suffix on the root. Bhāve takes Ātmanepada with the default third singular. Several distinct episodes or several instrumental participants leave that request singular. The four-predicate clause is directly attested only for the luṅ row. In the other nine rows, temporal conditions, imperatival wish, conditional nonfulfilment, ordinary potential, remote unwitnessed past, and blessing are supplied when the selected category requires them. The verse itself asserts none of these nine readings. The short and long future and blessing stems are distinct morphological readings. The preverb’s nasal has a separate boundary history. Before a vowel it remains m. Before bh or b the table uses anusvāra. The licensed homorganic alternative has m in the same position under 8.4.58–59 (*sambhūyate*, *sambabhūve*, likewise in the other consonant-initial rows). This transcription convention gives one inflectional reading per branch. The difference between *samabhāvi* and *samabhāviṣyata* also survives projection. Only the aorist contains actual ciṇ. ## Ownership and recovery

The PG43 rule and the previous [Bhaṭṭi word alignment](../bhatti_verse_concordance/CONCORDANCE.tsv) own the source pointer for *samabhāvi*. The latter registers its exact span and leaves its interpretation to a separate construction. PG974 used the preceding prowess clause as a control. Older unprefixed √bhū derivations supply the bases in this table. All ten slots here are specifically for the prefixed, impersonal arising of anger in the policies stated above. The morphology register contains ten positions/fourteen readings. One clause construction records four predicate interfaces and the scope of *muhuḥ*. No separate finite credit is taken for the other three predicates and no nominal credit for *kopena*.
