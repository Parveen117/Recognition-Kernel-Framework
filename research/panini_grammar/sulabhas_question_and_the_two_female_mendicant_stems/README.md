# PG876 · Sulabhā’s question and the two female mendicant stems

### A named speaker in the epic

Mahābhārata 12.308.7 names **Sulabhā** and calls her a *bhikṣukī*. She travels the earth alone. In verse 8 *tayā … aṭantyā* identifies her as the woman travelling to whom the account of the king of Mithilā becomes known. In verse 12 she reaches Mithilā and sees him under the pretext of seeking alms. In verse 15 the text again calls her *bhikṣukī*, now the subject of **codayām āsa**, with *rājānam* as object. His ministers surround him. Verse 16 explains her doubt about his liberation. The text names the object of inquiry in the narrative without placing two accusative objects beside the verb in verse 15. [Epic text, verses 7–16](https://gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de/gretil/1_sanskr/2_epic/mbh/sas/b12/b12c308.htm).

Under 3.1.25 the class affix on this branch is in the root’s own sense. Sulabhā herself addresses the king. Her words do not require a further causee made to ask him. The short light medial u takes guṇa under 7.3.86, giving **cod-**. On the suffix-final root, non-mantra liṭ licenses ām under 3.1.35. ### Where the feminine nouns differ

The epic lexical stem is *bhikṣukī*. Guṇaprabha’s *Pravrajyāvastu* uses **bhikṣuṇī** for the Buddhist nun. In §§582–583 the commentary explicitly replaces the monk by the nun in the stated going-forth functions. The plural instrumental *bhikṣuṇibhir* would falsely shorten the stem: the printed sandhi has **bhikṣuṇībhir eva**, with final r before a vowel. Section 584 restricts this replacement at full ordination, distinguishes the official conducting the act from the request onward, and identifies an earlier act in the assembly of nuns. Section 588 uses the accusative for a nun considered as preceptor (*bhikṣuṇīṃ kāñcic ca*). The later residence discussion supplies genitive plural *bhikṣuṇīnām*. These are grammatical roles in a specified text and procedure. They do not make Sulabhā a Buddhist officeholder. [Vinaya text and commentary](https://www.dsbcproject.org/canon-text/content/455/2177).

The *Lalitavistara*, Vaidya p. 65, has *bhikṣuṇyo vā* in the list of listeners alongside monks and lay adherents. The underlying nominative plural is **bhikṣuṇyaḥ**. This confirms the ī-stem plural independently of the Vinaya’s singular use. The distinct spellings of the epic and Buddhist heads are retained as supplied lexical stems. A derivation assigning the historical k/ṇ difference to one interchangeable productive feminine suffix remains open. The lectured lexical base *bhikṣu* and its masculine 3.2.168 schedule at PG875 do not derive either longer feminine by exchanging a case ending. [Lalitavistara, Vaidya edition in GRETIL](https://gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de/gretil/1_sanskr/4_rellit/buddh/bsu022_u.htm).

For these two ordinary feminine long-ī schedules, prepend **bhikṣuk** or **bhikṣuṇ** to each cell. Replace N in the plural genitive by ṇ for the former, n for the latter. For *bhikṣukīṇām*, ṣ precedes the dental n across u, k and ī, which are admitted interveners under 8.4.2. The existing stem-final ṇ of *bhikṣuṇī* interrupts the route from ṣ and does not itself trigger ṇati, giving the attested *bhikṣuṇīnām*. Ordinary long-ī inflection here is a written extension.
