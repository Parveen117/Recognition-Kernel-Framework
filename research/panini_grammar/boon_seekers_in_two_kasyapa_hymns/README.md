# PG828 · The boon-seekers in two prayers of Kaśyapa

## Two constructions at the same point in the hymn

In Harivaṃśa Appendix I 42B, between 2610 and 2611, Kaśyapa asks a singular protector to protect the speakers. PG827 already parsed *varārthino nas trāyasva* with the boon-seekers as the plural object. Read the adjective in apposition to accusative *naḥ*. Before the following voiced n, final *-aḥ* of *varārthinaḥ* gives *-o*. The masculine nominative and accusative plurals happen to coincide at pause. Agreement with the object of the imperative selects the accusative analysis here. The distinct Bhaviṣyaparvan chapter numbering supplies the same petition at **3.68.7**. That transcription prints बिभषिं in the neighbouring sustaining predicate. The appendix supports *bibharṣi* at that locus. Keep the anomalous transcription in the witness layer, without adding a finite alternative.

In Vāmanapurāṇa 26, the section marked 30 has a different predicate. Kaśyapa addresses the deity as *varadaḥ*, ‘giver of boons’, with *asi* and singular *tvam*. The boon-seekers appear in the **genitive plural**, *varārthinām*, written with anusvāra before the following v. They are the people for whom he grants boons. Keep this overt genitive with its own predicate. Substituting an imperative from the Harivaṃśa would erase both the case and the speech act. The Vāmana text also has *vaṃśa prāgvaṃśa*, supporting the second epithet that differs between PG827's two witnesses. The dictionary expressly cites Harivaṃśa 251 and its preceding wording differs from the Vāmana text. Agreement on an epithet supplies a textual parallel, while the dictionary's exemplar and the direction of transmission remain Open.

## The nominal construction

[Masculine case panel](CASES.tsv). *Vara* supplies the desired boon, and the final member *arthin* supplies ‘one who seeks’. MW 16071 explicitly admits the desired item as a compound member. The two hymns attest the compound itself. The stem junction is like-vowel lengthening under 6.1.101. Outer suP belongs to the entire seeker expression. We take the attested lexical stem *arthin* as input, leaving a productive inner affix analysis and other senses of *arthin* outside this construction. A distinct giver, *varada*, is the singular predicate in the Vāmana text. The written table is unaccented.

Whitney §§438–440 provides the masculine *-in* adapter. In NOMsg, final n drops and the vowel is long. In VOCsg the stem ends in n. Before vowel-initial oblique endings the n is retained, as in GENpl *-inām*. Before bh-endings and plural locative su, the stem has *-i*, with the latter ending realized as *-ṣu*. The preceding dental th keeps the lexical n dental despite the earlier r. Shared dual endings and the NOM/ACC/VOC plural homophony retain separate case addresses. Only GENpl supplies a first-time textual key. ACCpl was parsed in PG827 prose and takes zero credit here even though PG827 entered no nominal increment. The remaining twenty-two keys are written constructions, including the homophonous plural address. There is no new finite or *viśvapāḥ* credit.
