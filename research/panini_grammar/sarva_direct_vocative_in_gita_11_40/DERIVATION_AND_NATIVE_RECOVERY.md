# PG890 · Derivation and native recovery

## The verse decides a textual question, not a new paradigm cell

PG887 already constructed the ordinary freestanding `sarva` all/entire
vocative panel and explicitly left direct textual address Open. The pinned
Bhagavadgītā page now supplies the missing positive evidence at 11.40:

> namo 'stu te sarvata eva sarva / anantavīryāmitavikramas tvaṃ /
> sarvaṃ samāpnoṣi tato 'si sarvaḥ

The source context has second-person `te`, `tvam` and `samāpnoṣi`. More
decisively, two commentarial passages on the same printed page expand the
unmarked first token as `he sarva`. The verse then contrasts that address
with neuter accusative `sarvam`, the object of `samāpnoṣi`, and masculine
nominative `sarvaḥ`, the predicate after `asi`. The missing visarga on the
first token is therefore not a damaged nominative and the following `-m`
is not an alternative spelling of the address.

This closes exactly one previously Open evidence key:
`Gītā11.40 × sarva × all/entire epithet × M × VOC × SG`. PG887 already owns
its short-*a* sambuddhi morphology and nominal credit, so this stage adds no
word position or inflectional reading. The two other forms are source
controls, not fresh paradigm credits. The unaccented Classical witness does
not settle Vedic accent, and no direct F/N or dual/plural address is inferred.

## Source and semantic interfaces

One source-syntax construction retains three independently typed roles:

1. `te ... sarva` — addressed participant, M VOCsg;
2. `sarvam samāpnoṣi` — totality as N ACCsg patient/object;
3. `asi sarvaḥ` — M NOMsg predicate of the addressed second person.

The semantic record keeps the divine all-immanent epithet on the first and
third forms while retaining the syntactic distinction between address and
predication. It does not turn every occurrence of `sarva` into a vocative,
nor does it generalize the source's theological sense to every lexical use.
