# PG809 · Derivation and native recovery

Rule 27 assigns `astāti` to primitive direction words under direction, place or time, separately after locative, ablative and nominative inputs; it explicitly rejects derivative direction words, accusative government and the non-directional “former teacher” reading. Rule 28 replaces that path with `atasuc` after `dakṣiṇa/uttara`, while the south word cannot carry the temporal relation. Rule 29 makes `atasuc` optional against `astāti` after `para/avara`; Kāśikā prints `avastāt`, Vasu summarizes `avarastāt`, and rule 41 later positively licenses both histories, so the readings are source-labelled and priority remains Open.

Rule 30 deletes `astāti` after añc-final direction words and also deletes the feminine suffix under 1.2.49. Rules 31–33 own the irregular substitutions and suffixes of `upari/upariṣṭāt`, `paścāt`, the five displayed paśc compounds, and Vedic `paśca/paścā`; Vedic `paścāt` extends the existing rule-32 position. Rule 34 admits `āti` after `uttara/adhara/dakṣiṇa` in all three contexts without yathāsaṃkhya.

Rule 35 restricts optional `enap` to a near limit and non-ablative inputs. The displayed northern, lower and southern paths are constructed; `pūrveṇa grāmam/apareṇa grāmam` are retained as the explicitly reported wider-school interpretation, not generalized silently. Rule 36 gives non-ablative `dakṣiṇā` without the near restriction. Rules 37–38 add the distant southern and northern `ā/āhi` alternatives, while the repeated distant `dakṣiṇā` histories extend rule 36 rather than creating duplicate positions.

Rule 39 maps `pūrva/adhara/avara` to `pur/adh/av` before `asi` across all three case-contexts. Rule 40 carries the substitutions before `astāti` and adds histories to the already owned `purastāt/adhastāt/avastāt` positions. Rule 41 makes the last substitution optional, preserving `avastāt/avarastāt` with distinct histories.
