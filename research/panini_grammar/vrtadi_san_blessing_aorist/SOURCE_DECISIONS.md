# PG120 — A blessing for a desire and a completed desire

Monty · 11 November 2026

## H01. Qualify the outer event

Use the ten PG118 lexical/semantic/pada profiles and the twelve inner
san branches carried by PG119. The desirer must be the actor of the
desired action under 3.1.7. For āśīrliṅ select the liṅ member of 3.3.173
and supply an unattained desire event as the object of the blessing. For
luṅ select the ordinary completed desire in the inherited 3.2.110 past
context. Neither request asserts that the inner desired action occurred.
A missing role or context witness leaves the corresponding request open.

## H02. A same-word condition can look beyond the next affix

Mahābhāṣya 7.2.59 explicitly gives vivartiṣiṣyate for the intervening
future case, vartiṣīṣṭa for sīyuṭ, and a
plural with coalescence. Its resolution tests A within the same word.
Its examples vivṛtsitā/vivṛtsitum/vivṛtsitavyam illustrate the kṛt
contrast. Thus absolute adjacency of the finite A ending to the lexical
root is too narrow. The first example directly supports PG119's vṛt A
lṛṭ 3sg spelling. The quoted blessing example has a basic root; the
new san blessings below require the additional derived-root interface.

## H03. Admit the new ending and preserve the inner decision

P and A are selected through san under 1.3.92–93, with the A cause
of PG118 retained. In these two finite constructions the original root
and the outer personal ending are in the same grammatical word. Keep
PG118's dependent inner iṭ and kit sets. For example, kṛp A may
enter the outer construction as either cikalpiṣa or cikḷpsa.
Do not apply the PG117 **basic** kṛp/syand aorist choices to san.

## H04. Identify the outer recipient and its own iṭ

After 3.1.32 the completed multi-vowel san-dhātu is the recipient.
The PG88 H02/H05 source construction licenses 6.4.48 final san-a lopa.
P blessing yāsuṭ has initial y outside val and takes no outer iṭ.
A blessing sīyuṭ is preceded by short iṭ. Luṅ cli becomes sic under
3.1.43–44 and short outer iṭ is required in both padas. None of these
outer decisions reopens the already constructed inner root-to-san choice.
An inner ūdit lexical entry cannot make the outer derived input ūdit.

## H05. Keep primary luṅ and periphrastic liṭ distinct

Here cli belongs to the derived san-dhātu. It meets neither the
underived dyutādi aṅ condition nor the ṇic-dependent caṅ condition.
For liṭ, ām and the primary ending's luk would change the location of
the pronounced ending. That distinct same-word question receives no
positions or projected readings in PG120. The retained PG88 source
pins supply the selected ending and sound schedules for this stage.
