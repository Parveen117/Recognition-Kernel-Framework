## H06. Take the san branch before adding the outer blessing

Let C denote a licensed PG118 san-dhātu following deletion of its
final a. For P blessing use PG88's yāsuṭ tuple after C. Thus vṛt
and vṛdh P 3sg both read **vivṛtsyāt**. The yāsuṭ/kit property
belongs to the outer blessing; PG118's san-kit has a different owner.
In A the outer iṭ plus sīyuṭ gives Ciṣī… in the selected map.
Kṛp A 3sg gives **cikalpiṣiṣīṣṭa** and **cikḷpsiṣīṣṭa**.
The short outer i and long sīyuṭ ī are separately addressed. The
source-qualified dental dual st/sth and the A 2pl dh policy are PG88 H06.

## H07. Luṅ attaches sic to the completed san-dhātu

Retain C, short outer iṭ and the identity of sic. Take the appropriate
P/A nine-slot tuple from PG88 H09. Add initial aṭ before the completed
root's reduplicant. The P path of syand therefore gives **asisyantsīt**
in 3sg and **asisyantsiṣṭām** in 3du. Inner san-s survives in both.
Only the first receives ending-owned īṭ under 7.3.96 and loses sic-s
under 8.2.28. Outer iṭ and ending īṭ coalesce on the pinned PG88
schedule. Store the three augment addresses (initial aṭ, outer iṭ,
ending īṭ) independently.

## H08. Test which s the later rule can see

For P 3sg and 2sg, 8.2.28 addresses the sic-s after outer iṭ and
before ending īṭ. For A 2pl 8.2.25 removes outer sic-s before dhvam.
In the latter, the constituent before outer iṭ ends in san s/ṣ; the
8.3.78–79 iṇ condition for ḍh is absent. For kṛp the two A readings
are **acikalpiṣidhvam** and **acikḷpsidhvam**. Both preserve their
distinct inner histories, retain short iṭ, and have dental dh.
Where a suffix retains sic after iṭ, use its 8.3.59 ṣ reading and
the local stop contact from PG88 H09.

## H09. Use the grammatical role of the vowel

For vṛt P luṅ 3sg, lexical vṛt + san has no inner iṭ,
the completed san-dhātu obligatorily takes outer iṭ before sic,
and the ending receives īṭ: **avivṛtsīt**. In A luṅ 3sg, inner
and outer iṭ are both present and ending īṭ is absent, giving
**avivartiṣiṣṭa**. In P blessing 3sg none of these three iṭ/īṭ
events applies although the copy vi still contains an i. A procedure
counting written i vowels cannot recover these decisions.
