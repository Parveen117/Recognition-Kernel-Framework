# PG49 — Two addressed paths to हास्तिशीर्ष्या

Author: Monty. 4 November 2026.

The target is Kāśikā 6.1.62's unaccented feminine stem **हास्तिशीर्ष्या**.
Two supplied source policies reach that spelling through different histories.
The same field explicitly rejects **हास्तिशीर्षण्या**. The diagnostic
continuation below explains that unwanted result; it is not an accepted branch.

## Shared preparation and admitted input

Supply the lexical compound **हस्तिशिरस्**, its addressed final शिरस्
member, the commentary's descent relation and qualified non-ऋषि गोत्र
feminine context. Compound formation and unrestricted lexical/semantic
recognition are outside this construction. Kāśikā 4.1.96 lists शिरस्;
Kāśikā 6.1.62 explicitly licenses this compound example. Membership of an
ending alone is not an algorithm for admitting every compound bearing it.

Keep base B, final member M, suffix I = इञ्, and later feminine suffix F
as separate records. A replacement creates a new version of its addressed
owner; an added suffix creates another owner. All displays omit accent and
inflection. The source's masculine हास्तिशीर्षिः is a citation form; the
working stem at S4 is हास्तिशीर्षि.

R3 is a source-attested potential query, not permission to restore every old
sound in every consumer. R4 is not a general theorem about all prerequisites.
Padamañjarī raises the converse objection: S2 itself depended on a
vowel-initial suffix, but R1 changes that suffix to a य-initial one. It
answers **अनित्या सन्निपातपरिभाषा**. Consequently the record must retain
the local permission for R1 and the local restriction at R4 separately.
A uniform “never disturb an earlier condition” implementation would wrongly
block R1 too. [Source policy](SOURCE_POLICY.md) preserves the exact text.

Kāśikā 6.1.62 explicitly invokes यस्येति-lopa and its स्थानिवद्भाव
intervention. The supporting general clause is 1.1.57; Nyāsa 4.1.78 names it
in the closely related औडुलोम्या comparison. This account does not pronounce
the deleted इ again or treat any retained history as a license for any rule.

For this bounded comparison we retain the usual ष्यङ् name, markers and
चाप् realization. Padamañjarī also debates how many markers a redesigned
added-affix presentation needs. That alternative notation and its accent
argument are not separately reconstructed or counted here. No downstream
अपत्य-transfer theorem is inferred merely from this local successful spelling.
