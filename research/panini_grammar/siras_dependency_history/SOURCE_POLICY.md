## Two accounts, one stated target

Kāśikā 6.1.62 explicitly contrasts intended **हास्तिशीर्ष्या** with
unwanted **हास्तिशीर्षण्या**. It offers an added-affix account, in which
deleted इ remains an effective intervener, and calls for additional work
in the replacement account. Padamañjarī makes the alternatives explicit:
adopt addition, or retain replacement with the required safeguard.

Padamañjarī 4.1.78 associates the added-affix discussion with Vāmana and
reports Jayāditya's dependency argument for replacement. They are distinct
supplied policies and event histories. Their shared intended unaccented
output does not identify the records or settle all differences between the
accounts. PG48's fixed-policy negative model cannot reject Vāmana's separately
licensed अपत्य-transfer account merely by assuming its license absent.

## What the safeguard establishes

Padamañjarī 4.1.78 expressly says **अनित्या सन्निपातपरिभाषा**. The
written construction therefore records the particular permission and
restriction, rather than treating all enabling relations as immutable.
It permits the admitted इञ्→ष्यङ् step and blocks the particular later
शीर्षन् proposal under the chosen policy. General selection of applications
of that paribhāṣā remains open.

For the added-affix comparison, the standard ष्यङ् designation and its
चाप् realization are retained. The commentary's further debate about
reducing markers and its accent analysis are not separately constructed.
The इ deleted in this route is a different event from replacing the whole
इञ् suffix, and from deleting the earlier base-final अ.

## Source recovery and limits of checking

Three complete primary files—data, Kāśikā and Padamañjarī—were fetched
as immutable Git blobs and rechecked for byte count, SHA-256 and Git blob
identity against the earlier pins. The 32 selected sūtra texts and their
32 Kāśikā fields, plus four Padamañjarī fields, were compared directly
with those decoded primary files.

The full Nyāsa 4.1.78 field was read from PG48's immutable literal archive.
Its exact string is copied and verified against that rehashed archive.
The two empty Nyāsa 6.1.61/62 entries are inherited inspections from PG48,
not fresh independent primary-file inspections. They supply no commentary
on this example. Attempts to retrieve the complete Nyāsa file in this run
returned a transport error; its original whole-file identity is explicitly
marked inherited, not counted among the three fresh primary rehashes.

This provenance chain preserves a usable source witness without claiming a
new direct comparison that did not occur. [Pins](SOURCE_PINS.json) distinguish
freshly rehashed files from the inherited Nyāsa identity. The 69-field count
excludes the two empty entries and excludes other background readings not
selected for this packet.

The literal fields support source-qualified written analysis. They do not
independently verify Sanskrit semantics, full lexical admission, accent,
declension, a general scheduler or runtime behavior.
