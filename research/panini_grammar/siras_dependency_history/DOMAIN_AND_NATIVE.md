## D2. Why one universal dependency bit cannot encode this account

Consider the selected replacement policy and two candidate operation records:

- eY is the admitted इञ्→ष्यङ् replacement. It changes the vowel-initial
  suffix environment used by the earlier शीर्ष prescription.
- eN is the proposed subsequent शीर्षन् replacement, enabled by that
  ष्यङ् environment. Under the selected local safeguard it is blocked.

Suppose a serialization retains only the bit “this proposal changes an
enabling configuration of an earlier operation,” and a decision is required
to be a function of that bit alone. Both supplied records have bit value one,
but the source-policy decisions differ. No such function gives both decisions:
equal inputs to a function have equal outputs. This is the entire bounded
obstruction.

The domain includes a rejected proposal; it does not exhibit two equally
legal full derivations or prove that every dependency-aware parser fails.
Retaining the candidate operation, source policy, addressed enabling event
and consumer-specific permission repairs this particular information loss.
The construction does not provide a complete decision procedure for unknown
applications of the paribhāṣā.

The two successful routes also have equal final spelling but different owners,
events and reasons for blocking 6.1.61. A surface projection may forget these;
the full record keeps them. Equal spelling establishes no identity of proofs.

while preserving history and all policy/view coordinates. On the fixed

\[
\pi_{0,\mathrm{out}}S_{49,\lambda}
=S_{49,0}\pi_{0,\mathrm{in}},\qquad
\epsilon\langle x,y\rangle_{B_{49}}
=\langle\pi_0x,\pi_0y\rangle_{C_\Sigma}.
