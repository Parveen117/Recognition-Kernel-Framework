# PG14 — Written derivations from meaning to form

Author: Monty. 2 November 2026.

These are source-argued derivations, not executed examples. Each uses the
domain and role decisions of [V1–V7](VOICE_AND_PADA.md). A quoted word in a
commentary, a word obtained by the displayed rules, and a companion's stored
expectation are different kinds of evidence. The general paradigm below
is a written rule consequence, not nine independent literal citations.

## D1. One thematic A construction, with nine explicitly derived slots

Let an already licensed laṭ A aṅga be \(Ba\), where the final *a* has its
own owner. The 3.4.78 endings are *ta, ātām, jha, thās, āthām, dhvam, iṭ,
vahi, mahiṅ*. Marker removal preserves their identities; laṭ supplies the
ṭit condition. The non-pit sārvadhātuka endings have the required ṅit-like
status under 1.2.4, not a newly pronounced ṅ.

| Slot | Ending construction | Result after the aṅga-final a |
|---|---|---|
| 3sg | ta → te, 3.4.79 | Bate |
| 3du | ātām → āte, 3.4.79; ā → iy, 7.2.81; y-lopa, 6.1.66; a+i → e, 6.1.87 | Bete |
| 3pl | jha → jhe, 3.4.79; jh → ant, 7.1.3; a+a → a, 6.1.97 | Bante |
| 2sg | thās → se, specific 3.4.80 | Base |
| 2du | āthām → āthe; ā → iy; y-lopa; a+i → e, same rules as 3du | Bethe |
| 2pl | dhvam → dhve, 3.4.79 | Badhve |
| 1sg | iṭ → i by marker lopa; i → e, 3.4.79; a+e → e, 6.1.97 | Be |
| 1du | vahi → vahe, 3.4.79; a → ā before v, 7.3.101 | Bāvahe |
| 1pl | mahiṅ → mahi by marker lopa; → mahe, 3.4.79; a → ā before m, 7.3.101 | Bāmahe |

The ṭi selector in 3.4.79 uses 1.1.64, not “replace the last character.”
The dual chain specifically changes the ending's initial ā. It does not
shorten a root vowel. First singular's final spelling does not on its own
prove that 3.4.79 was applied: a+i could also surface as e. The displayed
history supplies that rule witness independently of its final string.

These are ending-junction forms before the retained Tripādī readout.
In the third plural, internal n before t undergoes 8.3.24 anusvāra and
then 8.4.58 restores the dental nasal before dental t. Thus the readout
of kārayante includes kārayaṃte → kārayante. The later restored n is not
retroactively exposed to earlier 8.4.1 ṇatva: 8.2.1 preserves the directed
visibility order. The unchanged final spelling must not hide these events.
For a general Ba, any further root-specific phonetic obligations are still
required; the tables below use the stated pac/kṛ formations.

For \(Ba=\textit{pacya}\), this gives

| Person | Singular | Dual | Plural |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | pacyate | pacyete | pacyante |
| 2 | pacyase | pacyethe | pacyadhve |
| 1 | pacye | pacyāvahe | pacyāmahe |

These nine slots describe a karma-passive paradigm when the expressed
patient has the corresponding discourse identity/number. They are **not**
a bhāva paradigm: that domain selects only 3sg before this construction.

For \(Ba=\textit{kāraya}\), in a causative A context licensed by V6, the
same owned-ending construction gives:

| Person | Singular | Dual | Plural |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | kārayate | kārayete | kārayante |
| 2 | kārayase | kārayethe | kārayadhve |
| 1 | kāraye | kārayāvahe | kārayāmahe |

Here the causer is the agreement controller. No inference of passive voice
from the ending *-te* is permitted.

## D2. Active pacati and karma-passive pacyate

Select pac 1011151 with an independent cook and intended food karman.

**Active P context.** The agent is expressed by tiṅ (3.4.69), and the
stated P interpretation selects tip for a third-person singular agent.
The kartṛ-expressing sārvadhātuka ending triggers śap (3.1.68). Its markers
are retained after lopa, giving *pac + a + ti → pacati*. The food remains
unexpressed by tiṅ, so its accusative follows 2.3.1–2; the ordinary agent
nominal receives nominative in the stated 2.3.46 conditions.

**Karma-passive.** Select the same event's food karman for expression.
1.3.13 supplies A and its third-person singular selects *ta*. The following
sārvadhātuka ending licenses yak by 3.1.67. Its k becomes an unpronounced
it, retaining kit:

\[
 [pac]_{root}+[yak]_{affix}+[ta]_{ending}
 \to [pac]+[ya]_{kit}+[te]\to pacyate.
\]

3.1.68's kartṛ-expression guard is false. The food's karman is already
expressed by tiṅ; the unexpressed cook is instrumental (2.3.18).
*Odanaḥ pacyate devadattena* is the relevant source-illustrated construction
(Bālamanoramā/Tattvabodhinī 3.4.69). For several food items the karman
controller selects 3pl and D1 gives *pacyante*. Several cooks alone do not.

This explains the contrast by meaning, roles, affixes and ownership before
surface observation. It is not a substitution of *yate* for *ati*.

## D3. Bhāva with several agents: bhūyate, not a plural by agreement

Select bhū 1010001 in the intransitive being frame. Choose bhāva by 3.4.69.
The expressed action does not co-refer with any instrumental agent. V3
therefore selects A 3sg, even if the agents include speaker, addressee and
several others. Siddhāntakaumudī 3.1.67 literally gives:

> त्वया मयाऽन्यैश्च भूयते ।

The owned affix chain is

\[
 bhū+yak+ta\to bhū+ya_{kit}+te\to bhūyate.
\]

1.1.5 blocks the yak-triggered guṇa that would otherwise change ū. The
unexpressed agents receive instrumental under 2.3.18. Replacing ta by jha
to produce *bhūyante* on this same frame is excluded **before** ending
formation. We make no universal spelling ban on every imaginable use of
that character string; the contradiction concerns this selected bhāva
construction. This is the missing distinction in the reviewed T110 table.

For svap 1020063, the same semantic argument selects 3sg. With kit yak,
6.1.15 changes the owned v to u, giving *suap*; 6.1.108 contracts the
following a into that samprasāraṇa vowel, giving *sup*. Thus
*svap + yak + ta → suap + ya + te → sup + ya + te → supyate*.
Kāśikā 1.3.13 supplies *supyate bhavatā*. Plural agents do not change it
to plural tiṅ in this bhāva reading.

## D4. nīyate, dīyate and sthīyate have different root operations

For nī 1011049 in the selected transitive frame, expression of its led
karman gives *nī + yak + ta*. The kit property of yak blocks guṇa (1.1.5),
so *nī + ya + te → nīyate*. In contrast, PG5's active *nayati* uses its
own śap-triggered guṇa and ayādi chain. The two formations cannot share
an already-guṇated stem without preserving which affix licensed it.

For dā 1030010, the identified ghu root, 6.4.66 changes its ā to ī before
the consonant-initial kit yak. Then *dī + ya + te → dīyate*. For sthā
1011077, the same cited rule gives *sthī + ya + te → sthīyate*.
The selected standing frame is intransitive and bhāva, so V3 permits only
3sg. The given-object frame of dā is karma-passive and can use the other
patient-controlled slots from D1.

Kāśikā 6.4.66 gives *dīyate* and *sthīyate*, and distinguishes drinking pā
from the protecting homonym. No “all roots written with final ā” shortcut
is inferred from this selected list.

## D5. Ṛ-final roots: kriyate and hriyate

Kāśikā 7.4.28 expressly supplies *kriyate* and *hriyate*. Before yak the
ṛ-final aṅga takes **riṅ**, not a general vowel-lengthening operation.
Its final ṅ is it; 1.1.53 marks replacement of the last sound even though
the pronounced replacement *ri* has two sounds. Preserve the original ṛ
and the replacement's source identity:

\[
 kṛ+yak+ta\to k[ri]_{ṛ\mapsto ri}+ya_{kit}+te\to kriyate,
\]

\[
 hṛ+yak+ta\to h[ri]_{ṛ\mapsto ri}+ya_{kit}+te\to hriyate.
\]

The kit trigger still prohibits ordinary guṇa of the replaced i. The
written *riṅ* is the short-i substitute in 7.4.28; it must not be silently
changed to *rīṅ*. These are new written root paths relative to the T110
scope, which expressly left kṛ out. Their meaningful A endings follow D1
only when the expressed karman has the relevant person/number.

## D6. Causative A from a hetumat frame: kārayate and pācayate

Start with kṛ 1080010, a maker b and a product. Add a distinct causer a
inducing b's activity. 1.4.55 gives a hetu and kartṛ; 3.1.26 licenses ṇic.
Let the intended principal result be a's, without an upapada conveying
that fact. V6 selects A by 1.3.74, and a third-person singular causer gives
ta. Choose either lawful causee-case branch of 1.4.53.

PG13's source argument gives the owned root/formation chain:

\[
 kṛ+ṇic\to [kār]_{root}+[i]_{ṇic}
 \to [[kār]+[i]]_{derived\ dhātu}+śap+ta.
\]

The root's vṛddhi is from 7.2.115 and its r-accompanied value from 1.1.51.
The root-plus-ṇic constituent is a dhātu by 3.1.32. Śap is licensed because
this is **kartari** despite A. Its own position makes ṇic's i the final
vowel of the preceding derived aṅga: 7.3.84 gives e and 6.1.78 gives ay.
The root's kār is not subjected to a second root-vṛddhi operation:

\[
 [kār]+[i]+[a]+[ta]
 \to[kār]+[e]+[a]+[te]
 \to[kār]+[ay]+[a]+[te]\to kārayate.
\]

For pac, PG13's 7.2.116 upadhā-vṛddhi gives pāc, and the same
Ṇic/śap/ending construction gives *pācayate*. Kāśikā 1.3.74 literally
supplies *kaṭaṃ kārayate* and *odanaṃ pācayate*. These are active
causatives in A, not passive forms.

## D7. Two independent optional relations must remain distinct

For the same kṛ frame, suppose an actual nearby upapada expresses the
causer-directed principal result. Kāśikā 1.3.77 explicitly extends its
optional A rule over 1.3.72–76, including ṇic. It licenses A and residual
P. Independently, 1.4.53 licenses causee as karman or retained kartṛ.

| Branch label | Causee role/case | Pada / 3sg word |
|---|---|---|
| (1.4.53:karman, 1.3.77:A) | karman / accusative | A / kārayate |
| (1.4.53:kartṛ, 1.3.77:A) | unexpressed kartṛ / instrumental | A / kārayate |
| (1.4.53:karman, 1.3.77:P) | karman / accusative | P / kārayati |
| (1.4.53:kartṛ, 1.3.77:P) | unexpressed kartṛ / instrumental | P / kārayati |

The four histories follow because neither predicate changes the other's
input in this stated frame. Two verb strings therefore do not mean two
complete derivations. This independence claim is local to kṛ in D_C;
the commentary's A-conditioned abhivādi/dṛś extension of 1.4.53 cannot be
imported into the same Cartesian-product argument without its pada guard.

Without positive upapada evidence, unknown beneficiary information does
not license this optionality. It is an open interpretation, as S5 explains.

## D8. pā, budh and nī separate three tempting shortcuts

**pā, drinking (1011074).** Its ingestion sense initially meets 1.3.87,
but 1.3.89 cancels that P exception. With causer-directed principal result
and no conveying upapada, 1.3.74 selects A. PG13's owned yuk (7.3.37) gives
*pā + y + i*; its k is it and u pronunciation-only in the augment's
teaching form. The derived-dhātu/śap chain changes ṇic i → e → ay,
and ta → te gives *pāyayate*, literally in Kāśikā 1.3.89.
With other-directed result, residual P gives *pāyayati*. The prohibition
does not ban P in every context. Protecting pā 1020051 is a different
lexical identity and its *pālayati* path is not substituted here.

**budh, understanding (1040068).** The base root's A classification does
not determine causative pada. 1.3.86 explicitly overrides the 1.3.74 A
result: even with the stated causer-directed result the causative is P.
PG13's root guṇa gives bodh; the derived-dhātu/śap chain gives bodhaya;
tip gives *bodhayati*. The causee is karman by cognition in 1.4.52.

**nī, leading (1011049).** Its base sense is movement, but Kāśikā 1.4.52
explicitly excludes nī from that causee-karman prescription:

> नाययति भारं देवदत्तेन ।

The causee retains the instrumental route. Conversely gam's transitive
goal frame gives the causee karman:

> गमयति माणवकं ग्रामम् ।

These quotations support a role distinction; they do not give permission
to conflate every root with the English label “motion.” Likewise 1.3.87's
calana is glossed kampana, a different pada predicate.

## D9. Explicit boundaries of the written construction

The source quotations above include bhūyate, supyate, dīyate, sthīyate,
kriyate, hriyate, kārayate, pācayate, pāyayate, bodhayati and the nī/gam
case examples. The full nine-slot tables and other A forms in V7 are
written consequences, not separate quotations. Semantic frames are
declared and evidenced, not recovered by a parser. PG13's kṛṣ word-witness
gap and earlier source gaps remain recorded.

No ṇic passive is inferred by appending yak to *kāraya*. That path needs
ṇic-lopa/remaining-root operations and the correct expressed karman when
there are two. Nor is present yak carried into future/aorist/benedictive
formations without their own sārvadhātuka/ārdhadhātuka and formation rules.
Those are mathematical next steps, not reasons to inflate this packet's
claim from a collection of familiar final words.
