## C1. T110's Cartesian slot table needs a bhāva gate

The implementation has `CELLS = [("3s", "ta"), ("3p", "jha")]` and
`forms_for` applies both cells to every root in `ROOTS`, including bhū,
sthā and svap. `oracle` likewise forms both strings from the selected stem.
The request has no separate event, transitivity, expressed-role or
agreement-controller field. These statements follow from reading the code;
they are not new runtime results.

Siddhāntakaumudī 3.1.67 explicitly gives default 3sg for bhāva, even in
*tvayā mayānyaiś ca bhūyate*. Bālamanoramā explains why plural instrumental
agents do not supply plural tiṅ. Therefore T110's paired string table
cannot by itself license a 3pl bhāva derivation for the unprefixed
intransitive frames selected in V1.

PG14 retains the useful yak mechanics and supplies the missing distinction:
karma-passive agrees with its expressed karman; bhāva selects only 3sg.
The claim is scoped to the stated frames, not a universal ban on a spelling
under every other possible lexical/semantic analysis. T110's old certificate
bytes are preserved; agreement of a generator with its own ending template
does not resolve the absent semantic premise.

## C2. T114's similarity label does not match 2.1.7

The T114 theorem's excluded-scope paragraph lists the declared senses as
“samīpa, vīpsā, adhikaraṇa, sādṛśya.” Its source list and yathāśakti row
invoke 2.1.7. The implementation's corresponding `NOT_claimed` text repeats
that list; the fixed case tuple does not carry a semantic guard.

Kāśikā 2.1.7 reads **asādṛśya**, and explicitly says the separate rule
excludes similarity. The result is a source-explanation qualification:
the yathā case must have an admitted non-similarity meaning, such as the
ordinary according-to interpretation, not the listed similarity label.
This finding does not claim the spelling *yathāśakti* itself is wrong.
No correction to the frozen companion packet or published certificate is
made here. A later compound construction must retain the semantic predicate.
