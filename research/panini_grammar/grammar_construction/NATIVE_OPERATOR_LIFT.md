# PG14 — A history-preserving native grammar operator family

Author: Monty. 2 November 2026.

This is an explicit admitted **algebraic instance**, not a claim that the
whole primitive lambda carrier is one scalar or that every native algebra
admits the projection below. It uses the canonical engine's cut field and
typed path-presentation principles. No shared engine code is changed.

## N1. Construct the coefficient carrier before its zero-reading

Start with the native cut field \(C=C_\Sigma\), its derived \(\iota\), and
its scalar dagger. Let \(E\) index the source-bearing, typed grammar events
of S2–S3. Choose one common native coefficient object and, for every event,
two formal residue-loop generators \(z_e,z_e^*\) at that object. The
coefficient algebra is the finite cut-path algebra

\[
 B=C\langle z_e,z_e^*:e\in E\rangle.
\]

Concretely, its elements are finite cut-linear combinations of ordered words
in those generators, including the empty word 1. Composition concatenates
words in operator order, with the rightmost acting first. Dagger conjugates
coefficients, reverses words, and interchanges \(z_e\) and \(z_e^*\).
No commutations or relations among distinct residue words are imposed.

This explicitly chosen free native path presentation retains source-event
labels and order. It is an algebraic carrier, not an analytic completion.
Its operators below act on a right B-module; it contains arbitrary finite
operator residues, not merely a line of scalar values. The symbol lambda
names this full operator construction. No \(z_e\) is identified as a clock,
coupling, derivative order, physical quantity or the entire lambda space.

Define \(\epsilon:B\to C\) by taking the empty-word coefficient, equivalently
\(\epsilon(z_e)=\epsilon(z_e^*)=0\) and \(\epsilon(c)=c\).
Products of nonempty words are nonempty, so

\[
 \epsilon(ab)=\epsilon(a)\epsilon(b),\quad
 \epsilon(a^*)=\overline{\epsilon(a)},\quad \epsilon(1)=1.
\]

Its kernel is the two-sided, dagger-stable ideal generated by the residue
letters. This is a constructed algebra homomorphism, not an arbitrary
matrix corner compression. Extra relations could be imposed only if their
ideal lies in ker(ε) and is dagger-stable. For example, declaring
\(z^*z=1\) destroys this augmentation: its two sides specialize to 0 and 1.
We do not impose it. A simple full-matrix native algebra cannot in general
be replaced by B while pretending its old relations have survived.

## N2. Grammar histories, type and pairing

Let \(\mathcal H\) consist of finite admitted histories, including their
initial frame, every intermediate state, event evidence, branches and open
obligations. Distinct initial lexical IDs and distinct branches remain
distinct even if they print the same word. Assume finitely many outgoing
events from each history. This is true of the finite source decisions and
explicit finite derivations constructed in PG14; arbitrary full-grammar
search requires its own finiteness argument.

Construct the right modules

\[
 M_\lambda=\bigoplus_{h\in\mathcal H}e_h B,
 \qquad M_0=\bigoplus_{h\in\mathcal H}e_h C.
\]

Only finite-support vectors are admitted. Object/state types grade the
summands, so a grammar arrow maps a declared source grade to a declared
target grade. Cross-type composition is excluded. The common coefficient
object in N1 supplies the same coefficient algebra to these different
grammar types; it does not identify their labels or grammatical domains.

Choose the explicit algebra-valued pairing

\[
 \langle x,y\rangle_B=\sum_h x_h^*y_h,
 \qquad \langle x,y\rangle_C=\sum_h\overline{x_h}y_h.
\]

This is a declared orthogonal direct-sum construction from the coefficient
dagger. The sums are finite. It is sesquilinear and algebraically
nondegenerate: pairing against each basis vector detects that coordinate.
No ordered positivity, norm, Hilbert completion or physical probability is
inferred. An adjoint formula derived from this pairing is not asserted to
be the dagger for a different, undeclared native pairing.

The typed state specialization is

\[
 \pi_{0,M}(\sum_h e_h a_h)=\sum_h e_h\epsilon(a_h).
\]

It retains the entire history label, original teaching forms, complementary
information, lexical/sense decisions and source evidence. Its section
\(j_M\) includes cut-scalar coefficients as constant paths. Thus
\(\pi_{0,M}j_M=1\), while \(j_M\pi_{0,M}\) need not be identity.

## N3. Cuts and rule operators

For any declared subset \(S\subseteq\mathcal H\), let \(P_S\) retain exactly
those history summands, with \(Q_S=1-P_S\). Then

\[
 P_S^2=P_S=P_S^*,\quad Q_S^2=Q_S=Q_S^*,\quad P_SQ_S=0.
\]

The equality with the adjoint follows from the displayed pairing, coordinate
by coordinate. These are native apertures in this constructed carrier.
For a three-valued guard the true, false and unknown subsets give three
orthogonal cuts whose sum is 1. Unknown is a retained channel, not the
same channel as source-excluded.

For an admitted event \(e:h\to h'\), choose its native payload

\[
 U_e=1+z_e.
\]

More generally any finite polynomial with ε(U_e)=1 is allowed. The local
operator is right-B-linear and defined by

\[
 T_{e,\lambda}(e_h a)=e_{h'}U_ea,
 \qquad T_{e,\lambda}(e_k a)=0\quad(k\ne h).
\]

The new history contains the **actual morphological/semantic action** and
its old/new owned sounds. The operator is not an identity on grammar state
with a name appended. Its source/target projections satisfy
\(T_e=P_{\{h'\}}T_eP_{\{h\}}\). The classical event operator has payload 1.

A rule operator is the locally finite sum of these edges for that rule and
branch label. Local finiteness makes its action on a finite-support vector
well-defined. Incoming edges are finite too: a noninitial history has its
unique immediate prefix and its retained final event. Consequently its
pairing adjoint is defined on the same finite-support module:

\[
 T_{e,\lambda}^*(e_{h'}a)=e_h U_e^*a.
\]

Indeed \(\langle T_ex,y\rangle=x_h^*U_e^*y_{h'}
=\langle x,T_e^*y\rangle\). This algebraic adjoint is not a licensed
reverse Sanskrit derivation; source lopa is not thereby made reversible
as a grammatical rule.

The admitted operator family is the algebraic category generated by these
typed events, finite sums, composition, the declared cuts and these
adjoints. Every expression has finite action on finite support. No infinite
sum, limit, metric or physical constant selection is required for this
algebraic grammar instance.

## N4. Typed zero-specialization theorem

For an operator matrix in this family, define \(\Pi_{0,\mathrm{op}}\)
entrywise by ε; it has the same typed history source/target. Coefficients
specialize by ε, states by \(\pi_{0,M}\), and scalar pairings by ε. These
are different maps with different types.

**Theorem N4.** For every admitted finite operator construction A and every
finite-support state x,

\[
 \pi_{0,M}(Ax)=\Pi_{0,\mathrm{op}}(A)\pi_{0,M}(x),
\]

and specialization preserves typed products, sums, cuts, complementary
cuts, the declared adjoint and pairing. In particular,

\[
 \pi_{0,M}(T_{e_n,\lambda}\cdots T_{e_1,\lambda}x)
   =T_{e_n,0}\cdots T_{e_1,0}\pi_{0,M}(x),
 \qquad
 \epsilon(\langle x,y\rangle_B)
   =\langle\pi_{0,M}x,\pi_{0,M}y\rangle_C.
\]

**Proof.** Each required identity on an event edge is ε(U_e a)=ε(a).
On a cut it is equality of unchanged membership indicators. On the adjoint
it uses ε(U_e*)=1. For a product, every coefficient is a finite sum of
products of matching typed entries, and ε is an algebra homomorphism.
Linearity gives sums, and induction gives every generated expression.
The pairing identity follows from the dagger identity for ε and its
preservation of finite sums/products. The complementary-cut identity follows
from preservation of 1 and subtraction. □

Guards and rule-relative visibility queries in this model inspect the
retained grammatical history, not residue coefficients. They consequently
have the same answer before/after specialization. This is a declared
interaction law. A future model whose semantic guards depend on invisible
residue requires an additional descent proof and is not covered by N4.

## N5. Exact branch recovery, not only one inclusion

Let a derivation start at \(e_{h_0}\) with unit coefficient. Its branch
coefficient after events \(e_1,\ldots,e_n\) is

\[
 (1+z_{e_n})\cdots(1+z_{e_1}).
\]

Its empty-word coefficient is 1. It therefore survives specialization;
the final history and all event witnesses are unchanged. Conversely every
classical branch in this presented grammar has precisely those lifted edges
by construction, with the same guards, visibility, decisions and accept
event. Induction on branch length gives equality of the sets of **labelled
finite accepted derivations** under \(\pi_0\), not just forward inclusion.
The same reasoning preserves Open/Excluded outcomes in their separate
history types. Finiteness of derivations is not a global termination claim.

Summing optional branches cannot cancel a history coordinate: different
branches have different history labels, and each has constant coefficient
1. This support statement is for the unit-start derivation relation.
It is not true for arbitrary signed linear combinations of inputs, whose
specializations can vanish. The algebraic equality N4 still holds for those.

A surface observation can map histories to their visible word labels and
sum coefficients there; ε commutes with this finite linear readout too.
Equal word labels do not identify the input histories. Deduplication of
printed alternatives is a separate set observation, never a reason to
discard the derivation witnesses.

If the local source relations satisfy S4's completeness premises, this
gives exact recovery of their classical grammar. It does not prove omitted
Sanskrit rules complete. Existing PG Python code has not been shown to be
an implementation of this whole operator presentation.

## N6. Nontrivial order and complementary-channel examples

Two consecutive events a then b give the coefficient

\[
 (1+z_b)(1+z_a)=1+z_a+z_b+z_bz_a.
\]

Reversing their order, if the grammar separately licenses it, gives the
different residue word \(z_az_b\). The difference is nonzero in the free
cut-path basis. Both specialize to constant coefficient 1 while their
history labels remain different. Thus zero-reading removes this selected
operator residue; it does not erase ordered grammar history.

For a complementary-channel example take lawful consecutive histories
\(h_0\xrightarrow{a}h_1\xrightarrow{b}h_2\). Let P retain \(h_0,h_2\)
and let Q retain \(h_1\) on their three-summand submodule. Then

\[
 P T_bP T_aP=0,
 \qquad
 P T_bT_aP(e_{h_0})=e_{h_2}(1+z_b)(1+z_a)\ne0.
\]

The missing contribution is exactly \(PT_bQT_aP\), the canonical N06
compression defect. If a is marker lopa and b a subsequent kit-sensitive
rule, dropping the intermediate history would destroy a real grammatical
dependency even when the eventual printed word looks familiar. After
specialization the return is still \(e_{h_2}\), so compression already
loses a classical path. This proves why cut observation P is **not** the
zero-specialization homomorphism ε.

Evolution here is ordered operator composition plus retained events.
Two events need not represent two equal durations; no physical elapsed-time
statement or imported clock follows from history length.

## N7. Exact remaining interface

The constructed instance closes the algebraic existence and zero-recovery
obligation for this presented, locally finite grammar relation. It leaves:
the source interpretation of unconstructed grammar domains; any additional
native operator relations and their compatible augmentation; completion for
infinite computations; and the implementation map from the existing owned
sound records to this operator category. None is replaced by a finite PASS.
