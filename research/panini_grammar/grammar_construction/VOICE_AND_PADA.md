# PG14 — From an intended event to voice, pada and agreement

Author: Monty. 2 November 2026.

Primary passages are identified by commentary and sūtra in
[SOURCE_EXCERPTS.json](SOURCE_EXCERPTS.json). Kāśikā is the default reading;
the bhāva person/number argument uses Siddhāntakaumudī and Bālamanoramā on
3.1.67. The following definitions formalize those readings, not a new
translation of every locative as a phonetic right context.

## V1. Domain of the simple voice construction

An admitted frame contains an identified root and sense, a present event,
its independent participant and (when intended) its karman, their discourse
person/number, and a choice of what the lakāra expresses. It is unaccented,
classical, unprefixed, noncausative, and excludes karmakartṛ, reciprocal
construction, idiomatic person changes under 1.4.106, coordinated mixed-person
controllers, and special upapada/case constructions. There is one selected
agreement controller where a participant is expressed by tiṅ.

For the displayed complete word paths the root/sense frames are:

| nī 1011049, प्रापणे | independent leader and intended led participant/object |

These are contextual selections, not permanent transitivity labels for
every use of a written root. Avivikṣita-karman readings are discussed in
Bālamanoramā 3.4.69 but are outside this domain: omission of an object noun
alone does not prove that its karman is not intended. A missing transitivity
decision remains open.

## V2. Role selection and expression

1.4.54 designates the participant intended as independent in accomplishing
the event as kartṛ. 1.4.49 supplies the selected īpsitatama-karman relation.
Other kinds of karman need their own rules; they are not inferred here from
an English subject/object label.

On these frames, Kāśikā 3.4.69 gives the following relation:

| Intended construction | Admitted root-use | What lakāra/tiṅ expresses | Pada |
|---|---|---|---|
| kartari | transitive or intransitive | the selected kartṛ | decide by its own active-pada rules |
| karmaṇi | a frame with the selected karman | that karman | A, by 1.3.13 |
| bhāve | the intransitive frame | the action, not its kartṛ | A, by 1.3.13 |

For the active contrasts in this packet, a valid active P context is
explicitly selected from the earlier PG domains. This table is not a
general simple-root active-pada resolver. V5–V7 below construct that decision
for a separate, precisely bounded causative domain.

In a simple finite clause where the selected role is expressed by tiṅ,
2.3.1 blocks assigning a second role-case just because the same entity is
kartṛ or karman. 2.3.2 assigns accusative to the unexpressed karman; 2.3.18
assigns instrumental to the unexpressed kartṛ (or karaṇa); 2.3.46 supplies
nominative in its stated residual nominal conditions. This is the adopted
grammar's abhihita/anabhihita analysis, not a claim to settle every school
of Sanskrit sentence semantics. Nominal stem formation is a separate
downstream construction.

Thus *odanaḥ pacyate devadattena* has its food karman expressed by tiṅ.
Changing that to *odanaṃ pacyate devadattena* while retaining the same
transitive karma-passive frame violates this case construction. An attempt
to relabel it bhāva still fails that frame's 3.4.69 condition.

## V3. Person/number belongs to the expressed role

For a participant expressed by tiṅ, 1.4.105 selects madhyama for the
co-referential yuṣmad participant; 1.4.107 selects uttama for asmad;
1.4.108 gives prathama otherwise. In modern person numbering these are
2, 1, 3 respectively. The omitted-pronoun provision in 1.4.105 means the
referent still controls even if its pronoun is not spoken. 1.4.21–22 and
1.4.101–102 select the matching number and slot.

The bhāva branch is different. Siddhāntakaumudī 3.1.67 states that the
expressed action is not co-referential with yuṣmad/asmad and has no intended
dual/plural participant number. It therefore gives prathama ekavacana.
Bālamanoramā explicitly treats *āsyate tvayā*, *āsyate mayā* and plural
instrumental agents; the default singular is not an assertion that exactly
one agent acted.

Formally, on the admitted domain,

\[
 \operatorname{slot}(F,V)=
 \begin{cases}
   (\operatorname{person}(c),\operatorname{number}(c)),
       &V=\mathrm{kartari}\text{ or }\mathrm{karmaṇi},\ c=\operatorname{expressed}(F,V),\\
   (3,\mathrm{sg}),&V=\mathrm{bhāve}.
 \end{cases}
\]

**Proposition V3.** V2–V3 select one agreement slot for every completed frame
in their domain. Changing only the number/person of an unexpressed agent
cannot change that slot in a karma or bhāva branch.

**Proof.** In kartari/karmaṇi the domain supplies one controller; its
co-reference and number select the stated slot. In bhāva the cited source
argument fixes (3,sg). Neither karma's selected karman nor bhāva's action is
replaced by an instrumental agent when that agent's features change. □

The existence of a possible suffix string *-yante* consequently does not
license a plural bhāva construction.

## V4. Affix selection follows the semantic decision

For the present, 3.2.123 supplies laṭ. Its tiṅ replacement is sārvadhātuka
by 3.4.113. With bhāva or karman expression, 3.1.67 supplies yak. Its k is
it (1.3.3), removed from pronunciation by 1.3.9 but retained as the affix's
kit property. The prohibition 1.1.5 is queried against that trigger.

Yak itself is ārdhadhātuka under 3.4.114: it is neither tiṅ nor śit. It is
introduced **before a sārvadhātuka ending**. Calling both the affix and the
following ending sārvadhātuka would confuse two owners. The same distinction
lets 6.4.66 operate on the selected dā/sthā bases before consonant-initial
kit yak. The ordinary active śap condition is kartṛ-expression (3.1.68);
it is false in these karma/bhāva frames. This is a guard decision, not a
competition won by whichever rule number is later.

## V5. The twelve-root causative domain

Let \(D_C\) consist of the twelve PG13 lexical identities in V7, with their
stated senses, classical unprefixed laṭ, one hetumat event and one base event.
It inherits V1's exclusions of special case/upapada constructions, 1.4.106
person changes and coordinated mixed-person controllers; the benefit-conveying
upapada of 1.3.77 is expressly admitted below as the one stated exception.
The causer \(a\) and base agent/causee \(b\) are distinct; the causer is not
the base karman. The base frame is not already ṇijanta. No karmakartṛ,
reciprocal, Vedic, denominative, prefixed, special fear/deception/honouring
reading, or *mithyā* repeated-utterance construction is present. In particular
1.3.67–71 have stated false guards; these conditions are not forgotten
because a root happens not to appear in a small spelling table.

The bhū/sthā base agents are conscious, and their base uses are intransitive.
All other listed base frames are transitive, with gam's intended goal
karman included. Every frame states whether the principal intended result
is for the causer, and whether a nearby upapada conveys that fact. The
current chapter selects kartari for the causative event; causative passive
needs an additional construction, especially when there are two karmans.

The finite role construction is:

1. The independent base participant has kartṛ status by 1.4.54.
2. The causer who induces that participant has hetu **and** kartṛ status
   by 1.4.55. The intended inducing activity licenses ṇic by 3.1.26.
3. The root constituent with ṇic is a derived dhātu by 3.1.32. Its
   formation permission is now witnessed by the event, not merely asserted.
4. The causer is expressed by the causative kartari tiṅ. Its person/number
   controls agreement; the causee's does not.
5. Determine the causee's outer grammatical relation with the table below.

| Base frame | Causee relation in this causative | Source |
|---|---|---|
| gam movement; vid/budh cognition; pā ingestion; bhū/sthā intransitive | karman | 1.4.52 |
| nī leading | retains kartṛ for this case assignment; instrumental when unexpressed | Kāśikā 1.4.52's nī/vah prohibition |
| kṛ making, hṛ carrying | either karman or retained kartṛ, with a branch witness | 1.4.53 |
| pac cooking, dā giving, kṛṣ furrowing | retained kartṛ; instrumental when unexpressed | residual 1.4.54 / 2.3.18; not in the admitted 1.4.52 classes |

The original karman is retained where supplied. 1.4.52 is not “every
causative causee takes accusative”; its commentary gives *pācayaty odanaṃ
devadattena*. The optional hṛ/kṛ relation is not optional spelling: it
changes which case condition holds. All case decisions here assume these
participants are not separately expressed by another kṛt/taddhita/compound.

## V6. Causative pada as a finite decision relation

Write \(F\) for “principal intended result belongs to the causer” and
\(U\) for “a nearby upapada conveys that intended result.” The principal
qualification comes from Kāśikā 1.3.72: wages or incidental benefit alone
do not make the intended result kartṛ-directed. The relevant kartṛ of the
causative finite verb is the causer, not automatically the causee.

Define the positively established exception predicate

\[
 E=(\text{listed in 1.3.86})\ \lor\
   ((\text{1.3.87 condition}\lor\text{1.3.88 condition})
      \land\neg\text{their 1.3.89 exception}).
\]

This expression is only evaluated after its source/sense predicates are
resolved. In \(D_C\), E is true for budh and for the stated conscious
intransitive bhū/sthā frames. For pā, ingestion would give 1.3.87 but
1.3.89 removes that exception to A. Gam's locomotion is not automatically
the *kampana* reading of calana glossed in Kāśikā 1.3.87.

The complete decision on \(D_C\) is

| Established condition | Licensed pada set | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| E true | {P} | 1.3.86 or surviving 1.3.87/88 exception |
| E false, F false | {P} | residual 1.3.78; 1.3.74's condition is absent |
| E false, F true, U false | {A} | 1.3.74 |
| E false, F true, U true | {A, P} | 1.3.77 makes A optional; residual P on the other branch |

Kāśikā 1.3.89 explicitly limits its prohibition to the preceding two
rules. It is not a permanent lexical ban on parasmaipada, and it does not
cancel 1.3.86. Kāśikā 1.3.77 explicitly includes the five-rule group
1.3.72–76. These source relationships, rather than global numerical
priority, justify the decision table.

If E is already known true, F/U are irrelevant to this pada result. If E is
false but F is unknown, there is a semantic obligation. If E is false,
F true and U unknown, A is stable but P is only a conditional possibility;
it must not be reported as already licensed free variation. See S5.

**Proposition V7.** For completed frames in \(D_C\), the construction gives
all and only the causee-case/pada branches licensed by the adopted rules,
and a unique agreement slot for each branch.

**Proof.** The lexical/sense partition puts every causee in exactly one
table row; hṛ/kṛ's row explicitly retains both 1.4.53 choices. The stated
domain excludes nested, identity-coincident and special readings that could
change the applicable role or pada rules. For pada, E either survives its
specific prohibition or does not. In the first case its P prescription
applies. In the second, the two values of F decide residual P versus the
1.3.74 A domain, and the two values of U split the latter into obligatory
or optional A by 1.3.77. These cases exhaust the completed frames. The
causer remains the one expressed kartṛ throughout, so V3 fixes its slot. □

This closes the written decision on \(D_C\); it does not declare all uses
of these twelve lexical entries complete. Raw semantic interpretation,
inanimate bhū/sthā frames, all prefixed/nested causatives, unrestricted
active pada and passive double-karman selection remain outside the proof.
