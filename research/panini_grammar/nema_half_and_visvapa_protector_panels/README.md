# PG826 · The half and the world-protector

Vāgīśa Śāstrī places the two paradigms together on printed p. 158. The left-hand table headings, stem lengths and nominative/accusative contrasts identify two different ending systems. All entries below are masculine. [Case panels](CASES.tsv).

## Nema in the half sense

Take the lexical base *nema* with two-part division as the sense. It has no verbal root, preverb, voice or derivational suffix in this request. Each outer agreement number can occur. The printed nominative and accusative dual both have *nemau*. The instrumental reads *nemena*, *nemābhyām*, *nemaiḥ* in the three columns. Those letters can be checked on the page image where machine extraction misreads the row. PG823 already owns DATsg, LOCsg and both readings of NOMpl. The last pair comes from 1.1.33's jas-specific option. The seven-case panel here has 21 positions and 22 readings, of which 18 positions and 18 readings are new. Retain *nemasmāt* as printed, on that word boundary. The neuter *nemam* in the earlier 7.1.25 discussion supplies no credit to the masculine accusative. The table does not display a vocative for *nema*. Its unreviewed address forms stay Open.

On p. 156 the Gītā quotation belongs to the *katara* section. On p. 157 the remark about both a prohibition and use in the dual belongs to *ubhaya*. Its own dual column is crossed out. Reading alternating lines across these two pages would falsely attach the Gītā verse to that number dispute. Gītā 2.6 asks which alternative would be better. Its connected *kataran no* reflects neuter singular *katarat* before the nasal. Neither the quotation nor the prose remark here is an attested *ubhaya* dual. The written dual policies have the direct owners established in PG824–825.

## Viśvapā as the one who protects the world

Śabdakalpadruma 33293 explicitly selects *pā* in the sense of protecting, with the world as object, and **vic**. The 2016 Prākśāstrī lesson locates this vic at 3.2.75. The nominal first member is an object upapada, with its internal case ending suppressed. It is not a verbal preverb. Vic licenses the agent reading and leaves no overt segment after its markers are removed. Retain √pā ‘protect’ and the affix event. Do not transfer the drinking interpretation of a form in *somapā*, a finite voice/pada ending, or the generic sarvanāma substitutions of free *viśva* to the whole. The particular referent of the lexicon is the supreme protector, identified as Viṣṇu in Śāstrī's table.

For NOMsg the original su contributes the final visarga. With dual au, 6.1.105 blocks the competing previous-vowel operation and permits the diphthongal result. For ACCpl, śas leaves a vowel-initial ending outside the sarvanāmasthāna series. The bha-grade root-final ā deletes under **6.4.140**, yielding *viśvapaḥ*. The same ownership gives the weak singular dative *viśvape*, compared with instrumental *viśvapā*, and the genitive/locative dual *viśvapoḥ*. When *bhyām* or *bhyas* begins with a consonant there is no bha-triggered loss. Śāstrī's printed *viśvapābhyaḥ* is the selected dative and ablative plural. Accusative singular and genitive plural happen to agree in the resulting *viśvapām*. Their endings and operations stay distinct. The twenty-four keys include direct address, which this table explicitly prints for this agent noun.

The MDU text's p. 111 supplies a second physical comparison. It correctly conditions root-final deletion on bha status and illustrates retention before *bhyām*. Its heading labels *āto dhātoḥ* **6.1.140**, where the rule is 6.4.140. Its two plural -bhyas cells visibly print *viśvapebhyaḥ* despite the stated consonant condition. Preserve this printed discrepancy at that edition. Śāstrī's ā agrees with the stated operation; the e has no established second grammatical licence here. The non-root-final Gandharva name *Hāhā* in the MDU discussion gives a blocking control for the deletion. No paradigm credit is claimed for that name.
