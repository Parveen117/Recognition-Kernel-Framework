## N2. A prohibition is addressed to an owner class

On the completed candidate-augmentation sector, let M be the māṅ cut;
let Q_A select aṭ/āṭ candidates and Q_I select ending-owned īṭ
candidates. They are diagonal cuts, Q_A Q_I=0. Define

\[
 K=I-MQ_A.
\]

Then K²=K=K*, KQ_I=Q_I, and KMQ_A=0. These identities follow
from commuting diagonal cuts and the disjoint owner classes. Thus the
prohibition removes the requested aṭ/āṭ operation without deleting īṭ.
Actual application is the retained branch edge after this admission cut.

**Owner-forgetting obstruction.** On a māṅ subdomain, let e_A,e_I
be one aṭ and one īṭ candidate and let p=(1,1) retain only the
coarse label “augment”. Then x=e_A−e_I lies in ker p, while
pKx=−1. Hence K does not descend through p. A global no-augment
flag cannot express P3 on this domain. An owner-preserving transport Φ
satisfies K′Φ=ΦK whenever it preserves the relevant M/Q_A predicates;
the statement is conditional on that explicit transport contract.

This concerns grammatical operations, not an empirical claim about
suppression in a physical system.

## N4. Branching is neither a Cartesian flag product nor a unitary map

For each completed source history h let E_h be its finite admitted
continuations. Include the source identity in the target basis label
(h,c), and define the right-linear branch operator

\[
 B e_h=\sum_{c\in E_h} e_{h,c}(1+z_{h,c}).
\]

P2 gives |E_h|=1 or 2 for its lakāra requests. K3 gives |E_h|=3
at the original san n. Its labels are retain/replace/delete, not the
four elements of two independent Boolean choices. The compatibility
cut on a proposed pair of flags is m_A+m_D≤1; the fourth proposed
history attempts a second consumption of the same source owner and
has no grammatical edge. No blanket optional-rule independence is assumed.

**Proposition.** On each finite branch block,

\[
B^*B=\operatorname{diag}_h
 \left(\sum_{c\in E_h}(1+z_{h,c}^*)(1+z_{h,c})\right),
\qquad
\Pi_{0,op}(B^*B)=\operatorname{diag}_h |E_h|.
\]

**Proof.** Different target histories (h,c) are distinct basis units;
off-diagonal pairings vanish. Right-linearity and the declared pairing
give the displayed ordered products. Applying ε sends each factor
to 1, so the diagonal entry is exactly the admitted branch count. □

This branch map is not generally isometric: its zero-reading has entries
2 or 3 on branching sources. No probability or arbitrary square-root
normalization is introduced. The branch count is a local relation
multiplicity; it is not a number of extra source positions. Forgetting
the h labels before pairing would be a different observer and could
introduce cross terms between unrelated histories.
