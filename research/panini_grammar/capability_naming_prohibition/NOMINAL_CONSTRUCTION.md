# PG30 — Ktic alternatives, ktin contrast and Devadatta

Author: Monty. 3 November 2026.

## K1. Affix identity survives pronounced ti

Retain each suffix's teaching form. Initial k and final c/n in ktic/ktin
are markers under the inherited 1.3.3/8/9 interface; pronounced ti is
not the whole affix history. Both are kit, but 6.4.39 selects **ktic**,
not every kit suffix pronounced ti. Kta likewise retains its initial k.
These non-tiṅ, non-śit root continuations are ārdhadhātuka by 3.4.114.
No finite u, śap or abhyāsa is carried into them.

Tan/san are seṭ lexical roots. The ti-kṛt prohibition 7.2.9 supplies
the missing-iṭ decision here; their words are not derived by relabelling
the roots aniṭ. For bhū, 7.2.11's kit/uganta prohibition independently
blocks iṭ; Tattvabodhinī 3.3.174 explicitly distinguishes that reason
from the more general ti account. Kit blocks the guṇa that would
otherwise yield a bhav- stem. Thus bhū+ktic gives **bhūti**.

Tan's retained root is tan after teaching-vowel lopa. For san, consume
PG12's exact class-8 entry /1080002 and its declared dental san root
interface. Preserve the literal षणुँ teaching row and its lexical note;
the present source also explicitly names sanoti/san. Initial ṣ→s alone
is not claimed to reconstruct every internal sound of that teaching
row. The inherited root n is not an erased marker or a finite nasal infix.

## K2. Tanti and the separate tati control

Before ktic, 6.4.39 blocks both the tanādi nasal deletion of 6.4.37
and the lengthening of 6.4.15 that would become relevant with n retained.
The result is **tan+ti=tanti**, with short a and retained root n.
Nyāsa makes the inherited root-class condition explicit: the rule does
not block all lengthening before every ktic on every root.

For the separately supplied feminine action meaning, 3.3.94 licenses
ktin. Here 6.4.39 is false. 6.4.37's later specific n-lopa gives
tan→ta before ti, hence **tati**. The consumed n is retained in
Smriti; the nasal-final lengthening branch does not then manufacture
*tāti*. This is a local owned derivation on tan, not a claim to classify
every word ending in ti or every ktin exception.

## K3. San's three alternatives are one addressed choice

6.4.42 supplies the broader jan/san/khan ā relation; **6.4.45** gives
the specific optional substitution and deletion before ktic. Kāśikā
and Kaumudī explicitly preserve all three outputs:

| Branch at the original san n | Owned continuation | Base |
|---|---|---|
| retain | san+ti | santi |
| substitute ā | sa+ā+ti, with 6.1.101 coalescence | sāti |
| delete | sa+ti | sati |

Nyāsa/Padamañjarī explain **asya**: the deletion targets the root's
nasal, not the nearby ktic. The three branches are mutually exclusive
histories at that owner. Treating substitution and deletion as two
independent optional flags creates an unlicensed fourth history that
tries to consume the same original constituent twice.

The branch relation is {retain,replace,delete}, with the exact source
guard. It neither erases the whole suffix nor takes a general product
of 6.4.39/42/45 options. The single san/ktic construction target has
three base readings; three readings are not three new lexical roots.

## K4. Dā-kta with its own root substitution

For C2's blessing/name dā frame, kta is kit and receives niṣṭhā status
under 1.1.26. Exact dā /1030010 has the inherited ghu qualification
under 1.1.20; its aniṭ account is retained. 7.4.46 replaces this root
before t-initial kit. Kāśikā discusses several descriptions of the
substitute and explicitly prefers a **th-final** one to avoid spurious
niṣṭhā-n/aspiration consequences. Adopt that local description:

\[
 dā\mid ta\longmapsto dath\mid ta\longmapsto datta.
\]

The last contact uses **8.4.55 khari ca**. Nyāsa 3.3.174 prints the
different numeric cross-reference 8.4.54; preserve it literally in the
archive and use the canonical sūtra table for the operation's identity.
The source's familiar dad label is not used to introduce extra unsourced
later substitutions. The actor/karman and blessing/name record remain.

## K5. The bounded name compound

Use the source's Devadatta naming intention and the supplied deva-agent
relation to the given referent. Under the selected 2.1.32 kartṛ-tṛtīyā
kṛt-tatpuruṣa interface, combine the deva nominal and kta datta:

\[
  deva_{agent}\;\mid\;datta_{karman}\longmapsto devadatta.
\]

The internal case/sup owners are removed by 2.4.71 after the compound's
prātipadika interface under 1.2.46 is formed. Their role evidence remains
in history. There is no special phonological seam at a+d. This does
not derive the full deva declension or grant compounding to every nearby
instrumental and kṛt word: 2.1.32's **bahulam** restriction is respected
by the explicit source-witnessed name domain.

Devadatta is one additional compound target, not a second independent
event of giving or a newly counted finite blessing. The compound can
function as a conventional name without proving its referent was in
fact given by deities.

## K6. Eight selected nominative readouts

Kṛt/compound prātipadika status and 4.1.2 supply nominative-singular
su. Retain its pronounced s and the supplied gender appropriate to the
selected appellative/action/name frame. No new universal gender assignment
is made. The source's i-stem nominatives use the common short-i interface;
tati is the explicitly feminine action noun. Datta/Devadatta here use
the selected masculine frame. At pause 8.2.66/8.3.15 give visarga.

Thus the readouts are **tantiḥ, sātiḥ, santiḥ, satiḥ, bhūtiḥ,
tatiḥ, dattaḥ, devadattaḥ**. These are eight selected nominal readings,
not full nominal declensions and not eight added finite positions.
