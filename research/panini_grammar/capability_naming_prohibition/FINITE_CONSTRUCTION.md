Author: Monty. 3 November 2026.

## F1. Shared ending owners and different vikaraṇas

Fix D's qualified kṛ/hṛ P inputs. Retain the original 3.4.78 tiṅ
owner and the ṅit lakāra identity. 3.4.100 removes P ending i;
3.4.101 gives tām/tam/ta/am; 3.4.99 removes final s in first-person
vas/mas. Their inherited source and pit information survive substitution.
P3 blocks aṭ before word realization; no past-prefix removal heuristic
is used.

Luṅ selects cli under 3.1.43 and its sic substitute under 3.1.44.
The pronounced s belongs to sic; its teaching i is enunciative and c
is retained as a marker. This is an ārdhadhātuka root-facing continuation,
not the laṅ u/śap present-stem branch. The following tiṅ has its own
sārvadhātuka type. These adjacent owners are not collapsed into one flag.

The exact kṛ/hṛ entries are aniṭ under the inherited 7.2.10 account.
7.2.1 gives root-final ṛ→ār before sic followed by P, retaining
1.1.51's r accompaniment. Thus the owned bases are kār|s and hār|s.
This specific vṛddhi preempts ordinary guṇa; no present u, o or śap
is imported into U.

## F2. All nine sic continuations

3.4.109 gives jus after actual sic at 3pl. Initial j is it by
1.3.7; its ending s is preserved under 1.3.4. Before 3sg t and
2sg s, 7.3.96 places **īṭ at the ending's beginning** by 1.1.46.
No īṭ is added before tām/tam/ta/am/va/ma/us: those are not the
selected single-consonant apṛkta endings.

The three possible sic-lopa guards fail here. In 8.2.26 the preceding
r is not jhal; in 8.2.27 the actual strengthened root is not short-
vowel-final; in 8.2.28 there is no root-facing iṭ before sic. Thus
sic remains even before īṭ. A root's historically short ṛ must not
be substituted for the actual post-vṛddhi environment in that query.

The non-pada-final affix s becomes ṣ after r under 8.3.55/59.
Where immediately followed by dental t, 8.4.41 gives ṭ. An intervening
īṭ prevents that contact at 3sg: kārṣīt, not a manufactured ṣṭī
sequence. Final ending s has the inherited ru/visarga pause readout.

For B=kār or hār, the nine-slot constructor is

\[
 U_P(B)=(Bṣīt,Bṣṭām,Bṣuḥ,Bṣīḥ,Bṣṭam,Bṣṭa,
          Bṣam,Bṣva,Bṣma).
\]

Sic's s, īṭ's ī and final ending s remain different constituents.
This packet selects the inherited non-doubling sound profile.

## F3. Hṛ's thematic laṅ

For J, 3.1.68 supplies śap's a; 7.3.84 and 1.1.51 give hṛ→har.
At 3pl, jhi receives ant by 7.1.3 rather than sic's jus. The owned
ending series is (t,tām,ant,s,tam,ta,am,va,ma).

The non-pada-final a+a contacts with ant/am take 6.1.97 pararūpa,
the source's stated exception to savarṇadīrgha. At va/ma, 7.3.101
lengthens the thematic a. At 3pl, final nt loses t under 8.2.23;
the retained n is protected at word end by 8.4.37 and does not acquire
a new ṇatva merely from the root's r.
At 2sg use ru/visarga at pause. Hence

\[
 J_P(hṛ)=(harat,haratām,haran,haraḥ,haratam,harata,
           haram,harāva,harāma).
\]

The hṛ frame is still supplied separately from its phonological stem;
similarity to another printed har- does not establish the lexical input.

## F4. Kṛ's owned u and actual weak/strong paths

3.1.79 supplies the u vikaraṇa. Consume the inherited PG6/PG19 kṛ
interface, but use the actual J endings instead of loṭ's hi/ni or
liṅ's yāsuṭ. At original pit 3sg/2sg/1sg, guṇa gives kar|o.
The consonant cases yield karot/karoḥ. Before 1sg am, 6.1.78 turns
o into av, yielding karavam.

The remaining endings are apit sārvadhātuka and receive 1.2.4's
ṅitvat condition. 6.4.110 gives the selected kur|u weak constituent;
1.1.5 prevents the incompatible strong guṇa. Before tām/tam/ta the
u remains. Before 3pl ant, 6.1.77 yields kurv-ant and 8.2.23 removes
final t. Before first-person va/ma, 6.4.108 removes the affix-owned u.
The inherited 1.1.58/8.2.79 kur contract blocks a spurious long ū.

Thus

\[
 J_P(kṛ)=(karot,kurutām,kurvan,karoḥ,kurutam,kuruta,
           karavam,kurva,kurma).
\]

No y-initial liṅ continuation is present, so 6.4.109's u-lopa must
not be applied indiscriminately. This is a fresh laṅ constructor on
inherited root machinery, not a copied nine-word loṭ table.
