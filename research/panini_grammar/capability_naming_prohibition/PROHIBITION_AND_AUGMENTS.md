# PG30 — Māṅ, sma and owned augment suppression

Author: Monty. 3 November 2026.

## P1. The particle has a retained lexical identity

Let M mean that the addressed clause has the prohibition particle
**māṅ**, retaining its ṅ marker after the pronounced word becomes mā.
This is not the root mā and is not inferred from the two printed sounds.
The selected Kaumudī/Nyāsa policy distinguishes another aṅit negative
mā. The pronominal mā is also outside M. Those other histories do not
receive 3.3.175 or 6.4.74 merely by surface matching.

The wider status and treatment of mā bhavatu/mā bhaviṣyati is disputed
in the preserved discussions. Kāśikā first rejects them for māṅ, then
records the separate-mā account; Bālamanoramā records competing analyses.
PG30 adopts the lexical distinction for its domain. It neither declares
all such usages invalid nor builds all of their alternative parses.

M is an addressed dependency, not immediate adjacency. Kāśikā 6.4.74
gives mā **bhavān** kārṣīt; Nyāsa expressly says that intervening
material does not invalidate the relation. A mā in another clause does
not supply this clause's evidence.

## P2. Sma adds a lakāra alternative

Let S mean the relevant sma is associated with this same māṅ frame.
For completed inputs:

| Guard | Admitted request family |
|---|---|
| M and no S | U, prohibitive luṅ, by 3.3.175 |
| M and S | U or J, prohibitive luṅ or laṅ, by 3.3.176 |

The ca of 3.3.176 retains luṅ. It does not replace it by laṅ alone.
These are the source's sarvalakāra exceptions on the stated domain;
ordinary loṭ or the root's ordinary past choice is not added by analogy.

Padamañjarī's entry keyed to 3.3.175 actually explains **uttara** in
3.3.176: additional association, not compulsory following direction.
Together with Prakriyāsarvasva's smaśabdayukta this supports the selected
association policy even when sma precedes. No unrestricted word-order
or parser theorem follows. Preserve the displaced source label explicitly.

Unknown M or S remains an obligation. In particular, “S not yet known”
is not the completed no-S input and does not justify silently closing
the laṅ branch.

## P3. Suppress aṭ/āṭ, preserve the other augment owners

6.4.71/72 supply aṭ/āṭ in their ordinary luṅ/laṅ/lṛṅ environments.
6.4.74 blocks those specified augment operations in māṅ-yoga. Nyāsa
explicitly includes both aṭ and āṭ and excludes operations prescribed
in other contexts from this ban. The rule is not “delete every augment”.

In the new consonant-initial kṛ/hṛ paradigms, the prospective aṭ
candidate is blocked. The root and its word are constructed without
that event. The forbidden candidate/source remains as a rejected
operation record; it is not falsely recorded as an augment that actually
occurred and was later erased.

By contrast, the **īṭ belonging to an apṛkta tiṅ after actual sic**
is still required at U 3sg/2sg by 7.3.96. Its owner is different.
Thus mā kārṣīt/mā kārṣīḥ retain ī, and the same holds for hārṣ-.
Root-facing iṭ is absent for these exact aniṭ roots; īṭ and iṭ
are also not one shared flag. A blanket “no augments” implementation
would lose these forms.

The āṭ rule is included in the suppression contract but no new
vowel-initial finite paradigm is counted. Likewise 6.4.74's mention
of lṛṅ does not license lṛṅ through P2. Grammar request admission and
the scope of a downstream prohibition are separate questions.

## P4. Meaning and controller are retained

The selected U/J forms have prohibitive force from P1–P2. Their lakāra
labels do not assert an actual past event. Tiṅ person/number remains
controlled by the supplied kartari participant; bhavān is third person
even when addressed. All nine grammatical slots are constructed for
each qualified P profile; this does not claim every discourse context
fits every person or number.

**Proposition.** The completed prohibition relation is nonempty, and
every admitted new finite branch lacks aṭ while preserving all other
applicable owners. With S, both U and J remain available.

**Proof.** P2 supplies either the singleton {U} or the pair {U,J}.
P3 removes only aṭ/āṭ candidates. F constructs the nine endings in
each admitted lakāra and retains U's independently required īṭ.
Controller and lexical/pada data never change. □
