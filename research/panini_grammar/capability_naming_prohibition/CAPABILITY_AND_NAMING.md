# PG30 — Capability and the whole-expression naming condition

Author: Monty. 3 November 2026.

## C1. Capability qualifies meaning, not a required printed śak

Kāśikā 3.3.172 explicitly treats śaki as a qualification of the root's
meaning. Supply Capable(actor,event). The selected relation is

\[
  \{O_{actor}\}\ \sqcup\
  \{K_{karman}:K\in\mathcal K(root)\}.
\]

The overt root śak, an independent śakta adjective and a token denoting
capacity are not required upapadas. The source's bhavān khalu bhāraṃ
vahet and bhāro voḍhavyaḥ/vahanīyaḥ/vāhyaḥ are grammatical capability
readings. They do not themselves prove anybody physically capable.

For kṛ/dā/vah, consume PG29's exact kṛtya catalogues, qualified O
interfaces and case/agreement relation. Repeating those words under a
new semantic guard does not create new finite positions or new bases.
The explicit kṛtya continuation prevents liṅ from excluding these
otherwise generally licensed expressions under the adopted local
vā-asarūpa policy; it is not a universal permission to union all moods.

Nyāsa's field keyed to 3.3.172 actually discusses 3.3.173's blessing
rule. It is not evidence for this capability clause. Kāśikā,
Kaumudī, Bālamanoramā and Padamañjarī at the proper key support C1.
The last also discusses exceptional kta readings; these are retained as
source context, outside the three-root kṛtya catalogue.

## C2. Two independent premises for 3.3.174

Supply both Blessing(event) and Appellative(whole expression,referent).
Kāśikā's **samudāyena** places the naming condition on the root-plus-
suffix expression; Nyāsa makes that scope explicit. An arbitrary wished
action is not automatically a proper name, and an already printed name
does not establish this particular derivational history.

Under those premises, retain distinct **ktic** and **kta** construction
families. Their coexistence is explicitly preserved by 3.3.174; it is
not an instruction to add both suffixes successively to one root.
This packet realizes ktic on tan/san/bhū and kta on dā. It does
not exhaust both suffixes on every root or every historical name.

The selected ktic appellatives use the general agent-expression
interface 3.4.67: tanutāt→tanti, sanutāt→sāti/santi/sati and
bhavatāt→bhūti. The source's blessing clauses explain the naming
intention; PG30 does not count them as newly derived finite forms.
In particular, its bhūti appellative is not a claim that every printed
bhūti has this naming sense or this suffix.

For dā, the source explicitly gives deva(s) wishing to give this
referent, producing Devadatta. The kta component expresses the given
karman under 3.4.70. Its use here retains the blessing/name premise;
the surface kta does not establish an accomplished historical gift.
The ordinary past niṣṭhā rule 3.2.102 is context for that distinction,
not the semantic license used to override the supplied blessing.

## C3. Ktic versus ktin has an observable consequence

Both suffixes can show ti after marker lopa, but their source identities
differ. Ktic preserves tan's n and blocks the competing lengthening by
6.4.39, yielding tanti. The separate feminine action-ktin channel under
3.3.94 has 6.4.37 n-lopa, yielding tati. The latter is a rule-derived
contrast, not a quotation falsely attributed to the 3.3.174 entry.

Retaining only pronounced ti would lose which prohibition applies.
Teaching c versus n, the root class and the naming/action meanings
remain in history. The two ti words cannot be reconstructed by choosing
a strong/weak spelling after that information is erased.

## C4. Bounded composition

**Proposition.** On D's completed capability and naming inputs, expression
selection preserves event and participant identity and changes only the
declared expression grade. The selected suffix alternatives are finite.

**Proof.** C1 reuses PG29's role-indexed O/kṛtya relation after its
new semantic guard. C2 requires a conjunction of blessing and whole-
expression naming before selecting either source family. C3's ktin
control belongs to a different input channel. K realizes the selected
suffixes with their distinct owners. No step infers a missing premise
from a word's spelling. □
