# PG149 · Intending a deed and undergoing a state

Monty · 11 November 2026

The Kāśikā under 3.1.14 gives five denominative presents. Two of their nominal bases also belong to the experience list under 3.1.18. A reader of *kṛcchrāyate* or *gahanāyate* must therefore retain two analyses until the event and its participants supply the conditions for a choice.

| Nominal | Kāśikā present | Histories admitted by the selected sources |
| --- | --- | --- |
| satra | satrāyate | intention to do wrong |
| kaṣṭa | kaṣṭāyate | undertaking for a wrongful purpose (dative), intention to do wrong (accusative) |
| kakṣa | kakṣāyate | intention to do wrong |
| kṛcchra | kṛcchrāyate | intention to do wrong, direct experience of the actor's own state |
| gahana | gahanāyate | intention to do wrong, direct experience of the actor's own state |

The dative is the case of the independent nominal in the analysis of the sūtra. In the reported extension the intended deed stands in the accusative. These histories can express closely related intentions. Distinct derivations do not require disjoint real-world events. The literal movement in *ajaḥ kaṣṭaṃ krāmati* supplies neither condition. Under 3.1.18 the actor must bear the state being experienced: observing somebody else's hardship fails that condition. The latter diagnostic applies the source's ownership contrast to the listed nominal.
