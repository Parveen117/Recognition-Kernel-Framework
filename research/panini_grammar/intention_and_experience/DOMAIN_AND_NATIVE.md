# Derivation and finite projection

## Nominal and event

The sūtra and discussion at 3.1.14 restrict the basic kaṣṭa construction to the wrongful undertaking. The Kāśikā gives five members in its reported extension. The Siddhāntakaumudī specifies accusative input for that extension. Satra follows the spelling printed in the five-form sequence in the pinned Kāśikā text. Other editions need an explicit spelling decision before entering this table. See [the two source pages](SOURCE_PINS.json).

The experience list in Kāśikā 3.1.18 explicitly includes gahana and kṛcchra. Resolve the state bearer against the actor of experiencing. This comparison belongs to the event. A change of person, number or perfect auxiliary has no authority to change its participants.

## Native relation

On this finite sector compose the typed cuts for nominal selection, licensed semantic guard, affix/pada, context, slot and ending realization. The word remains paired with its complete request and witness history. The guard also retains its rule, source authority, nominal role and conditions. For a fixed request r let G(r) be the finite list of licensed guards and W(r) its ordered, already written word list. The lifted fiber consists of the pairs (g,w) in G(r) × W(r) with the source table's ending history for w. The zero reading forgets the guard and ending history and collects the word support.

Every admitted word has a preimage because each admitted request has at least one guard. Every preimage projects to a listed word by the ending construction. Thus projection after the lifted construction and ordinary word construction agree on the declared requests. Conditioning on an event first takes the subset of guards satisfying its facts. A failed guard contributes no pair. Recovering an intention or an experiencer from a bare surface alone can leave a two-element fiber. The written construction assumes the caller's event facts and does not supply a general sentence parser.
