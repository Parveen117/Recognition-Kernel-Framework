# Written derivations

## H6. Future and agent-shaped endings

The kāmyac futures give putrakāmyiṣyati and putrakāmyitā, with y surviving next to iṭ. For putrīya write putrīyiṣyati; its two admitted role histories continue to project to exactly that word. The A agent-model future śyenāyiṣyate carries a separate comparison-standard input.

## H7. A minimal pair of blessing junctions

P 3sg from putrakāmya gives putrakāmyyāt or putrakāmyāt. From putrīya the stored normalized reading is putrīyyāt. In both cases the original y is morphologically retained; in the first case the short surface has a later sound-reduction history. This differs from the double preimage proved for stop-final samidh in PG126.

## H8. Outer aorist and conditional

The augment precedes the complete nominal-derived factor in all eight consonant-initial inputs, including prāsāda and paryaṅka. P 3sg aorist putrakāmyīt receives its long ī from the ending schedule; the augmented word is aputrakāmyīt. In A 2pl, retained y before iṭ licenses śyenāyidhvam/śyenāyiḍhvam after aṭ, hence aśyenāyidhvam/aśyenāyiḍhvam.

## H9. Periphrastic perfect has the same role ambiguity

Write putrīyām + the selected auxiliary. Putrīyāñcakāra therefore retains both self-wish and object-model paths, including the normalized seam. Putrakāmyāñcakāra has only the admitted self-wish history. An auxiliary ending cannot distinguish the two interpretations of putrīya.
