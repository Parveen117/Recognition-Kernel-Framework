# Source decisions

## H1. One affix, not any terminal ya

Kaumudī at 3.1.9 explicitly excludes 6.4.49 on kāmyac. Bālamanoramā relates this to taking a meaningful ya unit; the y inside kāmya is part of a larger affix. 6.4.50 selects kyac/kyaṅ, not kāmyac. The fact that m is a consonant is consequently insufficient to produce a future in kāmi-.

## H2. Vowel hosts of the remaining ky formations

The other six profiles end their pre-y bases in ī or ā, coming from PG128 7.4.33 or 7.4.25. The consonant-host condition of 6.4.50 fails; 6.4.48 removes final a before the selected outer ārdhadhātuka continuation. This leaves one primary stem per morphology.

## H3. The blessing sound junction

All six P profiles receive yāsuṭ in the established written table. Two have m-y-y with two adjacent y after m, so 8.4.64 gives the double- or single-y orthographic readout represented here. The remaining four P profiles have ī-y-y with a vowel before the first y. Their shorter spelling is outside this normalized schedule.

## H4. Source role stays with the primary event

The agent or patient comparison is determined on the denominative, including in a two-word perfect. 3.1.40 selects an auxiliary whose word carries the finite ending. The kṛ route transmits the P/A entitlement under 1.3.63; bhū and as take their established P paradigms here. A role does not migrate to a new independent event simply because its agreement has a different written host.

## H5. A future does not turn a model into a wish

The source role remains subject to its own premise across outer time and mood. The 3.1.10 patient, commentary locative and 3.1.11 agent are separate; future or conditional scope is an additional input. These are derived constructions seeded by the explicit PG128 present/phrase examples.
