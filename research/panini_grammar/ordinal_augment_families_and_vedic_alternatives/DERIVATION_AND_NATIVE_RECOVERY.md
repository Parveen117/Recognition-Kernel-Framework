# PG791 · Derivation and native recovery

## The completing relation

Rule 5.2.48 attaches `ḍaṭ` to a numeral in the genitive relation when the referent completes that number. `ekādaśaḥ` and `trayodaśaḥ` are ordinals because the added member completes eleven or thirteen. A pot that merely completes a collection of five handfuls is not thereby admitted; the input must be numerical completion.

Rule 5.2.49 inserts `maṭ` before `ḍaṭ` after a numeral ending in `n` when no preceding numeral is part of the base. Thus `pañcamaḥ` and `saptamaḥ`. The negative controls are structural: `viṃśaḥ` does not end in `n`, and compound `ekādaśaḥ` has a preceding numeral. Both still receive the general ordinal suffix, but not this augment.

## Vedic and special augments

In the Vedic register 5.2.50 permits `thaṭ` after the same n-final uncompounded numerals, while `maṭ` remains an alternative. The direct records give `pañcathāni`, `pañcathaḥ`, `saptathaḥ` and `pañcamam`. The Vedic fifth and seventh masculine positions therefore retain `tha` and `ma` readings with separate histories. This option is not projected into ordinary prose.

Rule 5.2.51 assigns `thuk` before `ḍaṭ` after `ṣaṣ`, `kati`, `katipaya` and `catur`. The rule itself licenses the otherwise non-numeral `katipaya` for this ordinal construction. The outputs are `ṣaṣṭhaḥ`, `katithaḥ`, `katipayathaḥ` and `caturthaḥ`. A supplement for `catur` instead deletes the first letter and admits `cha` or `yat`, producing the accepted alternatives `turīyaḥ` and `turyaḥ`. These alternatives do not rewrite the history of `caturthaḥ`.

Rule 5.2.52 inserts `tithuk` for `bahu`, `pūga`, `gaṇa` and `saṃgha`. The last three obtain ordinal admission from this very rule rather than from an invented general numeral status: `bahutithaḥ`, `pūgatithaḥ`, `gaṇatithaḥ`, `saṃghatithaḥ`.

## Accent and full-carrier recovery

The four Kāṭhaka records keep their written accent as source-layer readouts; no complete accent paradigm is inferred. Reuse PG14's full carrier `CΣ`, derived involution and `B=CΣ⟨zₑ,zₑ*⟩`. Type numeral or lexical base `I`, completion request `Q`, original `ḍaṭ` and augment history `H`, Vedic/register branch `R`, case/number word `W`, accent `A`, and source policy `S`. An admitted edge acts by `Tₑ(eₕb)=eₕ′(1+zₑ)b`, with its adjoint. Projections by augmentation retain each type, so `πout Tₑ=Tₑ,0 πin`; composition and pairing commute with specialization.

Typed zero-specialization prevents collapse. `pañcamaḥ` and Vedic `pañcathaḥ` may occupy one ordinal address but keep `maṭ`/`thaṭ` and register histories. `caturthaḥ`, `turīyaḥ` and `turyaḥ` are accepted alternatives with different operations. `pūgatithaḥ` retains rule-specific admission and cannot become evidence that every collective noun is a numeral. A nonnumerical completing pot has zero incidence on both carriers.
