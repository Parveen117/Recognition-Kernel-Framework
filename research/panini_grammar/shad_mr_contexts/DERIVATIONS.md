## H06. Different present histories with thematic A endings

Śad has pit śap under 3.1.68 and whole-root śīy under 7.3.78/1.1.55.
The initial indicatory-ś environment is retained after marker removal;
perfect eś is not this environment. Apply the thematic A maps to śīy.
Thus śīyate, śīyatām and śīyeta belong to a basic kartari event;
the printed y does not assert a passive yak.

Mṛ takes apit śa under 3.1.77, with 1.2.4/1.1.5 blocking ordinary
guṇa. Rule 7.4.28 replaces final ṛ by **short ri**, then 6.4.77
gives iyaṅ at its i before the vowel of śa. Both substitutions are
ṅit: **1.1.53** confines each to its final-sound recipient, retaining
the preceding root material. The resulting factor
is mriy, with thematic a. This is not the original root mṛ unchanged
before ya, nor an invented long-rī substitution. The y is owned by
iyaṅ, not passive yak. Apply the same A ending maps to this separately
constructed factor.

In order 3sg, 3du, 3pl, 2sg, 2du, 2pl, 1sg, 1du, 1pl, the maps
after either C=śīy or C=mriy are:

## H07. Two P future interfaces, not present-stem transport

Śad remains aniṭ before sya/tāsi. Keep root d until its late 8.4.55
devoicing before s or t: śad+sya → **śatsy**, śad+tās → **śatt**
as the finite readout factors. Sya-s remains dental. No śīy appears
in these non-śit contexts.

Mṛ has guṇa under 7.3.84, with ṛ→ar and 1.1.51 r ownership.
Before sya, required short iṭ 7.2.70 gives **mariṣy**, with the
sya-s addressed by 8.3.59. Before tāsi there is no such iṭ exception,
giving **mart**. This explains mariṣyati versus martā without declaring
mṛ globally seṭ or carrying its present mriy into the future.

Apply the P laṭ/laṅ maps to śatsy/mariṣy for lṛṭ/lṛṅ. For luṭ,
PG16's P suffixes after śatt/mart are ā, ārau, āraḥ, āsi, āsthaḥ,
āstha, āsmi, āsvaḥ, āsmaḥ. Keep the original P endings and the
2.4.85 third-person number substitutions. These are finite futures,
not newly counted agent nouns. All 54 future/conditional positions
are singletons, with short iṭ in mṛ's eighteen sya positions only.

## H08. Blessing separates P yāsuṭ from ṛ-final A kit

Śad has P kit liṅ under 3.4.104 and non-val yāsuṭ, hence no iṭ.
Its factor is the original **śad**, not śīy. The PG20 tuple is
śadyāt, śadyāstām, śadyāsuḥ, śadyāḥ, śadyāstam, śadyāsta,

Mṛ instead has A aniṭ sīyuṭ. The original short-ṛ root and actual
jhal-initial ending satisfy **1.2.12 kit**, blocking guṇa. The factor
through the first s→ṣ is **mṛṣ**. PG20 gives mṛṣīṣṭa,
mṛṣīyāstām, mṛṣīran, mṛṣīṣṭhāḥ, mṛṣīyāsthām,

ārdhadhātuka liṅ, false for this sīyuṭ interface. Thus no *mri-* is
manufactured here. The eighteen blessing positions are singletons.

## H09. P perfect: śad contraction versus retained mṛ copy

Both roots use simple P liṭ. Introduce their own 6.1.8 copies, śa
and ma; mṛ's copy ṛ is treated by 7.4.66 and consonant retention,
not replaced by a copy of the present mriy. Original P ending series
and weak 1.2.5 kit follow the pinned perfect contract.

For śad, 3sg has 7.2.116 upadhā-vṛddhi: śaśāda. At 1sg,
7.1.91's ṇit option gives **śaśada/śaśāda**; the non-ṇit ṇal
remains pit, so it does not invoke weak e contraction. The six weak
slots satisfy **6.4.120**, giving root e and copy-lopa: śedatuḥ,
śeduḥ, śedathuḥ, śeda, śediva, śedima. The last two have short iṭ.
At thal, the iṭ branch has **6.4.121** e and copy-lopa, śeditha;
the aniṭ branch preserves the copy and root a, with d→t before th,
śaśattha. The source explicitly gives this two-path contrast. There
are eleven readings over nine śad-perfect keys.

For mṛ, 3sg ṇal gives ṛ-vṛddhi 7.2.115/1.1.51: mamāra.
At 1sg the 7.1.91 option gives **mamara/mamāra**. The aniṭ thal
has guṇa and r, mamartha. Weak vowel-facing endings have ṛ→r under
6.1.77: mamratuḥ, mamruḥ, mamrathuḥ, mamra. The 1du/1pl short
iṭ likewise gives mamriva/mamrima. The copy ma is retained in every
slot: mṛ does not have śad's original a-between-single-consonants
condition for 6.4.120. There are ten mṛ-perfect readings over nine keys.

## H10. Śad aṅ, mṛ sic, and complete accounting

Śad's retained **ḷit** teaching and P satisfy 3.1.55: cli→aṅ,
not sic. After marker removal aṅ contributes thematic a, has no val-iṭ,
and is not śit. Therefore it neither changes pada to A nor selects
śīy. Use the ordinary P past endings and 7.1.3 ant at 3pl, not the
sic-conditioned jus. With initial aṭ the tuple is aśadat, aśadatām,
aśadan, aśadaḥ, aśadatam, aśadata, aśadam, aśadāva, aśadāma.

Mṛ retains A under 1.3.61 and ordinary cli→sic 3.1.44. Aniṭ plus
1.2.12 kit preserves root ṛ. PG32's ṛ-root A-sic tuple applies:
amṛta, amṛṣātām, amṛṣata, amṛthāḥ, amṛṣāthām,
**amṛḍhvam**, amṛṣi, amṛṣvahi, amṛṣmahi. Rules 8.2.27 and
8.2.25 delete sic-s at 3sg/2sg and 2pl respectively. The latter has
mandatory 8.3.78 ḍh after root ṛ. None of these A endings receives
apṛkta-hal īṭ. First-singular i is its original ending, not iṭ.
The Vedic aṅ extension for mṛ is outside the classical domain.

Initial aṭ is separate in laṅ/lṛṅ/luṅ and precedes the selected
consonant-initial root/readout; no prefix or initial-vowel āṭ issue is
introduced. All final-n and ant-family readouts follow the inherited
ordered policy; later dental n is not an earlier ṇatva input.
