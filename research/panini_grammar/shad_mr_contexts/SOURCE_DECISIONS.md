## H02. Śad's A license anticipates a source-certified śit relation

Kāśikā, Nyāsa, Padamañjarī and Bālamanoramā 1.3.60 explain
śidbhāvin or śit-prakṛti. Pada must be selected before the relevant
vikaraṇa is installed. Waiting for a fully realized śap and first
choosing residual P would miss the intended A construction.

For the four present-system requests, the lexical/formation/context
contract already entails the coming śap under 3.1.68. Retain that
prospective relation as the 1.3.60 witness, choose A and then instantiate
the specified affix. This is a finite dependency contract, not arbitrary
lookahead or physical future time. No such certificate is present at
sya, tāsi, āśīrliṅ, liṭ or aṅ, so those requests use residual P.
Kāśikā expressly contrasts śatsyati, aśatsyat and śiśatsati;
these non-śit interfaces receive no 1.3.60 A license.

## H03. Mṛṅ's marker is restricted, not erased

Rule 1.3.61 is a niyama on the A already available from ṅit. It
retains A for the śit, luṅ and liṅ environments, and excludes A
elsewhere. Its selected basic present interface is śa under 3.1.77.
Vidhiliṅ has that śit interface; āśīrliṅ has its own liṅ license.
Future, periphrastic future, conditional and perfect are P despite the
retained lexical ṅ marker. The table's pada is therefore indexed by
lakāra, not stored as one global output flag.

Nyāsa and Tattvabodhinī explain why the marker remains useful for
the accent condition in 6.1.180, illustrated with the mā-hi context.
This packet retains that provenance without adding an accented or
prohibitive table. Bālamanoramā's digitized h/ṅ confusions in this
field remain literal in the archive; they do not redefine the marker.
