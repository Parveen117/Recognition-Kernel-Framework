# PG849 · Vṛtra lay covering the world: the middle perfect and the accent of the repeated verb

Mādhyandina Śatapatha 1.1.3.4 places two occurrences of śiśye under the same Vṛtra subject. Vṛtvā supplies an earlier act of covering, with idaṃ sarvam as the covered extent. Śiśye supplies the posture. The text itself connects covering with the name Vṛtra. Kāṇva 2.1.3.2 preserves the narrative verb and the naming explanation in a shorter sequence. After the intervening description of heaven and earth it goes directly to tasmād vṛtro nāma. The second sleep verb belongs to the Mādhyandina repetition alone. The later finite jaghāna has Indra as agent and Vṛtra as object.

**Two clause scopes in one recension.** On the labelled Viśvāsa accent presentation, the main statement has unaccented śiśye. In the second clause sa yad idaṃ sarvaṃ vṛtvā śiśyé, tasmād … the verb retains its accent. The first yad idam in the description after the first verb cannot govern that preceding verb. The second yad introduces the explanatory clause containing the second verb. The file's grammatical notes assign 8.1.28 to the first and 8.1.66 to the second. Kāśikā on 8.1.66 explicitly says that a following finite verb in this domain escapes the usual anudātta. The rule preserves accent here, it does not prescribe final accent for every subordinate verb. Kāṇva's presentation labels the single śiśye anudātta. These three textual addresses give two distinct accent outcomes for one segmental grammatical word.
