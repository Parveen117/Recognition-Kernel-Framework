# PG865 · Doubt, lying nearby and the post called upaśaya

### Derivation of the two short-vowel nouns

PG44 already lists the two nominatives at 3.3.39. PG862 derives the contrasting stems and gives the long-vowel turn-taking paradigms. Retain the exact preverb and śīṅ ‘lie’. Haradatta's non-turn controls have ac under 3.3.56. After the marker disappears, guṇa and the following vowel give ī → e → ay. The nominal stem is thus *viśaya* for doubt and *upaśaya* for lying nearby. The long āy of the turn-taking ghañ heads belongs to a different affix history. The middle designation of the source root supplies no tiṅ ending to either noun.

### The two sibilants in Mīmāṃsāsūtra 2.3.16

| Witness | What can be read there |
| --- | --- |
| Śrīsiddhāntasudhānidhi on 3.3.39, revision 402326 | It quotes Jaimini with palatal **viśaye** to illustrate *viśayaḥ*, and explicitly glosses that noun as doubt. |
| Śabara text with the sūtra, electronic edition prepared by Andreas Pohlus | The rule has **viśaye**. In the following prose, the repetition *viśaye saṃśaye* establishes the locative and the sense. In the calf example the alternatives are taking hold of the calf and performing a sacrifice. The commentator selects taking hold as a preparatory act. |
| Sandal, Sacred Books of the Hindus XXVII, printed p. 71, rule 16 | The large-type sūtra and the smaller word gloss both have retroflex **विषये**. The English gloss nevertheless gives the sense “in a doubt”. The immediate commentary identifies the milking context and construes the instruction about the calf as a preparatory rite. |
| Separate Sansknet sūtra transcription | It has **viṣaye** and identifies Basu's Allahabad edition in its header. The scan confirms this spelling in that print. No conjectural correction of the transcription or the printed gloss is necessary. |

The case and the interpretation have different evidential histories. The Śabara text directly supports locative singular of the selected palatal lemma. Sandal witnesses the retroflex spelling with substantially the same interpretation. It does not license a palatal/retroflex sound change in this paradigm. The calf construction also shows why accusative singular plus *ālabheta* can establish the object before the particular ritual sense is decided. The specific calf belongs to the Mīmāṃsā example. It supplies no zoological identification for PG864's *patradatam*.

### The post and its predicate in Taittirīya Saṃhitā 6.6.4.4

| Context | Source-specific analysis |

| Explanation of the name | Genitive singular *upaśayasya* qualifies *upaśayatvam*, the abstract noun. The following expression presents the post itself as the nominative subject. |
| Position on the south | The Krama text distinguishes the source words behind the continuous sequence. Keith construes *upaśáye* as the predicate ‘lies near’. The passage subsequently contrasts this post with the other posts which have animals attached. The concrete referent supplies a separate lexical use. Neither a turn to sleep nor a particular Pāṇinian kṛt affix for the ritual object is asserted. One construction compares usage in Śabara and Sandal at two textual interfaces. One construction distinguishes the three post relations. None of the five clause interfaces creates a new finite cell.
