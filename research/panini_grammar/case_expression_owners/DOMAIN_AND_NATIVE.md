# An expression owner belongs to a role

## Role already expressed

Write the role as a triple (event, relation, entity). At its case gate ask which grammatical constituent has already expressed this particular triple. Kāśikā's interpretation of 2.3.1 lists tiṅ, kṛt, taddhita and samāsa. Nyāsa links the finite example to karmaṇi lakāra, the participle to kta and the two śata derivatives to 5.1.21. A separate nominal may then receive prathamā under 2.3.46. The Sanskrit examples establish the construction selected here; the Mahābhāṣya's argument about the need for formulating this rule is not treated as a fifth carrier.

## Three data types that look similar on a flat string

An independent nominative kaṭaḥ co-refers with an already expressed karman in both kriyate kaṭaḥ and kṛtaḥ kaṭaḥ. Śatyaḥ and śatikaḥ themselves have a nominative form, but there is no separate object noun in Kāśikā's displayed words. The field for the separate object's case is therefore null. Kāśikā 5.1.21 independently gives śatena krītaṃ śatikam / śatyam in the neuter: the price is the base and the thing bought is the derived referent. Keep the two facts independent of gender and case in the particular quotation. In prāptodako grāmaḥ the compound and the external word refer to the same village. The analytic paraphrase has yaṃ grāmam; the realized compound construction has grāmaḥ.

## Agreement is not a second semantic object

Kāśikā's kaṭaṃ karoti bhīṣmam udāraṃ darśanīyam shows why an ordinary accusative marking cannot globally suppress accusative on every agreeing modifier. The parse retains one mat entity with several concordant words. A global flag “some word already encoded accusative” would wrongly remove the modifier cases.

## Formal specialization

Extend the full PG138 event/role/participant carrier by an addressed expression edge (kind of carrier, host, role triple). Within each of the eight source cases, testing the edge and then projecting gives the same independent nominal case or absence of a separate nominal as the source construction. The equality is per role. A different role on the same entity, or the number of concordant adjectives, does not consume that edge. The incidence converse keeps both finite-passive and kṛt histories behind the same nominative mat. The classification is a finite worked domain, with no general Sanskrit parser or priority theorem asserted.
