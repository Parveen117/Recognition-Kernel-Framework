## C01 — Same letter, different owner

On hi, 7.4.62/8.4.54 change the copied h to j; 7.3.56 changes the
root h to gh. Applying kutva indiscriminately to both h occurrences
does not derive jighāya or jighīṣati. The root identity must survive

## C02 — A lexical iṭ label does not choose every affix branch

Hi is lexical aniṭ but has perfect iṭ outside the thal alternative.
Ṛdh is lexical seṭ but has optional san iṭ by 7.2.49. Record both the
lexical teaching and the affix-specific rule; never replace either by
one global seṭ/aniṭ boolean. Hi 1sg optional ṇit and hi 2sg optional
iṭ are also independent options on different positions.

## C03 — A source-qualified r-component is not a universal token rewrite

The weak ānṛdhatuḥ/ānṛdhuḥ witnesses defeat a nuṭ guard that requires
root guṇa first. They also defeat a guard requiring two ordinary
consonant tokens in the unexpanded root string. K 7.4.71 explicitly
admits ṛ's r-component for this condition. Preserve that rule-relative
view; do not rewrite every ṛ into ar or infer a consonant cluster for
1.2.5 from this particular dvi-hal admission.

## C04 — Actual san, s-initial san and san-like behavior are distinct

Ardidhiṣati has actual san but not the required s-initial view for
7.4.55. Hi san has actual s-initial san but not the lexical membership
for that rule. Causative caṅ may receive san-like abhyāsa operations
without becoming san. K 7.4.58 explicitly keeps its abhyāsa lopa out
of the sanvadbhāva domain, quoting अमीमपत्/अदीदपत् as controls.
