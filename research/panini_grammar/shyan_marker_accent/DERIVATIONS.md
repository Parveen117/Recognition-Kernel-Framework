# PG56 — ष्यङ् identity, feminine competitors and the final उदात्त

Author: Monty. 4 November 2026.

This packet adds an accent assignment to the two already written
**हास्तिशीर्ष्या** histories. It uses PG55's admission contract and
[PG49's morphological paths](../siras_dependency_history/DERIVATIONS.md)
at commit 51badc7a87c2058157331600224f356b4a77a77a. The compound,
descent preparation and feminine spelling are not counted again.

The positive result on both standard-source paths is **hāstiśīrṣyā́**:
the final आ is उदात्त. An acute accent here marks the selected vowel's
उदात्त location; it is editorial notation, not a literal accented quotation
or a complete recitation transcription. U means उदात्त and A means
अनुदात्त. Later dependent स्वरित, sentence accent and full inflection
are outside this stem-level assignment.

Two positive accent histories, three labelled diagnostic proposals and six
controls give eleven written units. The thirteen R/P stage rows are current
written records, not thirteen new sūtras or new nominal positions.

## Fixed prescriptions and the imported input

Retain D = इञ्, Y = ष्यङ् and F = चाप् as separate prescribed
identities with sound/version addresses. On the replacement path Y replaces
D; on the added-suffix path D and Y have separate histories. The imported
prefix has already recognized D's ञ् as इत् and obtained the initial
वृद्धि of PG49 S3. PG56 does not infer a marker from the final spelling.

Kāśikā 4.1.74 explicitly includes both ञ्यङ् and ष्यङ् in its
यङ् reference. Nyāsa explains why ष्यङ्'s ङ् supports this shared
reference. The inherited 4.1.78 discussion assigns ष् the complementary
role of preserving the intended shared selection. Ordinary marker projection
removes the pronounced markers under 1.3.3/6/7/9 but retains these prescription
facts. It does not rename the affix to an unclassified य.

For F, initial च् and final प् are markers. The प् supports the
shared आप् reference, and the च् also has the explicit चित् accent
consequence. F can be both चित् and पित्: keeping one designation
does not delete the other. Nyāsa and Padamañjarī 4.1.74 resolve the
3.1.4/6.1.163 competition in favour of the चित् accent.

## The two competing ई-suffix proposals

The following are Padamañjarī 4.1.78's scoped interpretations; they are
not a theorem that substitution preserves every affix attribute. In the अण्
comparison use PG53's already constructed replacement history. In the इञ्
comparison use the present imported history.

|Proposed competitor|Source-qualified guard|Decision after the specified ष्यङ् replacement|
|---|---|---|
|ङीप् through 4.1.15's अण् entry|The inherited अत् qualification is read as the अ belonging to अण्|The current Y-owned अ is not D's अ. The source explicitly invokes 1.1.56's अनल्विधि limit; borrowing historical अण् identity cannot supply this sound-dependent target|
|ङीष् through the इञ् उपसंख्यान at 4.1.65|The retained इतः qualification requires the relevant इ; the looser इञ्-ending/i-final formulation also needs current i-final shape|Y ends in अ, not इ. Historical D identity alone does not establish this guard|
|चाप् by 4.1.74|The actual standard ष्यङ् prescription is included in यङ्|Admit F = चाप् in the supplied feminine domain|

The first row cannot be replaced by two disconnected booleans, “current
अ-final” and “some old अण्”. Padamañjarī explicitly identifies that
weaker reading as giving the unwanted ङीप् opportunity. The second row's
उपसंख्यान extends beyond जाति but does not erase its vowel condition.

The fact that Y itself expresses feminine meaning does not forbid F:
4.1.74 explicitly includes this prescription, and the 4.1.78 discussion
also addresses two feminine exponents. Shared spelling and accent do not
identify H01/H02's histories or establish agreement on every later consumer.

## D01 — Replacing चाप् by टाप् is an accent-changing diagnostic

This is not an admitted alternative under unchanged 4.1.74. Deliberately
suppress that prescription while keeping the replacement account's ञित्
transfer, then propose F = टाप्. Its pronounced आ is A by 3.1.4;
it has no चित् fact to supply R4. Padamañjarī says the 6.1.197
initial-U accent would remain. In this fixed example the diagnostic is
**hā́stiśīrṣyā**, with the same unaccented letters but a different U address.
It does not recover the target accent and is not a new grammatical branch.

## D02 — Premature replacement cannot discard only the unwanted consequence

The source considers replacing D before its ञ् has received इत्
designation, in an attempt to avoid the initial-U inheritance in D01.
On that proposal the required ञित् premise for 7.2.117 is absent too.
The attempt therefore does not establish the initial वृद्धि of the intended
stem. It is rejected as a derivation strategy; no complete alternative form
is manufactured or counted. Marker recognition, marker pronunciation and
the already completed grade event are three different records.

## D03 — The redesigned added-affix argument is conditional

Padamañjarī argues that in a redesigned added-affix presentation, the
new Y's later 3.1.3 accent followed by A-marked टाप् can preserve the
same surface and accent: Y's अ(U) plus F's आ(A) gives आ(U) by
8.2.5. This accounts for the local **न रूपभेदो न स्वरभेदः** claim.
It is a comparison of proposed presentations, not permission to choose
टाप् freely while retaining the standard 4.1.74 prescription unchanged.

The same discussion still needs a distinguishing marker for 6.1.13.
A single ष् proposal attracts the 4.1.41 ङीष् objection; the proposed
ङ् notation in turn raises the लोलूयापुत्र/पाश्यापति and
धातु/आनन्तर्य questions. The inherited PG51 standard ष्यङ् construction
is not silently rewritten. The local accent comparison here does not prove
that the proposed reduced notation is globally interchangeable with the
standard grammar. Its complete downstream redesign remains open.

## C01 — ञ्, न् and ण् are not one marker class

6.1.197 names ञित् and dental नित्; it does not make every णित्
suffix initial-U by that rule. 7.2.117 instead refers to ञित्/णित्
for initial वृद्धि. H01's accent transfer uses D = इञ् specifically.
The separate अण्/ङीप् guard comparison is not evidence that अण्
automatically supplies 6.1.197's accent.

## C02 — A current vowel must belong to the guarded affix

The current Y-owned अ and historical D = अण् cannot be spliced into
the sound-dependent guard of the first competitor row. Similarly an old
इञ् record does not make Y i-final. These failures coexist with the
source-authorized ञित् accent transfer: 1.1.56 is consumer-qualified,
not a bulk copy or a bulk deletion of every property.

## C03 — Marker projection is different from whole-affix disappearance

The inherited ordinary इत् projection retains an active suffix's marker
classification. It is not the whole-affix-lopa situation discussed at
6.1.197. Kāśikā explicitly withholds the otherwise proposed प्रत्ययलक्षण
accent in its deleted-affix examples. H02 uses the source's new-Y accent
account; it does not grant every historical ञ् record continuing control
over every later word. The differing Nyāsa/Padamañjarī explanations of
the general restriction are preserved, not collapsed into a new theorem.

## C04 — चित् does not erase पित्

F = चाप् retains both markers' functions. The commentary resolves the
competing accent proposals; it does not redefine चाप् as non-पित्.
Kāśikā 6.1.163 describes the end of the base-plus-suffix formation,
not simply “the first vowel of whichever suffix looks like आ”. The
coalesced final vowel keeps both contributors for the 8.2.5 query.

## C05 — Accent precedence is not global rule-number sorting

6.1.158 explains interactions of later, obligatory, internal, exceptional
and सतिशिष्ट accents, and supplies limits on the last principle.
H01/H02 use the specific 4.1.74/78 decisions, rather than selecting the
largest rule number or the latest timestamp. Old accent proposals remain
in history without being simultaneously pronounced; 6.1.158 fixes the
other vowels as A in this selected domain. Full recitation is still open.

## C06 — Preserve damaged readings without using their wrong addresses

Nyāsa 4.1.74 quotes चितः but embeds **6.1.157**; the pinned
catalogue and the actual चितः fields give **6.1.163**, used here.
Padamañjarī 4.1.74 prints **डित्करण** in the shared-Y
discussion; the adopted ङ् interpretation follows the actual ष्यङ्
prescription and Nyāsa's explicit ङित्करण wording. Both literal
strings remain unchanged in the archive. No manuscript-wide correction,
new counterfactual paradigm or general marker-reduction theorem is claimed.
