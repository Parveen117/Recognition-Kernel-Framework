## H06. Present yacch and nonpresent yam remain distinct

Reuse PG86's yam root operations: 7.3.77 changes final m to ch before
actual śit; tuk and ścutva complete yacch. This is not dāṇ's whole-root
yacch substitute. The thematic A ending contracts retain separate śap,
loṭ ending āṭ and ordinary liṅ owners.

Non-śit future sya/tās retains yam, with the 7.2.10 iṭ refusal. Root m
becomes anusvāra before sya, and late dental n before tās. Keep
saṃyaṃsyate and finite saṃyantā as different addressed continuations.
The dental n is not fed backward into an earlier fresh ṇatva pass.

## H07. Blessing and perfect inherit their exact root owners

A blessing keeps yam until the late m→anusvāra operation. Its first s
stays dental; separate suṭ produces singular ṣṭ/ṣṭh. The 2pl is
saṃyaṃsīdhvam with dental dh, not a retroflex branch.

In liṭ, copy ya and original yam undergo 6.4.120's root-a→e and separate
copy lopa, giving yem. Four val-initial ending slots take iṭ; ending irec
and first-singular i remain distinct. The 2pl saṃyemidhve has dental dh.
These root/ending histories are exact PG86 dependencies; the new semantic
certificate and prefix interface do not alter them.

## H08. Non-kit sic retains root m in all nine slots

H04 excludes both sense-specific kit licences. From PG86's explicitly
retained-m continuation, the unaugmented A tuple is

\[
(yaṃsta,yaṃsātām,yaṃsata,yaṃsthāḥ,yaṃsāthām,
yandhvam,yaṃsi,yaṃsvahi,yaṃsmahi).
\]

Root m survives until Tripādī; short-vowel-final 8.2.27 therefore does not
delete sic-s before ta/thās. At 2pl 8.2.25 deletes sic-s, then root m
becomes anusvāra and dental n before dh. The late n is root-derived;
it is not the ending and not an earlier ṇatva input. No independent iṭ,
P sak+iṭ or passive ciṇvat route applies. All nine slots have one path.

## H09. Sam and initial aṭ have separate owners

Without initial aṭ, prefix sam before root y has selected retained
anusvāra: saṃyacchate, saṃyaṃsyate, saṃyeme. In laṅ/lṛṅ/luṅ the
prefix m precedes aṭ's vowel and remains m: samayacchata,
samayaṃsyata, samayaṃsta. There is no upa+aṭ→upā seam in this packet.
