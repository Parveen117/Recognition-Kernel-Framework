# PG960 · When the rope itself breaks

A cutter can cut a rope, a rope can be cut by someone, and a rope can give way of itself. Each sentence can concern the same event type and a single rope. The surface *chidyate* occurs in both of the last two descriptions. PG956 owns the expressed-patient form. Bhoja's Sarasvatīkaṇṭhābharaṇa with the Hṛdayahāriṇī, I.4.233, explicitly puts **छिद्यते स्वयमेव** beside **छिदुरा रज्जुः**. Here the rope itself is represented as acting. The lion breaking an elephant's pride in PG957 supplies a different relation. For a Pāṇinian construction start with rudhādi 07.0003, √chid in *dvaidhīkaraṇa*, simple and unprefixed. The entity which could undergo cutting now bears the subject's independent role in the object's own breaking action. Under 3.1.87 that agent receives the specified object-like verbal operations. Before the sārvadhātuka endings this selects yaK and A. The verb follows the rope's external number. An efficient sword has no earlier patient role in this frame. Kāśikā's *sādhv asiś chinatti* keeps an actor formation for the sword. A cutter deliberately cutting himself introduces a cutter and a reflexive object, which also fails this role-identity test. The rule does not require an overt *svayam* in every sentence. Here the witness provides it. [3.1.87 and adjoining exceptions](../compound_case_derivation/RULES_3_1_B.md)

Addressing an agent who is instructed to cut the rope would require another construction.

Bhoja I.4.232 specifies the karmakartṛ condition for √bhañj. Rule 233 introduces kurac for √chid and √bhid. Its commentary reports that some readers do not carry the preceding condition forward. That is a disagreement about inherited scope between two rules of Bhoja. Mādhavīya 1041 independently names Ātreya in a discussion of the vṛtti. Keep the unnamed readers distinct from him. The Kāśikā's 3.2.162 explanation explicitly names the condition for √bhid and √chid and gives the earlier rope adjective. Stages PG957 and PG960 therefore answer two different questions, one concerning an adjective's eligible referent, the other concerning the subject of a finite verb.
