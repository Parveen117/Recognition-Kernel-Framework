# PG100 — Root choice, prefix contact and eight finite labels

Monty · 10 November 2026

The [native carrier](DOMAIN_AND_NATIVE.md) retains the following
source-qualified constructions. PG98's exact ending proofs and
context codes are reused without re-executing a grammar engine.

## H06. One ṇic and the four present-system labels

After lāp or lāy, ṇic i receives guṇa and ay; the outer
śap belongs to the derived dhātu. No basic divādi śyan or
kryādi śnā is inserted instead. Laṭ, laṅ, loṭ and
vidhiliṅ use the inherited full nine-slot endings. Loṭ P2sg
has the specified hi-luk; ordinary vidhiliṅ retains its own
7.2.79 s-lopa and is not the blessing construction.

Named A gives ālāpayate or ullāpayate. Ordinary P gives
ullāpayati/ullāyayati. The first y in lāyay belongs to
root vṛddhi/āy, the second to ṇic; their different owners
remain visible even though both print y. No sibilant-root or
root-nasal work is introduced merely by analogy with PG98.

## H07. Future and periphrastic future retain ordinary iṭ

Lṛṭ and lṛṅ use sya; luṭ uses finite tāsi with its
inherited person endings. Ordinary 7.2.35 iṭ follows ṇic,
which consequently remains ay under the pinned val interface.
The lexicon's basic-root aniṭ tag does not suppress this
current derived-stem iṭ. No karmavat/ciṇvat alternative applies.

Examples are ullāpayiṣyate, ullāpayitā and P
ullāpayiṣyati/ullāyayiṣyati. A finite luṭ word ending
in tā is not an agent-noun derivation. Preserve original tiṅ
provenance and the distinct auxiliary-like finite replacements.

## H08. Blessing lopa, two y owners and A2pl dh

P āśīrliṅ's non-val yāsut licenses 6.4.51 ṇic-lopa.
The root ā/puk branch gives ullāpyāt; the retained-ī branch
gives ullāyyāt, with root y and yāsut y adjacent. PG98's
6.1.66 val restriction prevents deleting root y before y; the
required yy is not optional consonant doubling.

A blessing retains ṇic through ordinary iṭ and gives, for
example, ullāpayiṣīṣṭa. At 2pl, the pre-iṭ aṅga ends
in ṇic ay, allowing the pinned dental/retroflex alternative
under 8.3.79: ullāpayiṣīdhvam/ullāpayiṣīḍhvam.
Each of six A profiles has exactly this one two-word cell.
No extra root branch is created by the ending option.

## H09. Aṭ interrupts ud–l contact but merges with ā

Without aṭ, prefix ud directly precedes root l: 8.4.60 gives
ul+l, hence ullāpay-. The two l occurrences keep prefix and
root addresses. In laṅ/lṛṅ, 6.4.71 inserts aṭ after
ud and before the whole root. Now ud+a+l yields **udalāpay-**;
the earlier ud+l readout is not reused through the intervening
vowel. Original prefix d remains voiced before a.

With prefix ā, aṭ instead coalesces by 6.1.101, leaving
**ālāpay-** visibly unchanged at the left edge. Thus ālāpaye
can read as either present or past in selected slots while its
aṭ history differs. A surface observer cannot reconstruct the
lakāra/context or augment owner from that spelling alone.
