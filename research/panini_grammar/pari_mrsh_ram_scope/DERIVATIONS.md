## H06. Present systems and prefix-relative aṭ

Mṛṣ takes śyan under 3.1.69. Its ś-it prevents root guṇa under
1.1.5: mṛṣya, not marṣa. Ram takes śap under 3.1.68, giving rama.
The inherited nine-slot P/A interfaces distinguish laṭ, laṅ, loṭ
and vidhiliṅ; loṭ first-person āṭ and liṅ yāsuṭ/sīyuṭ are ending
owners, not root augments. Selected P loṭ 1sg has mṛṣyāṇi/ramāṇi;
late dental n in 3pl is not fed into an earlier ṇatva pass.

Initial aṭ is after the prefix and before the root/copy in laṅ,
lṛṅ and luṅ. Pari+a→parya and vi+a→vya by 6.1.77; ā+a→ā and
upa+a→upā by 6.1.101. Thus ārama(t) can hide initial aṭ, whereas
upārama(t) differs from unaugmented uparama-. Owners remain visible
even where the written prefix does not change.

## H07. Futures and blessings do not copy the present stem

Mṛṣ's non-śit seṭ sya/tās interfaces give marṣiṣya and marṣitā;
7.3.86 supplies ṛ→ar. Conditional uses the sya stem with initial
aṭ. P blessing is kit by 3.4.104, has no śyan and retains mṛṣ:
mṛṣyāt. Its y is the blessing ending interface, not present śyan.

Ram remains aniṭ before sya and tās. Root m→anusvāra before sya
gives raṃsya; before tās it gives late dental n, rantā, through
8.3.24/8.4.58. It is not a retained primitive n and does not license
backward ṇatva. Luṭ's 3sg/du/pl shared P/A readouts keep distinct
request/pada owners.

P blessing ramyāt keeps m: 6.4.37 requires jhal, and y is not jhal.
A blessing follows the pinned non-kit nasal-root interface of PG106:
raṃsīṣṭa, raṃsīyāstām, raṃsīran, raṃsīṣṭhāḥ, raṃsīyāsthām,
raṃsīdhvam, raṃsīya, raṃsīvahi, raṃsīmahi. The first s remains
dental; the singular suṭ/ṣṭ history is separate. No ḍh option is
introduced at 2pl after this consonant-final root.

## H08. Perfect keeps copy, root grade and ending iṭ distinct

Mṛṣ duplicates as ma+mṛṣ. Non-kit strong slots show mamarṣa and
mamarṣitha; kit weak slots retain mamṛṣ-. There is no root-a→e
substitution because the original root vowel is ṛ. Seṭ thal, va and
ma take iṭ. The first-person ṇal ṇit ON/OFF histories coincide at
mamarṣa: 7.2.116's a-upadhā condition is absent. Keep both paths
but count that written reading once.

Ram duplicates as ra+ram. 3sg ṇal gives rarāma; 1sg has rarāma /
rarama under the inherited optional ṇal-work policy. Weak kit slots
have root a→e and separate copy lopa by 6.4.120, giving rem-. Thal
has the 7.2.62–63 iṭ option: remitha by 6.4.121 versus rarantha
with retained copy and late dental n before th. Va/ma take iṭ.
For A all nine slots have rem-; val-initial endings take iṭ in
2sg, 2pl, 1du and 1pl. Remidhve keeps dental dh. The supplied
non-ud-it policy and these finite iṭ licences are distinct queries.

## H09. Aorist distinguishes ordinary seṭ, sak+iṭ and aniṭ A

Mṛṣ 1040060 is not in the selected divādi puṣādi subgroup and has
neither ir-it nor ḷ-it. Its ordinary P sic takes iṭ, blocking
halanta-vṛddhi by 7.2.4; ṛ guṇa gives marṣ-. The unaugmented tuple
is marṣīt, marṣiṣṭām, marṣiṣuḥ, marṣīḥ, marṣiṣṭam, marṣiṣṭa,
marṣiṣam, marṣiṣva, marṣiṣma. Do not borrow aṅ from other divādi
entries merely because śyan was present in their present systems.

Ram P has a more specific construction under **7.2.73**: sak is
an end-member augment of the root, while iṭ belongs to sic. Its
k is a marker and a is pronunciation-only. The first s in raṃs-
belongs to sak; the later ṣ in raṃsiṣṭām belongs to sic. Iṭ blocks
7.2.3 vṛddhi; in 3sg/2sg ending īṭ and 8.2.28 remove sic-s, not
root-owned sak-s. The tuple is raṃsīt, raṃsiṣṭām, raṃsiṣuḥ,
raṃsīḥ, raṃsiṣṭam, raṃsiṣṭa, raṃsiṣam, raṃsiṣva, raṃsiṣma.

Ram A gets neither sak nor this iṭ: raṃsta, raṃsātām, raṃsata,
raṃsthāḥ, raṃsāthām, randhvam, raṃsi, raṃsvahi, raṃsmahi.
At 2pl 8.2.25 removes sic-s; m→anusvāra→dental n follows.
The root is not ik-upadhā, so 1.2.11 does not make this sic kit.
No nasal lopa or forced ḍh follows. These three construction paths
retain separate root, sak, iṭ, sic, aṭ and ending īṭ owners.
