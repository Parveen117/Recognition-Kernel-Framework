## H01. Lexical identity precedes the prefix exception

Count divādi **1040060 मृषँ तितिक्षायाम्**, basic seṭ U mṛṣ,
with actual pari, a supplied ordinary agent c, an endured object q and
principal intended result for c. Kāśikā 1.3.82 identifies its svarita
teaching marker and overrides the actor-result A permission with P.
Nyāsa explicitly derives parimṛṣyati with śyan. Retain the teaching
marker and the root-vowel/iṭ query separately.

Do not merge this entry with bhvādi **1010804 मृषुँ**, whose ordinary
P parimarṣati needs no exception, or curādi **1100387 मृषँ**. The
Bālamanoramā extension to the latter's optional absence of ṇic is
recorded as a separate, uncounted domain. In particular parimarṣati
does not license deleting śyan from the counted divādi present.

## H03. Ram's three-prefix rule and its lexical policy

Select bhvādi **1010989 रमुँ क्रीडायाम्**, lexical A and aniṭ.
Kāśikā 1.3.83 expressly attributes baseline A to anudātta-it; vi,
āṅ and pari override it with P. They are three independently supplied
actual-prefix profiles, not one optional-prefix branch of a request.
The counted ordinary-agent frames are intransitive uses within the
cited ram domain. No result-recipient reversal is needed for P.

The lexical archive separately contains **1010953 रमँ**, a variant
teaching entry, and records differing opinions on ud-it for 1010989.
Select the Madhava/non-ud-it policy explicitly supported by
Bālamanoramā's ram subsection in 8.3.71. Do not create a second root
or carry an optional ktvā iṭ debate into these finite tables.
Abhi+ram is an uncounted A control, not a fourth P prefix.

## H04. Upa+ram has two role frames, not one unrestricted option

Kāśikā 1.3.84's devadattam/yajñadattam uparamati uses a transitive
ram with **antarbhāvita-ṇyartha** (causative meaning internal to the lexical
use).
The surface construction is basic ram: **nic_count=0**. An explanatory
uparamayati does not insert ṇic into the counted word.

For this supplied transitive frame keep agent c and stopped person q;
P is mandatory. For the separately supplied intransitive cessation
frame, 1.3.85 makes the previously prescribed P optional, leaving
baseline A on its complementary grammatical branch. Both uparamati
and uparamate are licensed. The distinction concerns the role frame,
not whether an accusative token happens to be printed in a sentence.
Nyāsa's yāvadbhuktam analysis must not be misread as a karman that
forces the transitive branch.

The separate 1.3.84 rule is precisely what confines 1.3.85's option
to upa. Do not propagate the option to vi/ā/pari. Unknown roles give
HOLD; an absent object mention is not a positive intransitivity proof.
