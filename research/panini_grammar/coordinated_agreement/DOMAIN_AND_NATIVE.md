# Controllers, person and number

## Two source layers

Kāśikā 1.4.105/107 assigns madhyama/uttama with a co-referential addressee/speaker, present or understood. 1.4.108 gives the residual prathama. 1.4.101–102 defines the person/number ending groups; 1.4.21–22 distinguishes one, two and more than two. These are the traditional labels used in the previous finite tables. A first-person speaker is uttama and a third-person other is prathama.

For mixed coordination the evidence here follows Panchal and Kulkarni, *Co-ordination in Sanskrit*, Indian Linguistics 80(1–2), 2019, pp. 67–69. Their note 3 relates person hierarchy to the traditional 1.2.72 discussion. Their finite examples and the choice of nearby member versus whole group are separately attributed to that article. The original PDF's hash, byte length, page locator and address are in the data. The Kāśikā at 1.2.72 directly discusses pronominal ekaśeṣa; the examples of mixed finite concord are a different evidence layer.

## Partial function on a group, relation on a clause

Keep distinct participant IDs, role, linear order and speech-act person. In the collective schedule, the presence of a speaker selects first person; otherwise an addressee selects second person, and the remaining groups select third person. Count the distinct members in each demonstrated group to get dual or plural. Group membership is decided first. A mention of the addressee in an object phrase does not join that person to the subject.

In the four mixed examples chosen here, the individual nearest the following verb also has the highest-ranked person. They therefore show a number difference between the two schedules and do **not** determine how a competing nearest-person rank would behave under every permutation. The three-other plural is independently exemplified by the article's figure 13. The inventory includes only the supported schedules. The pending 1.4.106 mocking construction needs a separate assignment.

## Native specialization

Extend the PG138 event frame by a typed controller object: either one entity ID or a set of member IDs with a retained source order. Agreement is computed on that choice, then passed to the finite-word table. On each listed schedule both the ordinary lookup and the full path give the same person/number and word. The inverse of gacchāmi has three distinct displayed group histories, so recovering one number ending cannot establish that the event involved exactly one participant. The same proof applies by union on this finite support. This is a formal source-conditioned slice of coordination, with wider orders and registers left open.
