## C3. T120's niṣṭhā iṭ statement needs a semantic repair

T120 K21 reads **7.2.15 yasya vibhāṣā** as a root *yas* receiving
{yastaḥ, yasitaḥ}. The pinned Kāśikā instead says: a root for which
iṭ has been taught optionally in the relevant earlier-described context
does **not** receive iṭ before niṣṭhā. Its examples include
**vidhūta/vidhūtavat**, **gūḍha/gūḍhavat** and
**vṛddha/vṛddhavat**, with an explicit qualification for patita.

Here *yasya* is the relative expression “of which [root]”, not a lexical
instruction naming *yas*. The K21 pair has no witness in this clause.
Repair the rule meaning, then separately source any desired *yas* entry
and forms. Do not just alter a rule-number label while keeping its
claimed optionality.

This matters directly at the boundary with PG17: dhūñ's and the
vṛt-group roots' future iṭ choices do not automatically survive into
niṣṭhā. The future branch table is scoped to sya/tās. Neither a global
`veṭ` flag nor a uniform independent option product is adequate.

## C4. Two marker guards need exact ownership

**Initial k of kta/ktavatu.** T120 L3 cites 1.3.3 for that marker.
Rule 1.3.3 concerns a final consonant in the teaching form. The pinned
Kāśikā **1.3.8** expressly identifies initial l/ś/k-class consonants
in non-taddhita affixes and gives kta/ktavatu's bhukta/bhuktavat examples.
Retain the valid kit result, with the correct initial-marker witness.

**7.3.52's ku guard.** T120 D3/L5 expands this to “ghit / ṇit / ṇyat”.
The pinned Kāśikā states **ghit or ṇyat**, not an independent generic
ṇit class. The expansion would incorrectly change c in ṇit ṇvul's
**pācaka**, one of T120's own examples. Keep ṇit vṛddhi separate from
the narrower ku condition. This also prevents an eventual shared marker
adapter from applying ku indiscriminately to ṇic constructions.

Both corrections affect guard proofs even where an already printed
example happens to remain correct. A marker-reading function alone does
not determine every rule's admissible marker class.

## C5. Sense remains part of the frame

T120 D2 itself lists multiple senses for kta and lyuṭ. Its L1 claim that
none of these affixes requires a declared sense should be narrowed:
the sources supply an **admissible sense relation**, while a completed
meaning-to-form input selects the intended event/role reading. For
example lyuṭ can express bhāva, karaṇa or adhikaraṇa under the named
rules. Its marker string alone does not choose one of those meanings.

An unresolved input can retain several labelled interpretations, but
that is different from several optional forms for one completed sense.
The PG14/PG17 carrier already preserves that distinction. No general
decoder from a suffix or a printed word to a unique meaning is proved.
