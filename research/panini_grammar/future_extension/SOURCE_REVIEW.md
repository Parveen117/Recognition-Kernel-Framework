## S2. Optionality has an affix and a lexical identity

Kāśikā 7.2.44 directly illustrates **svartā/svaritā** and
**dhotā/dhavitā**, differentiates dhūñ from non-ñ dhū, and explains
that 7.2.70 gives obligatory **svariṣyati**. The named exception,
not a global `veṭ` property, resolves that sya branch. The publisher's
svṛ lexical accent tag and Padamañjarī's description of its original
anudātta status are not reconciled here; the explicit option and sya
override determine the unaccented future forms without deciding that
metadata discrepancy. No accent-completion claim is made.

The long-ū class-5 dhūñ entry is fixed. Kaumudī's procedure stored
under 7.2.39 also discusses a short-u reading and a long alternative;
PG17 does not expand that into every homographic class or teaching form.
Principal-result P/A semantics remain a separate input from the optional
iṭ of the chosen entry.

Kāśikā 7.2.48 gives **eṣṭā/eṣitā** for the desire iṣ and uses
**eṣiṣyati** to show the t-initial restriction. It distinguishes
other iṣ entries. Kaumudī's procedure tail under 7.2.46 discusses a
wider reading for class-9 iṣ; that adjudication is outside the selected
class-6 object. Its tail under 1.2.2 gives the class-6 iṣ examples.
These storage locations are not asserted to be the literal future rules.

Kāśikā/Kaumudī 7.2.57 explicitly give **nartiṣyati/nartsyati**;
Kāśikā contrasts a t-initial example to show that the option does not
apply there. Nartitā is the written consequence of the base iṭ permission,
root guṇa and luṭ rules. Short u-it under 7.2.56 is a ktvā condition,
not the ū-it option of 7.2.44; keeping the teaching markers matters.

## S3. The five vṛt entries and the dependent prohibition

Kāśikā **1.3.92** enumerates exactly vṛt, vṛdh, śṛdh, syand and
kṛp in the relevant gaṇa and gives optional P before sya/san. Its
examples oppose **vartsyati** to **vartiṣyate/varddhiṣyate**.
Rule 1.3.93 specifically extends optional P to kṛp in luṭ while
retaining the earlier sya/san route. A source listing in a different
gaṇa with the same visible letters is not thereby admitted.

The prohibitions in **7.2.59–60** depend on that pada context. Kāśikā,
Nyāsa and Bālamanoramā explain why syand's ūdit option does not
survive on the P sya branch. Kaumudī explicitly gives
**syantsyati** against A **syandiṣyate/syantsyate**. Its kṛp
procedure gives P **kalptāsi/kalpsyati** against A
**kalpitāse/kalptāse** and **kalpiṣyate/kalpsyate**.
These are the decisive source pairs in B4.

Kāśikā's shorter A example lists alone are not exhaustive alternative
lists. We use Kaumudī/Bālamanoramā's explicit two-way A procedure;
we do not infer an A prohibition merely from an omitted example.

The commentaries explain the inherited parasmaipadeṣu language more
broadly through the absence of taṅ/āna and contexts with ending lopa
or kṛt. PG17 claims only overt finite P/A in the declared futures.
San, lṛṅ, intensive forms and kṛt extensions are not included merely
because the same commentary discusses them.

## S5. Retained causative ṇic is source-driven

Kāśikā **6.4.51** defines ṇic-lopa before an aniṭ ārdhadhātuka
and explicitly contrasts **kārayitā/hārayitā**. Its Bālamanoramā
discussion also explains why the aniṭ condition cannot be discarded.
This source supports the future branch of C3: outer iṭ is present,
so ṇic-lopa is not applicable. The distinct yak branch in PG15 remains
unchanged.

Rule **3.1.32** makes the sanādi-ending group a dhātu. Hence the
future-affix input includes ṇic; it is not identical to the nested bare
root's iṭ classification. Rules 7.2.35, 7.3.84, 6.1.78 and 1.1.6
then act on their own constituents. PG13's source-qualified root
strengthening, mit shortening and puk/yuk ownership are reused, not
reconstructed from a causative present word.

PG14 V5–V7 supplies the exact causal frames and 1.3.74/77/86–89
pada decision. Their continuation here does not drop the conscious
bhū/sthā restriction, budh exception, pā cancellation or gam's
selected sense. Source-derived future words beyond the literal
kārayitā/hārayitā examples remain labelled as written consequences.
The inherited kṛṣ exact-word evidence limitation stays open.
