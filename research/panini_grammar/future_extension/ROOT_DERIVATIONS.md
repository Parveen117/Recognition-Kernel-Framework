# PG17 — Root interfaces with optional augment branches

Author: Monty. 2 November 2026.

## R1. Typed affixes and inherited endings

The source-facing root state is followed by the owned sya or tās of
3.1.33 and an original finite tiṅ member. As in PG16, sya/tās is
ārdhadhātuka; the finite tiṅ member is sārvadhātuka. An iṭ introduced
by B4 belongs to the following affix and retains its own marker ancestry.
The ending iṭ of A first singular is a different occurrence.

Each table below is a **final interface summary**. Guṇa and ending
morphology occur before the relevant later Tripādī readings. A printed
H for luṭ does not authorize feeding late consonant changes back into
6.4.143, 6.4.24 or another earlier query. The nine-slot formulas are
given in P1 of PARADIGMS and inherit their full proof from PG16 E1–E6.

## R3. svṛ and dhūñ: the two different veṭ behaviours

Svṛ's sya branch has obligatory iṭ by 7.2.70, overriding 7.2.44's
option. Guṇa gives svar; affix s after augment i becomes ṣ by 8.3.59:

\[
 svṛ+i+sya+ti\to svar+i+sya+ti\to svariṣyati.
\]

Before tās, the 7.2.70 guard fails. Its two 7.2.44 branches yield
svar+i+tās and svar+tās. Apply luṭ third-singular replacement and
ṭi-lopa lawfully to obtain **svaritā** and **svartā**. Kāśikā 7.2.44
prints both, while explicitly requiring svariṣyati before sya.

Dhūñ 1050010 has the 7.2.44 option before either affix in each of
its separately licensed padas. With iṭ, guṇa gives dho+i and 6.1.78
gives dhav+i. Without iṭ, dho remains before s/t:

\[
 dhū+i+sya\to dho+i+sya\to dhav+i+sya\to dhaviṣya,
 \qquad dhū+sya\to dho+sya\to dhoṣya;
\]

\[
 dhū+i+tās\to dhav+i+tās,\qquad dhū+tās\to dho+tās.
\]

Thus a P 3sg frame gives **dhaviṣyati/dhoṣyati** or
**dhavitā/dhotā** according to the future meaning. An A 2du frame
gives **dhaviṣyethe/dhoṣyethe** or **dhavitāsāthe/dhotāsāthe**.
The latter keeps āthe after the consonant-final tās interface; it does
not borrow the thematic dual operation from lṛṭ.

The short-u or non-ñ dhū entries in other gaṇas are not silently
included. Kāśikā 7.2.44 expressly distinguishes dhū without ñ.

## R4. iṣ versus nṛt: opposite affix-conditioned options

For desire iṣ, 7.2.48's t-initial condition licenses two luṭ branches.
Root-upadhā guṇa gives eṣ. With iṭ, the junction is eṣ+i+tās,
yielding **eṣitā**. Without iṭ, the initial t of tās becomes ṭ after
root ṣ by 8.4.41, yielding **eṣṭā** after the preceding luṭ morphology.
The first iṣ-ṣ belongs to the root; a later sya-ṣ has a different owner.
Sya is s-initial, so 7.2.48 does not apply: **eṣiṣyati** is obligatory
in the present domain. Kāśikā explicitly gives that negative control.
The present-system iccha route is not reused.

Nṛt has the opposite distribution. Rule 7.2.57 licenses iṭ optionally
before s-initial non-sic sya, giving

\[
 nṛt+i+sya\to nart+i+sya\to nartiṣya,
 \qquad nṛt+sya\to nart+sya\to nartsya.
\]

The no-iṭ s is after dental t, not an admitted iṇ/ku trigger for
8.3.59. Kaumudī explicitly gives **nartiṣyati/nartsyati**. Before
tās the option is inapplicable, so 7.2.35 gives nart+i+tās and
**nartitā**. Calling both roots merely “optional iṭ” would erase the
opposite affix guards and generate unlicensed branches.

## R5. vṛt/vṛdh/śṛdh: P prohibition and A retention

Rule 1.3.92 gives optional P before sya. On that branch 7.2.59 removes
the iṭ permission. Guṇa supplies vart/vardh/śardh. The last two
root-final dh sounds become dental t before khar s by **8.4.55**:

\[
 vṛt+sya\to vart+sya\to vartsya,
\]
\[
 vṛdh+sya\to vardh+sya\to vart+sya\to vartsya,
\]
\[
 śṛdh+sya\to śardh+sya\to śart+sya\to śartsya.
\]

On A, 7.2.59's prohibitory condition is absent. The three are seṭ here,
giving vartiṣya/vardhiṣya/śardhiṣya. Their luṭ domain is A only,
with vartit/vardhit/śardhit as H. Examples are **vartitāsvahe**,
**vardhitādhve**, **śardhitāsāthe**. These use PG16's s-final luṭ
ending construction, not a thematic A template.

The source sometimes prints varddhiṣyate with an admitted consonant
doubling. PG17 selects the non-doubling profile, **vardhiṣyate**, while
preserving the source string. The root consonant's ancestry is retained
even where P's final readout makes vṛt and vṛdh indistinguishable.

## R6. syand: an A option that P does not retain

Syand's ū-it licenses 7.2.44's option. On the P sya branch, 7.2.59
overrides it: only syand+sya is admitted. On A, both syand+i+sya
and syand+sya are admitted. The later root d→t before s gives
**syantsyati** in P, and **syandiṣyate/syantsyate** in A.
Kaumudī and Bālamanoramā explicitly preserve this asymmetry; the
Kāśikā's shorter A example list is not a ban on the second A branch.

In both paths, the root's n before a jhal consonant has its 8.3.24
anusvāra and 8.4.58 dental-restoration history. This is not a newly
inserted num. The early root d remains visible to earlier queries even
when a later phonetic view has t.

In luṭ A, both syand+i+tās and syand+tās are admitted. The first
gives H=syandit. For the aniṭ 3sg path, the lawful operations are

\[
 syand+tās+ḍā\to syand+t+ā
 \to syaṃdtā\to syaṃttā\to syanttā\to syantā.
\]

After the ending substitution/marker/ṭi-lopa construction, these are
8.3.24, 8.4.55, 8.4.58 and the selected **8.4.65** deletion of the
first of two savarṇa jhar stops after a hal. The no-deletion double-t
reading is not called false; it is outside this packet's selected
single-t output profile. Kaumudī's **syanditā/syantā** agrees with
that selected reading. The same owned root/t junction is present in
every luṭ ending slot, giving for example **syanditāhe/syantāhe**.

Before Tripādī, the ending-facing aṅga after third-singular ṭi-lopa
contains syand+t, whose upadhā is d, not n. The later deletion cannot
be fed back to make 6.4.24 delete the now-nearer n. Thus this extension
does not silently resolve the larger tāsi-idit dispute by erasing
directional visibility.

## R7. kṛp/kḷp: the same source identity across r→l

The source entry is kṛpū 1010866. Short-upadhā guṇa gives karp.
Later **8.2.18** gives kalp; its commentary also explains the ṛ/ḷ
notation in the sūtra's kḷp reference. Original kṛp membership remains
available to B3/B4 after the visible l appears. This is not a new lexical
root chosen from a similar-looking string.

In P, 7.2.60 forbids iṭ before either future affix:

\[
 kṛp+sya\to karp+sya\to kalp+sya=kalpsya,
\]
\[
 kṛp+tās+ḍā\to karp+t+ā\to kalp+t+ā=kalptā.
\]

In A, ūdit optionality remains, giving **kalpiṣyate/kalpsyate** and
**kalpitāse/kalptāse**. The p before s provides no ṣatva trigger;
the augment i in the other branch does. The A luṭ 2pl alternatives
are **kalpitādhve/kalptādhve**, with the tās-s deletion belonging
to the ending junction and the p+t cluster retained.

## R8. The local nine-slot extension lemma

**Lemma R8.** On each B4 branch, the R2 interface composed with PG16
E1–E6 yields every nine-slot reading in P2, with the declared sound profile.

**Proof.** Each lṛṭ branch supplies a thematic a-final sya constituent;
its root/sya phonetic junction is unchanged by the nine subsequent ending
choices. Each luṭ branch retains the root-facing t while the early ending
rules act on tās's ās part or the following ending. Root-specific guṇa,
ayādi, ṣṭutva, char, r→l and the syand nasal/degemination sequence
therefore encounter the same owned junction in every slot. These facts
are checked by the exhaustive R2 cases, not assumed for arbitrary roots.
PG16 gives each ending path and the late pause/nasal reading. Composition
preserves owners, source evidence and directed visibility, so every
displayed substitution is a lawful final reading. □

The lemma is a restricted equality of final interfaces. It is not a
commutation law for all grammatical events or a licence to discard their
different native residues and histories.
