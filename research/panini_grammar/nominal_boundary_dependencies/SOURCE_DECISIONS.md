# Source decisions

## H1. The go/nau meeting

6.1.79 prescribes the v-final substitutions av and āv for o and au before a y-initial suffix. Under kyac the underlying nominal identities go and nau thus yield gav-y-a and nāv-y-a. The retained comparison with go-yānam and nau-yānam in the Kāśikā shows why the initial y must belong to an affix.

## H2. A consonant cannot supply its own cause of destruction

Kaumudī 1.4.15 expressly invokes the sannipāta-paribhāṣā and prohibits loss of kyac-y for these examples. It gives gavyāṃcakāra/gavyitā and nāvyāṃcakāra/nāvyitā. Bālamanoramā explains that the v obtained through substitution cannot supply the reason to remove the conditioning y. The ordinary stop-final samidh/dṛṣad of PG125 have a different provenance.

## H3. Rai is a source-given negative control

6.1.79 Kāśikā explicitly gives raiyati as the self-wish example in answer to “why v-final?”. Ai is not either of the two specified v-final replacements. The selected factor remains rai-y. A spelling key that groups all ec-final nominals into one replacement would fail this source witness.

## H4. Rājan in two formations

Kāśikā 1.4.15 explicitly lists rājīyati for kyac and rājāyate for kyaṅ. N-final nominal padatva licenses 8.2.7 n-lopa; the following kyac takes the remaining short a to ī under 7.4.33, and kyaṅ lengthens it under 7.4.25. The nominative surface rājā and the embedded nominal rājan are different input representations.

## H5. Visibility of nominal n-lopa

Bālamanoramā 1.4.15 expressly explains why the n removal can be seen in the ī operation, citing 8.2.2. Preserve that local exception to asiddha rather than assuming all later sound rules are visible to earlier morphology. The same source discussion includes a contested pur/gir example. It is outside the selected five profiles.
