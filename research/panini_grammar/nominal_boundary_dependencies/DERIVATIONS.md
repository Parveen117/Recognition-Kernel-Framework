# Written derivations

## H6. Three P self-wish stems and one n-final P stem

The four P stems are gavya, nāvya, raiya and rājīya. In laṭ 3sg write gavyati, nāvyati, raiyati and rājīyati. The corresponding P laṅ forms are agavyat, anāvyat, araiyat and arājīyat, with the augment before the whole derived root.

## H7. The actual agent controls rājāyate

In the agent-standard behavior construction a subject acts like a king. The nominal standard rājan controls the choice of stem but the acting subject controls the ending. Rājāyate (one actor) and rājāyete (two actors) can carry the same incorporated standard. The A 2pl in blessing/aorist retains the inherited y-before-iṭ alternatives.

## H8. Sannipāta does not suppress post-consonant sound reduction

In the P blessing gav-y-yāt and nāv-y-yāt can yield gavyyāt/gavyāt and nāvyyāt/nāvyāt under 8.4.64. On both readings 6.4.50 was unavailable. The two independent y owners arrive first; the sound rule then reduces their pair after v. In raiyyāt and rājīyyāt the first y follows a vowel.

## H9. Periphrastic and future checks across the same boundary

Gavyitā/nāvyitā and gavyāṃcakāra/nāvyāṃcakāra check both a consonantal outer suffix with iṭ and the vowel-initial ām route. The original noun remains go or nau even when only gav/nāv surfaces. The corresponding third singular for rājan in luṭ is rājīyitā on the self-wish P reading and rājāyitā on the behavioral A reading.
