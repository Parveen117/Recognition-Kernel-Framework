# PG759 · Two quotations whose grammatical provenance can be identified

At 4.4.140 Patañjali's grammatical discussion supplies the missing adjective as an actual masculine singular. This is positive evidence for the grammatical quotation itself. The received wording in the named Yajurvedic passages instead identifies Prajāpati as seventeenfold. Keep the location of the claimed Vedic wording Open. In particular the Mādhyandina passage first counts five utterances at 1.5.2.16 and only then counts their syllables at 17. The Taittirīya prose gives each count and names Prajāpati; the Kāṇva passage independently connects those utterances to the inexhaustible element. They are different texts.

The longer middle unit belongs to ye yajāmahe; yaja has two. In the Bhāṣya the subsequent own-sense conclusion gives a new masculine nominative plural, the deities themselves as subject of the wish that they protect. The familiar Atharvan instrumental and Ṛgvedic genitive remain at PG747. No additional material reading of vasavya follows.

At PS 5.11.8 the source construction is reinforced by the parallel ‘from you’ in the second clause and by Lubotsky’s translation. The displayed ātmana before enaṃ represents an ablative ātmanas at the word boundary. Neither loss of the visarga at the junction nor a proposed metrical contraction yields the suffix kha. Lubotsky also reports K. ātmanaṃ, and discusses the separate reading with eva cited by Patañjali at 6.4.141. His preferred restoration of the verb and his objection to Rau’s locative proposal are textual decisions, not new finite or nominal credits for either conjecture.

Under 6.4.141 the regular instrumental mantra form loses initial ā. RV 1.79.6 and the corresponding grammatical control distinguish tmanā from an ordinary ātmanā. The harder quotation in 10.110.10 is directly identified in the Pada. Sāyaṇa treats yā as a replacement for the instrumental singular. In his account the subject acts personally. Nyāsa calls the example a locative singular when explaining occasional extension beyond the regular instrumental. Brahmamuni instead takes a second-case singular, an object, using 7.1.39. All three derive or explain the observed word. They cannot all specify the same case on a single unlabelled analysis. The relation therefore retains three indexed histories at one verse position.
