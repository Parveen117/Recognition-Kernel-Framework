# PG878 · The *bhaikṣuka* life stage and its masculine cases

### The masculine head is not the neuter collective

PG877 kept two lexicographic entries apart. Monier-Williams 152969 has neuter **bhaikṣukam**, a collection of mendicants, while 152970 has masculine **bhaikṣuka**, with *āśrama* understood, for the fourth life stage. The latter is not merely a gender reassignment inferred from the former. The Śabdakalpadruma quotation from the *Mahānirvāṇatantra* actually reads *dvividhāv āśramau proktau gārhasthyo bhaikṣukas tathā*: two āśramas are stated, the householder and the *bhaikṣuka*. The source therefore supplies the masculine nominative singular in the life-stage contrast. [Śabdakalpadruma entry 2467](https://github.com/sanskrit-lexicon/csl-orig/blob/cf53a4240ac83c8f7dfd275533ebd0a0639e6b57/v02/skd/skd.txt#L24373).

The official *Mahānirvāṇatantra* file supplies a second, more explicit pair in chapter 8. Verse 8 contrasts *gṛhasthyaḥ bhaikṣukaś ca* as the two āśramas admitted there; verse 10 begins **bhaikṣukasya āśrame**, placing the masculine genitive under the locative head *āśrama*. Its commentary restates the pair as *gṛhastha-bhaikṣukau dvāv āśramau*. Chapter 14.141 again coordinates nominative singular *gārhasthaḥ* and *bhaikṣukaḥ* under dual *āśramau*. The three passages fix the life-stage sense and the understood head; they do not identify a new person, a collection, or a Buddhist order.

This is a lexicalized masculine relation to an āśrama. No root, preverb or voice is an input to its case realization. The derivational affix is deliberately **Open**. Kāśikā 4.2.45 assigns *añ* to the neuter collection, while the printed Vācaspatyam record names *ṭhañ* for that same collective output. Neither record by itself assigns the masculine life-stage use. The present stage therefore accepts the independently witnessed masculine stem and does not route it through either disputed collective history.

### Syntax ownership

Two constructions are recorded. In the first, dual *āśramau* is classified by two coordinated singular stage-names; the singular words enumerate types rather than agree in number with the dual head. In the second, genitive *bhaikṣukasya* modifies *āśrame* and names the stage whose rule is being stated. The alternative printings *gārhasthya/gṛhasthya/gārhastha* do not alter the stable *bhaikṣuka* member and remain source-labelled rather than normalized into an extra reading.
