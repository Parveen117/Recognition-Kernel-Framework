# PG867 · Jāmbavān’s comparison and Hanumān’s turn to fast

### Three consecutive stanzas and their two commentaries

In the 1887 Nirṇayasāgara Bhaṭṭikāvya with Jayamaṅgalā, printed p. 158, the stanzas are VII.74–76. In K. P. Trivedi’s edition with Mallinātha they are VII.76–78, printed p. 242. The sickly appearance of Jāmbavān, his resolve to follow Aṅgada, and Hanumān taking his turn establish the correspondence in that order.

| Position in the passage | Reading and evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Jāmbavān appears afflicted | Both commentators identify *praskandikām* as a particular disorder, under 3.3.108. Jayamaṅgalā explains the comparison by lack of enthusiasm. Mallinātha describes the speaker as *gadgada*, faltering. *Iva* makes this a comparison. Neither interpretation establishes a medical diagnosis. |
| Jāmbavān condemns the objects of desire | The 1887 verse has **विषयान्**. Jayamaṅgalā explains the objects as forms and the like. Trivedi also prints **विषयान्** and Mallinātha includes sound and the like. The older GRETIL Unicode text at 7.74-2 has **viśayān**. Its header says the verse numbers of the 1887 edition were added mechanically. That statement does not establish letter-for-letter dependence of its verse text. The retroflex printed lemma has masculine accusative plural, while the GRETIL string has the palatal of PG865’s doubt paradigm. This comparison establishes no independent doubt sense for the verse. |
| What the objects resemble | Jayamaṅgalā construes the first member of the comparison as a particular **game**. Mallinātha takes the referents to be **figurines** made of wood, ivory and similar materials, used in play. The verse has a compound with *-prakhya*, inflected in the masculine accusative plural to agree with *viṣayān*. The feminine first member has no independent accusative-plural ending here. Preserve Mallinātha’s citation of Yādava and the sibilant of that cited synonym on their own textual grade. |
| Jāmbavān follows the prince | The feminine accusatives *kārim* and *kriyām* belong to the relative and demonstrative halves of the sentence. The prince is Aṅgada. Both commentators assign *kārim* to kṛ with iñ under 3.3.110 and distinguish *kriyām* with śa. The Kāśikā at 3.3.110 gives both words among a larger set of options in question and answer. The verse demonstrates these two forms of the one action. No extra synonym from the rule is imported into the poem. |
| Hanumān takes his turn | The elder, Jāmbavān, has already sat down. Hanumān says that the fast has reached him in turn. Both commentators connect the accusative *prāyopaveśikām* with that succession. On p. 242 Mallinātha explicitly assigns **ṇvuc** under 3.3.111. The 1887 Jayamaṅgalā points to the same rule by its opening words but prints **ṇvul** in its gloss. The cited sūtra itself reads ṇvuc. The Kāśikā explains that the switch of affix from ṇvul matters for accent. The two literal labels therefore survive even though the segmental word agrees. The facsimiles do not establish who first introduced the l/c divergence. |

### From the named operations to three feminine nouns

For *praskandikā*, take pra + skand in the attested disease name. The *bahulam* in 3.3.108 limits which lexicalizations take ṇvul. It supplies no general disease sense for the simplex or a free alternative for every word. For the game, śāla gives the object of bhañj. The Kāśikā at 3.3.109 lists the name explicitly. At 6.2.74 it specifies ṇvul, the obligatory genitive compound of 2.2.17, and the special initial accent on the first member for an eastern game. The regular feminine forms below use this attested game head. The figurine reading is recorded as a competing interpretation of the simile. Its isolated accent remains open. Kāśikā’s northern-game and turn-taking controls prevent a general transfer of the game accent.

For the turn, prāya specifies the fast and upa + viś the sitting down. In the admitted *paryāya* sense use ṇvuc under 3.3.111 with the guṇa form veś. The optionality the Kāśikā carries into 3.3.111 does not turn every instance of sitting into a named fast. For all three selected segmental formations 7.1.1 supplies the aka shape from vu. Feminine ṭāp and the i before suffixal k under 7.3.44 give the ending -ikā. Keeping the markers of ṇvul and ṇvuc in different histories preserves the distinction needed by accent. The common direct dual in -e is licensed by 7.1.18. The instrumental singular in -ayā contrasts with the dative -āyai, the ablative/genitive -āyāḥ and the locative -āyām. Dual genitive and locative share -ayoḥ, while the single addressee takes -e. Case and number distinguish the addresses even when the word is the same. PG44 and the 6.2.74 register already give *śālabhañjikā* itself. Reserve its nominative singular and reading once across those two records. The prior disease and turn examples under PG44 concern other heads. This gives 24 + 23 + 24 = **71 new nominal positions and 71 new nominal readings**. The three schedules have 72 interfaces. Two syntactic constructions with four interfaces distinguish the agreement and variable comparison object in the first stanza, and the relative object and succession of actors in the next two. The *kārim/kriyām* pair, the isolated compound member and the GRETIL spelling receive no extra inflectional credit.
