# Source decisions

## H1. Behavioral subject and the ṅ of kyaṅ

3.1.11 inherits behavior with the incorporated nominal as the agent-standard. The finite event has its own acting subject and ṅ selects A in the admitted domain. The phrase option outside word formation is uncounted. Kaumudī explains ojas in this lexical use as designating one possessing vigor.

## H2. Kāśikā’s distribution

The Kāśikā gives obligatory s-loss on ojas and apsaras and a choice on payas. It gives both payāyate and payasyate. S-deletion is subsidiary to the affix instruction, so failure to remove s does not prevent kyaṅ. In the ojas/apsaras deletion branches the exposed a lengthens under the derived y environment.

## H3. Mahābhāṣya preserves a competing statement

In the initial discussion the two obligatory stems agree with the Kāśikā sense. The explicitly attributed apara āha section instead limits obligatory loss to apsaras and prescribes ojasyate, with the Vedic quotation explained by register. These are source policies. Selecting one edition/interpretation before evaluation can return a strict subset of the union shown in the ordinary tables.

## H4. Two further Kaumudī witnesses

Kaumudī gives yaśāyate/yaśasyate and vidvāyate/vidvasyate under its broader optional treatment. The latter displays vidvas as the pre-kyaṅ stem in the retained-s path. This packet uses the explicitly printed pair. No strong or weak case form of the associated nominal is blindly substituted into the word.

## H5. Internal s remains

Kāśikā names haṃsāyate and sārasāyate to illustrate the restriction to a final element. Here s is internal to an a-final nominal. Record the retained internal s and the lengthened final vowel in both paths. The s of apsaras is instead terminal in the input and actually disappears in the selected derivation.
