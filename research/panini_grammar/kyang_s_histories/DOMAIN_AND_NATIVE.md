# Domain and specialization

## N1. Carrier

Inputs carry the nominal, semantic role, register and source-policy selection. A history arrow records either loss of the final s, retention of the final s, or inapplicability to an internal s. After that the segmentally realized kyaṅ stem accepts the A ending.

## N2. Filtering the relation

Projection without a source filter produces the stated union. With a specified ojas policy only the supported path is admitted. With payas the standard source itself supplies two paths. These are different predicates on the path basis even when the unfiltered number of outcomes agrees.

## N3. Compatibility

For each admitted policy and input, applying its lexical guard and then specializing yields the same ordinary words as first restricting the ordinary relation by that policy. Multiple citations for one history have idempotent support. Union of sources preserves provenance and does not create a new morphological position. No Vedic finite paradigm is inferred from the cited participle.
