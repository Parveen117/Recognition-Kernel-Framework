## Verbal formation and evidence scope

**3.1.67/83/85:** यक् needs the actual भाव/कर्म-वाचिन् सार्वधातुक context.
The कर्मकर्तृ extension is explicitly supported, rather than lost to general
शप् competition. 83 replaces an existing consonant-preceded श्ना before हि;
its शानज् wording is retained as the source's sandhied affix label. It is not
a new affix that can be inserted after arbitrary roots. 85 preserves attested

turn every unlicensed sequence into a grammatical alternative.

**3.1.134:** the three gaṇas pair with three affixes in order, and Kāśikā
explicitly says the classes are extracted from named nominal formations,
not simply contiguous Dhātupāṭha roots. Naming, prefixes, negation, non-sentient
actor and special senses remain on their own clauses. The पचादि आकृतिगण
and stated wider अच् prescription are not a claim that a finite copied list
is exhaustive. The श्वपच discussion addresses competition with अण्. Lexical
recognition and full realization remain open.

**3.2.78:** a habitual construction differs from an occasional occurrence;
repeated सुप् also prevents treating a bare prefix as its sufficient input.
The expressly admitted prefix combinations and साधु/ब्रह्मन् clauses remain
source-qualified extensions, not silent weakening of the main gate.

**3.2.102:** निष्ठा is prospective in this suffix prescription. Requiring an

## The 3.2.111 source error and temporal distinctions

The Kāśikā body under **3.2.111 अनद्यतने लङ्** prints **लुङ् प्रत्ययो भवति**.
The sūtra text, its अकरोत्/अहरत् examples, Kaumudī and Nyāsa establish लङ्
as the operative selection. Preserve the literal wrong body word in the
archive and adopt लङ् with these declared supporting sources. The entire
Kāśikā field is not discarded: its non-today and mixed-reference discussion
remains relevant.

Nyāsa also clarifies the special public-event example: the event was within
the contemporary speaker's possible field of observation but was not actually
experienced, and was publicly known. This is narrower than allowing लङ् for
every remotely reported historical event. The ordinary 115 prescription
requires past, non-today and परोक्ष conditions; first-person obliviousness and
emphatic denial are separately discussed extensions. The uncertainty of the
speaker's evidence is an input, not inferred from a bare verb form.

**3.2.124:** preserve the non-nominative coreference condition and the proper
pada relation for शतृ/शानच्. Kāśikā's nominative and माङ्-curse examples are
additional stated uses. Its report that some teachers carry व्यवस्थित
विभाषा from 121 is recorded as a source policy, not converted into unrestricted
optional participles in every case.

**3.3.10/13/15/16/18:** purpose requires an action directed toward another
future action; a coincidentally predicted event is insufficient. The explicit
ण्वुल् in 10 does not restore all general agent suffixes by वासरूप. 13 includes
both ordinary future and purpose contexts, while 15's non-today restriction
excludes mixed today/tomorrow reference. At 16, future authority stops; स्पृश्
with उपताप and its other-sense अच् formation retain distinct affix/accent
histories. 18's सिद्ध nominal construal is not itself past tense or a universal
masculine-singular requirement.

**3.4.6/21:** Vedic tense transfer retains धातुसम्बन्ध. Ordinary prior-action
क्त्वा requires the same actor and temporal ordering, but is not restricted to
chains containing exactly two actions. The explicitly listed mouth-opening/
eye-closing constructions are attested extensions rather than a general
permission to ignore ordering.

## Four distinct taddhita authorities

**4.1.76, 82, 83 and 92** are typed **AUTHORITY_OR_DEFAULT_SCOPE**.
Their boundaries are different and must not be flattened into one global
`taddhita=true` rewriting instruction.

- **76:** तद्धित classification extends through chapter5, including admitted
  supplementary affixes. It does not itself emit an additional affix.
- **82:** connected meanings and the first element of the *rule's* semantic

- **92:** an अपत्य semantic relation connects to both earlier and later
  affix prescriptions. It is not a universal fixed-affix rule or a gender/
  number restriction from the wording तस्यापत्यम्.

**82's endpoint requires a consumer-specific account.** Kāśikā's 82 field
