## Correspondence and conflict are not global schedulers

**1.3.10** applies to equal-sized correlated lists in the qualified ordered
instruction. 4.3.94 explicitly confirms the four corresponding pairs.
1.4.90 instead has four meanings and three particles and permits each
particle in the specified meanings. Kāśikā also rejects a mechanical
“equal sizes always pair” test: 4.4.131–132 has two bases and two affix
alternatives, without the intended ordered pairing. K 132 explicitly says its
split defeats यथासंख्य. Its isolated `यत्` label after च is preserved
literally; the preceding prescription labels the affix यल्. No global
numbering or affix repair is inferred from that local inconsistency.

**1.4.2** requires simultaneous conflict at the same addressed opportunity
between rules of equal strength, each having an independent domain elsewhere.
Kāśikā and Nyāsa exclude the general/exception, obligatory/contingent and
internal/external strength comparisons from this tie-breaker's domain.
Kaumudī's example itself invokes पूर्वत्रासिद्धम्; K 8.2.1 expressly says
an invisible later operation cannot use 1.4.2 to defeat its earlier competitor.
This is neither a highest-ID scheduler nor a license to discard exceptions.

Nyāsa preserves an additional interpretive difference: on its जाति account,
a previously competing earlier operation may apply if newly applicable after
the later one (**पुनःप्रसङ्ग**); on its द्रव्य account, the defeated
operation stays defeated in that conflict. These are separate continuation
policies. The bounded contract selects the later operation at the qualifying
conflict and retains the policy for subsequent queries; it does not claim
these accounts have been unified.

## Designation and relation identities

1.2.48's स्त्री denotes a **feminine affix**, not any word whose referent
is feminine. The two उपसर्जन sources also differ:1.2.44 explicitly licenses
its designation **without first-member ordering**. Consequently 2.2.30 cannot
reorder निष्कौशाम्बिः just because its final member is उपसर्जन for shortening.

1.3.1 is a lexical धातु designation;3.1.32 separately designates the complete
सनादि derivative. 1.3.6 assigns इत् to the affix's initial ष्;1.3.9 is its
separate deletion consumer. A visible suffix fragment is not a substitute
for the original prescribed token and its markers.

**1.3.62 transfers the original licensing condition**, not any surface
आत्मनेपद form. The required शित् etc. condition of शद्/मृ is absent in
शिशत्सति/मुमूर्षति. Specific 57–59 rules remain active. Kaumudī offers its
own explanatory continuation of the prohibitive wording; the adopted result
and the explicit condition gate are supported by Kāśikā.

नदी/घि designation depends on the current affix context and the exceptions
in 1.4.4–6. The word must itself be feminine in the required lexical use;
a separately expressed female referent does not suffice. Likewise पद and
भ are addressed views:1.4.17 concerns the **base before** a qualifying
स्वादी affix, whereas 1.4.14 concerns the complete सुप्/तिङ्-ending form.
The सित् exception 1.4.16 must be considered before selecting 18's भ view.

1.4.99's परस्मैपद default applies to actual लकार replacements. Rule 100
assigns आत्मनेपद to its तङ्/आन subset and excludes a merely similar direct
चानश् formation. Affix origin is therefore required even when surfaces coincide.

## Kāraka does not collapse into case or physical ranking

1.4.23 requires relation to the addressed action. A possessive relation to
another participant does not automatically receive its कारक designation.
The case-affix consumers remain separate from the role designation.

At **1.4.24**, ध्रुव means a separation reference. Nyāsa expressly explains
that an already moving source can still serve as that reference. Its damaged
moving-source example remains literal; K's clean रथात् पतितः is a witness
for the written table. Nyāsa also treats ग्रामान्नागच्छति by assigning the
role in the construed action before attaching negation. Negation does not
make the grammatical reference meaningless.

At **1.4.32**, the default giving-recipient interpretation and the stated
action-mediated extensions are distinct. Nyāsa does not turn every destination
of a moving object into सम्प्रदान. Its discussion confines the apparent
कर्म/करण swap to **पशुना रुद्रं यजते**, and also offers a व्यत्यय analysis.
No universal exchange of role labels is adopted.

At **1.4.42**, most effective assistance is part of the expressed construal.
Nyāsa contrasts धनुषा विध्यति with धनुर्विध्यति: the same noun can serve
different roles in different descriptions. This is not an experimentally
measured maximum or a fixed property of the object. Its explanation also
preserves figurative supports under 1.4.45, such as गङ्गायां घोषः.

## Compound authority and the sixteen meanings of 2.1.6

2.1.3's **प्राक्** boundary excludes the named endpoint and permits समास
to coexist with the more specific compound labels. 2.1.22's तत्पुरुष
scope ends before 2.2.23. The default operands under 2.1.4 are सुबन्त;
reported finite combinations have their separately explained source route.
Neither a designation nor an ordering clause manufactures compound eligibility.

Kāśikā supplies these distinctions; the short Kaumudī field presents an
अव्यय योगविभाग. It is not adopted as a new unrestricted fallback bypassing
the explicit semantic gates. In particular **यथा in similarity is excluded
by 2.1.7**, even though other qualifying particles may convey similarity.

2.1.36's तदर्थ compound requires a **material/product** relation in this
source reading. A useful cooking pot alone does not qualify. Its अर्थ branch
has a separate obligatory-compound and gender-following clause. Kaumudī
identifies अश्वघास etc. as genitive compounds, rather than weakening the
material/product condition.

2.1.51 does not admit directional समाहार: its collection branch is numerical.
2.1.52 designates only the number-first compounds in 51's three contexts,
not every phrase starting with a number. 2.2.8 preserves 10/11's genitive
restrictions and the explicit कृत् exception; admitting a genitive case does
not itself admit a compound.

2.2.23's शेष classifies an already licensed compound. 2.2.24 admits more
than two members, but its ordinary co-reference and external-relation gates
remain. Reported nonordinary clauses own their particular deletions; optional
प्रादि and नञ् deletions are not freely attached to any compound. 2.2.29
similarly distinguishes इतरेतरयोग/समाहार from the unfit समुच्चय/आन्वाचय
uses of च. These distinctions remain independent of visible member order.
