## H06. Sya and tāsi on the denominative

Write C for the selected PG122 stem minus its final a, with the
underlying constituent history retained. Lṛṭ/lṛṅ take Ciṣy plus
the PG87 H07–08 thematic endings. Lṛṅ adds initial aṭ before the
entire formation. Luṭ takes Cit plus the finite PG16 tāsi/ending
maps: the shared third-person number replacements retain original
P/A provenance. Lohita P gives **lohitāyiṣyati**, **lohitāyitā**
and **alohitāyiṣyat** in 3sg. These affixes attach outside the
completed nominal formation. No second ḍāc is inserted in a future.

## H07. Blessing and the adjacent y sounds

Use PG88 H06's P blessing tuples after C. The preceding denominative
y and the y of yāsuṭ have separate owners. The latter is outside val,
so 6.1.66 does not delete the former. This gives **lohitāyyāt**
and **lohinīyyāt** on P. The vowel preceding the first y also
prevents importing a post-consonant yam reduction. Use PG67's
comparable adjacent-y control, retaining the different derivational
source of its first y. In A use Ciṣī… with PG88's endings and
H03's optional 2pl. For example śyāma gives **śyāmāyiṣīdhvam**
and **śyāmāyiṣīḍhvam**. ī here belongs to sīyuṭ.

## H08. Luṅ: retain inner y and address outer sic

Initial aṭ precedes C, outer short iṭ and sic. In P 3sg and 2sg
ending-owned īṭ and 8.2.28 sic-lopa give the long-vowel tuple of
PG88. Thus the red-state root gives **alohitāyīt** in P 3sg. In A
2pl, 8.2.25 removes the outer sic-s before dhvam. Denominative y
remains before iṭ: **alohitāyidhvam** and **alohitāyiḍhvam**.
With paṭapaṭā, the same aṭ goes before the whole imitation rather
than before just the final member. Other P/A sic slots use PG88 H09.

## H09. Assemble the two-word perfect

After 6.4.48 form C + ām. The six primary words are lohitāyām,
lohinīyām, paṭapaṭāyām, damadamāyām, maṭamaṭāyām and śyāmāyām.
Use the four kṛ-P/kṛ-A/bhū-P/as-P maps in PG88 H07; the
auxiliary has its own copy, iṭ, ending and grade. PG88 H08 gives
two m/ṃ or ñ/ṃ schedules before consonants and retained m before
vowel-initial as. Thus **lohitāyāñcakre** uses the transferred
A on kṛ whereas **lohitāyāmāsa** has a P auxiliary and can occur
for either eligible ām-base entitlement. Store the two members even
when the word-boundary readout is serialized continuously.
