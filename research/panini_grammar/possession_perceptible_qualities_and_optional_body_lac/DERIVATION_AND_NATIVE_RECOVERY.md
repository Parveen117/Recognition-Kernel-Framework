# PG797 · Derivation and native recovery

Rule 5.2.94 introduces `matup` under two relations: a possessed item belongs to a bearer (`tad asya asti`) or exists in a locus (`tad asmin asti`). The source then restricts ordinary matvarthīya use to abundance, censure, praise, permanent relation, excellence, association or explicit existence. PG797 retains these motives as history fields rather than pretending every occurrence of an object automatically licenses a derivative.

The displayed outputs include ordinary `gomān`, `vṛkṣavān`, `yavamān` and `plakṣavān`. Vasu prints `plakṣamān` despite explaining the `m→v` replacement; both spellings remain source-qualified. The censure examples also differ between the frozen fields, so the Kāśikā and Vasu items stay attributed. When a quality word itself is the base, the vārttika deletes `matup`: `śuklaḥ paṭaḥ`, `kṛṣṇaḥ`, `śvetaḥ`. This lopa is owned by the quality construction, not a free zero affix.

Rule 5.2.95 repeats `matup` for qualities in the rasādi class to block competing possession affixes. PG797 constructs the six Kāśikā-listed perceptible-quality outputs and the three explicit rare alternatives `rūpiṇī`, `rūpika`, `rasika`. The full transmitted roster and Vasu's `rasiko taṭaḥ` against Kāśikā `rasiko naṭaḥ` remain Open rather than silently normalized.

Rule 5.2.96 optionally gives `lac` only after a long-ā stem denoting an animal body member: `cūḍāla/cūḍāvān`, `karṇikāla/karṇikāvān`. The source controls show separate failures of the guard. A lamp's flame is not a living-body member; hand and foot bases do not end in long ā; desire-to-do and desire-to-take are not limbs. Their matup outputs are Accepted while only their `lac` branches are Excluded. The marker `c` protects the affix accent from the stated later svarita operation; no unwritten accented paradigm is credited.
