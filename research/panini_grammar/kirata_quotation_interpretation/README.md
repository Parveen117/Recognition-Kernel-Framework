# PG170 · A quoted future and an impersonal perfect in the same verse

Monty · 11 November 2026

At the end of canto 11, Indra speaks to his son Arjuna and disappears. The quoted *samutsukayitāsi* has a second-person ending. Arjuna is the person who, in Indra’s prediction, will win over the Fortune of his enemies. The locative absolute *prīte pinākini* puts Śiva’s being pleased inside the prediction. The narrator’s report resumes outside the quotation with the utterance and disappearance.

Mallinātha explicitly assigns *kartari luṭ* to the denominative from *utsuka* under the gaṇasūtra *tat karoti*. He assigns *bhāve liṭ* to *tirobabhūve* and identifies the instrumental *tena* as Indra. On that reading the disappearance has an agent but no personal agreement controller. The gerund *uccārya* shares that agent under 3.4.21. Indra as speaker and Arjuna as the second-person agent occupy different entries in the same frame.

The two models keep fixed the content of Indra’s utterance, his act of speaking and his subsequent disappearance. The predicted event occurs in one continuation and is absent in the other. Both satisfy what the narrator asserts in this verse. Later verses can add evidence about the prediction.
