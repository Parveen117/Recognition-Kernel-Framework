# PG243 · Two causative futures, blessings and the spoken word

Monty · 12 November 2026

The *Mādhavīyadhātuvṛtti* prints **niśāmayati vacaḥ** with a long vowel, immediately before an explanation invoking *mit* for an *am*-final root. It also prints **niśāmayati rūpam** and expressly excludes *mit* in seeing. The XML and the printed page agree on both words. By comparison, Puruṣakāra gives short **niśamayati ślokam** for hearing a verse. The vowel of the example with *vacas* and its accompanying grammatical explanation are preserved separately.

| Selected Puruṣakāra construction | Periphrastic future | Simple future | Conditional | Active blessing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Perceiving a form | niśāmayitā | niśāmayiṣyati | nyaśāmayiṣyat | niśāmyāt |
| Hearing a verse | niśamayitā | niśamayiṣyati | nyaśamayiṣyat | niśamyāt |

Each column gives the third singular. The written tables extend the comparison to all nine persons and numbers. *Ay* remains before the future *iṭ*. With the aniṭ active blessing, 6.4.51 removes ṇic, leaving the two root grades visible. The conditional places *aṭ* inside *ni* and gives *nya*. Kṣīrasvāmin independently contrasts divādi **śamayati rogam** and **niśāmayati rūpam**. His curādi entry also gives middle **niśāmayate**. Its root entry and voice carry separate evidence.
