# PG868 · Understanding Sītā’s fate and the prohibition against disbelief

### Two commands in the stanza immediately before the disputed āhadhvam passage

The 1887 Bhaṭṭikāvya with Jayamaṅgalā prints VII.100 on p. 164. Trivedi’s edition with Mallinātha has the corresponding stanza at VII.102 on p. 252. The speaker addresses the group eager for news of Sītā. The first predicate, *bhutsīdhvam*, urges them to find out quickly. The second, *mā ca bhuddhvam*, asks them to refrain from taking his statement as false. GRETIL 7.100 supplies both sequences, with final anusvāra in the connected text.

Jayamaṅgalā paraphrases the first as a command to know. Mallinātha expressly observes that a blessing is unintended in this use, although the word has āśīrliṅ morphology. He makes a similar observation about *kṛṣīḍhvam* in the following request to act for the master's benefit. The already reviewed √kṛ second plural (PG20 BA13) illustrates retroflex ḍh after the ṛ-bearing aṅga under 8.3.78. The dh in the √budh blessing is dental. The two verbs share the hearers while their stem/ending boundaries have different phonological histories.

At the negative predicate the printed *ca* and the apparatus variant *sma* in Trivedi have distinct owners. Mallinātha cites the rule for māṅ followed by sma and takes luṅ through its inclusive ca. Jayamaṅgalā’s reference to sic and its loss also selects luṅ. Māṅ by itself licenses luṅ under 3.3.175. With sma, 3.3.176 additionally admits laṅ. Neither the additional licence nor the occurrence of mā assigns laṅ to the written *bhuddhvam*. The preceding desiderative *bubhutsavaḥ* names the hearers, and the direct object Sītā is supplied with the idea of finding out about her. The explanation that the speaker is telling the truth belongs to the negative clause. These two commentators settle the contrast for this stanza. Its negative verb supplies no mood decision for the **different** āhadhvam variant in the following stanza.

### The lexical entry and its three finite schedules

Use simplex, unprefixed divādi **04.0068 / 1040068**, avagamane, kartari, lexically middle and aniṭ. The identically glossed bhvādi 01.0994 has P and seṭ. The bhvādi 01.1016 bodhane has both padas and seṭ. Their markers, iṭ and voice entitlements stay with their entries. The present-system śyan and the separate causative ṇic each require a different domain.

For the middle blessing, sīyuṭ belongs to liṅ. The u before final dh and the actual jhal-initial suffix admit 1.2.11. Kit blocks guṇa of root u. At the root boundary 8.2.37 aspirates the initial b before s and 8.4.55 devoices the root-final dh before s, giving *bhut-s*. The two source-owned s positions, y retention/loss, internal ṣṭ/ṣṭh and dental dh follow the PG344 ending schedule. The Kāśikā gives the third singular in 1.2.11 and both printed poetic witnesses give the second plural.

For the aorist, cli ordinarily takes sic. Under 3.1.61 this lexical entry additionally admits **ciṇ at actual middle third-singular ta**. The two readings given together in Kāśikā are *abodhi* (root guṇa, ciṇ-i, ending-luk under 6.4.104) and *abuddha* (aniṭ, kit sic, retained root u). The root/voice condition permits that ciṇ even when the person who understands is the grammatical agent. PG34 N11 gives the same printed *abodhi* with a causative passive history. Keep the two paths distinct. No other person or number inherits the option. The Siddhāntakaumudī’s lexical paragraph under the preceding rule 3.1.60 includes *abhutsātām*, explicitly controlling the dual.

On the sic path, 8.2.26 deletes the s between root-final dh and t/th in 3sg and 2sg. The exposed stop triggers 8.2.40 at t/th and 8.4.53 voices the root-final segment to d. Hence *buddha/buddhāḥ*, with initial b. In 2pl 8.2.25 removes sic before dhvam, and the surviving dhv context itself licenses 8.2.37, giving initial bh and the cluster ddh. Kāśikā 8.2.37 gives *abhuddhvam* here. In the other six slots sic remains, with initial bh and root-final t before s. The aṭ prefix on ordinary past requests and its suppression in māṅ contexts (6.4.71/74) affect neither that internal calculation nor the ciṇ restriction. The prohibitive column shows the inflected word after separately supplied mā. Its first persons express a prohibition including the speaker. Only the second plural is identified here as a token of this stanza.
