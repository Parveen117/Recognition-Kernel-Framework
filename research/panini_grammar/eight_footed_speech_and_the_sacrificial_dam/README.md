# PG985 · Eight-footed speech and the sacrificial dam

**Monty · 26 November 2026.** PG984 admits two explanations for the length in *aṣṭāpadī*. Four Vedic passages now identify particular uses of the feminine. Neither a verse nor its gloss chooses between the two derivations of the first member. The following registers use unaccented citation forms for counting. The saṃhitā and padapāṭha in the linked Śākala witnesses retain their own accent.

## Speech in two Śākala verses

In Ṛgveda 1.164.41 the feminine nominative *aṣṭāpadī* belongs to the series predicated of Gaurī. Sāyaṇa identifies speech in the middle region and explains the eight by the directions and intermediate directions. He also records a *kecit* analysis in terms of distinctions of speech, with address among the distinctions. Preserve both explanations. *Babhūvuṣī* agrees in the feminine and is a perfect participle. Its ending does not open a finite request. [Śākala 1.164.41 and commentaries](https://vishvasa.github.io/vedAH_Rk/shAkalam/saMhitA/sarva-prastutiH/01/164/)

In 8.76.12 the accusative *vācam* gives *aṣṭāpadīm* an explicit noun. The speaker supplies *aham*. Under the verbal attachment of separated *pari*, the request is measuring out, pari + √mā, actor, middle liṭ, first singular. The word is *mame*. The reduplicated perfect has the same singular surface under the third-person ending. PG249 already exhibits that syncretism for its **two-prefix** requests. Here *tanvam* intervenes between *pari* and the verb. Sāyaṇa's first-person paraphrase supports the speaker analysis. The VedaWeb panel records √mā-1, middle perfect, **third singular**. Keep its analysis attached to the panel. Jamison–Brereton also discuss taking *pari* with the ablative *indrāt*. That alternative changes the attachment and therefore the preverb key. The one credited reading selects the verbal attachment and *aham* as subject. The exact force of the ablative and *tanvam* remains disputed. [Śākala 8.76.12 with padapāṭha, VedaWeb, Sāyaṇa and translation notes](https://vishvasa.github.io/vedAH_Rk/shAkalam/saMhitA/sarva-prastutiH/08/076/)

## Counting the feet in the sacrificial formula

At Vājasaneyi Saṃhitā, Mādhyandina 8.30, Uvaṭa relates the eight to the feet of the embryo. Mahīdhara explicitly combines the dam's feet with those of her embryo and identifies the mother as the *anūbandhyā*. His construction takes the plural *bhuvanā* as controller of *anu prathantām*. The verbal history is anu + √prath in the becoming-known sense, thematic middle imperative, third plural, *pratha-ntām*. Madhavīya entry 468 gives the simple middle *prathate* alongside causative *prathayati*. Entry 1150 separately prints *prāthayati*. Mahīdhara's explanatory causative does not insert ṇic into the quoted imperative. The displayed Kāṇva commentary quotes *anu prayantām*. This establishes a reading in that commentary. Its independent saṃhitā witness and verbal analysis remain open. Dayānanda's interpretation of the Mādhyandina passage invokes speech. Preserve it alongside the sacrificial exegesis. [VS 8.30 and attributed commentary panels](https://vishvasa.github.io/vedAH_yajuH/vAjasaneyam/mAdhyandinam/saMhitA/sarva-prastutiH/08/)

Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa, Mādhyandina 4.5.2.1, describes examining the *vaśā* for an embryo. At 4.5.2.12 it quotes the formula and resumes in prose with *prathayati* and *enām*, a singular causative predicate with an expressed patient. Its closing comparison supplies an instrumental pair. In *aṣṭāpadyeṣṭvā*, first recover *aṣṭāpadyā + iṣṭvā* (6.1.87). The contrasting form in *yad u ca anaṣṭāpadyā* has privative nañ on the nominal compound. Before the vowel-initial member, 6.3.73–74 yield *an-aṣṭāpadī*. Feminine singular instrumental formation gives *anaṣṭāpadyā* with *y* before the vocalic ending. The negation describes the animal used in the sacrifice. The action of sacrificing remains common to the comparison. [ŚBM IV, 4.5.2.1 and 4.5.2.12](https://gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de/gretil/1_sanskr/1_veda/2_bra/satapath/sb_04_u.htm)
