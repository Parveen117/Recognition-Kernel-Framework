# The seventh Uṇādi suffix and the actor’s pacādi derivation

## The same place in the list has three source histories

Böhtlingk **1844**, printed p. 54, line after **matsyaḥ** in IV.107, gives **षण्ठः**, **ṣaṇṭhaḥ**. The root series in that heading has **ṣami**, and the joined suffix line has **ṭha** at the seventh address. The rounded printed aspiration graph here is ठ, without the body of ढ. This spelling has the retroflex initial familiar from the 4.104 Kaumudī account and the unvoiced stop of this print. The isolated form is positively admitted in the edition register. No gloss follows it before the separate instruction for **naṭa**, so the exact meaning of the 1844 target remains Open. It contributes no new case table.

Aufrecht’s **1859** edition of Ujjvaladatta, p. 112, gives the palatal **śamu** at the seventh address. The separated suffix roster gives **ṭha**. The actual line is **शंठोऽकृतदारः** in nasal orthography, where **akṛtadāra** expressly supplies the unmarried-person sense. It precedes the different **ḍit** instruction for the eighth suffix. Monier-Williams gives **śaṇṭha** with the class nasal and directly points to the commentary at IV.104, reporting the unmarried meaning and another reported meaning. Only the first of these is admitted to this written case request. The paired historical notices in the lexicon do not replace the immediate printed gloss.

In the 1859 history the **04.0098 śamu upaśame** root address supplies **śam** after its teaching vowel is removed. At the cited derivational slot, **ṭha** has sounded **ṭh + a**, as required by the actual print and the independent lexical reading. This is a localized source realization. It supplies no general exception to the treatment of initial **ṭu** as an it in **1.3.7**. Applying the taddhita ika substitution of 7.3.50 would use a different affix context. Under **8.3.24** the non-pada-final m of **śam** becomes anusvāra before the jhal stop; under **8.4.58** the following ṭh supplies its class nasal ṇ. The written output is **śaṇṭha**. The original anusvāra graph remains on its edition-specific history. The two graphs are representations of a local nasal contact, not two new inflectional alternatives. On the 1844 source the corresponding internal sound work is **ṣam + ṭha → ṣaṃṭha → ṣaṇṭha**, with the retroflex initial visible in the result. The **01.0963 ṣam** root identity is preserved next to the separately printed palatal root. The exact technical authority for its initial retention in the 1844 source remains Open.

Tattva’s known **4.104** account keeps root-initial ṣ under **bāhulaka** and pronounces its affix **ḍha**. Its explicit explanation of initial ḍh’s sound belongs to that history. Hence **ṣaṇḍha** differs from the old print spelling in both the voiced stop and the identity of the particular source policy. The **1.99** account has **śam + ḍha → śaṇḍha** in its own register. PG205 already writes both of these voiced fibres and the distinct unaspirated heads. They receive zero repeat credit. The difference between the old editions and the digital suffix list cannot move an attested 4.104 operand to the **4.105** extension. The four primary field assignments remain Open at **99/103**.

The 1859 contact with **akṛtadāraḥ** anchors the nominative form. On the grammatical readout **śaṇṭhaḥ** plus an initial short a, **8.2.66** supplies ru, **6.1.113** supplies the vowel replacement, **6.1.87** supplies o, and **6.1.109** preserves o across the loss of the following short a. The ordinary masculine a-stem endings then yield 24 case positions and 25 readings, counting the two inherited ablative singular pause sounds. ## The same masculine actor with a new affix authority and a neuter fibre

PG713 already develops the masculine **naṭa** from **nam + ḍaṭa** with actual **6.4.143** ṭi-lopa. The positive alternative from **naṭ + ac** has a separate source gate. The **Vyutpattiratnākara**, ad 329, note 9 (2003 print, p. 147), gives the acting/dancing predicate, names the **bhvādi parasmaipada seṭ** base, and explicitly says **pacādy ac**. This selects the third domain of Pāṇini **3.1.134** in that source's explanation. Its bare **naṭ nartane** has no preverb or causative layer. The lexical entry supplies the case result. Active **naṭati** is cited as an explanatory predicate. In the root derivative ṭ belongs to **naṭ**, and ac contributes sounded a. If the representative underlying record is taught as **ṇaṭa**, remove the final nasalized teaching vowel under **1.3.2**, retain root ṭ, and realize the root-initial n by **6.1.65**. For the affix itself remove final c under **1.3.3** and **1.3.9**. Thus at this source address **naṭ + a → naṭa** without the separate deletion of nam’s ending. No newly constructed finite key accompanies the source predicate.

Keep the root IDs separate. The Dhātupāṭha has entries **01.0347** and **01.0890** at different positions. The **Kṛdantarūpamālā** discusses the *ghaṭādi* entry in nṛtta/nṛtya, reports Kṣīrasvāmin’s bending reading and preserves other senses and causative conditions. The bare theatre noun has positive support from **naṭ nartane**, and these texts do not uniquely identify one numerical bhvādi entry for that citation. The noun history carries the exact attested root teaching/gloss and leaves that finer identity on an Open cut. Causative forms and the alternative movement and bending senses retain separate obligations. The feminine **naṭī** placement in gaurādi alongside ac is supported by the comparative source, and the existing PG212 case fibre is preserved.

Vyutpattiratnākara's **puṃ/klī** label is corroborated by Hemacandra’s own Abhidhānacintāmaṇi commentary at the actor lemma (viewer leaf 135, ad 3.4.242 §9). The gloss there likewise supplies **naṭati nṛtyati** and gives **puṃklībaliṅga**. This supplies two grammatical genders for the named lexical actor.
