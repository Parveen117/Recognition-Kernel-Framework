# PG195 · Three directly evidenced affixes and the differing 4.105 lists

Monty · 11 November 2026

Three earlier morphological assignments now have direct evidence at the level of a specific rule and affix.

| Primary rule and affix | Named example | Printed evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 4.98, da | *abda* ‘year’ from *ad*, ‘eat’ | Śvetavanavāsin 4.107, p. 162, names da and discusses accent. On p. 163 he separately gives dan for *kunda*. |
| 4.105, tvan | *netvaḥ* from *nī* | Nārāyaṇa 4.116, p. 103, specifies tvan and guṇa. The earlier *dhyātva* example retains its own inferred assignment. |
| 4.105, knin | *lūniḥ* from *lū* | Śvetavanavāsin lists knin, pairs the root with the short suffix citation *kniḥ*, and gives the word in the following vṛtti, pp. 165–166. The rice gloss comes from Ujjvaladatta. |

The register now has **98 directly exemplified fields, one inference and four open fields out of 103**. The inference is *tnaṇ* on *hautna* ‘sacrificer’. All four open fields belong to primary 4.105.

A difference between editions explains why four technical labels are difficult to align. The primary ordered list has nine fields. Each of the two newly examined printed recensions lists ten under its corresponding precursor. Śvetavanavāsin includes *sya* and uses it on the fish word in the precursor. That example already belonged to the preceding rule. His alternative commentary on the **extension** expressly gives short *aṭac* on *taṭa* ‘bank’, *vaṭa* ‘banyan’ and *avaṭa* ‘well’. It supplies positive extension evidence for its own short vowel. The primary list spells the ninth field *āṭac*.

The other open primary fields are *sya, ḍha* and *ḍaṭ*. The table preserves the spelling of every affix in all three ordered lists. A matching ordinal or a similarly sounding word is insufficient for transferring a different marker.

Zero new finite positions. Cumulative **41,333**.
