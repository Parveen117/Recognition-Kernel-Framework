# PG900 · Feminine *tvat* under the *sarit* address pattern

PG899 correctly stopped at an internally inconsistent Śabdamañjarī line:
`tvat` was called ī-final but also assigned to consonant-final `sarit`. A
separate current exposition at Ashtadhyayi.com now states the relevant choice
without that collision: `tvat` is t-final, means “different”, and follows
`sarit` in the feminine. The independently printed `sarit` address row then
supplies `tvat / tvatau / tvataḥ` for feminine singular, dual and plural.

These are three new gender-number-case positions and three readings. Their
surface strings equal the PG899 masculine strings, but gender and source
history are different, so no masculine key is recredited. There is no finite
addition and no globally new surface spelling.

The decision is narrow. It resolves the selected feminine vocative pattern;
it does not establish a direct literary address, accent, the complete
non-vocative feminine paradigm, or the textual history that produced the old
ī-final label. Those obligations remain Open.
