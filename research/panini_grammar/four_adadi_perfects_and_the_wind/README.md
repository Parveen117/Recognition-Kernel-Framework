# PG263 · Four complete adādi histories and the wind in two settings

Monty · 12 November 2026

Maitreya gives all four bare third-singular perfects: **yayau**, **vavau**, **dadrau** and **papsau**. In the final two, the copy retains the first consonant of the initial cluster. Each root also has two second-singular perfect readings, as in **yayātha** and **yayitha**. The *sic* aorists retain the long root vowel: *ayāsīt, avāsīt, adrāsīt, apsāsīt*.

| Third singular after *pari + ni* | Perfect | Aorist |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *yā* | pariṇiyayau | pariṇyayāsīt |
| *vā* | pariṇivavau | pariṇyavāsīt |
| *drā* | pariṇidadrau | pariṇyadrāsīt |
| *psā* | pariṇipapsau | pariṇyapsāsīt |

Viṣṇupurāṇa 1.9.38 gives a bare **yayau** for going to the northern shore of the milk ocean. The Bhāgavata twice uses **vavau vāyuḥ**, ‘the wind blew’. It is harsh to the touch at 3.17.5 and pleasant, carrying fragrance, at 10.3.4. Both words instantiate the same *vā* perfect. The descriptions of the wind carry the difference in meaning; the verb keeps the same person and number.

The last 144 positions have 152 readings. Across PG261–263 the four roots complete **720 of 720** positions with their two preverb pairs. The three literary forms are evidence for the unprefixed components.
