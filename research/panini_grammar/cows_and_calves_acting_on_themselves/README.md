# PG981 · When two calves seek their own release

The Benares Mādhavīya places two different constructions only three printed pages apart. On p. 344 the calf wants release. On p. 347 the cow undergoes confinement of its own accord. Number agreement can change in both constructions, but the apparent extra consonants in their singular verbs have different owners. ## The dental in the cow's present

The Benares print under *karmakartari* gives रुद्ध्यते and then अरुद्ध with the cow. The later Shastri print on p. 495 gives the doubled dental in the same present. This locates PG974's digital spelling in print. There is also a grammatical account of the spelling. After root vowel u, dh is a *yar* followed by consonant y. Duplication under 8.4.47 gives dh-dh-y. Before the second dh, 8.4.53 gives d for the first dh. The Nyāsa works this very consonant sequence in its explanation of दद्ध्यत्र. Without duplication, dh-y stands. The two outputs share one yaK stem. The commentary's other proposal about doubling a following semivowel has a different target and supplies no manuscript reading of this cow example. Neither print reveals whether an editor consciously applied the doubling rule. ## The calf's desire belongs to san

The preceding *vatsaṃ* in entry 1031 is accusative. It goes with the transitive desire to release a calf. The following nominative *vatsaḥ* and *svayam eva* assign the desire for its own release to the calf. Both frames have √muc, *mokṣaṇe*, and real aniṭ san. In the second frame 7.4.57 allows guṇa despite the usual kit environment. With guṇa, 7.4.58 removes the reduplicative syllable, giving mokṣa-. With retained u the syllable mu survives in mumukṣa-. The Kāśikā gives both singulars and the transitive control. Mādhava explicitly invokes the restriction on transferred yaK/ciṇ for a san stem. Compare also the Kaumudī's cited vārttika under 3.1.63. Thus the two middle endings attach to the two licensed desiderative stems. An independently selected guṇa and copy deletion would incorrectly admit two cross-combinations. The preceding future *mokṣyate* has another suffix.
