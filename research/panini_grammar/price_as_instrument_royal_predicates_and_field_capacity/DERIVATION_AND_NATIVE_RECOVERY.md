# PG753 · The same title in three requests

## Payment, event and a particular sacrifice

At 5.1.37 thirteen already licensed suffixes have their supporting case and meaning specified. The price must be what buys the article. An acting buyer and a flesh-and-blood hand have independent instrumental analyses but do not license this purchase derivative. For an unqualified capacity word the explicit blocked instrumental examples are prasthābhyām and prasthaiḥ. A specified numeral can encode a nonsingular price. A transaction with mung beans supplies an independently recoverable plural as payment and the derivative maudgikam. The pinned digital Kāśikā has modgikam with o; the Sanskrit Documents and Wikisource Kāśikā both explicitly display au, as expected under the ṭhak vṛddhi. Retain the digital discrepancy as an observation without adding a licensed short-vowel reading. The singular adjective describes the article and does not impose singularity on the purchase units. PG751's paid niṣka and hundred remain its existing predicates.

At 38 a genitive is the expected acquisition; an association or a traditionally interpreted event is treated as its occasion. The association with a wealthy man takes a masculine predicate whereas the movement of the right eye is neuter. The medical-style additions for vāta, pitta, śleṣman and their conjunction rest on an explicit supplement and the two readings of pacification or provocation. At 39 go has its own entry. A two-vowel base admits yat unless numeral, measure or one of the eight aśvādi items. Aśva returns to ṭhak, a number returns to its numeral affix, and a capacity is handled by its measure affix. Brahmavarcas has more than two vowels and requires the supplementary teacher-association example. Rule 40 adds cha to the earlier yat for putra. In the Rāmāyaṇa the offering is the object, so putrīyām is F ACCsg. The Sanskrit commentator explicitly cites putrāc cha ca and explains the rite as the occasion of obtaining a son. The accusative recurs in the next verse. Different sarga numbering and verse repetition are observations, not new positions.

## Three pairs with two spellings

At 41 the ordered pair is sarvabhūmi + aṇ and pṛthivī + añ. Sarvabhūmi belongs to anuśatikādi, so 7.3.20 strengthens both members. The earth base gives pārthiva. At 42 the repeated genitive tasya cuts off the old occasion predicate and installs īśvara. A person is the ruler of a domain, not the sign that a ruler might acquire it. At 43 tatra supplies a locative and vidita a new predicate. The same M NOMsg may be said of someone known in that domain, independent of any position of authority. This yields six inputs; each of the two output strings has three histories.

Aitareya Brāhmaṇa 8.15.1,3 expresses the wish of the officiant and of the anointed man. The predicative adjective is M NOMsg in both utterances, describing sole rule over land reaching the sea. This attests one sovereign case. For the second, GRETIL Raghuvaṃśa 11.3 has pārthivas tayor with a singular verb. Mallinātha expressly glosses it as earth-lord. The other site's verse display has an anomalous long vowel before s; agreement, GRETIL and the commentary support the singular. By contrast, in Ṛgveda 1.154.1 pārthivāni modifies the neuter plural regions that Viṣṇu measures. Sāyaṇa explicitly glosses earth-relatedness. That token is outside all three selected predicates; it cannot corroborate Vedic rulership or worldwide fame. At 44 the locative of loka or sarvaloka selects ṭhañ. The second has both-member vṛddhi; use the restricted known-in meaning, not every historical use of laukika.

## How much seed the land takes

Vāpa at 45 names the sowing place; Nyāsa gives the locative sense of ghañ, connected with 3.3.121 in the displayed commentary. The amount is in the genitive and the derivative describes the land where that much seed is sown. A prastha-price purchase and a prastha field can have the same predicate form but have different input relations and case supports. Droṇa retains the niṣkādi selection and uncompounded khārī needs the 33 supplement. At 46 the pātra is the seed measure, and the prescribed affix is ṣṭhan with a ṣ marker. Consequently the field takes N NOMsg pātrikam, but the feminine field-portion takes ṅīṣ at 4.1.41, pātrikī. The price adjective kaṃsikī at PG752 reached the same long feminine vowel with a different outer affix and a different feminine marker.
