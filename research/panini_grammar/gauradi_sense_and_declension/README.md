# PG212 · Feminine suffix histories across thirteen sense-specific declensions

Monty · 12 November 2026

Thirteen source-qualified feminine stems or senses now have all **24 case–number positions**, giving **312 written positions**. They represent eleven differently spelled heads. Every row names its base sense, so an instrument in the nominative, for example, can be distinguished from a locus in the nominative even when the words coincide.

| Input | Selected output or contrast |
| --- | --- |
| Instrument of searching and place of searching | Both give *eṣaṇī*. The Nyāsa distinguishes ṅīṣ on the instrument from ṅīp on the locus. Both can take all eight outer cases. |
| The *sumaṅgala* naming condition | *Sumaṅgalī* is licensed. The Nyāsa also gives ordinary *sumaṅgalā*. Monier-Williams separately records proper names in *-ā*, including a woman in the *Kathāsaritsāgara*. |
| Stellar and other readings | The stellar *rohiṇī* has the control *rohitā*. Beside stellar *revatī*, the Nyāsa gives nonstellar *revatā* on one analysis and *revatī* on the possessive *matup* analysis. |
| *Kaṭa* | *Kaṭī* for the hip and *kaṭā* in the Nyāsa’s control. |
| Two words for performers | *Naṭī* starts from the listed *naṭa*. In *nartakī* the occupational suffix *ṣvun* supplies the marker licensing the feminine. Each also has an independent dictionary and full-table comparison. |

Two Ṛgvedic examples preserve a form absent from the ordinary Classical nominative schedule. The Zürich/VedaWeb edition labels **sumaṅgalī́ḥ** as feminine nominative singular at **1.113.12c**, about Dawn, and **10.85.33a**, about a bride. In the wedding verse its word boundary appears as *sumaṅgalīr iyam*. The following *iyam* and *vadhūḥ* are both feminine nominative singular. The final sound and the descriptive use are both recorded. These tokens cannot simply be fed back as Classical proper-name paradigms.

The source archive includes complete independent head-specific comparisons for *naṭī*, *nartakī* and *rohiṇī* (72 cells). The remaining tables apply the reviewed regular ī- or ā-stem inflection to their selected lexical heads. The noun totals here have their own accounting.
