**838** tokens match their written form after accent and transliteration are normalized for comparison. In **63** more, the word for direct address has final *r* where the PG188 pause form has visarga. The remaining **132** consist of 67 forms of the Vedic *nápat-* entry, 64 direct duals with *-ā* corresponding to *-au*, and one *pitróḥ* annotated as ablative dual. The Kāśikā at 7.1.39 gives *yantārā* beside *yantārau* as an explicit Vedic dual comparison.

At RV 10.31.10c Sāyaṇa interprets the two parents as the fire sticks from which Agni appears. This supplies the origin relation for *pitróḥ*. The gloss’s express citation of a Vedic ending substitution occurs with a different word in the verse, *śamyām*. At 10.60.6a the Zürich entry groups *nádbhyaḥ* with *nápat-*; Sāyaṇa explains the beneficiaries as Agastya’s kin and others who bring him joy. Both decisions retain the words as transmitted.

The vocative *jāmātar* in 8.26.21 and the accusative *jā́mātaram* in 8.26.22 have the same genitive dependent, Tvaṣṭṛ. The source lemma *pótar-* contributes four priest tokens. The final-accented *potā́* ‘one able to purify’ belongs to another identifier. Likewise, the masculine ‘measurer’ has its own entry beside the feminine ‘mother’. Two adverbial rows sharing the father identifier and three rows with another annotated gender are retained outside the match count.
