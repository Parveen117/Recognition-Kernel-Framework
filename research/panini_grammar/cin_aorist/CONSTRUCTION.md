# PG34 — Ciṇ's i survives; ta and causal ṇic have separate lopa

Author: Monty. 4 November 2026.

## C1. Select cli, then actual ciṇ

For the admitted luṅ request, 3.1.43 supplies cli. The specific
bhāva/karma ta condition in 3.1.66 selects ciṇ instead of the general
sic of 3.1.44. This is an affix substitution with a stored selector,
not removal of s from a completed sic word. Ciṇ is ārdhadhātuka by
3.4.114; the separately selected ta is tiṅ/sārvadhātuka by 3.4.113.

Ciṇ's initial c and final ṇ are markers (1.3.7, 1.3.3), removed by
1.3.9 while their grammatical properties are retained. Its pronounced
**i is the affix itself**. It is neither enunciative sic-i, an iṭ
augment, nor A1sg's original ending. Its ṇit property licenses the
stated strengthening; the following apit ta does not make ciṇ kit.

Because ciṇ begins in actual i after marker treatment, the val-initial
condition of 7.2.35 does not admit an ordinary iṭ before it. Rule
6.4.62 does not name actual ciṇ among its targets and supplies no
ciṇvat iṭ here. Thus there is no optional extra i and no O/C split.

## C2. Ending-luk is not deletion of the affix ciṇ

Rule **6.4.104** gives luk of the affix *following* ciṇ: the selected
ta. The Sanskrit genitive/ablative distinction matters: the rule does
not say to delete ciṇ itself. Kāśikā and Bālamanoramā explicitly
identify that following target. Hence

\[
a|Root'|i_{ciṇ}|ta_{tiṅ}\ \longrightarrow\ a|Root'|i_{ciṇ}|\varnothing_{ta}.
\]

1.1.60 names the non-realization as lopa; 1.1.61 distinguishes the
specified whole-affix luk from other lopa labels. Store the original ta,
its 3sg controller certificate and the luk event. For inherited pada
designation, 1.1.62 expressly allows the lopped tiṅ to remain relevant
to 1.4.14. This does not license every aṅga operation from a missing
ending: 1.1.63 remains its separately restricted control.

No present/future ta→te or final i→e conversion is introduced. The
final i is ciṇ's vowel after the ending has disappeared. Selecting an
ending by looking backward from that final i would produce a false 1sg.

## C3. Basic root strengthening and contacts

Vowel-final bhū/kṛ/hṛ/nī/ci receive the 7.2.115 operation, with the
inherited guṇa/vṛddhi and r-accompaniment scheduling as applicable.
Ayādi (6.1.78) before affix i gives bhāv/nāy/cāy; kṛ/hṛ give kār/hār.
Original ā-final sthā/dā/pā receive **yuk by 7.3.33**, giving sthāy/
dāy/pāy. These are different y owners from nāy/cāy's āy substitute.

Bhū's bhāv-i path is not PG32's bhū-vuk-ant path. The vowel immediately
following the root here is cli's substitute ciṇ, not a vowel-initial
luṅ tiṅ substitute. The original ending is ta and is then lopped;
there is no ant/am junction licensing PG32's 6.4.88 operation. The v in
abhāvi comes from bhau+i→bhāv+i. Kāśikā's affix-relative 6.4.88 wording
and Kaumudī's direct abhāvi witness fix this bounded interpretation.
The differing general vuk-priority explanations are retained in S, not
flattened into a rule that every vowel anywhere in a luṅ word gets vuk.

Han retains n because 7.3.32 excludes ciṇ from its n→t operation;
7.3.54 changes h→gh and 7.2.116 gives ghān. Result: **aghāni**.
No chapter-2 vadh aorist replacement is transported from another context.

Grah and pac have eligible a-upadhā and give grāh/pāc (7.2.116):
**agrāhi, apāci**. Grah's final i is actual ciṇ, so the ordinary
iṭ-lengthening 7.2.37 does not apply. Dṛś has an i-initial affix:
6.1.58's jhal guard fails, and 7.3.86 gives darś, hence **adarśi**.
Nṛt likewise gives **anarti** by upadhā-guṇa; edh remains edh.

## C4. Original accent class matters even in unaccented output

Basic śam is udātta-upadeśa and m-final. Rule **7.3.34** blocks the
otherwise available 7.2.116 upadhā-vṛddhi before ciṇ, giving **aśami**.
It is not one of that rule's excluded ācam/other source exceptions.
Basic gam is explicitly **anudātta-upadeśa** in its lexical record,
so this prohibition does not apply: 7.2.116 gives **agāmi**.

This distinction queries original teaching evidence, not the accent
of a later derived word and not the absence of accent marks in our IAST.
Kāśikā/Nyāsa explain the original-stage query; Kaumudī/Bālamanoramā
directly contrast aśami and agāmi. Gam's lexical mit status alone does
not make a basic ciṇ form optionally short: 6.4.93 requires its ṇic
environment, present only in the admitted causal construction below.

## C5. Labh: optional num changes the vṛddhi guard

Rule **7.1.64** supplies labh's general num in the relevant vowel-initial
affix domain, excluding śap/liṭ. Rule **7.1.69** makes num optional
before actual ciṇ in this unprefixed frame. Its prefix-qualified
compulsory branch is recorded as a scope control, not added here.

On the selected num branch, the augment goes after the last vowel under
1.1.47: labh→la-n-bh. The num operation remains applicable whether or
not strengthening was first contemplated, while inserting it destroys
the a-upadhā condition. Take that stable num operation first on this
branch; the penultimate sound is now n, so 7.2.116 has no a target.
The late anusvāra/parasavarṇa steps (8.3.24/8.4.58) give lambh-i:
**alambhi**. Without num, labh retains a-upadhā and gives **alābhi**.

Kāśikā/Nyāsa 7.1.69 directly supply the pair. There is no Cartesian
product {num,no-num}×{ā,a}: *alāmbhi and *alabhi are not additional
branches of this selected construction. The ordering argument here is
local to the source-witnessed pair, not a universal scheduling algorithm.

## C6. Actual ciṇ after a causal formation

Start with the inherited causal R_root|i_ṇic. Actual ciṇ is **iṭ-free**
even though its own vowel is i. Thus 6.4.51's aniṭ condition holds
directly, unlike PG33's query that hides an i_cv augment. The two i
occurrences have different owners:

\[
R_{root}|i_{ṇic}|i_{ciṇ}|ta
\longrightarrow R_{root}|\varnothing_{ṇic}|i_{ciṇ}|\varnothing_{ta}.
\]

Ṇic-lopa and ta-luk are different rules and targets. Neither deletes
ciṇ-i. Rule 6.4.51 is the source-stated exception to the competing
vowel operations on ṇic; no retained-ay branch is supplied here.
Retain the previous root strengthening, puk/yuk and 1.1.57 boundary
queries from PG18 C3. Deleted ṇic does not expose a new bare root for
an unrestricted second strengthening. Thus causal sthā gives **asthāpi**,
not basic sthā's **asthāyi**, while causal budh retains **abodhi**.

In causal gam/śam, the inherited mit relation and actual ciṇ activate
6.4.93: **agami/agāmi**, **aśami/aśāmi**. Causal śam's optional
long form comes from this dedicated length rule, not from discarding
the basic 7.3.34 source distinction. Other admitted causal roots have
one form under the inherited policy. There is no ordinary/ciṇvat pair.

## C7. Initial augment and complete local reading

Ordinary luṅ has aṭ (6.4.71); basic edh instead has āṭ (6.4.72),
and 6.1.90 gives **aidhi**. All other admitted factors are consonant-
initial and take aṭ. Initial augmentation, ciṇ-i and any root-owned
num/puk/yuk remain separately addressed constituents.

D exhausts the admitted original ta inputs. C1–C6 exhaust their
substitution, root and lopa branches, and C7 supplies their sole initial
augment route. The register therefore contains exactly the 30 declared
positions and 33 readings. This is written bounded exhaustion, without
an executable grammar run or independent corpus attestation.
