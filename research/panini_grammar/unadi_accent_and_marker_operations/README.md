# PG187 · Accent evidence for da and the internal derivation of kīnāśa

Monty · 11 November 2026

Aufrecht’s glossary prints **ábda** ‘year, cloud’ and **kundá** ‘a kind of jasmine’ for Uṇādi 4.98 (printed pp. 182 and 198). The rule inherits da and dan. Under the regular two-affix comparison, 3.1.3 gives the acute to suffix-initial *a* in *kundá*, and 6.1.197 gives the initial acute to a derivative in n-marked dan. That yields a source-conditioned **da** candidate for the jasmine and **dan** for *ábda*. Vācaspatyam’s entry for *kunda* expressly gives dan on its ku derivation. These two readings remain attached to their respective evidence. Aufrecht’s preface also distinguishes the accents supplied by grammarians from Vedic accents.

This moves one of the six unassigned fields into the argued class. Of the original 103 fields, **94** retain direct lexical or operational evidence, **four** have argued selections, and **five** still lack an individual assignment. These five are tṛn at 2.94 and sya, ḍha, ḍaṭ and āṭac at 4.105. In the glossary the entries for *mantṛ* ‘wise man’ and *hantṛ* ‘thief’ do not print an acute that could settle the first of those.

### The three instructions inside kīnāśa

For the selected *kliś vibādhane* root, Uṇādi 5.56 specifies an ī in place of the root’s short penultimate i, deletion of root l, and insertion of nām. The Kośa explicitly places the augment after the last root vowel. Rule 1.1.47 gives the location for this m-marked augment. The root ś survives, and kan contributes a surface *a*. Keeping the original targets addresses the apparent ambiguity about which vowel was meant. The assembled word is **k + ī + nā + ś + a = kīnāśa**.

Once the lexical ī is in place, 7.3.86 cannot give ordinary guṇa on a short penultimate i. After the nā insertion the segment before the final ś is itself long ā. Rule 7.3.84 also requires vowel-final ik. Comparing these two operations with and without the k marker therefore gives the same result under the selected fixed lexical instructions. This gives a second explicit restriction on the historical puzzle. PG181 had already isolated the independent kuṭādi guṇa block on the chosen root for *kuṭaru*.

There is a further guard on both histories. Kāśikā 6.1.165 restricts its kit-based final acute to a taddhita suffix. Both affixes here are Uṇādi derivatives of verbal roots, so that rule supplies no historical purpose for their kit. The recorded agricultural *kīnā́śā* in Ṛgveda 4.57.8 has its accent on nā. The lexical and accent evidence locates the spoken vowel, while the traditional explanation of the extra k remains open at both rules.
