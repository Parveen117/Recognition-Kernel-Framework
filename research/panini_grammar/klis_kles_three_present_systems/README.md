# PG232 · Three kleś and kliś presents and the object of kliśnāti

Monty · 12 November 2026

Kālidāsa’s *Kumārasaṃbhava* 2.40 has **kliśnāti bhuvanatrayam**: the subject afflicts the three worlds. Mallinātha glosses the verb with *pīḍayati*. This is a transitive use of class-nine *kliś*, whose digital Dhātupāṭha entry marks it intransitive and records a transitive reading in a note. GRETIL begins the line with *ittham*; the grammar’s quotation begins with *evam*. Both have the same verb and object.

| Entry | Present singular | A form that distinguishes the system | Source condition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 01.0691 *kleś*, class one | kleśate | kleśasva, 2sg imperative | The digital head and Mādhava give indistinct speech. Maitreya expressly gives distinct speech. |
| 04.0057 *kliś*, class four | kliśyate | kliśyasva, 2sg imperative | The *y* belongs to the lexical class marker *śyan* on the ‘suffer’ reading. |
| 09.0058 *kliś*, class nine | kliśnāti | kliśāna, 2sg imperative | 3.1.83 replaces *śnā* before *hi* after a consonant. Compare 3du *kliśnītaḥ* and 3pl *kliśnanti*. |

The dental nasal after *ś* in *kliśnāti* follows 8.4.44. The Mādhavīya transcription has *klināsi* at one point in the same paragraph; the table retains *kliśnāsi* and records that source difference.

The three entries give **108 new finite positions** across the present, imperative, imperfect and optative. The literary occurrence supports one of these positions and supplies its object.
