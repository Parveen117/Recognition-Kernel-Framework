## H02. A witness is not an unrestricted register license

On Kṣīrataraṅgiṇī p. 243 the group is described as **छान्दसा अमी जिघर्त्यादिवन्नियतविषयाः**. Together with the p. 242 chandas heading, this declares restricted contexts, comparing jigharti and similar usages. It does not map every requested finite context to this teaching. This is the exact quotation; “following attested usage” is an interpretive gloss, not a replacement Sanskrit reading. PG64 collated Puruṣakāra p. 24: Maitreya's wider bhāṣā report there concerns **kṣi**, while Kṣīrasvāmin's differently segmented ṛkṣi follows as another account. SK's Bālamanoramā likewise explicitly identifies kṣi as the root receiving the classical exception.

The Dhātukāvya commentary reports both the ṛkṣi variant and a wider daghādi interpretation, but does not identify which variant teaching belongs to which teachers behind that wider report. Its poem demonstrates the particular participle, not every finite request. PG64's held cross-tradition join therefore remains held. This is a decision about the inspected evidence, not a universal assertion that no wider ṛkṣi tradition exists.

The admission relation binds **authority, source location, segmentation, teaching-role assignment, register, construction and context together**. Projecting these fields separately and taking their product invents combinations absent from the witnesses. In particular:

HOLD is an open evidence obligation, not a historical impossibility proof. The register stores the reason and complementary channel. No ṛkṣi witness or held request increases the finite-position total.

## H03. The positive kṣi continuation and its exact identity

Use **SK_KSI_REPORTED_BHASHA_1050033**: svādi kṣi, hiṃsā, kartari P, one retained root vowel, no teaching-i marker, aniṭ in its basic ekāc/anudātta profile. PG12's laṭ admission and PG60 H03 are unchanged. The modern Vedic tag remains literal. This packet adopts the reported classical exception; it does not erase the narrower competing tradition.

KRM 303's p. 317 heading identifies svādi, transitive, aniṭ, P and the narrower Vedic classification. Its continuation on p. 318 attributes ordinary-language use to Maitreya and others, and compares the other formations with bhvādi kṣi in entry 302. The end of that entry, visible on p. 317, has three distinct passive-participle stems: **kṣīya**, **kṣāyya**, **cikṣīṣya**. The comparison supports their morphological transport; it does not identify the senses or kāraka frames of the two entries. The present śnu is specific to svādi kṣi.

The positive request retains **1050033 + gaṇa 5 + hiṃsā + its source policy**. It is not 1010269 kṣi in decay, 1060143 kṣi in dwelling/movement, 1080004 kṣiṇ, or 1090042 kṣīṣ. In particular a tanādi kṣiṇ word can look like a svādi kṣi word while assigning the nasal to a different owner. KRM's remark about the absence of a separate tanādi entry in some teachers' systems is preserved as their report; it does not delete that entry from our pinned modern catalogue.

The finite words in [PARADIGMS.md](PARADIGMS.md) are our written extensions under this qualified admission. KRM's participles are stem controls, not nine-cell finite quotations. The new ṛkṣi decision and the new kṣi paradigms are separate results with separate accounting.
