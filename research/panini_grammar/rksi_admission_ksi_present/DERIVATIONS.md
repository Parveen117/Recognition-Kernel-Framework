## H04. Context, voice and controller

Laṭ denotes the selected present under 3.2.123. Ordinary loṭ and vidhiliṅ use qualified 3.3.161–162 requests, with a suitable deliberative/request context for the first person. Laṅ uses a witnessed past explicitly outside today under 3.2.111 and initial aṭ under 6.4.71. There is no māṅ, prefix, blessing-loṭ extension, Vedic context or accent request. Each slot fixes its speaker/addressee relation and number.

The base event has actor A and distinct affected patient O. Under 3.1.7, san has desire actor A identical to the actor of the desired injury; the selected transitive san frame admits O as the expressed patient. Under 3.1.26, ṇic introduces causer C while retaining causee A and O. The selected 1.4.52–55 analysis leaves the causee as an agent and assigns C the hetu designation. Do not infer a patient's agreement from C's person/number.

## H05. Basic svādi kṣi has an affix-owned nasal

Introduce śnu by 3.1.73. Its ś marker gives sārvadhātuka, and apit śnu is ṅitvat by 1.2.4, blocking root guṇa by 1.1.5. Its own u has an independent ending-facing query. Root i is retained; it is neither an it-marker nor iṭ. Apply ṇatva to the śnu nasal after root ṣ across i by 8.4.1–2.

## H06. Basic passive lengthening has a yak owner

Under 3.1.67, karmaṇi introduces yak outside kṣi and before the A ending. Yak is kit and ārdhadhātuka at the root-facing seam. Its y is outside val, so no valādi iṭ occurs. At the non-kṛt, non-sārvadhātuka, kṅit y boundary 7.4.25 gives kṣi→kṣī. The interface **kṣī | ya** gives kṣīyate and the other P2 words. The basic root's aniṭ property and the absence of a val-initial affix are distinct facts; this y boundary itself already excludes ordinary iṭ.

KRM p. 317's basic passive participle and its 7.4.25 note corroborate this stem. That lengthening cannot be moved to the basic vidhiliṅ word kṣiṇuyāt: its root faces śnu and its later yāsuṭ has the sārvadhātuka setting.

## H07. Causal root āy survives loss of the separate ṇic-i

3.1.26 supplies ṇic. Its ṇit history gives root-final i→ai by 7.2.115, followed by ai→āy before ṇic-i under 6.1.78. The derived dhātu is **kṣāy | i**, not a single unowned string.

In kartari the outer pit śap allows ṇic-i→e by 7.3.84, then e→ay before śap-a. Thus **kṣāy | ay | a** yields kṣāyayati or kṣāyayate on the corresponding P/A frame. The root's aniṭ status does not remove ṇic's actual i. These are distinct grammatical objects.

In karmaṇi the y-initial, aniṭ ārdhadhātuka yak triggers 6.4.51: delete ṇic-i but retain its history and the already effected root āy. The result **kṣāy | ∅ | ya** yields kṣāyyate. The two y sounds have root and yak owners. PG64 H07's 6.1.66 boundary argument applies: the second y is outside val and does not delete the first y. The ordinary unaccented, non-doubling sound policy is fixed. KRM p. 317's causal passive participle independently controls this interface.

## H08. Aniṭ san lengthens the root; the copy stays short

Select the same-actor desire construction of 3.1.7. Kṣi is ekāc and anudātta in the selected teaching, so 7.2.10 blocks ordinary iṭ before san; none of the selected exceptional san iṭ conditions applies. By 1.2.9 the jhal-initial san after ik is kit. Consequently guṇa is blocked and 6.4.16 lengthens the original final i: **kṣī | sa**.

6.1.9 supplies the san copy. Apply 7.4.59 hrasva to the copied vowel, 7.4.60 halādi retention to the copied kṣ cluster, and 7.4.62 palatalization to its remaining k. The copy is **ci**; the original cluster remains **kṣ**, and the original vowel remains **ī**. Under 8.3.59 the san s after ī reads ṣ. The owned result is **ci | kṣī | ṣa**, giving cikṣīṣati with outer śap and 6.1.97. There is no iṭ before this san, no root ay, and no second san.

For karmaṇi, 6.4.48 removes the san-derived final a before yak. The derivation **ci | kṣī | ṣ∅ | ya** gives cikṣīṣyate. The inner ṣ belongs to san and the following y to yak; this is not an unanalysed future sya. KRM p. 317 supplies the matching desiderative passive-participle stem. The whole derived dhātu's history survives a-lopa.

The cluster simplification affects only the copy. An implementation that deletes ṣ from the original root, retains a long vowel in ci, imports ciri's san-iṭ, or identifies the passive y with sya fails this construction's addressed premises.

- Thematic first dual/plural ā before v/m belongs to the present-system a-ending interface under 7.3.101. It is unrelated to the optional śnu-u deletion in H05.

## E01. Exhaustion and non-overlap

The seven source-qualified families have four present-system requests each. Their full product has **7 × 4 × 9 = 252** positions. PG12 already supplies the nine basic kartari laṭ positions; PG68 supplies the other **243**. The only new multiple-reading positions are basic laṅ 1du and 1pl under H05, giving **245 readings and 245 paths**. The selected ordinary sound policy introduces no other optional branch here.

The inherited PG21 modal register lists its exact roots and does not include svādi 1050033; its kṣiṇ is tanādi 1080004. PG60's broad pvādi cells use 1090042 kṣīṣ and do not overlap either. The new table retains lexical identity, formation, voice, pada, context, controller and agreement on every row. Ṛkṣi source controls, quotations and open joins have zero position credit.

This is finite exhaustion of the declared written request product. It does not establish wider ṛkṣi usage, every kṣi ārdhadhātuka form, complete Vedic/accent grammar or executable correctness.
