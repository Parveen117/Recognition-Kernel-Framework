# PG154 · Dividing goods, lowering the tail and an understood causer

Monty · 11 November 2026

Prakriyāsarvasva under 3.1.20 reports an additional use from Śṛṅgāra. *Vibhāṇḍayate* denotes division. Its initial vi, nominal bhāṇḍa and action form a sixth lexical request distinct from the previous sam + bhāṇḍa collection request. The commentary gives the 3sg present. The remaining cells are written derivations on the same ṇiṅ and A boundary. The underlying nominal copy in the aorist comes from [the b-form collation](../prefixed_ning_aorist/README.md), giving *vyababhāṇḍata* after vi + a. In laṅ the corresponding *vyabhāṇḍayata* has retained ay and no copy.

The Kaumudī describes vi-puccha with varied or opposed raising. The dog example preserves the separate source description. The cow example explicitly admits a causal meaning inside the existing formation. Its accusative participant must be available to clause interpretation. A second overt causative suffix would need separate licensing.

The two prior profiles each have 90 cells and 127 readings. Recording an additional source history for each of those 254 position-readings leaves the previous total untouched. In the sixth profile, vi + a produces vya and there is no sam-prefix nasal branch. At liṭ the ām–auxiliary junction still has its regular choices. At āśīrliṅ 2pl the independent dh/ḍh choice survives.

The native relation retains source authority and action as labels on the finite prefix/nominal/ending history. Forgetting those labels recovers each previously written word. Adding the sixth prefix–nominal pair expands the request domain. Adding a source interpretation to the other two pairs expands the inverse fibers over those pre-existing requests.
