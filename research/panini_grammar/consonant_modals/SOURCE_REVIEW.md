## R1. Inherited scope and new reading

PG11 supplies exact lexical identities, qualified padas, source-local
tripādī policies and its declared vas source choice. PG19–23 supply
modal meanings, ending construction, effective markers, āṭ and tātaṅ.

īḍ/īś ending host and mṛj's weak-vowel option.

The source archive is a dependency set, not a claim that every rule or
out-of-domain example in a quoted commentary is applied here. In
particular, a commentary's location and the rule operative in its
lexical discussion must remain separate.

## R2. Īḍ/īś: original ending versus admitted descendant

Kāśikā 7.2.77 gives īśiṣe and īśiṣva. At 7.2.78 it gives
īḍidhve/īḍidhvam and īḍiṣe/īḍiṣva, carries the relevant prescription
to īś, and explicitly distinguishes original laṅ dhvam from loṭ's
partially transformed dhve descendant. Kaumudī agrees: its loṭ
īḍidhvam contrasts with laṅ's unaugmented contact. Bālamanoramā
explains why naming a transformed ending does not in reverse name
every instance of its original form. Tattvabodhinī supplies the īś
extension and the loṭ forms as well.

The decisive distinction is therefore **directed host descent**, with
the inherited actual-val condition. It is not blanket replacement of
dhvam by idhvam, nor a symmetric equivalence between original and
transformed endings. D4/M1 adopt this account. No independent
ārdhadhātuka iṭ rule is imported.

The Bālamanoramā transcription includes fragments such as ईडाम्,
ईडिध्व and ईड्ढ्वमित्यत्र. Retain them literally. Their surrounding
argument and Kāśikā/Kaumudī distinguish loṭ from laṅ; the fragments
do not establish a contrary loṭ paradigm or an invented disagreement
between the two commentaries. The laṅ control is outside P's count.

## R3. Mṛj: strong, weak-vowel and weak-consonant fields

Kāśikā 7.2.114 explains vṛddhi before a dhātu affix and excludes
nominal contexts such as perimṛdbhyām. Kaumudī, Bālamanoramā and
Tattvabodhinī preserve the vārttika **क्ङित्यजादौ वेष्यते**.
On the selected account this supplies optional vṛddhi at an actual
vowel-initial weak ending, not at all weak endings.

Bālamanoramā explicitly supplies mārṣṭu, mṛṣṭāt, mṛṣṭām,
mṛḍḍhi, first-person mārjāni/mārjāva/mārjāma and optative
mṛjyāt/mṛjyātām. Its imperative 3pl passage has the damaged
literal मृज्नतु beside मार्जन्तु. PG24 constructs **mṛjantu**
from the retained weak mṛj plus antu; it does not claim that the
damaged token is an exact clean witness. Kaumudī's undamaged
mṛjanti/mārjanti independently shows the same weak-vowel partition
in the inherited present context.

The earlier PG11 discussion of the anomalous Bālamanoramā मार्षि
is preserved. No frozen PG11 evidence is rewritten. M3's modal
partition rests on the stated rule/vārttika and addressed modal forms,
not every literal form elsewhere in the entry.

## R4. Duh/dih: dhv is not every dh

Kāśikā 8.2.31/32 fixes the ordinary h→ḍh and d-root h→gh
exception. At 8.2.37 Kāśikā explicitly requires the v after dh
and contrasts a bare dhi context. Kaumudī/Bālamanoramā explain
why a general jhal-based bhaṣ trigger would overgenerate. The
Nyāsa and Tattvabodhinī entries are retained with that discussion.
S2 continues the PG11 adopted s/dhv source contract.

The lexical commentary at **3.4.112** includes the modal discussion
of dviṣ, duh, dih and lih. Its host rule about a laṅ jus option is
not a newly applied PG24 operation. Kaumudī there has the raw token
धुग्धि. Bālamanoramā instead explicitly explains दुग्धीति by
hi→dhi and gh/jaś, consistent with **dugdhi** and the absence of
a following v. The Laghu entry at 6.4.66 also supplies दुग्धि.
PG24 retains the anomalous Kaumudī token without choosing it over
the addressed rule account.

Laghu 6.4.66 also contains damaged धुग्घ्वम् and दुह्रात्.
Neither is used as a positive spelling witness. The desired dhugdhvam
and duh optative forms are constructed from their owned constituents.
The host 6.4.66 rule is not an operation on this packet's roots.

## R5. Lih: local ṣṭutva, root-owned lopa, then length

Kāśikā 8.3.13 explicitly admits the later ṣṭutva result into
the local ḍh-lopa query. It supplies the needed permission for S3;
a global increasing-sūtra-number scheduler is insufficient.
6.3.111 lengthens the eligible preceding short a/i/u after the
specified ḍh/r deletion, including the present non-uttarapada context. The
first-ṇ aṇ class does not make ṛ or strong e additional targets.

The root-owned first ḍh is deleted, while the ending-owned second
survives. This yields the mandatory līḍh- contacts, with no optional
8.4.65 duplicate-loss branch. The Laghu lexical discussion at 7.3.72
also supplies līḍhi; the host śamādi rule is not applied to lih.
S3's likṣva and vowel/y-facing forms have different contact premises.

## R6. Vas and the remaining sound boundary

PG11 selected the Kaumudī/Bālamanoramā broad reading at 8.2.25
for basic vas before a dh-initial affix. Kāśikā's sic-restricted
discussion remains an alternative account. PG24 continues that
declared choice for **vadhvam**, without claiming an edition-wide
reconciliation. The source relation is local: the intervening modal
ī in vasīdhvam prevents direct root-s/dh contact.

Kāśikā 6.4.101, 8.2.36/40/41, 8.3.59 and 8.4.41/53/55/65
fix the remaining hi, retroflex, voicing and collapse fields. Dviṣ
and mṛj's ḍ∣ḍh contacts do not meet lih's ḍh∣ḍh deletion premise.
Īḍ's root ḍ is not jhaṣ, so its t contact gives īṭṭām rather
than an aspirated substitute. The preceding vowels exclude the
additional optional-collapse branches itemized in S6.
