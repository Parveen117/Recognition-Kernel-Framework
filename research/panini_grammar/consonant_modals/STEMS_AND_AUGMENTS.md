# PG24 — Augment hosts, strength and the mṛj option

Author: Monty. 2 November 2026.

## M1. īḍ/īś iṭ follows the admitted ending descent

The local Host_77/78 relation of D4 gives **iṭ at ordinary loṭ A 2sg
and 2pl** for both roots. Its ṭ supplies ending-initial placement;
root and augment owners remain distinct. Thus:

| Root-facing input | Owned augment and subsequent readout |
|---|---|
| īḍ∣sva with se ancestry | īḍ∣i∣sva → īḍiṣva |
| īś∣sva with se ancestry | īś∣i∣sva → īśiṣva |
| īḍ∣dhvam with loṭ dhve ancestry | īḍ∣i∣dhvam → īḍidhvam |
| īś∣dhvam with loṭ dhve ancestry | īś∣i∣dhvam → īśidhvam |

The first two ṣ sounds belong to the affix s under 8.3.59 after iṭ.
The root ś in īśiṣva remains ś because its actual following sound
is i. The augment prevents the unaugmented stop contacts at both slots.

No other ordinary slot has the named host. O does not first make
se/dhve, and its actual root-facing ī also fails the retained val
condition. Thus O uses īḍīta/īśīta and īḍīdhvam/īśīdhvam,
with the **long ī from the modal augment**, not an extra short iṭ.
B A repeats the ordinary I construction on its own supplied meaning;
there is no A tātaṅ.

This is the named-root prescription 7.2.77/78, not rudādi 7.2.76
or generic ārdhadhātuka 7.2.35. It does not turn the weak endings pit.

## M2. Ordinary light-upadhā guṇa and unchanged long vowels

For dviṣ, duh, dih and lih, 7.3.86 gives the strong vowels e, o,
e and e respectively. The weak states retain i, u, i and i.
The actual consonant operations are deferred to S.

| Root | Strong stem before the new boundary | Weak stem |
|---|---|---|
| dviṣ | dveṣ | dviṣ |
| duh | doh | duh |
| dih | deh | dih |
| lih | leh | lih |
| īḍ | īḍ | īḍ |
| īś | īś | īś |
| vas | vas | vas |

The ī of īḍ/īś is long, so the light-upadhā guṇa premise is false.
Vas's a is not an ik target. There is no imported śī lexical guṇa
exception. First-person āṭ is strong in both padas; for example
dohāni/dohai use doh even though the ordinary A non-first slots use duh.
No weak laṭ first-person stem is copied into these loṭ positions.

## M3. Mṛj has a three-way source partition

7.2.114 gives mṛj's vṛddhi before the admitted dhātu affix and
preempts the ordinary guṇa outcome. Preserve ṛ as the target and
the r-bearing ār result. Kaumudī/Bālamanoramā at this rule also
give the cited vārttika: **optional vṛddhi before a vowel-initial
kit/ṅit ending**.

Under that adopted account the partition is:

| Ending state / actual first sound | Root result | PG24 positions |
|---|---|---|
| strong ordinary tu or first-person āṭ | mārj | I/B 3sg and first persons |
| weak, actual vowel first | {mṛj,mārj} | I/B 3pl before antu |
| weak, actual consonant first | mṛj | ordinary hi/tām/tam/ta, O y, B tāt |

The optional weak-vowel clause does not create an unrestricted weak
vṛddhi switch. Bālamanoramā explicitly gives **mṛjyāt** for
vidhiliṅ. Its y is neither the vowel u behind it in 3pl jus nor
a vowel just because a later sandhi is pending. Thus **mṛjyuḥ**
has one root factor here, while **mṛjantu/mārjantu** has two.

Likewise the new tāt branch is weak and t-initial, so mṛṣṭāt has
no mārṣṭāt alternative on D. Ordinary hi gives mṛḍḍhi by S,
not a strong first-person stem with a different ending attached.

Mārj's r does not turn the ending n retroflex in mārjāni: the
intervening palatal j is outside the admitted 8.4.2 interval. By contrast
dveṣāṇi has ṣ and only the permitted ā before n.

## M4. Augments and choices are not independent menus

There are four īḍ/īś iṭ sites in each ordinary I/B mood, all in A.
They are mandatory at their admitted hosts and absent elsewhere.
Mṛj's two alternatives occur only at one ordinary P 3pl per I/B.
Tātaṅ adds a separate choice at five P roots' two singulars and
requires the new weak-consonant stem calculation.

**Proposition M4.** On D2 the only new optional stem fibre has two
members at mṛj I/B 3pl. Every O stem fibre and every new tātaṅ
stem fibre has one member.

**Proof.** M1's augment relation is determinate, and M2 is fixed by
the ending's source-resolved strength. M3 exhausts the strong, weak-vowel
and weak-consonant alternatives. Only its second row has two outputs,
and D3 supplies a vowel first only at ordinary mṛj 3pl among weak
PG24 inputs. O's actual y and B tātaṅ's t exclude that row. □
