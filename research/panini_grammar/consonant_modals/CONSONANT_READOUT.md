# PG24 — Actual contact, local visibility and mandatory lopa

Author: Monty. 2 November 2026.

## S1. Direct contact is read after the owned augment

Preserve PG11's immutable lexical source and local tripādī readout.
Read the actual root-facing vowel/consonant supplied by D/M. An augment
can separate a root from a bare t/dh/s; deleting a different earlier
sound does not give permission to ignore that surviving augment.

The source decisions are local: 8.2.32 is the d-initial h exception
to 8.2.31; 8.2.36 selects mṛj and ś-final īś before jhal;
8.2.37 distinguishes s/dhv from plain dh; and S3 uses the explicit
8.3.13 permission for ṣṭutva. No global numerical queue resolves
all these relationships.

## S2. Duh and dih: gh before contact, bhaṣ only in its own field

Their original d-initial lexical identity selects h→gh under 8.2.32
before actual jhal. There is no root replacement before the vowels of
ordinary A dual/plural or before O's y/ī.

- Before t/th, 8.2.40 gives dh; 8.4.53 gives g for root gh.
  This derives dogdhu/degdhu, dugdhām/digdhām and their tam/ta forms.
- Ordinary P hi first becomes dhi by 6.4.101 because the source
  root is jhal-final. The following bare dh is **not dhv**.
  Root gh→g gives **dugdhi/digdhi**, with initial d unchanged.
- Before A sva, h→gh and the s condition licenses original d→dh
  by 8.2.37. The affix s takes ṣ after ku by 8.3.59 and root gh
  devoices by 8.4.55: **dhukṣva/dhikṣva**.
- Before actual A dhvam, 8.2.37 again gives initial dh, while
  root gh→g gives **dhugdhvam/dhigdhvam**.

Kāśikā 8.2.37 expressly distinguishes dh followed by v from plain
dhi; Kaumudī/Bālamanoramā reject a blanket jhal-triggered bhaṣ rule.
In particular the dh created from following t is not a fresh generic
bhaṣ trigger. The B tāt route gives **dugdhāt/digdhāt**, with d,
rather than copying the initial dh of dhugdhvam.

## S3. Lih: retain the deleted root consonant and lengthening cause

Before actual jhal, lih has h→ḍh by 8.2.31. At a t/th contact,
8.2.40 first gives following dh. For ordinary hi, 6.4.101 already
supplies that dh. The addressed 8.4.41 ṣṭutva gives following ḍh.

Kāśikā 8.3.13 explicitly allows that later ṣṭutva result to be
visible for its local **ḍh∣ḍh** deletion. Delete the root-owned first
ḍh, retain the ending-owned second ḍh, and apply **6.3.111** to
the preceding eligible short i. Consequently:

\[
 lih\mid hi\ \to\ lih\mid dhi\ \to\ li\!ḍh\mid dhi\
 \to\ li\!ḍh\mid ḍhi\ \to\ li\mid ḍhi\ \to\ līḍhi.
\]

The same construction gives līḍhām/līḍham/līḍha/līḍhvam and
B **līḍhāt**. Strong leh yields **leḍhu**: e is not the short
a/i/u member of the first-ṇ aṇ class targeted by this lengthening.

Before A sva, root ḍh instead takes k by 8.2.41, and the affix s
takes ṣ: **likṣva**. No adjacent ḍh pair is formed, so no deleted-ḍh
lengthening is licensed. Before O's actual y/ī, h remains h:
lihyāt/lihīta. One cannot use the ordinary līḍh- contact form as
the root for every modal ending.

The lopa is mandatory on this source-local contact, not an optional
8.4.65 collapse. Its Smriti is the premise for 6.3.111; deletion from
pronunciation does not erase the source event.

## S4. Dviṣ and mṛj: the same retroflex contact has different roots

Dviṣ retains its original ṣ. In mṛj, 8.2.36 supplies ṣ from j
before actual jhal; its strong/weak vowel is already fixed by M3.

| Boundary | Dviṣ path | Mṛj path |
|---|---|---|
| strong tu | dveṣ∣tu → dveṣṭu | mārj∣tu → mārṣṭu |
| weak tām/tam/ta or tāt | dviṣṭ- | mṛṣṭ- |
| weak P hi | dviṣ∣dhi → dviḍḍhi | mṛj∣dhi → mṛṣ∣dhi → mṛḍḍhi |
| O y | dviṣy- | mṛjy- |

At dhi, 8.4.41 makes following dh retroflex and 8.4.53 voices
the preceding ṣ to ḍ. Neither root has original ḍh here, so lih's
8.3.13/6.3.111 deletion-lengthening path does not apply.

Dviṣ A sva gives dvikṣva by 8.2.41 and 8.3.59. Its dhvam
contact gives dviḍḍhvam by ṣṭutva/jaś. At O ī, the original ṣ
remains separated from the later dental dhvam: **dviṣīdhvam**.
Mṛj has no A profile in this packet.

## S5. Īḍ, īś and vas

For īḍ at ordinary A tām, 8.4.41 gives ṭ and 8.4.55 devoices
root ḍ, yielding **īṭṭām**. Root ḍ is not a jhaṣ sound that
would license 8.2.40's dh substitution. Before ordinary vowels it
remains ḍ. M1's iṭ prevents this stop contact at sva/dhvam.

Īś at ordinary tām has ś→ṣ by 8.2.36 and t→ṭ by 8.4.41:
**īṣṭām**. Its vowel-facing ś remains, including īśiṣva/īśidhvam
and O īśīta. These are not obtained by carrying īṣṭ- into every slot.

For vas, retain PG11's explicitly selected Kaumudī/Bālamanoramā
reading of **8.2.25**: root s is deleted before the direct dh-initial
affix, yielding **vadhvam**. The Kāśikā sic-restriction discussion is
retained as an alternative source account, not silently reconciled.
This packet continues the selected basic-root vas contract.

Ordinary sva gives **vassva**, with no iṇ-triggered ṣ and no preceding
hal that would license deletion of the first of the two s sounds.
At O ī the root s is separated from dhvam and is retained:
**vasīdhvam**. No new root-wide s-lopa is allowed.

## S6. Exhaustion of sound options

There is no eligible 8.4.65 optional collapse in these new profiles.
The doubled retroflex contacts dviḍḍhi/mṛḍḍhi, dviḍḍhvam and
īṭṭām have a vowel immediately before the pair. Vas's ss likewise
follows a vowel. Duh/dih's g∣dh is not savarṇa, and ṣ∣ṭ does not
share the required articulation manner. Lih's separate mandatory
ḍh-lopa has already been applied. Optional doubling is outside D.

Dveṣāṇi has the independently licensed ṣ…ā…n retroflexion;
mārjāni retains dental n because j interrupts the admitted interval.
The other first-person n contexts have no applicable r/ṣ trigger.

**Proposition S6.** Each admitted M stem/ending branch has exactly
one word reading under D's sound policy.

**Proof.** S2–S5 partition the eight exact identities and exhaust
their vowel, y, t/th, hi, sva and dhvam contacts. The source-local
exception/deletion choices are fixed, and S6 excludes an additional
optional collapse. The only retained branch multiplicity is M3's
root-vowel option or D3's separately chosen B ending. □
