# PG833 · The wife compound at Mahābhāṣya 4.1.150 in two printings

Both the 1883 Bombay volume and the 1965 Poona revision print **फाण्टाहृताभार्यः** at 4.1.150, volume II, printed p. 261. In each image the separate feminine ends in **ता**, and the first compound member likewise ends in **ता** before **भा**. PG389’s Wikisource text has **फाण्टाहृतभार्यः**, with short a in the compound. The indexed GRETIL transcription also gives *phāṇṭāhṛtabhāryaḥ*. Record long ā as the positive reading of both inspected printings and short a as the electronic reading. Their correspondence establishes the printed form and leaves the source of the electronic difference open. The two printings belong to the Kielhorn edition family.

### From the printed example to the case panel

The earlier paragraph at 4.1.90 (1883, pp. 242–243, PDF pp. 268–269) explicitly uses ṇa for a descendant of Phāṇṭāhṛti in the Sauvīra-gotra examples and then discusses a young descendant’s students. The 4.1.150 paragraph asks why ṇ is marked when the proper base is already vṛddha. The second answer gives the prohibition of puṃvadbhāva. This lets a later operation consult the original affix even on a branch without a visible new initial lengthening. The following objection remains part of the debate. These examples support this particular base–affix pairing. They do not settle the rival allocation of ṇa and phiñ across **both** Phāṇṭāhṛti and Mimata.

Select the sourced feminine *phāṇṭāhṛtā*, qualified by descent, in apposition to *bhāryā* ‘wife’. The first member keeps its feminine ā on the 6.3.39 account. The possessor is external to both, giving a bahuvrīhi under 2.2.24. Internal sup endings drop under 2.4.71. For a masculine possessor the other member gives the compound-final *-bhārya-* on the same outer history as PG230, which already distinguishes four possible first-member outcomes. This case has neither a verbal root nor a preverb, voice or lakāra input. An unsuffixed adjective with the same initial long vowel supplies no authority for this affix-specific prohibition.

The printed nominative singular is one attested position. The [other 23 case-number positions](CASES.tsv) apply the established masculine ending panel to that stem. Internal ā survives in the genitive dual *phāṇṭāhṛtābhāryayoḥ* as it does in the vocative singular. The r of the last member conditions ṇ in the instrumental singular and genitive plural. The ablative singular keeps the two pausal readings of PG825. This gives 24 new nominal positions and 25 readings. The short electronic compound remains a source reading on the frozen PG389 record. No short-vowel paradigm is inferred. The other genders, exact accent and manuscript priority remain open.
