# PG971 · Four negated actions and four properties in Gītā 2.23–24

In 2.23 the weapons and waters govern plural verbs, the fire and wind singular verbs. The accusative *enam* denotes the affected referent. The fourth predicate *na śoṣayati mārutaḥ* is active, with the wind as causer. Compare the causee-expressing *śoṣyante* in [PG970](../drying_and_falling_in_the_vamana_purana/README.md). Negating the proposition retains the syntactic participants without asserting that the event happened. In 2.24 the referent becomes the nominative *ayam*. Four privative adjectives agree with it. Śrīdhara explains the cutting and wetting descriptions by absence of parts, burning by absence of form and drying by absence of fluidity. Viśvanātha on 2.23 also offers the particular weapons associated with the elements. These are attributed interpretations. The two verse constructions preserve the change in predicate and agreement. The four ending sequences in continuous recitation are conditioned by the following sounds. In particular the last member occurs before *eva*, yielding *aśoṣya eva*. Its lack of a visible visarga at that boundary leaves the underlying masculine nominative intact. The four finite verbs serve as clause evidence and receive zero new finite credit. A negated occurrence and an assertion of general incapacity also require different semantic premises. The progression in these particular verses is explicit. No universal inference from an isolated negated finite event is assumed. ## Recovering each ordinary masculine word

The four predicates give four independent nominative singular controls. For the same property, under an explicitly supplied masculine controller, the rest of the ordinary sup panel follows from the final a-stem. Let B be exactly one of **acchedy, adāhy, akledy, aśoṣy**. Append the displayed material to B. In the two cells with N, select ṇ after aśoṣy and n after each other B. The ṣ of aśoṣy and the following y and vowel satisfy the same-pada conditions of 8.4.1–2. The n in the accusative plural is final and remains dental under 8.4.37. These choices retain full words, for example *aśoṣyeṇa, aśoṣyāṇām, acchedyena, acchedyān*. ## Specialization of the argument and case operations

In PG14's paired history modules retain source locus, commentary policy, polarity scope, finite actor and affected referent, modal adjective, affix owners and controller agreement. A supported transition h → j maps h b to j (1+zₑ)b. A new verse edge may reuse a referent while changing its grammatical role, only where the source supplies both contexts. The case edge is graded by the new controller. Giving an adjective the plural of the weapons that govern *chindanti* fails this guard. The semantic cut for ritual ineligibility has its own lexical history, disjoint from the verse's inability reading. Since ε(1+zₑ)=1, the projected target j ε(b) equals the ordinary edge on h ε(b). On a failed guard both paths are zero. Multiplicativity and the involution law carry this identity through ordered words, cuts and pairings.
