# PG793 · Derivation and native recovery

Rule 5.2.59 uses `cha` in the possessive sense when a sūkta or sāman contains the named word or quoted phrase. Multiword quotation is treated as its cited shape, licensing `asyavāmīyam` without inventing an ordinary compound. The frozen Kāśikā has `kayāśubhīyam`; Vasu English prints `kayāpumīyam`. Both stay source-qualified.

Rule 5.2.60 implies the same suffix for an adhyāya or anuvāka but optionally deletes it: `gardabhāṇḍa/gardabhāṇḍīya`, `dīrghajīvita/dīrghajīvitīya`, `palitastambha/palitastambhīya`. Vasu's differing long derivative spelling is retained as another source reading. Rules 61–62 select listed `aṇ` or `vun` histories; they do not make every chapter title productive.

Rules 63–64 change the predicate. `pathaka` denotes one skilled in a path; `ākarṣaka` and `tsaruka` denote skill in listed domains. Vasu's `ākaṣa` base variant is retained without overriding Kāśikā `ākarṣa`.

Reuse PG14's full `CΣ`, involution and `B=CΣ⟨zₑ,zₑ*⟩`. Type cited shape/base `I`, containing-text or skill predicate `Q`, affix/luk history `H`, title/domain bearer `D`, word `W`, source `S`. For `Tₑ(eₕb)=eₕ′(1+zₑ)b`, typed augmentation projections satisfy `πout Tₑ=Tₑ,0 πin`; composition and pairing commute. Optional luk cannot merge a bare lexical word with an unlicensed title, and same-surface source variants retain distinct witness histories.
