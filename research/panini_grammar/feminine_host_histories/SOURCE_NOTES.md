## S1. The principal परम branch

Kāśikā 6.1.13 expressly witnesses परमकारीषगन्धीपुत्रः and the पति
counterpart. The Nyāsa field connects the inner praise compound to 2.1.61
and explains the adjective's पुंवद्भाव. Kāśikā 6.3.34 supplies its
conditions; the actual catalogue ID is used in P5. The exception to the
ordinary तदादि restriction depends on the same feminine bearer's remaining
principal within the expanded first member. It does not remove the other
6.1.13 conditions or the query's exact compound-node address.

The feminine intermediate route is supported by combining explicit rule
domains: 2.2.18 supplies the surpassing/accusative construction; 1.2.44/48
the subordinate-ending condition and shortening; 4.1.3/4 the fresh feminine
suffix on the resulting अ-final whole compound; 1.3.3/7/9 its marker
handling; and 6.1.101 its vowel combination. This combination is PG52's
declared reconstruction. No selected commentary sentence is misrepresented
as explicitly narrating that entire route, and no universal rule saying
“every अति word takes टाप्” is inferred.

Kāśikā 4.1.14 calls अनुपसर्जनात् a prospective heading. It governs
the subsequent prescriptions, including the relevant later suffix region;
it does not retrospectively prohibit 4.1.4. A new whole-word टाप् is
not a second application of the old object's चाप् prescription. The
source's masculine and feminine inputs therefore cannot be merged solely
because they share the same lexical object.

## S3. The inner shortening scope is explicit

Kāśikā 1.2.48 reads स्त्री as the specified feminine suffix class,
not every word that means a woman. Nyāsa and Padamañjarī explain why
the compound must end in the relevant subordinate feminine constituent.
They specifically exclude a careless extension to राजकुमारीपुत्र.
Padamañjarī locates the relevant principal/subordinate comparison within
the feminine-host compound, not against a subsequent external पुत्र.

The 1.2.44 अपूर्वनिपात clause keeps the fixed-case object's उपसर्जन
designation from reversing the प्रादि constituent order. The अति meaning
comes from the stated 2.2.18 वार्तिक, not from a generic prefix heuristic.
The final-target condition of 1.1.52 and the inherited 1.2.47 scope identify
the shortened vowel. The inherited shortening and stem scope does not carry
1.2.47's neuter restriction into these masculine/feminine 1.2.48 examples.
These are distinct from the optional इक्-shortening
question settled locally at 6.3.61/139 for the positive संप्रसारण forms.

## S4. Field packaging and reference corrections

The edition's `nyaas` key for 6.1.13 contains a Nyāsa discussion followed
by material explicitly labelled बाल-मनोरमा. It remains **one archived
field**, with the appended authorship identified; it is not counted as a
second independent field. The same field embeds two inaccurate catalogue
references: इग्यणः is assigned 1.1.44, and स्त्रियाः पुंवत् 6.3.33.
The actual pinned catalogue places them at **1.1.45** and **6.3.34**.
Kāśikā 6.3.33 concerns the Vedic पितरामातरा expression and cannot
supply P5's rule. The original references remain unchanged in the archive.

Damaged spellings and the broken कौमुदगन्धी/पतिः line are also retained.
PG51's adopted spelling policy is inherited where relevant. None of these
editorial defects is promoted into an additional sound rule. The current
archive separates literal transcription from the adopted histories and from
the unresolved mixed readings in S2.

## S5. Evidence boundary

The written construction starts with a qualified supplied X, not arbitrary
letters. The next lexical/descent packet must supply its earlier dependencies.
No new complete paradigms or catalogue IDs are credited here. Literal,
membership, hash and link checks establish document integrity; they do not
execute grammar, prove all Sanskrit coverage or resolve every source variant.
