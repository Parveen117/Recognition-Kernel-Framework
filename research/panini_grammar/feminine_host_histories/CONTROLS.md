# PG52 — Scoped ownership and source-policy controls

Author: Monty. 4 November 2026.

These are written decision records. They are not executed tests. BLOCKED
means failure of the named rule query, not a declaration that the whole word
or every alternative derivation is impossible. OPEN retains a missing premise.

## C01 — A visible ध्या ending does not supply the suffix owner

The three histories start with the admitted 4.1.78/74 formation X. If only
the spelling कारीषगन्ध्या or a similar ending is supplied without its
lexical/source provenance, the proposed 6.1.13 application is OPEN until
the actual ष्यङ् owner is established. A resolved different suffix blocks
this query. No broad substring-to-affix conversion is introduced.

## C02 — Outer subordination is not inner feminine prominence

At the outer 2.2.8 compound, both परमकारीषगन्ध्या and
अतिकारीषगन्ध्या are first members with the genitive subordinate role.
Yet H01 admits 6.1.13 and H03 blocks it. The difference is Y's prominence
within the expanded first member, as explained in the 6.1.13 commentaries.
One unscoped `is_subordinate` flag cannot encode those two questions.

## C03 — A feminine first member is not always shortened

Kāśikā and the two commentaries at 1.2.48 restrict shortening to the
specified compound ending in a subordinate feminine constituent. Nyāsa
and Padamañjarī explicitly distinguish राजकुमारीपुत्र from the required
inner configuration. Thus H01's outer genitive role is not a new reason
to shorten its principal feminine host. The target of 1.2.48 is also
different from the optional इक् shortening discussed at 6.3.61/139.

## C04 — एकविभक्ति does not reverse the अति compound

In the supplied surpassing relation X stays accusative as the principal
referent's case changes. Its 1.2.44 उपसर्जन status licenses the appropriate
internal work but explicitly excludes the ordinary पूर्वनिपात consequence.
Do not feed that designation unqualified into 2.2.30 and output an X+अति
compound. The selected 2.2.18 prescription supplies the actual construction.

## C05 — Missing whole-word gender leaves suffix selection open

After M5/F4 the old ending has become short. Masculine H02 has no fresh
feminine request; feminine H03 admits टाप् by 4.1.3/4. If that request
is missing, choosing between these continuations remains OPEN. The already
resolved fact that old Y is subordinate can still block its expanded-host
6.1.13 query: one unresolved continuation need not erase independent evidence.

## C06 — Two different suffix owners cannot satisfy one guard

In H03 the old Y is ष्यङ् but its bearer is subordinate. The new T has
a principal female bearer but is टाप्. Neither is both the prescribed
ष्यङ् and principal in the required scope. Combining Y's identity with
T's prominence would fabricate an admitted owner. The [native argument](DOMAIN_AND_NATIVE.md)
makes that mismatch explicit. A fresh feminine suffix does not transfer
its semantic role to an earlier one.

## C07 — A restored आ does not erase or repeat the shortening

F4 shortens the old subordinate ending; F5–F7 introduce and combine the
new principal feminine suffix. The visible आ after F7 does not establish
that F4 never happened. Nor is that new principal suffix automatically
another 1.2.48 target: the required current compound/ending/role configuration
must be supplied anew. This bounded history is not a universal no-reapplication
or termination theorem.

## C08 — The immediate उत्तरपद and compound type remain necessary

The principal परम host does not waive the other 6.1.13 premises.
An immediate complete पुत्रकुल or परमपुत्र, or the source's बहुव्रीहि
configuration with पति, still fails the specified query. Retain PG51's
actual-node distinction; a completed inner operation is not undone merely
because a later outer node fails its own query. A yet-unparsed compound is
OPEN, not automatically accepted from its ending.

## C09 — Literal variants and incorrect references stay distinguishable

Kāśikā's feminine long-आ branch and the appended Bālamanoramā masculine
short branch have distinct declared inputs. H03 supplies a rule-based
feminine reconstruction, not an emendation of every long-आ occurrence.
Padamañjarī's mixed masculine/long wording remains unresolved. In the
Nyāsa field, the embedded references for इग्यणः and स्त्रियाः पुंवत्
are 1.1.44 and 6.3.33; this edition's actual catalogue IDs are **1.1.45**
and **6.3.34**. Use the correct rule IDs while preserving the literal
source references in the archive. A reference correction is not a new rule
or a claim that the full source tradition has been collated.
