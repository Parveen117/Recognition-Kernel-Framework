# PG280 · Cutting: imperative and blessing have different stems and conditions

Monty · 13 November 2026

The same root has three different mood surfaces: ordinary optative *praṇiṣyet*, ordinary imperative *praṇiṣyatu*, and blessing-liṅ *praṇiṣeyāt*. Kāśikā 6.4.66 explicitly identifies *ṣo antakarmaṇi*; 6.4.67 then gives the independent word *avaseyāt*. The e-grade of this blessing therefore has a primary grammatical witness.

Rule **7.1.35** does not make *-tāt* a free alternative in every command. Its Kāśikā explicitly contrasts an ordinary village-going command with the blessing examples. The word tables therefore carry four individual context qualifications: the *-tāt* reading in third and second singular under each prefix order requires āśis. A generic imperative table that merely lists the strings loses that condition.

The third and second singular may share the *-tāt* surface but remain different positions. The first-person imperative is *praṇiṣyāni*, whereas the first-person blessing-liṅ is *praṇiṣeyāsam*. Neither form has a past augment. The selected source's pausal *-t* words are not multiplied by its contextual *-d* spellings.
