# PG840 · Two roots for Sītā’s words about the enemies in Bhaṭṭi 14.57

PG839 distinguished two adjacent citations in the Mādhavīya. The **second** is now identifiable in Bhaṭṭi 14.57. Three predicates share the addressee: “you crossed the sea”, “you struck the enemies for my sake”, “you died”. In 14.54–59 Sītā sees the stricken Rāma, takes him for dead and laments. The singular subject *tvam* is Rāma. *Arīn* supplies the accusative plural object of the disputed predicate and *madarthe* identifies Sītā as the beneficiary. PG361’s citizens and Daśaratha belong to the other quotation.

**Two written finite requests.** Both have active actor agreement, P, liṭ, second singular and a direct object. (1) On the unprefixed rudhādi root हिसिँ ‘to injure’, the lexical nasal and the perfect copy have separate owners. The Mādhavīya’s entry 1415 explicitly lists जिहिंसिथ. The reduplicant gives ji-, the retained nasal before s gives ṃ, and the ending with iṭ gives -itha. The present-class infix śnam has no place in this perfect. This path yields one new position and one poem-attested reading. (2) Under single ni + adādi han, killing an enemy, preserve ni, the perfect reduplicant ja- and the strong root grade. In the editor’s selection, the iṭ and zero-iṭ branches give ni-ja-ghan-itha and ni-ja-ghan-tha. These yield one position and two readings. The reported nijaghnitha retains the different lopa history. Mallinātha applies the 6.4.98 contraction, while Sāyaṇa’s objection turns on 1.2.5 apit versus original pit sip. A thal surface by itself supplies no kit premise. Attestation of the contracted reading is positive even when the selected strong-ending construction withholds it. No unrestricted exception or third generated word is introduced.

PG311 has single-ni present 3sg in the thief frame. PG325 has ā + han own-limb middle liṭ and expressly kept the electronic vi-reading outside its grid. The early hiṃs modal records use laṭ-family stems. The concordance indexed 6.4.122 at 14.57 for the sea-crossing verb. It never supplied either of the two root-specific enemy-striking keys.
