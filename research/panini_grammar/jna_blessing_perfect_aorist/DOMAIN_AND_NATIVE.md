## N2. Addressed visibility and a bounded non-injectivity witness

For an admitted elementary event put
\(U_d(e_hb)=e_{h'}(1+z_d)b\), with h′ appending source, premises, recipient
and stage. HOLD/EXCLUDED transitions have coefficient 1 and no word output.
Alternative source-policy histories remain separately tagged, even though
this packet counts one selected Kaumudī copy/lopa schedule per key. Evolution
is finite ordered composition; an elapsed-time meaning would need a separate
clock adapter.

The copy query sees original jñā under the declared 1.1.59 schedule. It
constructs copied ja before the addressed root-ā lopa. Later readout sees
ja+jñ+ending, while Smriti retains both vowel owners and the deleted root
vowel. This is a scoped visibility map, not restoration of every deleted
sound for every later rule. The original vowel-final root also supplies
1.2.5's kit guard before that lopa.

Define W to observe only the unaccented perfect word and agreement slot,
discarding root identity and source/recipient history. The admitted basic
jñā A 3sg path gives **jajñe** through root-final-ā lopa, 6.4.64. The
traditional jan A 3sg control gives the same word through **upadhā-a lopa**,
6.4.98, followed by ścutva of dental n. The root identities and deletion
recipients differ, but W agrees. Hence W is not injective on these two
declared histories and no inverse of W alone can recover both identities.
The jan control is not an additional counted lexical profile. Keeping root
and recipient history resolves this witness; no universal decoding or
minimal-memory theorem follows.

The same discipline separates sīyuṭ's ī, perfect iṭ/irec/ending-i, and
aorist ending i. At sic 2pl, selection survives in Smriti after 8.2.25

role, source, prefix, context, voice/pada, affix, copy, recipient, visibility

\[
\pi_{0,out}\operatorname{Construct}_{80,\lambda}

\]

\[
\epsilon(\langle x,y\rangle_{B_{80}})
