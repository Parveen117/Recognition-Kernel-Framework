# PG198 · The thief’s tṛc and both recorded markers for all ten ṛ-stems

Monty · 11 November 2026

For the previously open **hantā ‘thief’**, the glossarial index to Mahādeva gives **han + tṛc** on p. 258. Its 2.95 corresponds to the primary 2.94. Verse 299 supplies the thief sense on p. 45. The same index page separately gives *hṛ + tṛc*, *hartṛ*, also used for a thief. The roots, the two headwords and the verse numbers distinguish the entries. PG192’s Ṛgvedic slayer has its own lexical record.

The 1956 index permits both markers expressly for *naptṛ* and *neṣṭṛ*. Satyavrata gives one marker per word and assigns **tṛn** to *jāmātṛ* ‘son-in-law’. The 1956 index and Śvetavanavāsin select **tṛc** for that same relative. Both paths give *jāmātarau* under the selected nominative-dual schedule.

The two new sources together give both labels for eight stems. The earlier lexica also assign *tṛn* to *potṛ* and *bhrātṛ*. Across all the named authorities, **each of the ten stems now has a documented tṛn reading and a documented tṛc reading**. Meanings can differ. For instance, the 1956 *potṛ* is a priest and Satyavrata glosses his *potā* as Viṣṇu. The per-source records preserve that distinction.
