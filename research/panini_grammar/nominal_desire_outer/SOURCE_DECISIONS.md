# Source decisions

## H1. Preserve special and ordinary meanings

Use the source-conditioned pairs of PG130. For food and wealth the special aśanāya/dhanāya and ordinary aśanīya/dhanīya all have a vowel before y. A missing purpose retains both sense candidates. A completed purpose selects its own stem before any future, blessing or perfect is built.

## H2. Apply the same outer rule to separate affix histories

6.4.48 removes the final a; the pre-y vowel prevents 6.4.50 on the two credited families. P blessing consequently has a vowel-y-y sequence. The ending y and the nominal y retain separate ownership; a vowel before them prevents the post-consonant reduction from PG126.

## H3. Separate current water support from the disputed alternative

The ordinary udakīya has the same vowel protection as the two closed families. On the special thirst branch retention of y is permitted both if 6.4.50 fails and if the rule applies optionally. Its retained n-y-y P blessing can undergo 8.4.64. These positive witnesses are independent of the unresolved deletion analysis.

## H4. Why udan requires a separate judgment

7.4.34 is a lexicalized kyac-final formation. Bālamanoramā requires substitution of udan and preservation of n. A literal sound-only approach would use this n as a consonant licensing 6.4.50. Kaumudī 1.4.15 shows that apparently consonantal gav/nāv cannot be used to destroy the y on which their formation depends. The two rules do not explicitly adjudicate whether the same protection applies to this nipātana. Keep both interpretations identified as hypotheses.

## H5. A witness that could discriminate

A primary discussion naming the y-loss rule on udanya, or an unambiguous cognate outer form with the denominative y absent on a vowel-initial suffix, would distinguish the alternatives. An attested retained-y form alone is compatible with both. A single-y blessing alone can also arise through later yam reduction. Present udanyati and the noun udanyā do not decide this opposition.
