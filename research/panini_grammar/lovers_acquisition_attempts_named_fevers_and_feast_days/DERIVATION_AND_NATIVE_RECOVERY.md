# PG795 · Derivation and native recovery

Rule 5.2.74 stipulates `anuka`, `abhika` and long-vowel `abhīka` in the lover/desirer predicate. Rule 75 uses instrumental `pārśvena` for gaining by a crooked means. Rule 76 owns two different affixes: `ṭhak` after `ayaḥśūla` and `ṭhañ` after `daṇḍājina`, yielding violent and hypocritical means respectively. Their similar gloss does not merge affix history.

Rule 5.2.77 has two correlated branch families. For a book or knowledge acquisition, `kan` follows an ordinal and the ordinal suffix is optionally deleted: `dvikam/dvitīyakam`, `trikam/tṛtīyakam`, and `catuṣkam/caturthakam`. Vasu's printed `tritīyakam` is retained as a competing source reading. For the person who succeeds after that many attempts, the ordinal suffix is obligatorily deleted: `ṣaṭka`, `pañcaka`, `catuṣka`. The book-only restriction is explicit; these are not free numeral derivatives.

Rules 78–80 construct three unrelated `ka` histories: a group named for its leader (`devadattakāḥ`, `yajñadattakāḥ`), `śṛṅkhalaka` as a young camel whose principal fetter is a clog, and stipulated `utka` for a yearning bearer. The sentence in which Devadatta is an enemy is a source-given negative control for the leader branch, not a global exclusion.

Rule 81 names diseases from time, cause or effect. Its `dvitīyaka` is therefore not the book-acquisition history of rule 77, and its `uṣṇaka/śītaka` are not the manner-of-acting positions of rule 72. Kāśikā has `kāśapuṣpaka`; Vasu English prints `kāṣapuṣpaka`. Both remain source-qualified readings.

Rules 82–83 name full-moon occasions by predominant food. `guḍāpūpikā` and `tilāpūpikā` take the main rule's `kan`; the vārttika gives `vaṭakinī`; Vasu additionally lists `kṛśarikā` and `tripuṭikā`; `kaulmāṣī` instead owns `añ`, vṛddhi and accent marking. No unlisted feast name is inferred.

Rule 84 stipulates accented `śro꣡triya` in the meaning of one who has studied the Veda. The commentary also admits `chāndasa` under 4.2.59 and records competing policies: free optionality, or a semantic division between one who acts on the Vedic purport and a student generally. PG795 accepts the two displayed alternatives while leaving their critical semantic priority Open.
