# PG918 · The night–day dual and the purpose of the fortnight in Manu

The nominative dual at Manu 1.67 has two published word readings. Patrick Olivelle’s May 2021 electronic edition prints *rātryahnī* here and *rātryahanī* at 1.66. The two verses are separated explicitly in the chapter file. Jha’s 1932 Devanāgarī pages print the longer form in both verses, as does the GRETIL text used in PG914. The electronic editor consulted four printed texts and describes their dependencies. Attribution of the short reading to a particular printing or manuscript remains Open. The contrast is verifiable in the chapter and on the two page images. Medhātithi introduces the dative as a suitable reading and explains the verse’s locative through the domain of the intended purpose and the demands of metre. The two case assignments have different textual owners. The analogue *svapnāya* and the singular *ceṣṭāyai* in 1.65 receive no credit. Both plural requests start from the supplied compound activity noun. Its internal formation stays Open. There is no finite root, preverb, voice, pada or verbal affix in the three requests.

Two additional comparisons constrain the existing case register. At 1.68 the commentary glosses *kṣapāhasya* with *ahorātrasya*, establishing the whole time period as the measure’s referent. It supplies no derivation of the final a of *kṣapāha*. At 1.74 Olivelle has ṣ in the electronic *aharniṣasyānte*. Jha’s verse has श, which is also visible in the following explanation of *niśā*. GRETIL has ś. Record the electronic spelling diplomatically. Its origin and independent grammatical status remain Open. Neither comparison earns an additional case reading.
