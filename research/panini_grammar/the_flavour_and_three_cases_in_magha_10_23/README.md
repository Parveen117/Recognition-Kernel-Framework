# PG855 · The flavour of the drink and three case readings in Māgha 10.23

In his entry for śīṅ the Mādhavīya questions Māgha's wording for an exceptional flavour. Printed p. 340 points to Śiśupālavadha 10.23. The surrounding poem describes a drink given by a lover. Its flavour is the instrumental rasa, and the second half asks what the drink did for the women. Editions and commentators differ on *two different words*.

**What the grammar debate concerns.** Sāyaṇa construes “present in excellence” with ṭhañ by the adhyātmādi prescription under 4.3.60. He invokes the anuśatikādi operation (7.3.20) for both members, yielding ātiśāyika. His objection bears on the second member's vowel in the quoted reading. Mallinātha invokes vinayādi (5.4.34) and interprets abundance. These are different analyses of the adjective. A gloss beginning at a vowel junction cannot independently decide every spelling in the poem's transmission. Bhoja supplies external testimony to the short-medial form in an actual quotation with a different plural case. The shorter digital string has no independent grammatical admission here. The poet's autograph and priority of readings remain Open.

### One perfect word, two expressed roles

1. Under the main reading the drink, mukhasuram, is the neuter singular subject. The commentary explains **sasvade** by 'was pleasing'. The women receive the feeling of pleasure. Rule 1.4.33 assigns them the recipient role and 2.3.13 supplies dative **pramadābhyaḥ**, heard **pramadābhyo** before nāma. The Mādhavīya independently illustrates this very sense of svad with curds and Devadatta in entry 16. This is the unprefixed simple root, bhvādi ṣvada̐ in the entry's teaching form, agentive liṭ, middle third singular. Reduplication supplies sa, the root contributes svad and the middle perfect ending yields -e.
2. With the *reported* instrumental **pramadābhiḥ** Mallinātha explains the word by tasting or consuming. The women supply the unexpressed agent in the instrumental. The drink is the expressed object and governs the singular verb. The commentary explicitly assigns karmaṇi liṭ; the reduplicated **sasvade** has the same letters as in the previous clause. His explanatory periphrasis has no independent poem or new paradigm credit. No present-system yak enters either perfect.
3. Bhoja's **pramadānām** supplies a genitive relation in the discussion of dravyāpekṣa. Retain the genitive on the nominal side. The cited textual difference alone does not license a third verbal voice or a free exchange of DAT, INSTR and GEN in prose.

**The other finite predicate.** With **vyudapādi** the subject is *nāma*, the women's established name, which now proves meaningful. Mallinātha assigns agentive luṅ to simple pad with vi + ud, expressly without ṇic. Here the named-root rule 3.1.60 replaces cli by actual ciṇ before ta. The ṇ-marked suffix gives the root's penultimate a vṛddhi by 7.2.116. Ciṇ leaves i and removes the following ending by 6.4.104. The prefix sequence and past augment give vi + ud + a + pād + i → vyudapādi. The nominative subject is the name. The women who tasted or enjoyed the drink do not become its plural controller. This rule for pad in agentive usage is independent of PG854's vowel-final karmakartṛ rule.

**Credit.** Three finite positions with three position-indexed readings and two distinct word spellings. Their keys are unprefixed svad agentive liṭ 3sg, unprefixed svad object-expressing liṭ 3sg and vi + ud + pad agentive luṅ 3sg. One construction contrasts the three source-conditioned case frames of the first clause. The two vinayādi/adhyātmādi interpretations are source decisions; the extracted case words add no nominal paradigm credit.
