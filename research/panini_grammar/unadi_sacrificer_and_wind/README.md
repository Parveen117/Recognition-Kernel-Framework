# PG197 · The tnaṇ field, two sacrificer spellings and the two saraṭa suffixes

Monty · 11 November 2026

The glossarial index in Kunjunni Raja’s edition of Mahādeva gives **hu + tnaṇ** at **4.105**, labels the result a sacrificer and points to verse 585. The index (p. 260) and the verse (p. 91) both have **hotna** with *o*. Its entry for *cyautna* at 4.104 (p. 165) furnishes a positive control for the affix spelling. The neighbouring *hotṛ* ‘officiating priest’ and *hotra* ‘oblation’ have their own entries.

The field now has an explicit lexical example. Across the 103 fields this gives **99 direct, zero supported only by inference, and four open**. In Aufrecht’s Ujjvaladatta and Satyavrata’s word list the sacrificer is **hautna**, with *au*. The ordinary ṇit vṛddhi of 7.2.115 predicts *au* from *u*. The 1956 index supplies an affix for its own spelling. It supplies no conditioning rule that would produce *o* and *au* freely. The derivation of the exact *au* word in Ujjvaladatta remains an inference on that witness.

Two further identifications concern individual words. Satyavrata explicitly gives *dhyai + tvan* for *dhyātva* ‘thought’ on p. 200. For *saraṭa*, the 1956 index prints consecutive entries on p. 249. The chameleon has *sṛ + aṭan* at 4.81. The strong wind has *sṛ + aṭac* at 4.105. This directly supports the short-vowel analysis proposed in PG190. Satyavrata independently prints short *aṭac* for the wind. The primary digital list still has *āṭac* with long ā.

In Jñānendra Sarasvatī’s commentary, *bhṛ + śak* under the extension supplies *bhṛśam*, the second word in the primary digital commentary. This analysis goes with *bhṛśam*. The first example, *petvam*, takes *itvan*. The repository records both bases and their suffixes separately.
