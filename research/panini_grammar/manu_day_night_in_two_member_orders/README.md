# PG914 · Two orders of day and night in Manu 1.65–67

The sun divides the day and night in 1.65. In 1.66 and 1.67 a month and a year respectively are equated with the night and day of different beings. The text changes both the grammatical function of the pair and its final member. **Day first.** In 1.65 the expressed subject is singular and the finite middle *vi-bhaj* takes the pair as object. A locative singular would leave both the parallel qualifier and that object relation unexplained. PG448 already registers neuter ACC dual under the supplied chandas policy. Manu supplies a direct Smṛti occurrence of the same grammatical key. On the received compound *ahorātra*, the right member *rātri* has the licensed samāsānta ac (5.4.87). Neuter dual śī in place of auṭ (7.1.19) and coalescence with stem-final a give *ahorātre*. The attestation establishes the usage here. The historical reason this Smṛti poet uses the neuter is Open. The ordinary masculine model remains independently sourced.

**Night first.** The analytic text segments the opposite order in both following verses. Use *rātri + ahan*, in the attested sense of a pair, with last-member neuter gender under 2.4.26. At the internal boundary 6.1.77 gives *rātry-ahan*. On this surface branch the final n of *ahan* is present before the vowel ending. The neuter nominative dual replaces au by śī (7.1.19), leaving long ī. Before śī the a-deletion of 6.4.134 is optional by 6.4.136. Retaining a yields *rātryahanī*. The optional deletion would give a contracted stem juncture in this analysis. A contracted reading in either verse requires its own witness and receives no textual credit. The ac edge with *rātri* as final member cannot operate on final *ahan*. The ordinary word-internal analysis of the attested order does not itself establish freedom to permute the members in other contexts. No verbal root, voice, pada or verbal affix is a constituent of either nominal request.
