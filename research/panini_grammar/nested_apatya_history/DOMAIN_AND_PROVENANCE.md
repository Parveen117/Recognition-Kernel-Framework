## D1. Declared source and input domain

The main construction adopts the addressed **replacement policy** of
Kāśikā/Nyāsa 4.1.78. Its spelling policy uses Padamañjarī's coherent
घ-family: **उदमेघ → औदमेघि → औदमेघ्या → औदमेघेय**. Nyāsa's field
mixes उदमेघ with ध-bearing later forms; those literal bytes are retained.
This local adopted reading is not a manuscript collation or silent correction.

Padamañjarī also reports Vāmana's distinct new-affix account with a licensed
अपत्य transfer. That account remains a separate policy, not an optional
surface branch of the main derivation. The countermodel below expressly lacks
such a license. [Source review](SOURCE_REVIEW.md) identifies the distinction.

Input supplies the lexical entry, meaningful descent relation, relevant
गोत्र and non-ऋषि conditions, feminine reference and source authority.
A further outer अपत्य request has its own relation to the feminine base.
These are separate grammatical relations, not a genealogy inferred from the
visible name. Ordinary affix exclusions and the special feminine-reversion
boundary before ढक् must also be resolved. Unknown necessary evidence stays
OPEN under PG47's decision convention.

Both catalogue-ID sets already equal 3,983. This packet adds no sūtras or
full agreement positions. The [main history](DERIVATION.md) constructs one
bounded unaccented stem from its explicitly supplied lexical/semantic frame.
Accent, inflection and general recognition of such frames remain obligations.

## D3. Origin is a consumer-qualified certificate

For this packet, an origin certificate is a retained derivational witness,
not an executed proof certificate. It records

\[
\mathsf{Orig}(f,c)=
(\text{prescription},\text{meaning relation},\text{replacement chain},
\text{source clause/policy},\text{admitted consumer }c).
\]

For the adopted replacement policy, the original इञ् is admitted in the
अपत्य domain. Nyāsa 4.1.78 explicitly transports that status to its ष्यङ्
replacement for the later 6.4.151 query under the qualified स्थानिवत्
account. This transports the relevant grammatical cause, not every original
sound feature. The current suffix is not pronounced इ merely because its
origin was इञ्, and later sound-conditioned operations still need their own
admitted current or historical views.

The consumer record contains target y₁, its consonant predecessor, its
उपधा view, the following तद्धित token f₂, f₂'s admitted non-आ onset,
the applicable भ domain, the origin certificate and the current revision.
Each condition is assessed at its own address. ढक् is realized through
7.1.2 at prescription time; its actual एय environment must not be replaced
by a guess from the spelling ढक् or by early deletion of ढ् as a marker.

The first and second instances of 6.4.148 have different triggering suffixes:
inner इञ् and outer ढक्. Equal rule numbers do not identify their events
or their आश्रय. Any historical query must name which deletion it sees.

## D4. Current state and an authorized earlier view

PG47's freshness rule remains in force: each new query is bound to the current
revision. It may use a source-authorized earlier view without reusing a stale
admission record. The record must identify the earlier event, its conditioning
suffix and the exact feature that the consumer is permitted to read.

H01 uses य-lopa while y₁ is the current penultimate sound, then deletes the
final long vowel before outer एय. Its comparison of an alternative 148-first
query distinguishes two source mechanisms:

1. **6.4.22:** under the stated same-conditioning-suffix relation, an earlier
   vowel deletion is असिद्धवत् for the later उपधा query. The older
   penultimate relation is visible to that query; no deleted vowel is restored
   to the active word or pronounced twice.
2. **1.1.58:** the lopa-specific restriction on स्थानिवद्भाव in a य-lopa
   consumer is a different permission. It is not a blanket rule that all
   retained vowel history blocks, or licenses, every later deletion.

The commentary at 6.4.149 supplies the same-versus-different conditioning
comparison used to qualify this local construction. This does not establish
that all operation orders commute or that a general scheduler is complete.
See the detailed source decisions in [A notes](SOURCE_NOTES_A.md).

## D5. A bounded obstruction to discarding origin

Fix the requested consumer **6.4.151**, its target y₁, the necessary
phonological/suffix gates, the unaccented surface and the main replacement
policy. Consider supplied **candidate/query records**, including a rejected
analysis; do not assume every member is a legal completed Sanskrit derivation.

|Record|Origin evidence|Local result|
|---|---|---|
|hR|The admitted inner इञ् replacement and its licensed अपत्य transfer|APPLIED for the stated y₁ deletion|
|hN|A proposed added ष्यङ् with no admitted अपत्य transfer under this fixed policy|BLOCKED under the source's stated non-अपत्य analysis|
|h?|Necessary origin/transfer evidence is unresolved|OPEN; no successful deletion event|

The records can have the same visible stem and outer suffix. Let σ discard
their origin/policy evidence and retain that surface, and let Q give the
scoped query outcome. Then Q does **not** factor through σ on this declared
record domain: if Q = F∘σ, equal σ-images would have equal Q-values, contrary
to the table. This is the entire proof.

The result does not demonstrate two legal Sanskrit analyses of one word,
or prove that every parser using surface input is incapable of recovering
provenance from a lexicon, context or other grammatical evidence. It identifies
exactly the information lost by this specified serialization of supplied
candidate records. Vāmana's separately licensed new-affix policy is outside
the fixed-policy hN comparison and is not refuted by it.

**Local repair.** Retain the target/consumer address, policy and the actual
origin/transfer witness or its explicitly unresolved status. With the other
fixed gates, these records distinguish the three outcomes. A bare boolean
“has some y” or an unqualified “apatya somewhere in the word” cannot provide
the missing cause. The repair is a bounded record construction, not a general
lexical recognizer or a universal hallucination guarantee.
