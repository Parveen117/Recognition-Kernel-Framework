# PG191 · Three histories for kartā and the case of the mat

Monty · 11 November 2026

The unaccented words **kartā, kartārau, kartāraḥ** each have three derivations in the selected construction. Two name a maker and one predicts a future act. The Kāśikā itself contrasts *kartārau kaṭasya* and *kartārau kaṭān* at 6.4.11. That pair changes the number of mats as well as the case. The written relation now holds their number fixed and varies the derivation separately.

| History with two makers and two mats | Word pair | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| kṛ + tṛc, with a nominative dual | kartārau kaṭayoḥ | the two makers of the two mats |
| kṛ + tṛn, with a nominative dual | kartārau kaṭau | two people habitually making the two mats |
| Third-person dual luṭ | kartārau kaṭau | the two will make the two mats, in the stated non-today future |

**27 written readings give 18 distinct unaccented pairs.** Nine pairs with a genitive select the tṛc route on this domain. Each of the nine accusative pairs admits two routes. The request’s nominal meaning or its assertion of the future distinguishes them. A bare *kartārau* identifies the dual in this comparison and leaves all three routes available.

For the productive agent noun, 2.3.65 gives the object genitive. For the habitual agent noun, the prohibition at 2.3.69 leaves the independent object to 2.3.2. Rule 3.2.134 also admits customary practice and skill for the tṛn family. The source gives a separate example for each meaning. The unaccented kinship expression *pituḥ putraḥ* illustrates the distinct relational genitive of 2.3.50. The Kāśikā's *śastreṇa bhettā* keeps the weapon instrumental. A case diagnostic needs the relation of the dependent to its own head.

This develops the prior comparison between the agent noun and luṭ by adding the third history and an explicit inverse for every number pair. The two Uṇādi affixes at 2.94 have their own lexical conditions. Case government on productive *kartṛ* alone does not assign either affix to the thief.
