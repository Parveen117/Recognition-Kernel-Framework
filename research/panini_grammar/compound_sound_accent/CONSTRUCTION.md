# PG38 — Written construction: owned operations and accent histories

Author: Monty. 4 November 2026.

## C1. Selection and retained operations

For an evidence-bearing input of [Domain](DOMAIN.md), select only a clause
whose decisive gates are T under the chosen source policy. F excludes that
clause; ? leaves it Open. Store the original affix, marker ownership,
compound tree, scope, target and event order before any realization.
Optional clauses branch only after admission. A prohibition removes its
specified candidate, not every other operation on the same visible word.

|Operation|Written target and retained record|
|---|---|
|Compound ending|Append selected affix as final constituent of the actual compound under 5.4.68; retain class and inner suffixes|
|Final-segment replacement|5.4.131/132 replace terminal स् by अन्; 134 replaces जाया's terminal आ by नि; retain original stem and ङित् marker|
|Copy|6.1.1–3 select a maximal relevant one-vowel unit; first occurrence becomes अभ्यास, community becomes अभ्यस्त|
|Designation without copy|6.1.6's seven-root class gains अभ्यस्त status without manufacturing a second occurrence|
|Samprasāraṇa|Act on owned ष्यङ् य्, root यण् or अभ्यास, as selected; retain rule-relative causal ordering|
|Augment|6.1.58/59 insert मित् अम् after last root vowel; 73's तुक् belongs to vowel; 135's सुट् remains अभक्त|
|Joint replacement|Create one output from two addressed inputs; preserve both origins and separate designation/visibility views|
|Accent|Register a positive placement, route-specific block, preservation or source-conditioned alternative on its own owner|

An existing written spelling is neither a selection witness nor proof of
an operation's prerequisites. Already deleted markers and suffixes remain
available only to rules whose particular history/स्थानिवत् contracts admit
them. There is no blanket policy of “all deleted affixes still count.”

## C2. Compound controls

At 5.4.91 a राजन्-final तत्पुरुष can select टच्. The apparent same
ending in a praise सु/अति construction is subject to inherited 5.4.69;
a नञ् तत्पुरुष is subject to 71. These restrictions do not prohibit
every बहुव्रीहि. At 92, an **inner taddhita's लुक्** blocks टच्;
ordinary सुप् deletion does not satisfy that condition.

At 5.4.113 the body-part सक्थि/अक्षि बहुव्रीहि selects **षच्**,
not the earlier टच्. Marker identity is material for later feminine and
accent work. Two अङ्गुलि as a measure and an अङ्गुलि-shaped wooden
implement belong to different classes (86 versus 114), even where visible
forms converge. 5.4.154's optional कप् needs proof that no prescribed
compound ending applies; an unresolved earlier selector is not residual.
155 blocks that residual route, whereas 156 blocks all relevant कप्
routes, including the feminine नदी route.

## C3. Repetition and sound order

The repetition relation retains which unit was copied, why it was copied
and whether the root already had अभ्यास. Vowel-initial inputs use 6.1.2/3,
not a character-prefix copy. Root samprasāraṇa may precede repetition;
अभ्यास samprasāraṇa is a different operation. A declined optional root
branch at 6.1.30 cannot gain the same treatment again via an indiscriminate
application of 17. 37 blocks the earlier eligible यण् after the later
site's samprasāraṇa; it is not a universal one-change-per-word limit.

6.1.45 can operate on original एच् before the following affix is selected.
“Original,” “current” and “caused by this suffix” are distinct gates; the
last is decisive at 80. 59's इट् option changes the initial consonant
environment and hence the अम् candidates. Its three source histories
must not be replaced by a free two-by-two product.

## C4. Two origins, one substitute

Write a joint event as `(left_address, right_address, result_address,
rule, policy)`. Only one result is active. 6.1.85 supplies the relevant
end/beginning constituent membership; it does not license every later
letter-based operation as though the old letters survived unchanged.
6.1.86 uses the specified pre-coalescence view for षत्व/तुक्, with the
source's samprasāraṇa/ङि/इट् exceptions. The actual result is not erased
when a particular observer uses the earlier view.

## C5. Accent as a structured claim

Each accent claim records `(owner, vowel_address, value, rule, evidence,
policy, priority_dependencies)`. Resolve qualified competing claims before
applying 6.1.158 to the remainder of that पद. Default compound-final
accent at 6.1.223 does not override a qualifying first-member preservation
rule. Conversely, 6.2.1 cannot preserve an udātta/svarita that an
all-anudātta first member never had.

|Distinction|Required treatment|
|---|---|
|Lost udātta|6.1.161 needs the following element to be the actual deletion context; deletion before that suffix existed does not qualify|
|Original ending versus current string|6.1.168/182 read different case forms; 6.2.50 reads original त्-initial suffix even after इट्|
|Negative accent rule|6.1.175 blocks one ending-udātta route; independently licensed svarita can remain|
|Two simultaneous accents|6.1.200 owns initial/final तवै-community vowels; 6.2.51 owns गति accent plus तवै final vowel|
|First-member preservation|6.2.1–54 preserve established lexical/derived accent where prescribed; they do not uniformly accent the first syllable|
|Deleted प्रत्यय history|6.1.191/198 admit specified retained ending evidence; 197/199 expressly do not admit their compared deleted-affix routes|

The source supplies conditional पर/नित्य/अन्तरङ्ग/अपवाद/सतिशिष्ट
relations and explicit exceptions. No global numerical rule queue or
universal last-write-wins accent resolver is constructed here. Unaccented
witness strings identify cases; the literal archive supplies accent-bearing
evidence, including damaged readings that still require source policy.
