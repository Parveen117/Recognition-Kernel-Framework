# A discourse guard on two separate finite words

## Identify the narrowly licensed pattern

The source requires prahāsa and the specified manya-upapada relation. In its absence, ordinary 1.4.105/107 govern their respective co-referential participants. Bālamanoramā reads 1.4.106 as two prescriptions: on the other verb the expected uttama is displaced by madhyama, and on the manya verb expected madhyama is displaced by uttama with ekavat. The singular treatment belongs to the thinking verb. The rule does not universally swap first and second persons whenever an utterance sounds sarcastic.

## A manye token has no unique discourse classification

Ordinary manye can describe a speaker thinking. In this construction the indicated person of the thinker has a different relation to the agreement morphology. Nyāsa explicitly mentions two or more thinkers as the place where ekavat matters. Store their semantic number independently from the singular output. A single word, without evidence for the linked construction, leaves the pragmatic reading open. The printed food and chariot situations illustrate prahāsa, without supplying a universal test for humor and without asserting those situations in other utterances.
