# PG233 · Why two middle futures agree and the ū-marked future divides

Monty · 12 November 2026

The two middle roots that differ as *kleśate* and *kliśyate* in the present produce exactly the same words in all **36** corresponding positions of the two futures, conditional and benedictive. The lexical *e* of class one survives. In class four, *śyan* is absent from these formations and the radical *i* strengthens to *e*.

| Request | Middle, both entries | Active, ū-marked class nine |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Periphrastic future, 3sg | kleśitā | kleśitā / kleṣṭā |
| Simple future, 3sg | kleśiṣyate | kleśiṣyati / klekṣyati |
| Conditional, 3sg | akleśiṣyata | akleśiṣyat / aklekṣyat |
| Benedictive, 3sg | kleśiṣīṣṭa | kliśyāt |

In class nine, 7.2.44 makes *iṭ* optional in the relevant consonant-initial formations. On the route without *iṭ*, the consonant that follows the radical determines the cluster: *ṣṭ* before the future *t*, and *kṣ* before *s*. Mādhava directly gives both future pairs. Kṣīrasvāmin and Maitreya independently give the two periphrastic futures.

The active benedictive preserves the radical short *i*. The comparison archive has *kliśyāt*, while the Mādhavīya transcription at this spot prints *kliṣyāt*. The literal difference is retained as a source observation. Across the three profiles the tables add **108 positions and 135 selected readings**.
