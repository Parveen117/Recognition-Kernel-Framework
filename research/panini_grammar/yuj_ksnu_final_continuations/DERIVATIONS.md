# PG91 — Three final interfaces for the original roots

Monty · 9 November 2026

## H06. Blessings use yuj and kṣṇav, not the present vikaraṇa

The present śnam is absent from yuj's ārdhadhātuka blessing. P kit
leaves original **yuj** before yāsuṭ. Apply PG20's exact tuple
yāt, yāstām, yāsuḥ, yāḥ, yāstam, yāsta, yāsam, yāsva, yāsma.
There is no inserted nasal and no short iṭ before non-val y.
The separate yāsuṭ/suṭ s occurrences retain the inherited schedule.

For A, aniṭ and **1.2.11 kit** preserve root u. Before sīyuṭ,
8.2.30 gives j→g and the qualified s/velar contact yields **yukṣ**.
With PG20's A tuple the result is yukṣīṣṭa, yukṣīyāstām,
yukṣīran, yukṣīṣṭhāḥ, yukṣīyāsthām, **yukṣīdhvam**,
yukṣīya, yukṣīvahi, yukṣīmahi. The surviving dual st/sth after
ā stays dental. The 2pl dh is also dental under H04.

Kṣṇu instead receives short iṭ, 7.3.84 guṇa u→o and 6.1.78 av
before that i, giving **kṣṇaviṣ** through the first s→ṣ. Use the
same A tuple with dh/ḍh at 2pl. Thus kṣṇaviṣīṣṭa differs from
the ordinary optative kṣṇuvīta both in root-grade history and in the
retained sīyuṭ s. The long ī is augment-owned. These 63 keys have
64 readings/paths because only sam+kṣṇu 2pl has two spellings.

## H07. Simple perfect copies and ending-owned iṭ

Both roots take simple liṭ. Under 6.1.8 introduce a root copy only;
the prefixes are not copied. By 7.4.60 the yuj copy retains **yu**.
For kṣṇu, the copy retains initial k and short u; 7.4.62 makes
copy k→c, giving **cu**. Its ṣ is not before a khay consonant in
the field of 7.4.61, so that rule does not replace the selected copy
by a different initial cluster. The original root kṣṇu is retained.

All selected A liṭ endings are apit; the original roots are not
consonant-cluster-final. **1.2.5 kit** therefore blocks root guṇa.
Yuj gives **yuyuj**, with e, āte, ire, iṣe, āthe, idhve, e,
ivahe, imahe as the resolved endings. The four initial i occurrences
are separate short iṭ on their val-initial endings. Root j before
those vowels is not replaced by a velar.

Kṣṇu faces a vowel at every completed A ending, including inserted
iṭ. **6.4.77/1.1.53 uvaṅ** yields **cukṣṇuv**, followed by the
same endings. H04 adds the 2pl iḍhve alternative. This is uvaṅ on a
kit perfect, not the guṇa/av construction of kṣṇaviṣīṣṭa.
Both root and copy remain short-u histories at the relevant stages.

## H08. P yuj perfect and coincident first-person choices

Use 3.4.82's P series. Strong 3sg, thal and 1sg preserve the original
pit ending condition and take light-upadhā guṇa 7.3.86: **yuyoja**,
**yuyojitha**, **yuyoja**. The 7.2.115/116 vṛddhi conditions are
false for this consonant-final u-upadhā root even on the ṇit branch.
Neither a hypothetical *yuyauja* nor aniṭ *yuyoktha* is added.

The six weak slots have 1.2.5 kit and retain u: **yuyujatuḥ,
yuyujuḥ, yuyujathuḥ, yuyuja, yuyujiva, yuyujima**. Only the last
two receive short iṭ; thal's iṭ was already counted in the strong
group. 7.1.91 has two coincident histories at each P 1sg, as in H04.

Across all seven profiles the 63 perfect keys have **64 readings and
66 paths**: one kṣṇu 2pl spelling option and two coincident P 1sg
choices. No periphrastic ām or auxiliary verb is selected here.

## H09. P irit aṅ versus sic, A kit sic versus seṭ sic

For P yuj, construct both 3.1.57 branches before adding the common
prefix and past aṭ. Aṅ is ṅit and vowel-initial, so root u remains
and no iṭ is inserted. With the ordinary thematic secondary endings
the unprefixed aṭ-bearing tuple is

ayujat, ayujatām, ayujan, ayujaḥ, ayujatam, ayujata,
ayujam, ayujāva, ayujāma.

Its 3pl uses ant under 7.1.3, not the sic-conditioned jus. The sic
branch is aniṭ, lacks A's kit guard and receives **7.2.3 u→au**:

ayaukṣīt, ayaukṣṭām, ayaukṣuḥ, ayaukṣīḥ, ayaukṣṭam,
ayaukṣṭa, ayaukṣam, ayaukṣva, ayaukṣma.

P 3sg/2sg have separate ending **7.3.96 īṭ**. Since sic has no
preceding iṭ, 8.2.28 does not delete it. Its 3pl jus and final
visarga, addressed ṣatva and ṣṭ contact follow the retained source
schedule. There is no current śnam in either branch.

A yuj remains aniṭ and **1.2.11 kit**, hence root u. Before 3sg
ta and 2sg thās, **8.2.26** deletes the sic s between jhal sounds.
At 2pl **8.2.25** deletes it before dh. Other slots retain sic.
Ordered j→g/k and s→ṣ yield

ayukta, ayukṣātām, ayukṣata, ayukthāḥ, ayukṣāthām,
ayugdhvam, ayukṣi, ayukṣvahi, ayukṣmahi.

The g before dh remains voiced; 2pl has no iṇ-triggered ḍh.
First-singular i is its tiṅ ending, not iṭ. A receives no apṛkta-hal
īṭ. Kṣṇu has short iṭ, guṇa/av and the exact PG32 seṭ-A tuple:

akṣṇaviṣṭa, akṣṇaviṣātām, akṣṇaviṣata, akṣṇaviṣṭhāḥ,
akṣṇaviṣāthām, akṣṇavidhvam/akṣṇaviḍhvam, akṣṇaviṣi,
akṣṇaviṣvahi, akṣṇaviṣmahi.

At kṣṇu 3sg/2sg, affix-owned iṭ does not satisfy the root-short-
vowel condition of 8.2.27; the sic s survives. Its 2pl has 8.2.25
lopa and H04's independent dh option. These 63 aorist keys have
**82 readings/paths**: eighteen P aṅ/sic doubletons and one 2pl pair.

## H10. Prefix seams, complete accounting and remaining obligations

Reuse PG90's constituent order. At luṅ aṭ follows the prefix:
pra+a→prā, upa+a→upā, ni+a→nya, ud+a→uda, sam+a→sama.
At liṭ the visible yu/cu copy does not alter prefix licensing. Sam
retains its selected padānta anusvāra before the copy or root consonant;
before aṭ's vowel m remains m. No prefix or copied vowel is relabelled
as the original root. The same non-doubling/devoiced-pause policy applies.

The new family contains **168 singleton and 21 doubleton word sets**,
giving **210 readings on 189 keys**. Two singleton P perfect 1sg
sets each carry two ṇit-work histories, giving **212 paths**. All
**21 paradigms** are complete. The new keys equal PG90's missing set,
are disjoint from its 441 written keys, and close the union at **630**
keys with **651 readings/653 paths**. The cumulative register is
**18,032 + 189 = 18,221**. Wider grammar and actual execution remain
open; neither the 630-key closure nor its static checks close them.
