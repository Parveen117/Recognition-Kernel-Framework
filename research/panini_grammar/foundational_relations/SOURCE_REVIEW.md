## S2. Designations and interpretation

**1.1.75 — eastern geography and optional policy.** Kāśikā supplies the
geographical eastern-place reading used by the authored clause; its western
देवदत्त village is a negative control. Kaumudī gives an optional वृद्ध
designation for ए/ओ-initial place names. Vasu discusses the geographical and
eastern-teacher readings. These policies are not merged into a single assertion
of consensus. A different adopted policy needs a separate labelled clause.

**1.2.53–57 — interpretation is not speculative deletion.** The preceding
1.2.51–52 analysis uses लुप् and agreement. The conventional-name analysis in
53–55 does not first manufacture an affix and then delete it; name usage
supplies gender and number. Nyāsa at 53 explains the earlier statement in the
context of the ensuing negation. Clauses 56–57 concern semantic attribution
and ordinary usage, including time. They do not delete the operational
उपसर्जन rules or supply a universal semantic oracle. The five register entries
are `INTERPRETIVE_BOUNDARY`, not sound-rewriting operations.

**1.2.69 — scope of optionality.** Kāśikā, Kaumudī and Nyāsa support retention
of the neuter with optional singular treatment in the admitted mixed-gender
frame. Vasu's opening formulation can suggest that retention itself is
optional. The construction follows the qualified Sanskrit analysis: the option
belongs to एकवत्. It does not extend singular treatment to 70–71 or to an
all-neuter coordination lacking the stated contrast.

**Accent and duration.** At 1.2.32 the initial high component of स्वरित is
the stated half-mora, not half the duration of every long or pluta vowel.
At 1.4.109 the traditional half-mora convention is a grammatical annotation;
it is not an identified physical clock for the native carrier.

## S3. Pada and causative frames

**1.3.20.** Kāśikā and Kaumudī exclude mouth opening and analogous splitting,
including a cracked foot or riverbank. The other-creature-body clause reopens
आत्मनेपद in its qualified example. A simple own-body versus other-body Boolean
would lose the rule's actual frame. Kaumudī's literal `विद्यमादत्ते` is retained
in the archive; the table uses Kāśikā's `विद्यामादत्ते` witness.

**1.3.29.** The row names seven root labels and retains the two ऋ classes.
The entry ऋच्छ् is not an accidental match on a substitute spelling of ऋ.
Vasu states an aorist-only restriction for the ऋ option; that restriction is
not adopted from the general Kāśikā and Kaumudī clause. The separately stated
दृश् extension stays labelled as an extension, not an eighth root silently
inserted into the sūtra list.

**1.3.43.** This is the unprefixed अप्राप्तविभाषा outside the obligatory
क्रम् senses of 38. It does not make all the earlier obligatory choices optional.

**1.3.67.** Kāśikā requires the same action, the original non-णिच् object as
the current actor, no different object, and exclusion of recollection. Retained
णिच् history alone does not mean that an additional causal act is currently
intended. Nyāsa distinguishes the णिच् scope here from the हेतुमण्णिच् analysis
of 88. Kaumudī's examples explain the successive frames and the qualified
priority over 77/88. The table therefore requires explicit participant and
action identity evidence; ordinary causative object-to-actor wording alone is
insufficient.

Kaumudī contains the literal sequence `स्यान्नत्वनाध्याने`, whose negative
wording conflicts with the stated अनाध्याने exclusion. Preserve it as a damaged
or unresolved reading; use Kāśikā, Nyāsa and the explicit recollection control
for the adopted clause. Do not silently repair the archive or claim the
damage has been settled by a manuscript collation.

## S4. तिरस् + कृ: dependent branches, not a Cartesian product

Kāśikā at **1.4.72** lists तिरःकृत्य, तिरस्कृत्य, तिरःकृत्वा and तिरस्कृत्वा.
Kāśikā at **8.3.42** explicitly retains गति as a requirement for its optional
स् replacement before कुपु. Kaumudī at 1.4.72 lists `तिरस्कृत्य`, `तिर≍कृत्य`
and `तिरः कृत्वा`; the middle glyph remains literal in the archive and is
displayed as visarga in the authored construction.

The adopted relation has three clauses. Selecting गति admits the relevant
compound, **7.1.37 ल्यप्** and **6.1.71 तुक्**; the 8.3.42 choice then gives
तिरस्कृत्य or तिरःकृत्य. The non-गति clause retains the separate क्त्वा expression
तिरः कृत्वा and lacks that स् license. The two downstream controls are archived
and read, with their own conditions retained.

The extra Kāśikā **तिरस्कृत्वा** remains **unresolved**. It is not licensed by
freely combining a non-गति choice with the गति-conditioned sound effect.
The empty Nyāsa fields provide no further resolution. This is an explicit
adopted policy, not a claim that the variant is impossible under every analysis.
PG41's 8.3.42 already retained the required गति condition; no frozen contract
needs to be rewritten for this finding.

## S5. Query boundaries and editorial references

**2.1.2.** Kāśikā's following-member treatment is an accent view. It does not
merge words into a common षत्व/णत्व domain, and the preceding member can still
count for the own-boundary query at 8.1.55. Vasu's `कुण्डे नाटन्` splits the
कुण्डेन + अटन् example incorrectly. Its final sound-control discussion prints
स → `च` and cites `7.4.2`; Kāśikā's corresponding controls concern षत्व and
णत्व at **8.3.59** and **8.4.2**. Use the latter mapping without altering the
literal English commentary.

**2.3.10 control.** The Kāśikā field associates its अप example with
`1.4.89`. In the pinned catalogue, अप/परि in exclusion belong to **1.4.88**;
**1.4.89** is आङ्. This is a local cross-reference correction, not a global
numbering offset.

Full gaṇa passages, extra vārttikas and lexical illustrations are evidence,
not blanket implementation credit. For example, additional root clauses in
the commentary on 1.3.21 do not become new sūtra contracts merely by appearing
in the archive. Historical biological-origin and divination examples at
1.4.30 and 1.4.39 are grammatical witnesses, not physical or social classifiers.

Source disagreements, unknown lexical membership and unresolved semantic
