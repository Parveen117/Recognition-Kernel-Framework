## E2. Why 6.4.107 adds no modal reading here

In laṭ, a weak direct v/m ending can admit optional final-affix-u loss:
tan∣u∣vas yields tanvaḥ/tanuvaḥ. That premise is not preserved merely
by keeping the person/number label while changing the lakāra.

- Loṭ first person receives āṭ first. The addressed right context is
  vowel-initial, not direct v/m. The ending is also pit and u takes guṇa.
  Kaumudī 6.4.106 states the priority of āṭ before u-lopa;
  Bālamanoramā explains ni/āṭ's nitya argument and the resulting guṇa/av.
- Vidhiliṅ P has yāsuṭ, whose resolved first sound is y. Its later
  va/ma cannot be read across that augment as a direct m/v context.
- Vidhiliṅ A has the resolved **īy** after 7.2.79's non-final s-lopa.
  That vowel also separates the u from a later v/m ending.
- No remaining ordinary loṭ slot supplies the required direct m/v.
  The optional tāt ending is t-initial and supplies no such premise.

Thus **6.4.107 has no positive modal branch on D2**. This is a written
source/adjacency argument, not a conclusion from a run with zero firings.
The source's laṭ alternatives remain valid in their own earlier domain.

6.4.109's y-context u-lopa is explicitly **karoti-specific**. None of
D2's roots is that kṛ. Thus tan∣u∣yā remains tanuyā, while PG19's
kur∣u∣yā gives kuryā. No generic “weak u disappears before y” rule exists.
