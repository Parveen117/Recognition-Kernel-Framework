# PG21 — Owned nasal stems and the cluster seen by a later rule

Author: Monty. 2 November 2026.

## S1. Root and outer aṅga are different query domains

3.1.73/74 supplies śnu for the selected svādi/śru inputs; 3.1.79
supplies u for tanādi; 3.1.81 supplies śnā for kryādi; 3.1.82 admits
both śnu/śnā for its five named roots. Śnu and śnā are śit,
sārvadhātuka and apit, so their root-facing ṅitvat condition blocks
ordinary root guṇa. U is ś-less and ārdhadhātuka, with a different
root query. All three are distinct from the following tiṅ.

At that outer boundary the mood-specific ending can strengthen a final
vikaraṇa-u, preserve śnā's ā, or prescribe a weak continuation. Thus
an outer pit ending does not bypass śnu to strengthen su's root vowel.
Conversely an outer ṅit ending does not erase the separately licensed
root-guṇa policy before u. PG19's addressed-boundary discipline applies.

## S2. Root procedures retained from their exact contracts

- Su retains PG6's original ṣ marker/phonetic normalization to s;
  ci, āp and śak retain their exact roots. Śru's **śṛ** and śnu are
  jointly prescribed by 3.1.74, with the old śru owner retained.
- Pū/lū receive 7.3.80's root ū→u before their actual śit affix.
  These two are unambiguous members of PG12's selected pvādi subdivision.
  The root u and the later śnā-vowel are separate objects.
- In the four stambh roots, 6.4.24 deletes the **root-owned penultimate n**
  before the chosen ṅitvat affix. The following new n belongs to śnu/śnā
  and survives: stanbh → stabh, then stabh∣nu or stabh∣nā. The other
  three roots have the corresponding stubh/skabh/skubh factors.
- In the four G-policy roots, u permits the source-qualified 7.3.86
  query. G0 retains kṣiṇ/ṛṇ/tṛṇ/ghṛṇ; G1 gives kṣeṇ/arṇ/tarṇ/gharṇ,
  with 1.1.51's r accompaniment in the ṛ cases. No root policy is added
  to tan or to a śit-triggered input.

The original lexical markers and sounds remain in history after lopa,
replacement or shortening. None of these operations is a change of root
identity or permission to import another gaṇa's sense/pada.

## S3. A complete interface for u-ending branches

Write the owned u-ending aṅga as **Q∣u**. Q is a convenient display
factor with all constituent owners still attached. Let c(Q) mean that
the actual sounds immediately before this u form a consonant cluster.
It inspects across the root/affix seam; the value is not a property of
the printed suffix name alone.

Let J be the weak vowel-junction factor:

\[
 J(Q,\nu)=
 \begin{cases}
 Qv&\nu=śnu,\text{ multiple-vowel aṅga and }\neg c(Q),\quad6.4.87;\\
 Quv&\nu=śnu,\ c(Q),\quad6.4.77;\\
 Qv&\nu=u,\quad6.1.77.
 \end{cases}
\]

Every admitted śnu input has the needed multiple-vowel aṅga. The u
of a tanādi u-affix is not the śnu-u or a root-final u of 6.4.77.
In particular **arṇ∣u∣anti → arṇvanti** uses 6.1.77, as Bālamanoramā
2.4.79 explicitly says. A consonant cluster alone cannot decide uv versus v.

For the ordinary loṭ P 2sg define the source-derived hi readout

\[
 H(Q)=\begin{cases}Qu&\neg c(Q),\\Quhi&c(Q).\end{cases}
\]

This is 6.4.106's **affix-u** condition. Both affix identity and the
actual cluster matter; it is not an instruction to remove any final hi.
The Vedic option to retain hi is outside D4.

teaching jh cannot force an early ī into the eventual vowel-initial plural.

## S6. Finite stem theorem

**Theorem S6.** Tables S4–S5 exhaust the stem branches for D2's
selected lexical/pada/source inputs. Their actual owner/cluster views
determine E's continuation without guessing from a laṭ output.

**Proof.** The eight PG6 roots have one branch each. Each of the five
3.1.82 roots has exactly its two affixes. Each of the four further
tanādi roots has one branch for each admitted G policy. S2 gives their
root operations; S3 resolves the disjoint affix-u junction cases; S5
resolves śnā. These root groups are disjoint, so no input acquires
both an unsupported G choice and an unsupported affix option. □
