# PG923 · The remaining actor categories of √chid

PG7 records rudhādi 07.0003, *छिदिँर् द्वैधीकरणे*, as an ubhayapada root and owns its two selected present grids. PG22 owns the corresponding imperative and ordinary optative grids. The root entry in the Mādhavīyadhātuvṛtti independently prints the same root and sense, gives *chinatti* and says that its finite behaviour is like *bhinatti*. The published conjugation table labels both padas and all ten categories. This stage constructs only the seven categories not previously owned: laṅ, lṛṭ, luṭ, liṭ, luṅ, āśīrliṅ and lṛṅ. ## Construction and ownership

**Imperfect.** Laṅ takes aṭ, not a preverb. The class-7 śnam infix follows the root vowel under 3.1.78. Strong P singular cells retain its vowel; weak cells delete it and preserve the infix nasal before root-final d. The table distinguishes dental-cluster and optional deletion histories in *acchintām/acchinttām*, *acchintthāḥ/acchinthāḥ* and *acchinddhvam/acchindhvam*. Final t/d readings and the P second-singular visarga are boundary readings of the same typed cells. The same surface *acchinta/acchintta* at P 2pl and A 3sg retains different ending and pada histories.

**Futures and conditional.** This root is aniṭ in the cited tradition: the root meets sya directly. Guṇa i gives e, and the dental+sibilant route yields *chetsya-*; lṛṭ therefore begins *chetsyati/chetsyate*. Luṭ uses the agentive future base *chettā-*. The P and A third-person words coincide, but their ending schedules and actor-result premises do not. Lṛṅ adds aṭ to the future base and secondary endings; only P 3sg has the source-recorded pause pair *acchetsyat/acchetsyad*.

**Perfect.** Reduplication gives *ci-cchid*. Strong P singular cells have e in *ciccheda/ciccheditha*; the other P cells and all A cells retain i. The 1.2.5 example *cicchidatuḥ* independently confirms the kit treatment of the non-pit perfect ending in precisely this root. Ending-owned i in *cicchidhire* and augment i in *cicchididhve* remain separate events. Identical P 3sg/1sg and A 3sg/1sg words remain four typed positions.

**Aorist.** The lexical ir marker admits the inherited P aṅ alternative, producing the short path *acchidat/acchidad, acchidatām, acchidan*. The competing P sic path strengthens i to ai: *acchaitsīt/acchaitsīd*, with the remaining consonant and vowel junctions recorded cell by cell. Thus every P cell has two branch readings and P 3sg has two additional pause readings, for 20 readings over nine positions. A does not inherit the P aṅ guard. Its sic route begins *acchitta, acchitsātām, acchitsata*; the root vowel remains unstrengthened under the middle kit condition. Patient/passive ciṇ, causative and prefixed requests are outside this stage.

**Blessing.** P uses the yāsuṭ path, giving *chidyāt/chidyād* at 3sg and one reading elsewhere. A has its separate sīyuṭ series, beginning *chitsīṣṭa*. These are not the PG22 ordinary optative words *chindyāt/chindīta*: the absence of present-system śnam and the marker histories remain visible.

## Full carrier and zero-specialization

Reuse PG14’s free involutive coefficient algebra B and paired right modules on ordered histories. A basis input retains source, lexical ID, sense, preverb, voice, actor-result premise, pada, category, person, number, augment, reduplication, root grade, present infix, actual cli replacement, kit status, suffix and ending owners, junction choice and boundary reading. For each licensed operation e from history h to k define

`T_e(e_h b) = e_k(1 + z_e)b`.

Every lexical, semantic, pada and category guard has zero incidence outside its typed domain; the complementary channel retains the rejected labels. With the input and output maps of their respective module types,

`π_out(T_e(e_h b)) = e_k ε(b) = T_e,0(π_in(e_h b))`.

Both sides are zero outside the guard. Linearity handles alternative successors, ordered composition gives the result for the complete word path, and the involution gives it for adjoints and the retained pairing. This proves typed zero-specialization on the extension’s actual domain without replacing the full carrier by its terminal spelling. Equal words in two padas or persons therefore do not erase source, result, ending or history ownership.
