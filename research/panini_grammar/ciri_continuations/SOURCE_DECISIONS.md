# PG64 — Three textual decisions

Monty · 6 November 2026

## H01. Jiri under a second reading of the svādi restriction

The *Dhātukāvya* and its commentary, in *Kāvyamālā*, tenth collection, third edition (Nirṇayasāgara, Bombay, 1915), put verse 2.71 and its explanation on printed p. 190, PDF leaf 192. The poem itself has **jiriṇvan**. The commentator parses it as jiri with śatṛ, **जिरेः शता**, and glosses the activity as destroying. The same page explicitly reports a rival division of the lexical text. Some teachers read **छन्दस्यह व्याप्तौ** as an entry for *ah* and recognize the group beginning with *dagh* in ordinary usage too. [Printed page](DHATUKAVYA_P190.jpg) · [Transcription revision](https://sa.wikisource.org/?oldid=296454)

The steps licensing P11 have different evidential roles.

| Identity | The page names jiri, parses jiriṇvan and groups jiri with the roots in the sense hiṃsā. KRM 603 specifies svādi, P, two-vowel behavior and the comparison to ciri. | The selected entry is svādi jiri, associated with 1050035. |
| Scope | The commentator attributes the *ah*-only restriction and wider *daghādi* usage to some teachers. Earlier on p. 190 the commentator also records the narrower chāndasa reading with a specific observation about kṣi. | Retain both readings, with a named report and a qualifier on the wider one. |

Kaumudī's list at 7.4.72 independently places dagh before jiri. | Jiri belongs to the stated range on the aligned svādi order. | Word construction | The earlier Kāśikā witness at 8.2.78 uses jiriṇoti. KRM pp. 571–572 describes the present participle with śnu and its u → v junction. | On the reported wider policy the ordinary kartari present-system rules apply. Four paradigms occupy 36 positions. |

This is an explicit inference from the commentator's reported scope and the identified entry's membership. The poem directly attests the participle. The finite words are our written extensions under that policy. KRM p. 572 compares **forms**, and KRM 603 retains its own chāndasa label. The separately qualified ciri policy in PG63 comes from KRM 526. All three decisions remain addressable independently. Earlier teachers behind the *Dhātukāvya* report are unnamed. No other tail entry receives position credit through this range in PG64.

## H02. What the ṛkṣi witnesses settle

The *Puruṣakāra* of Kṛṣṇalīlāśuka on Deva's *Daiva*, edited by T. Gaṇapati Śāstrī, Trivandrum Sanskrit Series I (1905), printed p. 24, PDF leaf 49, was collated directly. It quotes Kṣīrasvāmin's reading ṛkṣi and his present **ṛṅkṣṇoti** and perfect **ānṛṅkṣa**. This resolves PG63's outstanding check on the cited book. Its historical source for these two forms is Kṣīrasvāmin himself, so the quotation and its source constitute a single line of testimony. On this page Maitreya's reported additional bhāṣā usage attaches to *kṣi*. [Printed page](PURUSAKARA_P24.jpg) · [Transcription revision](https://sa.wikisource.org/?oldid=242824)

The *Dhātukāvya* commentator on 2.71 also isolates **ṛkṣiṇvatām**, analyzes the alternate initial entry **ṛkṣi**, and assigns śatṛ. The printed word keeps i between ṣ and the śnu nasal, with no root nasal before k. The verse line is joined to the preceding word. The commentary's separated lemma is the addressed evidence. It agrees in these two local features with the Kaumudī present ṛkṣiṇoti. This gives positive support for an i-bearing present-system branch alongside the idit/num account of Kṣīrataraṅgiṇī. [Source page](DHATUKAVYA_P190.jpg)

| Witness | Material visible in the selected reading | Admission in this packet |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Modern 1050038 | Root headword ṛkṣ, teaching ṛkṣi with pronunciation i | Preserve the source-relative analysis. |
| Kaumudī and *Dhātukāvya* commentary | ṛkṣiṇoti and ṛkṣiṇvatām both keep i immediately before the affix nasal | A present and a participial witness. |
| Kṣīrasvāmin as quoted by *Puruṣakāra* | ṛṅkṣṇoti and ānṛṅkṣa, with root nasal before k | Direct confirmation of the quotation. PG63 supplies the editor's idit/num account. |

The source passages also discuss a split ri/kṣi reading beside the single ṛkṣi entry. Combining the wider *daghādi* report with the separate variant segmentation would require evidence about which readings belong to the same teaching tradition. Wider finite requests for ṛkṣi therefore stay open. The two cited participles and the repeated perfect supply zero finite-position credit.

## H03. The printed continuation of KRM 526

The new collation uses printed pp. 512–513 of entry 526 and p. 572 of entry 603. PG63 supplies the opening pages, edition and digitization attribution. The five primary pages relevant to the new evidence (three KRM leaves, *Dhātukāvya* p. 190 and *Puruṣakāra* p. 24) have exact edition/file addresses in the [source pins](SOURCE_PINS.json). The KRM PDFs and the full Cologne electronic text are externally attributed and pinned. The two archival images in this packet are page renderings of the identified public-domain books.

1. On p. 512 the three tṛ-type columns and the infinitives give distinct basic, causative and desiderative factors. The agent noun **cirayitā** matches the spelling of a luṭ third singular. Its noun entry supplies stem evidence. The finite interpretation requires the luṭ ending construction including 2.4.85.
2. The passive participle row displays **cirīyamāṇaḥ**, **cirāyyamāṇaḥ** and **cicirayiṣyamāṇaḥ**. Footnote 7 explicitly connects the basic long ī with yak and 7.4.25. These are controls for the three different passive aṅgas in P2, P7 and P10.
3. In the middle future participle column p. 512 actually prints an additional *iṣ*, including **cirāyayiṣiṣyan**. The archive preserves this diplomatic reading. For the simple single-ṇic finite future the addressed sequence is root āy, ṇic ay, one iṭ, one sya. It gives **cirāyayiṣyati**. In the san future the inner san and the outer sya supply two separate sibilants. The cause of the printed middle-column reading remains an editorial question. It supplies no second finite variant on the selected derivation.
4. Footnote 2 on p. 512 cites “svādibhyaḥ” as **3.1.63**. The sūtrapāṭha assigns the śnu prescription to **3.1.73**, which the jiri continuation cites correctly. The digital p. 512 transliteration `cirRivan` also differs from the scan's **चिरिण्वन्**. Printed readings, digital readings and adopted rule addresses are recorded separately.
5. Page 513 ends the ciri entry before the next headword. Its prefixed and repeated adverbial examples belong to a different construction. Page 572 completes the jiri comparison, identifies śnu and the u → v contact, and cites *Dhātukāvya* 2.71. The poem and commentary themselves were collated on their own page.
