## H06. Root i at three different boundaries

Basic kartari P āśīrliṅ first receives the 3.4.116 ārdhadhātuka designation and the whole ending's 3.4.104 kit with yāsuṭ. Its initial y excludes the val-initial iṭ prescription. At this y-initial, kṅit, non-kṛt, non-sārvadhātuka boundary, 7.4.25 lengthens the root i. The resulting interface is **cirī | yās**. PG20 E1–E4 derives the nine endings, including the distinct yāsuṭ and suṭ identities in **cirīyāt** and **cirīyāstām**. Kāśikā 7.4.25 contrasts this environment with the present-system optative.

In basic karmaṇi, 3.1.67 introduces **yak** before the sārvadhātuka ending. The k-marker belongs to yak. That affix is ārdhadhātuka under 3.4.114 at its root-facing seam. Its y therefore gives the same lengthening, this time in **cirī | ya**. Laṭ A gives **cirīyate** and laṅ gives **acirīyata**. The long ī has a different following owner from the blessing's ī. KRM footnote 7 prints the yak/7.4.25 argument.

P1's luṭ and lṛṅ instead select 3.1.33's tās or sya. Each is val-initial and takes an owned iṭ under 7.2.35. Ordinary guṇa changes root i → e under 7.3.84. Before the augment's vowel 6.1.78 gives e → ay. Thus **ciray | i | tās** and **a | ciray | i | sya** yield **cirayitā** and **acirayiṣyat**. The iṭ retains the 1.1.6 restriction against grade on the augment itself. The distinct tiṅ endings are supplied by H09.

## H07. Ṇic owns one i and the root owns āy

Start with *ciri* and the causation frame of H04. Insert ṇic under 3.1.26 and retain both markers when removing their pronunciation. Its ṇit status gives vṛddhi of root-final i under 7.2.115, **i → ai**. Before the surviving ṇic-i, 6.1.78 gives **ai → āy**. The derived dhātu designated by 3.1.32 is **cirāy | i**, with the first y belonging to the root. The first i stays short. In the lexical view r is followed by the second i, so the hal-followed r condition at 8.2.78 fails (PG63 H02).

## H09. Ending interfaces and representative complete junctions

Use the source-pinned PG19 E2–E7 for ordinary thematic loṭ/vidhiliṅ, PG16 E1–E6 for the thematic primary and tās endings, and the applicable secondary ending rows of PG31 E1–E4. For laṅ the same secondary substitutions apply because the recipient lakāra is ṅit, with the present-system aṅga and its own temporal evidence. For lṛṅ the recipient sits outside sya. Each of P2–P10 retains the identity of its last a even when the ending readout coincides.

- In P3 and P8, ending-owned loṭ āṭ and ni give **cirāyayāṇi** and **cicirayiṣāṇi**. In the former the r is separated from n only by interveners allowed by 8.4.2. In the latter ṣ supplies the trigger. For -anti/-antām the nasal before t instead undergoes the ordered 8.3.24/8.4.58 readout already proved in PG19.
- In P7, **cirāyyete**, **cirāyyetām** and **cirāyyeyātām** retain different dual ending routes. Vidhiliṅ has sīyuṭ with the sārvadhātuka 7.2.79 loss of nonfinal s and retention of y before the vowel. In laṅ the dual uses 7.2.81 and 6.1.66 at its ending after initial aṭ, giving **acirāyyetām**.
- In P5–P6 luṭ, 2.4.85 selects ḍā/rau/ras by third-person number in either pada. First singular P retains **-āsmi**. A first singular has the 7.4.52 tās-s → h route giving **cirāyayitāhe**, A second dual keeps **-āsāthe**, and the adopted Kaumudī 8.2.25 gives A second plural **-ādhve**. A ṭit substitution and an a+i junction can both print e elsewhere. The former belongs to lṛṭ A first singular, the latter to laṅ/lṛṅ.
- In P9, the future iṭ belongs to sya even if the conditional prefix aṭ and the first-person ending are both present. For 1sg **acicirayiṣiṣyam**, follow aṭ, the copied/graded root, san, outer iṭ, sya and mip → am. 6.1.97 resolves the final a+a contact. P9's 1du long ā instead follows the thematic a before v under 7.3.101.
