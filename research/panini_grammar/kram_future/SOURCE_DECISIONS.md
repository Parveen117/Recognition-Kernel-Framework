## H02. Rule 7.2.36 is a causal restriction, not a lexical seṭ override everywhere

Kāśikā calls **snu-kramor anātmanepada-nimitte** a niyama: both roots would otherwise receive iṭ from their udātta teaching. It distinguishes actual root-dependent A from mere eligibility for A. Nyāsa develops that distinction through the meaning of nimitta. For this packet's basic kram, the selected A licences really depend on the root with its supplied contextual qualifications, so the restriction blocks root-facing iṭ. In the selected bare P branch, the restriction does not block 7.2.35.

Padamañjarī expressly gives **anupasargād vā — kraṃsyate** while discussing why the root remains the A cause even when meaning conditions refine its domain. Optional A therefore does not make iṭ independently optional inside that A position. The ordinary bare request has kramiṣyati in P and kraṃsyate in A; these are different pada positions, each with one reading in this packet.

The word nimitta cannot be replaced by immediate phonetic adjacency to an A ending: the source discusses intervening sya and sīyuṭ and san-dependent examples. Conversely, a kyaṅ-derived A word need not have the underlying snu root as its A cause. Padamañjarī raises further questions about san/ṇic and a same-word vārttika formulation. Those derived formations remain outside the present basic domain; this packet does not claim to settle their whole cause calculus.

## H03. Finite luṭ is not the agent-noun vārttika

Kāśikā 7.2.36 separately extends the prohibition to a kartari kṛt suffix in a qualified A domain even though no A ending is actually present. Padamañjarī and Siddhāntakaumudī's procedure under **3.1.133** record different readings of that kṛt qualification for the optionally A bare root: one admits only kramitā, another also krantā. These are **kṛt-policy alternatives**, not optional iṭ branches to import into this finite luṭ table.

Here first select the finite pada, then evaluate 7.2.35/36 at the root-facing tās. P has kram+i+tās; admitted A has kram+tās. Luṭ third-person ḍā/rau/ras substitution does not undo that decision. Its retained original pada is evidence for the cause query, while the substitute has its own direct-target behaviour under the ending rules. Do not use the printed word krantā alone to decide whether the source was finite luṭ or an agent noun.
