# PG972 · Water that does not dry up

Varāhamihira supplies a physical referent for aśoṣya. At Bṛhatsaṃhitā 53.14 in the GRETIL edition, *jalam* is the subject of *bhavati*. The adjective *aśoṣyam* agrees with the water in the neuter nominative singular. At 53.75 the same adjective goes with neuter *ambhas* in a description of saline water. The water passages share one inflectional key. They locate the second citation in MW entry 19629 independently of the Gītā. The Sanskrit says water in both places. A pond is an illustration supplied by the dictionary. At 45.46 and 96.05 the genitive plural *aśoṣyāṇām* occurs in descriptions of an exceptional drying up. The first passage mentions rivers and lakes, and the latter leaves the noun implicit in this phrase. The genitive plural has the same form in all three genders here. These two occurrences alone cannot identify the gender. [Varāhamihira, text with edition numbering](https://gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de/gretil/corpustei/transformations/html/sa_varAhamihira-bRhatsaMhitA.htm)

**A source for the kṛtya of akledya.** The Vyoma Sanskrit lesson on Gītā 2.24 (PDF p. 28, bottom grammar table) gives the patient-affix analysis chid + ṇyat and applies the pattern to dah, klid and śuṣ. It also separates all four privatives as nañ compounds. This establishes an explicit pedagogical ṇyat reading for klid, the missing attribution at PG971. Preserve the source difference: Vācaspatyam prints yat for chid and gives an intervening ṇic for śuṣ, whereas the lesson displays the root correspondences in one abbreviated row. The lesson gives causative infinitives in its glosses at PDF p. 30. A semantic paraphrase cannot by itself prove an unprinted derivational step, and the abbreviated row cannot disprove a causal history. No new surface form is credited for these affix decisions. ## Feminine and neuter case requests

The 1872 Monier-Williams gives masculine, feminine ā and neuter am for all four adjectives. See records 152, 415, 1015 and 7228. The last places aśoṣya beside aśoṣaṇīya. The latter is a separate lexical formation. Its extra syllables cannot enter the aśoṣya panel. These gender licences admit formal agreement with an appropriate referent. Only the neuter water examples here fix the gender from an explicit agreeing noun and its clause. [MW 1872 transcription](https://github.com/sanskrit-lexicon/csl-orig/blob/776f75662b804497939e179f2ae1468b83ad55a7/v02/mw72/mw72.txt)

Take B from **acchedy, adāhy, akledy, aśoṣy** and append each displayed ending to obtain a word. N is ṇ after aśoṣy and n elsewhere in these panels. The feminine ā is the realized ṭāp, with the relevant shorter or substituted grade before an ending. The neuter takes no ṭāp. Its direct plural nasal is **num** under 7.1.72 on the śi path. The genitive plural nasal belongs to nuṭ, and the neuter instrumental singular nasal to the ina substitution. The long ā of the feminine locative plural leaves s dental. Compare *aśoṣyāsu* with neuter *aśoṣyeṣu*. In the genitive plural the two genders coincide at *aśoṣyāṇām*. A shared e in feminine and neuter direct dual similarly conceals distinct gender and affix histories. The four feminine panels supply 96 positions and 96 readings. The four neuter panels supply 96 positions and 100 readings, with the inherited voiced/devoiced pause pair in each ablative singular. The feminine and neuter total is 192 new positions and 196 readings. The 96 masculine positions at PG971 and the two genitive plural citations receive zero further credit.
