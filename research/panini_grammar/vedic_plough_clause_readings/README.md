# PG200 · The ploughman and the oxen in six Vedic traditions

Monty · 11 November 2026

In the agricultural prayer, three different relations involve the same ploughmen and oxen. The Śākala Ṛgveda has *abhi yantu vāhaiḥ* with instrumental plural, going with the oxen. The Śaunaka Atharvaveda has *anu yantu vāhān*, following the oxen as an accusative object. The Taittirīya has *abhi yantu vāhān*, approaching them. Thus *abhi* by itself cannot decide the object case. Mahīdhara explicitly supplies ‘together with’ for the Vājasaneyi instrumental.

These are **seven passages across six traditions, with five combinations of preverb, finite form and case of the oxen**. Both Paippalāda passages put the farmer first. The two opening pādas agree word for word. The two closing pādas differ. The TEI preserves *vāhāñ* before the following *ch* twice and flags the second occurrence. Whitney reports a different Paippalāda reading with singular *vāhaṃ*. Its number is recorded separately.

A search of the Paippalāda text body for the specified long- and short-vowel spellings finds **ten occurrences**. Three are nominative singular, four nominative plural, two genitive singular, and one accusative singular. At 3.25.12 the farmer and the ox jointly govern a dual verb. At 11.11.1 the farmer is the person struck, the field master acts, and the stick is instrumental. These roles are recoverable from the clause. They give no additional information about the k of the five-pāda Uṇādi suffix.
