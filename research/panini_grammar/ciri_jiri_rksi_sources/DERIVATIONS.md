## H01 — A named classical policy for ciri, not for the whole tail

Kṛdantarūpamālā, entry **526**, printed page **511**, explicitly
reports classical use according to some teachers, marks ciri as P/seṭ,
and states that its second i belongs to the two-vowel root. The page
image was inspected, not inferred from a search snippet. The modern
1050034 note can now be traced to that identified printed witness.

| Stage | Input, operation and retained qualification |
| --- | --- |
| L1 | Select `KRM526_REPORTED_BHASHA_CIRI`, ciri, svādi, हिंसायाम्, P; associate modern 1050034 without replacing its Vedic tag. |
| L2 | Admit classical, non-Mantra requests under this named reported policy. Retain `according_to_some` and the unidentified earlier teachers. |
| L3 | Preserve c-i-r-i as the root. The second i is neither a nasal it nor a pronunciation aid; 1.3.2/9 does not delete it. |
| L4 | Require the ordinary kartari/context/pada conditions of the requested construction. Source admission is only one of its gates. |

This is a source-qualified addition. Kāśikā 8.2.78 independently
uses ciri/jiri present words with both vowels; it does not by itself
grant a new classical register. Kṣīrataraṅgiṇī pp. 242–243 explicitly
retains a restricted chāndasa scope. Neither source is silently replaced.

Reuse only PG21's śnu interface and its resolved I/O ending proof,
not its historical tanādi-u policy. Thus the ordinary imperative is
**ciriṇu**, the first-person imperative is **ciriṇavāni**, and the
optative begins **ciriṇuyāt**. Kāśikā 8.2.78 expressly uses
**ciriṇoti/jiriṇoti** as controls: r followed by root i is not the
hal-followed r required for lengthening. Do not produce cīriṇoti.

Laṭ 2sg's ending s becomes ṣ after the vikaraṇa o by 8.3.59:
**ciriṇoṣi**. In the later -anti/-antu and -āni endings, retain the
PG21 dental-n readout: the affix's ṇ is not permission to retroflex
every later n. The table has eleven laṭ readings, nine loṭ readings
and nine vidhiliṅ readings over twenty-seven positions.

There are 10 kṛ morphological readings, 9 bhū and 9 as. The first

## H08 — Jiri retains two vowels and its own register gate

KRM entry **603**, printed page **571**, explicitly labels jiri
chāndasa and excludes ordinary yaṅ because of anekāc status. Its
continuation compares forms to ciri. That form-level comparison does
not state that ciri's separately reported classical admission transfers.
The inspected Kṣīra passage likewise retains both root vowels and

## C01 — A modern Boolean tag is not the source policy

The same tagged ciri is held by PG60's selected SK policy and admitted
by PG63's named KRM report. Keep both policies. The later admission
does not turn the earlier source-relative withholding into a universal
error, or erase ciri's tag in the modern object.

## C02 — Equal letters do not imply equal owners

Root-final i, pronunciation-only i, marker i, iṭ and an auxiliary's iṭ
have distinct sources and effects. Neither a global final-i deletion
nor a global final-i retention reproduces the three ṛkṣi records.
The two ciri vowels remain visible to anekāc even after later guṇa.

## C03 — Non-Mantra is necessary for this ām policy, not sufficient

A non-Mantra request still needs lexical/register admission and the
anekāc or other specified ām license. Conversely, chandas alone does
not decide the non-Mantra question. Preserve these independent cuts.

## C04 — Yaṅ does not follow merely from having a san paradigm

3.1.22 requires ekāc and hal-initial input, apart from its separately
named extensions. Ciri/jiri fail ekāc. Their construction of san does
not remove a vowel or grant one of those named yaṅ exceptions.

## C05 — Grammar, readout and counting have different projections

The perfect has 3 auxiliary choices, 28 morphological readings and

words and conditional sketches add zero; no source-held request is
