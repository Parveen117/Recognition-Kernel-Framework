# PG244 · The merged causative aorist and two readings of the printed example

Monty · 12 November 2026

The two written predications ‘see a form’ and ‘hear a verse’ share **nyaśīśamat** in the causative aorist. The root vowel shortens before *caṅ*, while the reduplicated syllable takes long *ī*. The complete nine-person sets coincide. The perfect retains the distinction on its first member, as in **niśāmayāñcakāra** and **niśamayāñcakāra**. Each also admits *as* and *bhū* as auxiliaries.

| Evidence | With *vacas*, an utterance | With *rūpa*, a form |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mādhavīya, printed p. 434 | niśamayati vacaḥ | niśāmayati rūpam |
| Mādhavīya, electronic entry 842 | niśāmayati vacaḥ | niśāmayati rūpam |

An enlarged comparison of the adjacent lines corrects PG243’s first reading of the printed word. The medial long-ā sign appears in the printed example with *rūpam*. The example with *vacas* has short *a*, consistent with the following explanation invoking *mit*. The XML independently transmits long *ā* there. PG243’s current source record and prose carry the correction. Its 72 person–number positions follow Puruṣakāra and keep the same forms.

Across the ten lakāras, **81 of 90 paired positions** have disjoint sets of written forms. The other nine are the *caṅ* aorist. Counting each sense-qualified request once gives **180 of 180 positions**, of which 36 are newly published here. A parser can associate the shared aorist with both histories without treating the different perfect auxiliaries as different positions.
