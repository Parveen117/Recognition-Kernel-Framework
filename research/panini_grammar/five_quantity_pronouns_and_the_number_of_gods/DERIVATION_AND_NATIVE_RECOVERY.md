# PG788 · Quantity, likeness and count

Rule 5.2.39 attaches vatup to a nominative source qualifying a genitive bearer with regard to quantity. The repository’s 6.3.91 substitutes ā in the pronominal base. The strong masculine nominative in -vān uses the ugit nasal and lengthening. The genitive in -vataḥ lacks that strong nominative shape. The Nyāsa distinguishes overall amount from the standard of measurement. Thus tanmātra remains available when the demonstrative expresses the standard. A relative quantity can in turn serve as a standard in yāvanmātra. This extra step has an additional source and bearer history. Gītā 2.46 pairs the extent of an artha in the relative with the corresponding extent in the main clause; the two masculine nominatives have different inputs. RV 10.90.3 supplies deictic etāvān with mahimā.

The supplementary Vedic rule changes the semantic input to likeness. Yuṣmad and asmad give tvāvat and māvat through their personal-pronoun replacements and ā. RV 7.32.23 addresses Indra and denies the existence of another such one. The Pada gives nominative tvāvān. RV 8.46.1 has a genitive, despite the adjacent vocatives. Sāyaṇa first explains comparison, then the contextual identification with Indra. In RV 1.142.2 he explicitly takes māvataḥ with the sacrificing agent and cites the likeness supplement. These three identifications use the Pada and the clause, rather than reading a case off a dictionary stem.

At 5.2.40 record either the inference of vatup from the otherwise idle substitution or the two successive operations obtained by dividing the rule. Both apply the gha replacement to initial v of the affix. Keep separate the corresponding īś/kī base orders at 6.3.90, vowel loss under 6.4.148 and the 7.1.2 iy replacement for the affix-initial gha. Bhāgavata 6.16.42 queries the extent of a benefit using kiyān. Bhāgavata 10.38.27 points to an extent using iyān. Both qualify artha. Forms after upa in TS 5.6.8 instead concern the act of approaching. Its Pada explicitly separates iyāt.

Under 5.2.41 an actual request for a number admits ḍati or vatup. For kati, retain ṭi-lopa on kim, the ṣaṭ designation at 1.1.25 and the loss of jas at 7.1.22. The alternative retains agreement on kiyantaḥ. The Kāśikā offers two analyses of the compound in the sūtra; both require numerical delimitation. Its dismissive example with ten already specified is outside this request. In Bṛhadāraṇyaka 3.9.1 the dialogue asks how many gods, cites how many are named in a litany, and repeats the question until the answer is one. The concluding katame changes the request to identification. Repeated inflected words and the contextual glide of katy eva do not add positions.

RV 8.46.1 also supplies smasi, with vayam as subject. The inherited PG8 as (02.0060) laṭ P first plural uses the weak stem s- under 6.4.111 and ordinary mas. The Vedic final i at 7.1.46 gives s-mas-i. Sāyaṇa glosses this with the already owned smaḥ. One additional finite reading attaches to one existing request, with zero new finite positions.
