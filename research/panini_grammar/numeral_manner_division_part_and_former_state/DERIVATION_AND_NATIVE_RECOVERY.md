# PG810 · Derivation and native recovery

Rule 42 assigns `dhā` to a numeral only when it classifies the manner of an action. Rule 43 reuses it for a change in the numerical distribution of a substance; `ekadhā` and `pañcadhā` therefore gain new relation readings, while displayed `aṣṭadhā` is a new position. Rule 44 optionally replaces `dhā` after `eka` by `dhyamuñ` in both relations. Rules 45–46 distinguish optional `dhamuñ` (`dvaidham/traidham`) from `edhāc` (`dvedhā/tredhā`), and retain the vārttika's further `ḍa` histories in `matidvaidhāni/matitraidhāni`.

Rule 47 licenses `pāśap` only when the very quality responsible for the designation is censured: a technically proficient grammarian or sacrificer with unrelated bad conduct is an explicit control. Rules 48–50 construct the part relation rather than re-crediting ordinal completion. The surface may remain identical while the affix changes accent; sixth and eighth also admit `ñ` forms `ṣāṣṭha/āṣṭama`. Rule 51 specializes sixth to a measure and eighth to an animal body-part, preserving `kan/luk` ownership and the ordinary part alternatives.

Rule 52 uses nonnumeral `eka` “without companion,” with `ākini`, `kan` and lopa alternatives; the cited dual and plural prove that the result is not merely numeral singular. Rule 53 makes former-state `caraṭ`, including its feminine owner, while rule 54 changes the input to a genitive possessor and gives former ownership through `rūpya/caraṭ` alternatives.
