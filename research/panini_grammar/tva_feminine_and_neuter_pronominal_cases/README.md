# PG910 · Feminine and neuter cases of indefinite *tva*

Whitney §503b assigns older-language *tva* ‘one, another’ to the ordinary pronominal declension. His §496 distinguishes the feminine singular *sy*, the masculine/neuter *sma*, the regular dual and the genitive plural in *s*. Macdonell §396 inventories two occurring feminine forms. PG907 traced both to their verses. A grammatical pattern and an inventory of occurrences answer different questions. Here the pattern supplies an unaccented written construction. Each row identifies its evidence. ## Ending histories

Feminine ṭāp gives *tvā*. In NOM sg su disappears under 6.1.68. ACC sg *am* preserves its m after 6.1.107. NOM and ACC dual replace their respective endings with long śī at 7.1.18, giving *tve*. Their plurals preserve separate jas/śas paths to *tvāḥ*. Before instrumental singular ā and dual *os*, the āp → e operation (7.3.105) precedes the e → ay glide (6.1.78), giving *tvayā* and *tvayoḥ*. Long ā remains before the plural bh-endings, dual *bhyām* and locative plural *su*. Genitive plural *tvāsām* preserves dental s from suṭ (7.1.52), distinct from the case ending *su* in *tvāsu*.

In the four feminine singular ṅit obliques 7.3.114 shortens the āp vowel and supplies syāṭ. With dative e, vṛddhi gives *tvasyai*. With ablative/genitive as the long vowels coalesce and the final s has the ordinary pause output *tvasyāḥ*. Original locative ṅi takes ām at 7.3.116. Its ṅit history survives and yields *tvasyām*. Kāśikā at 7.1.52 limits the competing suṭ to genitive plural. Bālamanoramā at 7.3.114 instead resolves the locative competition by priority. Both histories remain available for the one written locative. [PG419 commentary and rules](../eka_singular_pronominal_endings_and_feminine_syad/DERIVATION_AND_NATIVE_RECOVERY.md)

Neuter NOM/ACC dual instead takes long śī by 7.1.19. In the plural, short śi at 7.1.20 triggers num at 7.1.72 and vowel lengthening at 6.4.8. The stem *tva* supplies no retroflexion trigger, hence *tvāni* for each original ending. The dental n in instrumental *tvena* belongs to ina, a different operation. In the obliques, *smai/smāt/smin* and genitive *sya* attach to short a. The neuter ablative pause retains PG905’s two allowed terminal readings by 8.4.56. Before dual *bhyām* the stem lengthens. Instrumental plural takes ais. Before plural bhyas and su, and before genitive plural suṭ + ām, the neuter has e. It conditions ṣ in *tveṣu, tveṣām*, contrasting with the dental s after feminine ā. Both *os* cases have the ay glide.
