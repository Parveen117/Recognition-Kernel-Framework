## H02. Mandatory meanings and the local prefix restriction

Kāśikā/Siddhāntakaumudī 1.3.38 distinguish **vṛtti = unimpeded functioning**, **sarga = enthusiasm/effort**, and **tāyana = growth/expansion**. They are not synonyms for all walking. The quoted study-effort, functioning intellect and expanding śāstra examples discharge different meaning certificates.

Rule 1.3.39 is an **upasarga-niyama for that 1.3.38 licence**: in those senses a prefixed kram admits A from this channel only with upa or parā. It does not make every upa/parā use A, nor prohibit the independently licensed āṅ/vi/pra contexts of 1.3.40–42. Kāśikā contrasts upakrāmati/parākrāmati outside the retained meanings and saṃkrāmati for an excluded prefix within the relevant comparison.

The counted parā profile selects sarga. Unprefixed mandatory-A 1.3.38 meanings reuse the BARE_ORDINARY_A morphological table through separate semantic admissions, and upa under 1.3.39 reuses UPA_BEGIN's morphology through its own admission. These are **zero-position-credit aliases**, not assertions that effort, beginning and ordinary stepping have the same meaning. Tāyana/vṛtti are not additional ninety-word claims.

## H03. Three further prefix conditions

**Āṅ, 1.3.40.** Rising is necessary but not sufficient: Kāśikā's vārttika requires **jyotirudgamana**, illustrated by the sun, moon and lights. Rising smoke is the counterexample. Padamañjarī also distinguishes a moon spreading through the sky from its rising: being a celestial body alone does not settle the verb's meaning. Nyāsa explains the qualification through vyavasthitavibhāṣā; that explanation is not a free P alternative for the counted rising-light profile.

**Vi, 1.3.41.** The actual event must be foot movement, as in the horse's vikramate. The lexical gloss pādavikṣepa does not make every use satisfy it; roots have more than one use. The splitting/opening seam, vikrāmati sandhiḥ, is the source control.

**Pra/upa, 1.3.42.** Samartha here means that the two prefixes have the equivalent beginning sense. The request contains one of them, not an obligatory chain [pra,upa]. Prakramate/upakramate bhoktum are the source model. Ordinary going with pra or approaching with upa does not establish this licence. The supplied action complement and its controller relation remain in the meaning certificate.

## H04. Aprāptavibhāṣā does not loosen mandatory A

Rule 1.3.43 applies to the unprefixed ordinary domain where the preceding A prescription has not already applied. It permits A beside residual P. Kāśikā calls it **aprāptavibhāṣā**; Siddhāntakaumudī explicitly keeps A obligatory in vṛtti and the other 1.3.38 senses. Tattvabodhinī/Padamañjarī explain why the earlier rule cannot be dismissed as merely satisfied in prefixed cases after 1.3.39's restriction.

Consequently an admitted ordinary bare request branches to P/A, while an admitted bare sarga request stays A. Prefix absence must be certified, not inferred from an empty or missing data field. Unknown meaning or prefix relation yields HOLD. Failure of one special rule is not by itself a proof of residual P if another A licence remains possible.

After pada admission, the independent 3.1.70 śap/śyan option applies to kram in the selected kartari sārvadhātuka domain. Its source condition has no P-only restriction. Applying it to the contextual A profiles is an explicit rule-based construction, not a claim that the source directly quotes every resulting prefixed śyan word. Each counted A or P position has two vikaraṇa readings. The ordinary bare semantic request therefore has four paths across its two pada-specific positions.
