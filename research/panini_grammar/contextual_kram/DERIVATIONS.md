## H06. Two vikaraṇas and a pada-dependent dīrgha

Ordinary 3.1.68 śap and optional 3.1.70 śyan are separately owned affixes. After marker removal their realizations are a and ya. Śap retains ś/p history; śyan retains ś/n history and its apit/ṅit-like condition under 1.2.4. Both are śit/sārvadhātuka. There is no root-facing iṭ in this present system, despite the lexical seṭ profile.

Rule 7.3.76 inherits **śiti** and requires that śit affix to be followed by P. It lengthens root a to ā on **both** P branches: krām+a and krām+ya. In A, the P guard fails and kram+a/kram+ya remain short. This is a prescribed dīrgha; it is not guṇa/vṛddhi blocked merely because śyan is ṅit-like.

Use the following four owned thematic interfaces with PG19's exact ending constructors:

| Pada | Vikaraṇa | Constructor factor B | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| P | śap | krām | krāmati |
| P | śyan | krāmy | krāmyati |
| A | śap | kram | kramate |
| A | śyan | kramy | kramyate |

The factor B omits the final thematic a but retains all of this history. An A ya-containing output is not automatically a passive yak construction: here 3.1.70 supplies kartari śyan, and the agent controls agreement.

For P loṭ 2sg, sip→hi then 6.4.105 hi-luk gives **krāma/krāmya**. The 1.1.63 question must be asked at the right boundary. Kāśikā 7.3.76 expressly says kram is the aṅga relative to surviving śap, not the deleted hi; Nyāsa/Padamañjarī/Bālamanoramā explain the same recipient distinction. For the śyan branch the same reasoning applies to its surviving śit recipient. The long root vowel therefore remains. It is not justified by pretending hi is still pronounced or by ignoring 1.1.63 generally.
