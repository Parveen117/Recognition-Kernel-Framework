# PG252 · The dental well and the retroflex ritual pit

Monty · 12 November 2026

Ṛgveda 10.101.5c–7c uses **avatám**, a well or cistern, with dental *t*. In Vājasaneyi Saṃhitā 11.61a the pit is addressed as **avaṭa**, with retroflex *ṭ*. The Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa quotes the address at 6.5.4.3, then describes the pit with nominative **avaṭaḥ** in the connected shape *avaṭo*. Thus the quotation and its prose explanation use two cases of the same retroflex word.

The long Ṛgvedic plural **avatā́saḥ** at 1.55.8c illustrates the Vedic *asuk* of 7.1.50. The shorter **avatā́ḥ** occurs at 4.50.3c. The Śatapatha’s **avaṭeṣu** at 3.5.4.19 appears as *avaṭeṣv-* before the following vowel. Its singular *avaṭe* occurs in the pouring formula at both 3.6.1.13 and 3.7.1.6.

The dental lemma adds **seven attested nominal positions and eight readings**. The eight retroflex observations occupy five of PG251’s case positions. The written register now has evidence for the letters and cases of both ‘well’ words. The technical quantity of the primary 4.105 suffix remains open.
