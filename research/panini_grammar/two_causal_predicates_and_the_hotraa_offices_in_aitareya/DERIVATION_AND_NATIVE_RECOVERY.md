# PG772 · What the gods propose and what the narrator reports

Read AB 6.14.4 in three clauses. First, Indra gives the recitations to the Hotṛ: hotre is the masculine dative of hotṛ with pradadau. Then the gods speak in the first person. Ime hotre is a feminine dual object, and vācā is the means. The two offices are the Potṛ and the Neṣṭṛ. Sāyaṇa also brings their human bearers into the explanation as those to be favoured. The narrator goes on to describe a further act in the third person. The object is singular āgnīdhrīyām, the function of the Agnīdhra. One additional verse, ṛcā, is its instrument. Sāyaṇa explains the outcome as making that function more abundant. The gods control both finite words. Their shift from speakers to reported actors changes person without changing their number. Neither the two offices nor the single ṛc controls finite agreement.

## The first oblique and the old recipient

AB 6.14.2 has āgnīdhrīyāyā before uktham where the next two offices have overt -yai. In his exposition Sāyaṇa uses both a dative allocation and a genitive expression for the action which the priest performs. This permits the source-specific genitive restoration āgnīdhrīyāyāḥ, with visarga loss before u, on the reading “the uktha of the office.” It gives no additional attested -yai for this token. PG771 retains the other two datives and the accusative in the later clause. The earlier masculine hotre remains in the existing ṛ-stem register. Only the feminine dual and the new qualified genitive enter the nominal panel. The [three role records](CLAUSE_ROLES.json) distinguish the prior giving from the two causatives.
