# PG913 · The command in a dated Devanāgarī manuscript of Bhaṭṭi

**The 1757 Chinmaya manuscript reads *modayadhvam* (‘gladden’) in the body of the verse.** The line runs across two photographed sides. The separate prohibition *āhadhvaṃ mā* reported by Mallinātha still requires a named manuscript carrying that reading. This witness settles the wording for one identifiable copy.

The British Library describes the Chinmaya International Foundation paper manuscript, shelfmark EAP729/1/2/11, as a Devanāgarī *Bhaṭṭikāvyam*, Śaka 1679 (1757 CE). Canvas 46 shows the beginning of canto VII with the wind and rain-cloud verses, fixing the section independently of the title. Canvas 54 bears the folio number 27. On its last line the verse beginning *bubhutsavaḥ* is followed by *samagadhvaṃ puraḥ śatror* and the group **मोदयध्वं** before the beginning of *raghūttamam*. Canvas 55 completes the object and continues with fear, Sītā and Daśānana. The next verse turns to the monkeys’ departure for Mahendra. The local numeral following the target is २ after the hundred boundary. GRETIL calls it VII.101 and Trivedi calls it VII.103. The clause sequence and the leaf turn give a firmer alignment than transferring an edition’s verse number. ### Two grammatical decisions

1. **The command.** The stem *modaya-* and the middle plural imperative ending *-dhvam* instruct the hearers to gladden the best of the Raghus. The selected root is *mud* ‘rejoice’, here with causal meaning and an expressed accusative object. The y in the written command and the order of the adjoining groups are diagnostic. The joined prohibition *modāhadhvam* and the separately ordered *āhadhvaṃ mā* have their own analyses involving *ā + han*. Neither is the body reading on these two sides. Distinguish the positive imperative from the following negative clause. No new finite position or causative licence is credited.
2. **The preceding prohibition.** The same final line has **मा च** in the *budh* passage. This supplies direct manuscript support for the *ca* branch. PG874 separately reports Trivedi’s Paṭṭaṇa witness P with *sma*. That is an editor’s collation of another manuscript. Keep both particle histories and the earlier PG868 construction. Agreement between this 1757 copy and the 1874 Grantha print on *modayadhvam* establishes two dated witnesses, without proving their genealogical independence.
