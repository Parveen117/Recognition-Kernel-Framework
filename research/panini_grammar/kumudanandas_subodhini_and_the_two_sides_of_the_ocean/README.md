# PG871 · Kumudānanda’s Subodhinī and the two sides of the ocean

### Identifying a witness for S1

In his 1898 introduction (xxiii–xxiv), Trivedi lists **two distinct commentaries called Subodhinī** on Bhaṭṭi, one by Kumudānanda and another by Rāmacandravācaspati. His footnote to the discussion of Mallinātha’s criticism of a *Subodhinīkāra* points to VII.103. On p. 253 Mallinātha reports that commentator’s analysis of the joined *modāhadhvam*. The separate *āhadhvaṃ mā raghūttamam* follows as a *kvacit* reading. The mere mention of a Subodhinī therefore establishes neither the author of the joined reading nor the mood of the separate one.

Rājendralāla Mitra, *Notices of Sanskrit MSS*, IV (1877), printed p. 209, no. 1636 describes 115 palm leaves in Bengali script in the collection of Gopīnātha Bhaṭṭācārya at Gāṅguṭe, post Purendrapura, Birbhum. Both the opening and the colophon as transcribed in the notice name **Kumudānanda**. The catalogue attributes to this work a Pāṇinian account of the forms. The neighbouring entry 1637 describes another commentator, Naranārāyaṇa Vidyāvinoda, using Saṅkṣiptasāra grammar. It has 108 leaves. The historical collection name and the two entry numbers distinguish the records. The date of copying and the current custodian of 1636 have yet to be established.

The end excerpt in 1636 can be identified more closely than the colophon’s reference to the eighth canto. Its sequence sea → north → Rāma dwelling → salt water → south occurs at **VIII.107** in Jayamaṅgalā, Mallinātha and GRETIL. Mitra’s printed extract has the spaced sequence *uttarā hi* and *lavaṇatoyato dakṣiṇataḥ*. These are words of the catalogued explanation. The poem’s two editions print *uttarāhi* and genitive *lavaṇatoyasya* in the stanza. Record the agreement of passage and the difference of wording. The end excerpt and colophon do not establish a verse total for the manuscript. The notice reproduces no folio from VII.103. Precisely the relevant leaf with its context, or a separately attributed explicit analysis of the exact variant, is still needed. The catalogue is usable evidence even though its two quotations cannot decide that question.

### How Sītā locates the monkey’s journey

In VIII.107 Sītā asks how Rāma, living north of the sea, knew of the demons’ city to its south. The same ocean supplies two standards of comparison. The direction word governs the standard’s case independently of the subject of dwelling or the place occupied. In VIII.108 she recalls the route by which she was taken. In VIII.109 she questions who could undertake such a passage apart from the wind or Garuḍa. The two printed commentaries explain all five incidences on pp. 194 and 297–298 respectively.

| Verse and standard | Derivation and allocation |
| --- | --- |
| VIII.107 *samudrāt*, north relative to the ocean | *uttara* with āhi on the distant-location reading (5.3.37–38). Its underlying location belongs to Rāma. The associated ocean noun receives the ablative by 2.3.29. The ocean is a reference point for dwelling, without an event of departure from it. |
| VIII.107 *lavaṇatoyasya*, south relative to salt water | *dakṣiṇa* takes atasuc (5.3.28). Its atas-sense suffix calls for the genitive of the reference noun under 2.3.30. The city is located on this side. The case contrast within a single stanza supplies a control against assigning one invariant case to all terms of direction. |
| VIII.108 *daṇḍakān*, south of the Daṇḍakas | The commentators select near-space enap under 5.3.35 and accusative of its standard under 2.3.31. In contrast *saritaḥ, adrīn, vanāni* are things traversed under *atikramya*. Their parallel-looking accusatives have different governors. Kāśikā also admits genitive with enap through yogavibhāga. That is a grammatical alternative. The two printed stanzas attest the accusative. |
| VIII.109 *nabhasvataḥ*, apart from the wind | *pṛthak* admits the ablative under 2.3.32. The accompanying explicit ablative *caṇḍāt* disambiguates the case of *nabhasvataḥ*, whose isolated ending could also be genitive singular. Both commentators identify the wind. |
| VIII.109 *vainateyena*, without Garuḍa | *vinā* admits the instrumental under the same rule. This second standard is the son of Vinatā. The option belongs to the construction. Each verse word preserves its witnessed case. |

These are five connected syntax constructions with ten edition-specific interfaces. The catalogue concordance supplies evidence for the stanza separately. The poem attests the environmental conditions of these instances. In particular, its *dakṣiṇa* example is admitted by both the narrow and the broader policies for 5.3.35 and cannot discriminate their scope on other stems.
