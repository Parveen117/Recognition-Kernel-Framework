# PG210 · Perceiving, hearing and listening in Bhāgavata clauses

Monty · 12 November 2026

The 12 books of the GRETIL *Bhāgavatapurāṇa* contain **117 written occurrences** of the two selected strings, *niśam* and *niśām* immediately before *y* or *ay*. Five are identical repeated XML lines at the same verse. The concordance retains the addresses of all 117 and counts **112 independent token locations**. Twelve passages have individually checked contextual readings. **Short *niśamya* can express seeing.** At 9.6.28 the observers notice an empty waterpot. At 10.19.13 the cowherds open their eyes and see themselves and their cows saved. The same short absolutive at 6.1.30 describes hearing someone chant. Long *niśāmya* at 10.34.19 describes witnessing a display. Separately, the long-vowel imperatives address hearing a speaker (4.29.52), considering spoken words (4.19.34), and recognizing *rajas* by its symptoms (11.25.17). These are textual assignments. PG209’s finite grammatical examples retain their own stated scope.

At **GRETIL 5.3.18**, the following word *merudevyāḥ* helps identify the genitive singular *niśāmayantyāḥ* despite loss of the audible visarga before *m*. The word-for-word edition prints this at **5.3.19**. In 5.4.19 the citizens supply the plural head of *niśāmayantīnām*. In 10.36.22 two wrestlers are addressed, but the passive command *niśamyatām* agrees with singular *etat*, ‘let this be heard’. This single token would be misclassified by an absolutive search that stopped at the prefix *niśamya-*.
