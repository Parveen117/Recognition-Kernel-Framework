# PG216 · Adjacent and intervening prefixes in the past participles of styai

Monty · 12 November 2026

With the two *styai* roots, moving *pra* one place back changes which part of the grammar can still see it. The Kāśikā gives **prastītaḥ / prastīmaḥ** after *pra*. With *sam* between *pra* and the root it gives **prasaṃstītaḥ**. Haradatta’s Padamañjarī explicitly excludes the *m* alternative in this latter construction.

| Prefixes | *kta*, masculine nominative singular | *ktavatu*, masculine nominative singular |
| --- | --- | --- |
| None | styānaḥ | styānavān |
| *sam* | saṃstyānaḥ | saṃstyānavān |
| *pra* | prastītaḥ / prastīmaḥ | prastītavān / prastīmavān |
| *pra* + *sam* | prasaṃstītaḥ | prasaṃstītavān |

Rule 6.1.23 applies to the whole *pra*-initial complex. It vocalizes root *y*; the result is long *ī*. Once this happens, 8.2.43 loses the *y* and ā it would require to replace niṣṭhā *t* with *n*. The separate rule 8.2.54 admits **m** after direct *pra*. Intervening *sam* leaves the vowel change in force and blocks the m substitution. The bare and *sam*-only rows retain the conditions for **n**.

The two archived kṛt tables diverge at a point that affects ordinary words. The smaller table at 01.1058 has **styānaḥ** and **styānavān**. The Vidyut-derived archive writes *styātaḥ* and *styātavān* under both root IDs. That is a difference in four root–affix fields. The written selection follows the stated rule, the Kaumudī’s unprefixed example and the independently listed word *styāna*. In the same archive *styānam* is also a *lyuṭ* action noun, so matching that one neuter word would not settle its affix.

The four prefix contexts, two suffixes and three genders give **576 case–number positions and 745 readings**. The two roots share these nominal schedules; their underlying histories are preserved without counting the rows twice. One other ambiguity survives even within the past participles: *prastītavatī* can be feminine nominative singular or neuter dual (nominative, accusative or vocative).
