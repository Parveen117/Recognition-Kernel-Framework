# PG192 · Accent and the dependent in 29 Ṛgvedic agent-noun occurrences

Monty · 11 November 2026

**All 15 occurrences in the two accented Zürich *hantṛ* entries** have an identifiable dependent in the same pāda. In the six initial-accent occurrences it is accusative singular *vṛtrám*. In the nine suffix-accent occurrences it is genitive. Two of those nine heads are themselves accusative. For example, in RV 10.166.1c the speaker asks to be made a slayer of enemies. *Hantā́ram* is accusative and *śátrūṇām*, its dependent, is genitive plural.

The six *kártar-* tokens each have an accusative dependent. RV 6.23.3c also supplies a dative beneficiary of the space made. On the suffix-accented side, one of the three genitives, *sukṛtoḥ* in RV 3.31.2d, has an uncertain referent. Three instrumental heads and the annotated locative with an editorial plus are retained without a dependent. Every decision has a verse, a pāda and separate token addresses.

The sole *bhettár-* token is RV 8.17.14c. The Kāśikā at 2.3.65 cites the strongholds before their breaker. The verse places the breaker first, followed by the strongholds and their genitive-plural adjective. The two orders support the same government. RV 4.17.8c illustrates the opposite hazard: the next accusative *vā́jam* belongs to a second agent, *sánitā*.

The corpus supplies a reading of the slayer and the patient in each selected verse. It does not give *hantā* in the specific ‘thief’ sense of the Uṇādi illustration. Identifying a Vedic accent and its dependent does not identify that lexical derivation.
