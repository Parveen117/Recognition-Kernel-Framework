## Powder and possession

With entity *cūrṇa* in the
ingredient slot, 4.4.23 deploys
*ini* as an exception to the
otherwise admitted *ṭhak*.
For the mixed cake example,
*apūpāḥ* is the target and
*cūrṇaiḥ* the instrumental.
Patañjali at Kielhorn II.330.14–15
considers the special prescription
dispensable for the forms
*cūrṇī*, *cūrṇinau* and
*cūrṇinaḥ*. He appeals to the
possessive *ini*, independently
licensable under 5.2.115.
Nyāsa and Padamañjarī accept
that explanation for possessing
powder and give a distinct
answer when mixture is what
the speaker intends. In the
latter frame, ordinary ṭhak is
the presenting competitor and
4.4.23 displaces it. The
Kāśikā and Patañjali views are
stored as distinct source and
meaning histories. The generic
possessive clause has its own
*asya cūrṇam asti* analysis.
No free extension of possession
to all mixture descriptions
is licensed here.

In both *ini* paths the ending
i of the teaching form supports
pronunciation of the affix as
given. The sounded suffix is
*in*, whose n remains an affix
member. After support sup has
been deleted under 2.4.71 and
the vowel-initial *in* licenses
bha under 1.4.18, 6.4.148
deletes the final base-owned a.
There is no initial grade
event. The base already has ū
and ṇ. The n of the new
suffix remains dental because
the intervening lexical ṇ is
outside the transparent class
in 8.4.2.

For M nom.sg attach su. Lengthen
affixal i with 6.4.13 under
the 6.4.7 upadhā scope. 6.1.68
elides the consonant-adjacent
single surviving ending s. The
pada remains available after
that lopa and 8.2.7 removes
the nominal final n, yielding
*cūrṇī*. Sambuddhi does not get
that lengthening and 8.2.8
retains n after the vocative
ending loss. Locative *cūrṇini*
keeps n. For the written
bh-initial endings and locative
plural sup
1.4.17 licenses a pada view
of the base, and 8.2.7 removes
its n. After the resulting i
boundary, 8.3.57/59 gives
*cūrṇiṣu*. Vowel-initial endings
in the stated masculine fibre
otherwise keep the n. Thus
*cūrṇinau* and *cūrṇinaḥ* keep
the actual affix n. In the
cake quotation, the nominative
plural has *-aḥ + a-* and
ru → u, *a + u → o*, followed
by the 6.1.109 retained earlier
o. This is the exact *cūrṇino
’pūpāḥ* contact.

Kāśikā also prints *cūrṇino
dhānāḥ*. The second host is
ordinarily feminine according
to the named dictionaries.
Retain the clause exactly
as printed. The Jain Quantum
Siddhahema page 422 separates
the two affixes of 6.4.7 by
yathāsaṅkhya. Its OCR has
*cūrṇinyo* and later *dhānāḥ*
in interleaved columns.
Printed-column collation is
needed to assign the entire
clause or a correction.
This mixed evidence does not
supply a new Pāṇinian
feminine cūrṇin grid.

## Whole deletion and the status of the noun

Kāśikā expressly invokes the
already selected *ṭhak* under
*saṃsṛṣṭe*. Its displayed
cross-reference 4.3.22 does
not match that lemma by
number. The wording, immediate
order and PG36 pin identify
4.4.22 as the operative
parent. Under 4.4.24 select
*lavaṇa* as mixable salt
material. Nyāsa and
Padamañjarī use actual salt
kinds as the positive class
and distinguish the taste
quality. A quality expression
can characterize food through
attribution without the
mixed-substance relation.
It supplies no 4.4.24 luk.

1.1.61 labels the loss of the
whole selected suffix as luk.
Its former identity remains in
the ordered history. The
active grade and base-vowel
consumers cannot read this
deleted cause as a continuing
*ṭhak* under 1.1.63. Thus both
a vowels survive and the
discarded *ika* path never
surfaces. The feminine
*lavaṇā* uses ordinary ṭāp
under 4.1.4 given the head's
feminine agreement. The vanished
ṭhak cannot supply an affix-final
class for 4.1.15. Kāśikā at
1.2.51 explicitly uses the
three salted food examples to
differentiate luk here from
lup's special gender/number
transfer. Masculine *sūpaḥ*,
neuter *śākam* and feminine
*yavāgūḥ* therefore determine
their respective adjectives.
The adjective and the input
neuter salt noun remain
different typed lexical items.

The Sanskrit Documents and
Kāśikā TextGrid text show
the feminine singular.
A Wilbour Hall PDF text layer
gives a plural at 4.4.24 and
a singular at 1.2.51.
The layer shows other
distortions in the adjoining
examples. It is retained here
as a text-layer observation
with visual/edition review Open,
rather than authorization for
a new textual reading.

## Real aṇ and the mung dish

In 4.4.25 *mudga* occupies
the instrumental ingredient
role. The actual aṇ overrides
the general *ṭhak*. The
affix's original ṇ supplies
7.2.117 first-vowel vṛddhi,
u → au, and later gives
feminine ṅīp under 4.1.15.
At the vowel-initial suffix
boundary the original final a
of *mudga* is deleted under
6.4.148. The resulting *maudga*
carries a from *aṇ*. That a
is itself removed before
feminine ṅīp to give *maudgī*.
The longer grade is not a
residual of an unselected ṭhak.

Kāśikā gives presentational
*maudga odanaḥ*, a final
*aḥ* of masculine agreement
followed by o of the headword.
The witnessed joined path is
8.2.66 ru, 8.3.17 y before
voiced o after a, followed
by Śākalya's 8.3.19 lopa.
The neighbouring vowels
remain because the late
boundary operation does not
reopen the earlier vowel
sandhi environment under
8.2.1. *Maudgī yavāgūḥ*
is the independently supplied
feminine with the same outer
prescription. No growing-field
or qualifying surname sense
is licensed by these frames.

Suśruta Ci. 18.51 gives
*rasāś ca maudgāḥ*. The M
nom.pl adjective agrees with
*rasāḥ*. Cūrṇa and salt remain
independent base types.
The 1938 comparison and
provisional Nepalese version
agree on this nominal phrase.
The latter puts *ca śastā*
where the vulgate puts
*samūtrā* earlier in the verse.
Ḍalhaṇa explains the phrase
through *mudgayūṣāḥ*, establishing
the soup use and its actual
pulse. He does not assign a
sūtra number to the verse.
This is an independent whole-word,
gender and lexical attestation.
The exact affix scope at the
medical locus stays distinct
from the commentary's
rice/porridge derivations.
At *rasāś ca*, ru after
ā reaches visarga before the
khar c and the sibilant
substitute via 8.3.34. The
palatal context gives ś by
8.4.40. The following *maudgāḥ*
keeps its own case event.
