# PG230 · Seven pippalyādi examples and four possessive compounds

Monty · 12 November 2026

The Nyāsa gives **pṛthivībhāryaḥ**, ‘one whose wife is Earth’, to show why *pṛthivī* is listed with its final ī at 4.1.41. The Padamañjarī preserves the example and asks whether this specifically feminine earth-word could have undergone the usual masculine-like change in the first place. Both readings give the same compound.

| First member before *bhāryā* | Masculine possessor | Condition in the source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *pṛthivī*, Earth | pṛthivībhāryaḥ | Listed ī survives. The need for a special block is disputed. |
| *darśanīyā*, attractive | darśanīyabhāryaḥ | The adjective also describes a man on the same ground. Its feminine ending yields to the masculine-like form. |
| *khaṭvā*, a bed | khaṭvābhāryaḥ | The matching masculine use required by 6.3.34 is absent. |
| *brahmabandhū* | brahmabandhūbhāryaḥ | An earlier *ūṅ* excludes the change. |

In all four masculine compounds the external possessor determines case and number. The shortening of the final subordinate *bhāryā* under 1.2.48 operates independently of the fate of the first member.

Following the *pippalyādi* directive, the Kāśikā prints seven consecutive ī-final words: *pippalī, harītakī, kośātakī, śamī, karīrī, pṛthivī, kroṣṭrī*. The Nyāsa attributes the presentation of examples to the commentator. The Bālamanoramā singles out the fixed feminine gender of *pippalī*. The seven words and the four attested compounds give **264 nominal positions and 268 readings**. Six complete feminine word tables agree with head-specific archive entries. For *kroṣṭrī* the archive has a plant gloss; the female jackal is independently identified in the dictionary.
