# PG875 · The Shantipur command and the agents who wish or beg

### What the Shantipur manuscript actually reads

EAP781/1/1/513 is a fragmentary Bengali-script manuscript of the poem held by Santipur Bangiya Puran Parishad. The collection describes thirty folios, with losses, beginning in canto V. On **canvas 39, line 2**, the poem itself gives the gathering before the enemy, **modayadhvaṃ** with the best of the Raghus, the negative **nopāyadhvam** with fear, and Sītā with Daśānana. These are normalized readings of the individual words in a continuous Bengali-script line. The verse crosses neither a commentary boundary nor a change of speaker on this leaf. Its address is GRETIL VII.101, Trivedi VII.103. On the preceding line the *bubhutsu* passage has *mā ca* before the negative budh command. The Paṭṭaṇa collation in PG874 instead reports *sma* for *ca*. The following line moves on to the monkeys and Mount Mahendra. These adjacent words independently fix the location.

This establishes the reading of a **named manuscript at the disputed locus**. On this leaf the poem gives the causative command of √mud. The 1874 Grantha print also puts that command in the verse but puts *āhadhvaṃ mā raghūttamam* in Mallinātha’s commentary. The Bengali witness therefore answers which reading this particular manuscript transmits. It does not supply a manuscript carrying the separately quoted variant, an explicit mood for that variant, or the missing Subodhinī folio. The manuscript’s copying date is not established by the date of the poem. ### Who wants what in Bhaṭṭi

* Lakṣmī in V.17 longs for association with a man. The object is *puṃyogam*. Jayamaṅgalā’s feminine gloss *abhilaṣantī* confirms the agreement of *āśaṃsuḥ* with Lakṣmī. *Patyuḥ* belongs to proximity to her husband, for which the commentator gives the optional genitive of 2.3.34. It is distinct from the object of desiring.
* Sugrīva in VII.24 desires his own safety (*kṣemam ātmanaḥ*) and favour (*prasādam*). The verse assigns two masculine adjectives, *āśaṃsuḥ* and *icchuḥ*, to him. Jayamaṅgalā explicitly cites the prohibition of the kṛt genitive (2.3.69) after identifying the u suffix. The u suffix expresses the agent. Since it does not express the object, the separate noun naming the object takes the accusative under 2.3.2. The possessive genitive *ātmanaḥ* depends on his welfare. The 1887 verse reads **praṇaman**, which the commentator construes as bowing with the women. The pinned GRETIL verse reads **praṇayan**. The bowing gloss has the former as its source.
* In VII.79 the bird anticipates something like food. The same masculine nominative singular is written *āśaṃsur mahān* at the boundary. That following consonant does not create a second nominative position. The feminine in V.17 and masculine in VII.24 are independently identifiable even at the same pause form.

### Three lexical formations and four inflectional schedules

1. For **āśaṃsu**, select āṅ + bhvādi **01.0716 śasi**, wishing. The lexical i-marker supplies num under 7.1.58 and the internal nasal precedes the sibilant (8.3.24). The desire sense and prefix license u directly under 3.2.168. The Kāśikā excludes the praise-denoting entry **01.0829**, even though its head consonants coincide. Keep the root designation and the outer kṛt agent separate. No san is inserted into this branch.
2. For masculine **bhikṣu**, select unprefixed bhvādi **01.0690 bhikṣ** in asking or begging. It takes the same lexical u. Two analogies in Kāśikā 3.3.46 show precisely where the wish for acquisition belongs. A mendicant goes about grasping a bowl to obtain alms. A dvija grasps the ladle in the hope of a fee. In both, the acquisition goal licenses the action noun with pra + grah and ghañ, giving *pragrāha* inside the instrumental compound. The ordinary *pragraha* of Devadatta is the explicit control. Ghañ is on the grasping action. It is not the agent’s u, and pra belongs to grah, not to bhikṣ. The example commits to the mendicant’s motive, not to successful receipt of food.
3. For **icchu**, select tudādi **06.0078 iṣ**, desire. Kāśikā 3.2.169 stipulates ch and u for this root, paired in the rule with a different operation (num) for vid. Short i before ch receives tuk under 6.1.73, with dental-to-palatal assimilation under 8.4.40, yielding *icchu*. The result does not import num from its neighbour or san from 3.2.168. The motion and repetition homonyms 04.0022 and 09.0061 carry different senses.

PG869 and PG870 supply the ordinary short-u endings. Each column below is literal after prepending its indicated L. N is replaced by the indicated single consonant. Each group of three entries is singular, dual and plural in that order. The four singular feminine oblique alternatives occupy single requests. Masculine **bhikṣ** has retroflex ṇ after ṣ in the nasal-bearing endings. **āśaṃs** ends in dental s and **icch** has no ṇati trigger.

These are paradigms extended from the admitted stems, with the above verse tokens and grammatical examples distinguished from the rest. Four schedules give 96 interfaces and 100 readings. Three syntactic comparisons cover the nine named requests. No new credit for the earlier budh or mud commands, already displayed san agents, generic carrier, or mere copies of the rule.

### Typed recovery

On PG14’s paired right history modules label the lexical input with root, sense, prefix, source and requested grammatical relation. The designated kṛt governs an agent, the dependent case has its own edge, and the agent’s gender/case/number selects the inflection. The witness grade in the first section distinguishes main text from quoted commentary even when the verse address agrees. For each licensed edge j the full arrow sends eₕb to eₖ(1+zⱼ)b. Taking the target coefficient augmentation gives eₖε(b), the zero arrow on the specialized source history. The proof holds on both feminine oblique branches, hence on their sum, and under composition and adjunction on PG14’s pairing. A praise-to-desire transfer, a lexical-u-to-san transfer, or a move of the action noun’s ghañ onto the mendicant has zero component in both modules. The constant section lifts each retained graded history; projection preserves the witness and the choices contributing to a common word.
