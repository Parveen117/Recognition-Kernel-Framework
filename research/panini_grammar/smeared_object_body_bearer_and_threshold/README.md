# PG269 · The smeared object, the threshold and the one who has a body

Monty · 12 November 2026

The Mādhavīya’s *dehanī* answers ‘what is smeared or plastered?’ Its use of *lyuṭ* in the patient sense is expressly lexical (*bāhulaka*). The feminine ending *ṅīṣ* comes from the gaurādi treatment at 4.1.41. Kṣīrasvāmin also gives *dehanaḥ ghaṭaḥ*, a pot, and names *dehanī* under that list.

| Word | Reading in the source | Inflection selected here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *dehanī* | the object of smearing, Mādhavīya | feminine ī-stem |
| *dehalī* | the threshold, specifically the wood below a door in Kṣīrasvāmin | feminine ī-stem |
| *deha* | the body, masculine and neuter in Monier-Williams | both genders |
| *dehin* | one who has a body, possessive *ini* in Mādhava | masculine in-stem, nominative singular *dehī* |

The *n* and *l* distinguish two feminine words. For **dehalī** itself there are also two source histories. Kṣīrasvāmin places the threshold word under Uṇādi; Śabdakalpadruma analyses *deha* ‘plaster’ + *lā* ‘take’, cites *ka* at 3.2.3, then invokes the feminine ending under gaurādi. Neither passage supplies one of the outstanding affix assignments under 4.105.

Viṣṇupurāṇa 1.17.71 sets singular **dehī** beside locative plural **deheṣu**. The same opposition occurs with the locative singular in Viṣṇupurāṇa 6.7.12 and Bhāgavata 4.29.24. The inflection *dehe* in these settings allows either grammatical gender assigned to *deha*. In Bhāgavata 3.23.18 a palace has a coral threshold, *vidruma-dehalyā*, an instrumental singular. The five lexical/gender selections add **120 positions**. The four Purāṇic verses supply seven selected word occurrences.
