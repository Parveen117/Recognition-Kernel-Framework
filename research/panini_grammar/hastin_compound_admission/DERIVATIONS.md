# PG55 — हस्तिन् formation, compound preparation and witnessed इञ् admission

Author: Monty. 4 November 2026.

The suffix histories **हस्तिशिरस् → हास्तिशीर्षि → हास्तिशीर्ष्या**
already exist in [PG49](../siras_dependency_history/DERIVATIONS.md), frozen
here by commit bf8ab5edafbcad3ab3c92012648b6bfa161327dc. PG55 supplies
their previously admitted compound input and makes the lexical admission
contract explicit. It does not count those two old suffix paths again.

There are two construction histories H01/H02, one admission contract A01
and eight controls. The four L stages, five Q stages and five G stages
are addressed records in this packet; the G4/G5 calls explicitly reuse
PG49. These are not fourteen new rules or complete inflection positions.

## Input and source policy

Start from supplied lexical **हस्त** and **शिरस्**, the conventional
elephant-class use of **हस्तिन्**, and a supplied genitive relation
between the compound's two members. Their root/Uṇādi etymologies are not
assumed constructed. H02 selects a षष्ठी तत्पुरुष expansion, informally
**हस्तिनः शिरः**. This is our declared compound analysis under 2.2.8,
not a quotation of a unique analysis in the 6.1.62 commentary.

The resulting head-denoting compound and its person-name use are distinct
semantic records. The person-bearing use needed for descent is admitted
from the explicit हस्तिशिरस् example in Kāśikā 6.1.62 and the
4.1.96 discussion; it is not inferred from an elephant's anatomy. A
बहुव्रीहि/similitude analysis is not silently substituted for H02.

Kāśikā 5.2.133 describes इनि as restricted in the मत्वर्थ setting
when the whole word denotes a जाति. Padamañjarī distinguishes this
conventional class designation from an assertion of actual possession:
even a विहस्त elephant can be called हस्तिन्. H01 adopts that stated
class-designation reading. The general 5.2.94/115 possession frame is
retained as background, not turned into a physical possession test for
every token of the class word. The two commentaries' descriptions of
नियम/विधि remain separately attributed in the archive.

Owners are B = हस्त, I = इनि, M = शिरस्, U/V = internal case
suffixes, C = compound, D = later इञ्, Y = ष्यङ्, F = चाप्.
In particular I's न् is not the न् of a later proposed शीर्षन्.

## C01 — Class designation is not a live possession sensor

Under the selected Padamañjarī 5.2.133 reading, a supplied elephant-class
member lacking the relevant limb still receives the conventional class word.
A human's possession of hands does not itself grant that class admission.
Kāśikā's हस्तवान् पुरुषः is the contrasting possession witness.
Unresolved intended sense gives OPEN; 5.2.133 is not a taxonomy detector.

## C02 — Two इ-bearing suffixes and two vowel deletions

L3 removes only I's final teaching marker; L4 removes B's final अ.
Neither deletes I's initial pronounced इ. G4's D = इञ् is a later,
different suffix. In PG49's added-suffix route, the इ later removed by
6.4.148 belongs to D; it is not the surviving इ of the compound's
first member. Equal letters never substitute for owner/version addresses.

## C03 — A compound reading does not infer its bearer

Q1–Q5 construct the declared genitive compound. No operation there proves
that it names a person, that the person is an ancestor, or that an admitted
descendant has the technical गोत्र relation. G1/G5 supply those different
premises. A bare anatomical reading cannot silently enter the positive
descent history. Other compound parses require their own construction.

## C04 — Whole सुप-लुक is not marker loss or universal erasure

Q3 retains the source-authorized पद designation after whole internal
सुप-लुक. Marker loss alone is not that event. Conversely 1.1.62 does
not override 1.1.63 for every अङ्ग operation: retain the latter's
लुमत् restriction and the actual consumer type. The stored suffix is
not restored to pronunciation.

## C05 — First-member न् and replacement न् are different targets

8.2.7 at Q4 acts on the final न् of the first member's retained पद.
It is not a global removal of every न् in C or its descendants. The
later न् in PG49's rejected शीर्षन् continuation belongs to M's
new replacement version and encounters 6.4.168. The earlier deletion
cannot repair that diagnostic branch or replace its separate णत्व step.

## C06 — The नलोप view is restricted by consumer

Kāśikā 8.2.2 explicitly treats its list as a restriction: नलोप is
पूर्वत्रासिद्ध for the listed सुप/स्वर/संज्ञा/कृत्-तुक् consumers,
not for every earlier operation. Its राजाश्व example demonstrates a
visible vowel-coalescence consequence. That citation is not a new completed
word derivation here. Do not misread the rule as making नलोप visible
precisely in the four domains where it says the opposite.

## C07 — A lexical witness is stronger than a spelling match

Nyāsa 4.1.96 contrasts the traditionally recognized founder called बाहु,
whose qualified descendants take इञ्, with an otherwise supplied recent
person of that same name, for whom it gives अण्. This is a source-level
control, not two new declined paradigms. Traditional designation, technical
गोत्र and bare spelling are different fields. Unknown identity gives OPEN.

The same field explicitly admits the शिरस्-ending compound but contains
a damaged subsequent clause with an embedded 6.1.61 reference. Preserve
it literally; do not use that reference to produce अ-final शीर्ष.
Kāśikā 6.1.62 supplies the exact vowel-initial replacement and example.

## C08 — Reuse the suffix paths without flattening their policies

The G5 continuation imports PG49's exact two policies and their limitations.
PG54's successful औदमेघेय comparison does not prove their global
equivalence. In particular the local सन्निपात permission/restriction
and the different deleted-इ witness cannot be interchanged. The old paths
and their diagnostic हास्तिशीर्षण्या receive zero new count credit.
Accent, complete nominal inflection and general source-policy selection remain
open; the next task isolates the competing feminine suffix/marker and accent
arguments in the 4.1.78 discussion.
