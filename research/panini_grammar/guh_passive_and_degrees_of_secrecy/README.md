# PG993 · The patient of concealing and the degrees of secrecy

Monty · 27 November 2026

## Whose person and number appear on the verb?

Bṛhaddhāturūpāvalī §258, printed p. 128, labels **guhyate** karmaṇi. Start with the transitive concealment sense of 01.1043 *guhū̃*. Rule 3.4.69 admits the expressed patient, 1.3.13 selects the middle endings for this voice, and 3.1.67 supplies yaK before the sārvadhātuka ending. The root is followed by y in *guh-ya-te*. The vowel-initial condition of 6.4.89 is absent. The same root in the active thematic present of PG989 has *gūhati*. The yaK formation also differs from PG992's retained long ū in the single-causative absolutive. Select three patient frames, each with one simple root and an expressed object of the underlying transitive action. In the bare and ni frames it is the person or thing concealed. In the upa frame it is the person embraced. PG991 pins the preverb senses. First person requires a speaker patient, second an addressee patient. A supplied person doing the concealing or embracing occupies the agent's independent instrumental slot. This guard also distinguishes a middle actor from a patient when the ending has the same shape.

## Which noun is actually in the text?

Kāśikā 3.1.109 reports the optional extension of kyap to śaṃs, duh and guh. For guh it explicitly gives **guhyam** and **gohyam**. On the marked kyap route, kit blocks ordinary guṇa. The consonant-final root also licenses ṇyat (3.1.124), with guṇa of the short light penult (7.3.86). Both suffixes yield a ya surface. Neither derivation selects passive yaK. Keep this pair for the grammatical derivation of what is to be concealed. A poet citing one lexicalized member supplies evidence only for the form at that address.

| Witness | Direct reading | Case and construction |
|---|---|---|
| Mahābhārata 12.131.7 | *gūhati pāpāni … guhyam* | The active verb has sins as its plural object. The garment in the comparison covers a woman's private part. This supplies an accusative singular neuter of the bodily sense and a context for PG989's existing active third singular. |
| Bhagavadgītā 10.38 | *guhyānām* | The substantivized neuter genitive plural gives the class of secrets in the statement about silence. |
| Bhagavadgītā 18.63 | *guhyād guhyataram* | In the comparison, ablative singular *guhyāt* is written with final d before g. Comparative *guhyatara-* agrees with neuter nominative singular *jñānam*, the patient of *ākhyātam*. The speaker is instrumental *mayā*. |
| Bhagavadgītā 15.20 | *guhyatamaṃ śāstram* | Plain superlative, neuter nominative singular in the passive result with *uktam*. |
| Bhagavadgītā 18.64 | *sarvaguhyatamaṃ … vacaḥ* | Compound superlative, neuter accusative singular. The hearer is commanded to listen, and the word or utterance is heard. |
| Bhagavadgītā 18.68 | *idaṃ paramaṃ guhyaṃ* | The secret communicated is the neuter accusative singular object of the speaker-to-be's future verb. |

At 18.63, 5.3.57 supplies *tarap* with a comparison between terms. At 15.20 and in the degree component of 18.64, 5.3.55 supplies *tamap*. The final marker disappears on each derivation. Śrīdhara at 18.64 paraphrases the comparison using the ablative plural of things kept secret. Baladeva uses a locative plural to delimit the comparison class. Both explain the same compound in the verse. Their cases remain in their commentaries. Neither creates a variant case of the word governed by *śṛṇu*. At 12.131.7 the antecedent of feminine *sā* is unnecessary to the two object assignments.
