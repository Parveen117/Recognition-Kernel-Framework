# PG964 · The flour animal as an independent noun and an inner member

Viśvaguṇādarśacampū, Karṇāṭadeśavarṇanam 182, writes *piṣṭapaśvādaro ’pi*. The predicate *utkṛṣṭaḥ syāt* agrees with masculine *ādaraḥ*, esteem or acceptance. The object of that regard is expressed within the compound. Bālakṛṣṇa takes it apart as *piṣṭapaśoḥ ādaraḥ*. The genitive belongs to the flour animal. The nominative belongs to esteem. The commentator subsequently has *piṣṭapaśvālambhanasya*, with neuter action noun *ālambhana* as the head of an outer genitive. These are three different requests. The two internal *paśv-* sequences follow 6.1.77 when the case-free compound member ending in u meets ā. The v in the verse supplies no evidence for an independent genitive in *-vas*. Before *api*, the underlying ending of *ādaraḥ* has its normal o and initial-a loss. For the masculine u-stems, accusative singular keeps stem u followed by m. Instrumental singular has *-unā*, dative has *-ave*, ablative and genitive have *-oḥ*, locative has *-au* and vocative has *-o*. Singular u is long in the direct dual and in accusative plural before n. In the plural distinguish nominative/vocative *-avaḥ* from accusative *-ūn*. In these two words the dental n remains in *-unā* and *-ūnām*. The ṛ of the first member in *ghṛtapaśu* lies before dental t, and the final member contains no trigger for retroflexing n. In the oblique dual the vowel-to-glide junction yields *paśvoḥ*.

For feminine *pratikṛti*, the alternative nadī designation under 1.4.6 before the appropriate ṅit endings changes the singular dative, ablative, genitive and locative. Admit *pratikṛtaye / pratikṛtyai*, *pratikṛteḥ / pratikṛtyāḥ* in each of the two separate cases and *pratikṛtau / pratikṛtyām*. Instrumental *pratikṛtyā* has no second spelling on this account. The dual direct *pratikṛtī* differs from the plural accusative *pratikṛtīḥ*. The genitive plural has dental n after the intervening t. The material in the much longer stem does not change any of these four optional slots.
