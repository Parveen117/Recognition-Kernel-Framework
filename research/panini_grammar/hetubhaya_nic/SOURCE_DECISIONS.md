## H02. Hetu names the causer, not any causal influence

Kāśikā/Nyāsa 1.3.68 take hetu as the technical prayojaka,
the current controller c of the ṇyanta. The A certificate says
that fear or wonder in experiencer q arises directly from c.
Retain q as current karman, and the distinct current agent c.
The source explicitly permits A with other-directed principal
result; that is the selected result condition in these profiles.

Bhaya is an upalakṣaṇa for smi's wonder, not a requirement
that its participant literally be afraid. Nyāsa 6.1.57 explains
the common mental disturbance used in this semantic extension.
The source's muṇḍa/jaṭila examples supply qualified frames,
not a general rule that any appearance automatically proves the guard.

## H03. Instrument controls require an independent P frame

The two P certificates retain causer c, experiencer/object q and
instrument k. The fear/wonder source is k, not c as prayojaka.
Kāśikā gives kuñcikayainaṃ bhāyayati and rūpeṇa vismāyayati.
These are positive ordinary one-ṇic, other-result P requests under
1.3.78. A failed special-A guard alone does not assign P.

Kaumudī/Bālamanoramā 6.1.57 discuss the literary vismāpayan
reading through either a reported vismāyayan text or nested
causation in which human speech changes its role. Both discussions
are archived, but neither silently enlarges this one-ṇic domain.
Do not identify an instrument with a causer just to preserve a
favoured spelling. Missing role/source evidence remains HOLD.

## H04. Dependent ā/puk and ī/ṣuk branches

In the qualified bhī A frame, 6.1.56 admits optional ā.
The ā branch takes puk by 7.3.36, giving bhāp. The other
branch retains ī and takes ṣuk by 7.3.40, giving bhīṣ.
Nyāsa, Kaumudī and Bālamanoramā explicitly use the ī-praśleṣa
reading to exclude ṣuk after ā. The source-dependent relation
is therefore {ā+puk, ī+ṣuk}; no ā+ṣuk or ī+puk branch is
added. Puk/ṣuk are root-final augments, not h replacements.

In the qualified vi+smi A frame, 6.1.57 makes ā mandatory;
7.3.36 supplies puk, giving smāp. The bhī option is not copied
to smi. In the instrument frames the special ā and ṣuk guards
fail. Ordinary 7.2.115 vṛddhi and 6.1.78 give root-owned
bhāy/smāy, with the later ṇic vowel still separately present.

Kaumudī/Bālamanoramā describe the ā operation on the ec output
of vṛddhi; retain that intermediate owner on the ā branch.
In the ī/ṣuk branch the augment makes the root non-final-ik
and the long ī is not light upadhā. No ordinary ai/ay is
then inferred from an earlier lexical vowel alone. These local
source contracts do not establish a global rule-number scheduler.
