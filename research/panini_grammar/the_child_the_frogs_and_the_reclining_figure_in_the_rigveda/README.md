# PG859 · The child, the frogs and the reclining figure in the Ṛgveda

Two adjacent stanzas of the Śākala frog hymn put different participles of śī ‘lie’ into the same scene. The frogs have lain through a year. When rain comes, the waters reach a frog lying in a pond. In the childbirth hymn a child has lain for ten months. The change of vowel, accent and case is visible in the words of these hymns.

**Evidence for the case at 7.103.2.** The VedaWeb field printed in the pinned compilation labels śáyānam masculine *nominative* singular. Its Hellwig field labels it accusative. The accusative pronoun, the ending -am of the masculine adjective, and the plural nominative subject of āyan decide the local analysis. Sāyaṇa glosses the phrase as the group of frogs there residing. Macdonell translates the waters as coming upon the one lying there. At 2.12.11 the VedaWeb field itself assigns the same spelling to the masculine accusative. Preserve the conflicting 7.103.2 label as an observation. It supplies no masculine nominative variant. PG40 had already quoted the lake phrase in Vasu's 7.1.39 entry. Reserve the present masculine accusative as an existing position and reading at both occurrences.

**Participles and readouts.** For the Vedic perfect, 3.2.105–106 admits liṭ and its optional participial replacement kānac. The selected śaśayāná- grade and its full-grade root syllable are supplied by the two Śākala witnesses. Sāyaṇa glosses the plural with śiśyānāḥ. This is a commentarial gloss; transcribing it as the hymn would lose its recited vocalism. The foreign-looking reduplication of the perfect is not evidence of a yaṅ intensive. The present uses middle participial -āna with the simple root in guṇa, e → ay before ā, then masculine accusative -am. The two present attestations share the stem accent on śá-, while the perfect has accented -ná- in the singular and -nā́- in the plural. Accent here identifies four recitation readouts. The citation gives no basis for assigning those four particular accents to another paradigm.

At 5.78.9 the continuous text joins the final nasal of māsān to the following initial sibilant; the word text restores the two entries. Similarly the continuous plural in 7.103.1 precedes voiced brāhmaṇā and its final visarga is absent. In the word text the nominative plural is explicit. Thus the exposed -ā of the continuous passage has no feminine singular agreement here.

**The pond.** Sāyaṇa invokes the gaurādi feminine suffix, loss of the locative ending under 7.1.39 and pragṛhya at a final long vowel used in the locative sense under 1.1.19. The Jamison–Brereton notes in the pinned file discuss the expected short-vowel locative of sáras- and tentatively prefer an ī-stem. The Hellwig annotation identifies *saras* and marks the gender neuter. These analyses agree that the pond is the location. The file's different stems and genders stay attributed to their sources.
