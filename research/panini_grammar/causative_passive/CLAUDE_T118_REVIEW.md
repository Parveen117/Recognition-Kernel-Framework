## C1. Useful progress and the accepted 2.1.7 correction

T118 separates semantic input, samarthya, compound class, ordering,
sup-lopa/aluk, samāsānta and output category, with seventeen labelled
worked cases and explicit unresolved steps. This is useful development
in the intended independent lane; it does not duplicate PG15's causal
passive construction. Its record of open W3/W14/W16 steps should be
preserved when completing them.

Its T114 correction is substantively supported: 2.1.7 is read as
yathā + **asādṛśye**, excluding the similarity sense. The primary
Kāśikā explicitly says *asādṛśye vartamānam* and gives the excluded
Devadatta/Yajñadatta comparison. Siddhāntakaumudī says *asādṛśye eva*;
Bālamanoramā explicitly supplies the word division **asādṛśye iti chedaḥ**
and names yogyatā, vīpsā and padārthānativṛtti. This resolves the scope
qualification recorded in PG14 without showing the yathāśakti word wrong.

The evidence is stronger when attributed precisely: the unseparated
sūtrapāṭha word *yathāsādṛśye* alone permits the orthographic ambiguity;
the commentaries resolve the segmentation. T118's listed remaining
senses are present explicitly in the pinned Bālamanoramā, while the
pinned Kāśikā's literal examples are yathāvṛddham/yathādhyāpakam.
Do not treat a quote-match certificate for the sūtrapāṭha as verification
of every commentary attribution or semantic inference.

## C2. Nitya must be inherited, not located only by an explicit word

T118 L5 says the post-2.1.11 domain is vibhāṣā unless the local rule says
nityam or na. W3 correspondingly labels 2.2.18 vibhāṣā. But the pinned
Kāśikā begins **both 2.2.18 and 2.2.19** with *nityam iti vartate*.
Its 2.2.18 discussion expressly includes the niṣkauśāmbi example used
in W3. Therefore the modal decision must carry inherited nityam, not
inspect only the current rule's printed words.

This changes the licensed option relation even if the compound spelling
stays the same. Further, Kāśikā 2.2.19 explains why the relevant
gati/kāraka/upapada compound formation can occur **before sup production**.
T118's initial “two already sup-ending padas” carrier is consequently not
a complete carrier for every class it later includes. The follow-up
should distinguish ordinary sup-bearing inputs from the source's
pre-sup upapada inputs, or narrow the declared domain.

## C3. Yudhiṣṭhira requires the named ṣatva rule

T118 L4/W5 attributes yudhi+sthira → yudhiṣṭhira to 8.3.59 because the
retained locative is inside the compound. Kāśikā 8.3.59, however, requires
the **s itself** to be an ādeśa or belong to a pratyaya. Placing lexical
sthira inside a compound does not turn its original s into either.

The pinned 8.3.95 is the explicit rule **gavi-yudhibhyāṃ sthiraḥ**.
Kāśikā gives gaviṣṭhiraḥ and yudhiṣṭhiraḥ there. The appropriate chain
uses 8.3.95 for s→ṣ, followed by the stated dental-to-retroflex adjustment
8.4.41 for th→ṭh. Aluk and the compound's identity remain relevant,
but they are not a substitute for that ṣatva license. The target word
can be retained while repairing its rule witness.

## C4. The nonnasal stokāt+mukta branch needs jaśtva

T118 W6 gives stokānmukta alongside stokātmukta, treating absence of
8.4.45's optional nasal substitution as retention of original t.
The retained inner pada-final t is subject to 8.2.39 jaśtva first.
Before m there is no khar condition to devoice that d. The conventional
two branches are therefore stokānmukta and **stokādmukta**, with the
same retained aluk context. Kāśikā 8.4.45's own examples contrast a nasal
with a voiced stop, such as agnicin/agnicid in the corresponding nasal
environment (its Sanskrit is retained verbatim in the source extract).

Kāśikā 6.3.2 supplies stokānmukta and also limits this compound use of
the stokādi words to its attested number context. An unrestricted
number-domain expansion would need that qualification as well.

## C5. Relations, native lift and the next scope

Kāśikā 2.2.32–34 preserves ordering freedom in stated multiple-member
cases. T118's notation ORD as a total function needs either a set-valued
target or an explicit branch relation and tie policy on its declared
domain. Its indrāgnī citation belongs to Kāśikā 2.2.33's contrast with
the ghi rule; equal vowel counts there do not themselves demonstrate
2.2.34 beating 2.2.33. These are reasons to pin the actual inherited
conditions and conflict examples instead of replacing them with a
universal “later rule wins” scheduler.

T118 provides classical grammatical records and operations. It does not
yet provide the full native carrier, declared pairing and typed
zero-specialization proof required by the repository construction policy.
That is useful next mathematical work, alongside the source repairs and
the unfinished 6.3 uttarapada paths. It should extend the established
carrier additively, not create another shared engine or silently erase
the alternative branch histories in a set of printed words.

This is a **targeted source and construction review**, not a verdict on
every lemma or example in T118. No audit of its reported 141 quote matches,
runtime certificates, gaṇa catalogue, full samāsānta map or workflow changes
is claimed. The findings are recorded here without editing Claude's branch.
