# PG15 — Causative passive, expressed karman and retained ṇic

Author: Monty. 2 November 2026.

PG15 continues the written grammar construction. It constructs classical
present karma-passives of PG13's twelve identified causative roots, including
the choice of expressed karman when the frame contains two. It distinguishes
formation, role selection, agreement and the loss of ṇic's pronounced vowel.

The result is a source-argued construction on a declared domain, not a new
Python adapter or certificate. Its native operators extend PG14's admitted
cut-path carrier and commute with its typed zero-specialization. A concrete
gamyate collision proves why a surface-only state cannot retain the causal
decision; a two-channel repair is constructed with its transported pairing.

## Exact advance

The written relation now selects the expressed karman on twelve causative
root/sense frames, then computes its person/number and the other participants'
cases. The same causative root can consequently take different endings
when two different karmans are eligible. The formation yields bhāvya, kārya,
nāyya, hārya, pācya, gamya, sthāpya, dāpya, pāyya, vedya, bodhya and karṣya
as the stated pre-ending passive aṅgas. The nine endings are derived through
PG14's owned thematic A construction, not appended as an unexplained oracle.
Displayed paradigms select the non-doubling options of 8.4.46–47; they
do not enumerate every optional phonetic repetition.

A small separate comparison domain covers the source's noncausative
double-karman frames for duh, nī, hṛ, kṛṣ and vah. It prevents the causal
selection policy from being mistakenly reused for a basic lexical root.

The adopted new source policy is Siddhāntakaumudī's end-of-bhāva/karma
discussion, indexed in the publisher data under 7.1.69 and explained by
Bālamanoramā/Tattvabodhinī. It is **not the literal prescription of 7.1.69**.
The underlying Mahābhāṣya discussion at 1.4.51 is retained with its different
voices; the stage does not assert that every historical formulation is one
unqualified rule.

The old implementation counts remain unchanged: 228 simple-present root
entries / 2,610 cells, plus 108 separately counted causative P cells. Written
PG15 forms do not become new executed/validated cells. The existing Python
APIs, PG1–PG14 frozen evidence, theorem/certificate files and shared operator engine
remain unchanged. Full grammar, unrestricted parsing and runtime integration
remain open; testing is deferred under the owner's mathematics-first direction.
