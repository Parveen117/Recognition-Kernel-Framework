# PG15 — Causative passive derivations from a completed frame

Author: Monty. 2 November 2026.

These are written consequences of K1–K6 and L1–L6, with PG14's ending
construction. They are not executed Python examples or new coverage cells.
Literal source examples are identified separately from illustrations
constructed here. Romanized clauses below normally leave words separate;
they are not a new account of every external-sandhi option.

Ṭi in 3.4.79 is the 1.1.64 selector. The relevant non-pit sārvadhātuka
endings carry the 1.2.4 ṅit-like status. The short i in the first-person
ending and the deleted i of ṇic have different owners; the former is not
an occurrence of the latter restored by lopa memory.

The dual's ending-initial iy loses its y before t/th. That is a true
6.1.66 environment, unlike the two adjacent y's of nāyya/pāyya. A rule
must inspect the addressed owner and next sound; the mere presence of
two y occurrences somewhere in a state is not its guard.

In 3pl the internal n before t passes through 8.3.24 anusvāra and
8.4.58's dental restoration. Thus *kāryante → kāryaṃte → kāryante* is a
retained Tripādī path. The n restored by the later rule is not exposed
retroactively to earlier 8.4.1 ṇatva, by 8.2.1. The r in kār/hār/karṣ
does not authorize *kāryaṇte/hāryaṇte/karṣyaṇte* by ignoring that order.
This is inherited directed visibility, not an assertion that the history
is empty whenever its initial and final spellings match.

The word reading selects the non-doubling branches of 8.4.46–47, whose
optionality is explicit in Siddhāntakaumudī. This matters both after r
(for example kāryate) and in nāyya/pāyya's y pair. L5 describes the latter
local extension. The tables are therefore complete for the stated
conventional reading, not a list of all optional phonetic repetitions.

## D3. Three different kṛ formations

**Basic karma-passive.** A maker b and a product c form the basic event.
Select c as the expressed karman. The source chain in PG14 is

\[
 kṛ+yak+ta\longrightarrow k[ri]_{7.4.28}+ya+te
 \longrightarrow \textit{kriyate}.
\]

**Causative karma-passive.** Introduce a distinct causer a and ṇic. The
formation yields kār|i. For χ=K, K3 selects the causee b; for χ=Agt, it
selects product c. Either branch with a third-person singular controller
continues

\[
 kār|i_{\text{ṇic}}|ya_{\text{yak}}|te
 \xrightarrow{6.4.51}
 kār|\varnothing_{\text{ṇic}}|ya|te
 \longrightarrow \textit{kāryate}.
\]

**Causative kartari A.** In the appropriate PG14 beneficiary/upapada
context the causer is expressed. Śap, not yak, is inserted; ṇic's i
takes its active-present guṇa/ayādi path, producing kār|ay|a|te and
*kārayate*. The applicable pada decision here has semantic qualifications
which are not inferred from the letters *-te*.

These three forms differ before tiṅ realization. In particular the
passive kāryate is neither the basic kriyate with a new semantic label
nor the active kārayate with an unchanged root-plus-affix analysis.

## D4. Cognition: one causal frame, two possible agreement controllers

Let a be one teacher, b several learners, and c one teaching-content
karman. For budh 1040068 in its admitted understanding sense, 1.4.52
makes b karman; c remains the base karman. The adopted cognition policy
permits either q=b or q=c. The common stem is

\[
 budh\longrightarrow bodh|i_{\text{ṇic}}
 \longrightarrow bodh|\varnothing|ya\longrightarrow bodhya.
\]

Two constructed illustrations are:

| Selected q | Cases and agreement | Resulting clause |
|---|---|---|
| Learners b | b nominative plural; c accusative singular; a instrumental; A 3pl | māṇavakāḥ dharmaṃ bodhyante guruṇā |
| Content c | c nominative singular; b accusative plural; a instrumental; A 3sg | dharmaḥ māṇavakān bodhyate guruṇā |

The source gives the same choice with singular participants. Here different
numbers make the agreement consequence visible. Neither branch changes
the stem or removes the causal interpretation. Conversely, replacing
*bodhyante* by *bodhyate* in the first completed frame would disagree
with its selected q even though the teacher remains singular.

The same distinction governs person. If b is the addressee, q=b selects
2sg *bodhyase*. If the same frame instead selects third-person singular
c, the verb is *bodhyate* and the unexpressed addressee-karman is
accusative *tvām*. It is the expressed kāraka, not whichever participant
happens to be an addressee, that supplies the agreement slot.

Vid in the admitted knowing sense follows the same selection proof,
with vedy- instead of bodhy-. Drinking pā follows the ingestion policy,
with pāyy-; it is not reclassified as a cognition root merely because
the selector has the same two alternatives.

## D5. hṛ: optional causee designation is not a free second selector

Let a be Devadatta, b several servants, and c one burden. The source
1.4.53 admits χ=K or χ=Agt for hṛ. The formation is hṛ → hār|i,
then hār|∅|ya → hārya. The two admitted branches give these constructed
illustrations:

| Branch | Selected karman and residual roles | Clause |
|---|---|---|
| (K,b) | b expressed; c unexpressed karman; a unexpressed causer | bhṛtyāḥ bhāraṃ hāryante devadattena |
| (Agt,c) | c expressed; b retained kartṛ; a distinct causer | bhāraḥ bhṛtyaiḥ hāryate devadattena |

On the second branch both b and a are instrumental, with distinct event
indices. On the first branch c is also karman, but the adopted non-cognition
causative policy selects b. Thus (K,c) is not an additional option in
this policy. On the second branch b is not karman, so (Agt,b) is ill-typed.
These are reasons for exactly two branches; no test-generated table is
needed to supply the argument. Kṛ has the same branch shape with its
making/product interpretation and kārya stem.

## D6. gam: identical verb, different formation and role graph

The simple passive of the admitted goal-taking gam frame expresses the
goal karman. With a singular goal it has *gam + ya + te → gamyate*.
An illustrative clause is *grāmaḥ gamyate devadattena*: the goal is
expressed and the mover is instrumental.

For the causal frame, a induces b's movement to c. Causee b becomes
karman by 1.4.52; the non-cognition causative selector expresses b.
The mit formation and lopa give

\[
 gam\longrightarrow gām|i\longrightarrow gam|i
 \longrightarrow gam|\varnothing|ya|te
 \longrightarrow \textit{gamyate}.
\]

Siddhāntakaumudī literally supplies *devadatto grāmaṃ gamyate* in this
causative discussion. Bālamanoramā explains the distinct inducing agent.
The causee is nominative, the goal accusative, and the causer instrumental
when supplied. With several causees the verb is *gamyante*; several
goals alone do not force that plural.

The basic and causal third-person singular words are identical. Their
formation, controller and causal frame are not. This supplies the actual
grammar collision used in N4's no-descent proof. It does not claim that
every full clause is ambiguous once all meanings and cases are supplied.

## D7. A base intransitive root need not produce a bhāva causative

Take bhū or sthā with a conscious causee b and no base karman c. The
causative relation introduces a, and 1.4.52 designates b as karman.
K3 therefore selects b as the unique expressed karman. If b is plural,
the actual causal event is transitive and D1 gives *bhāvyante/sthāpyante*.

For bhū, the retained path is bhū → bhāv|i → bhāv|∅|ya → bhāvya.
For sthā it is sthā|p|i → sthā|p|∅|ya → sthāpya. The latter retains
puk and does not take basic-passive sthā → sthī at a root-only address.

By contrast, the **basic** intransitive bhāva constructions in PG14 use
*bhūyate/sthīyate* and select 3sg even with several instrumental agents.
The root dictionary label “intransitive” cannot be reused as the final
voice/agreement decision after a valency-changing affix has been added.

## D8. Leading and drinking: two different first y owners

For nī the root vṛddhi/ayādi gives n[āy]|i, then 6.4.51 gives
n[āy]|∅|ya|te → *nāyyate*. Under K2, this selected nī causee is Agt,
so the led base karman c is expressed. The leading causee b and causer
a remain separately instrumental. The doubled y does not mean a second
causative has been added.

For drinking pā, the augment path gives pā|[y]yuk|i, then
pā|[y]yuk|∅|ya|te → *pāyyate*. Its causee is K, and the ingestion
selector permits b or the drink karman c to be expressed. The first y
is an augment, not nī's root-vowel substitute; the second is yak in
both paths. L5 explains why neither pair undergoes a generic y collapse.

These are the exact written chains supporting the table, with the
word-attestation limitation stated in L5. The protecting homonym of pā
and an unqualified root *pay* are not substitute evidence for drinking pā.

## D9. Separate noncausative double-karman constructions

Use K6's already designated basic frames. The basic source chooses the
secondary karman for its duh group, but the principal karman for
nī/hṛ/kṛṣ/vah. This decision precedes agreement. The source examples
have third-person singular controllers:

| Selected root/frame | Owned morphological continuation | Source-selected expression |
|---|---|---|
| duh, cow/milk | duh + yak + ta → duh + ya + te | gaur duhyate payaḥ |
| nī, led entity/destination | nī + yak + ta → nī + ya + te; kit blocks guṇa | ajā grāmaṃ nīyate |
| hṛ, carried entity/destination | hṛ + yak + ta → h[ri] + ya + te, 7.4.28 | ajā grāmaṃ hriyate |
| kṛṣ, source's drawing frame | kṛṣ + yak + ta → kṛṣ + ya + te; kit blocks guṇa | ajā grāmaṃ kṛṣyate |
| vah, conveyed entity/destination | vah → uah by 6.1.15; uah → uh by 6.1.108; + ya + te | ajā grāmaṃ uhyate |

The retained kit condition is local to these basic formations. It does
not erase the prior guṇa of PG13's causative karṣ|i. Hence *kṛṣyate*
and *karṣyate* have different derivations, even before their role frames
are compared. The source's unqualified kṛṣ example has the lexical-binding
qualification in SOURCE_REVIEW.

The apparent *gaur* is the quoted clause form of nominative *gauḥ* in
its source context; PG15's role assignment is cow-nominative, milk-accusative.
It does not construct the entire external-sandhi subsystem from this
single example. Milk *payaḥ* has surface case syncretism, another reason
that the role/case decision cannot be reconstructed from suffix spelling
alone.

## D10. What the written construction has established

K5 gives the finite source-policy branches for a completed semantic frame.
L6 gives the owned passive stem on every one of the twelve formation
rows. D1 supplies the unique ending path for its selected discourse slot.
Their composition therefore determines the displayed meaning-to-form
relation without choosing an ending by guessing from the causer's number.

Ambiguity in an **input's meaning or lexical identity** remains an open
obligation until resolved; it is not converted into one of K3's licensed
grammatical options. Conversely a real K3 option is retained as two
histories even if both controllers happen to have the same person/number
and therefore print the same verb. N3–N5 preserve both kinds of information
at the operator level and establish the stated zero-reading.
