# PG15 — Which karman does the causative passive express?

Author: Monty. 2 November 2026.

## K1. Carrier and admitted frames

Begin on the operator family in [NATIVE_EXTENSION.md](NATIVE_EXTENSION.md),
which extends PG14's cut-field path algebra, history module, cuts and pairing.
The following semantic objects label its source and target grades. Its
zero-reading retains the same labels and source evidence; it does not infer
meaning from a final word.

An input frame has a base event e, a distinct causer a and causee b, an
optional base karman c, an explicit hetumat relation, exact lexical identity,
selected sense, and participant person/number. The original event's b is
kartṛ. In the causative event a is hetu and kartṛ (1.4.54–55), licensing
ṇic by 3.1.26. These are typed roles indexed by event: the two uses of the
word kartṛ do not identify a with b.

The domain D_C^pass uses the twelve PG13 identities. Its frames are
unaccented, unprefixed, classical laṭ with one ṇic, no prior san/yaṅ or
nested causative, no karmakartṛ, no reciprocal construction, and no
participant coalescence. The bhū/sthā base frames are genuinely intransitive
with conscious b. The other ten have one intended base karman c; gam's c
is its intended goal. Nī's c is the led entity, not an additional destination
karman. A three-object causative of an already double-object base is excluded.

The displayed word reading selects the non-doubling alternatives of
8.4.46–47. It is the conventional undoubled consonant reading, not an
enumeration of all optional phonetic repetitions. L5 records the local
double/triple-y distinction without expanding this domain to all phonetic
branches.

The frame excludes temporal/spatial secondary karmans, avivakṣita-karman
extensions, special upapada cases, coordinated mixed-person controllers and
1.4.106's special person construction. No role is separately expressed by
a kṛt/taddhita/compound. Nominal stems and their case realization are supplied
by the nominal grammar; this chapter assigns cases rather than rederiving
all nominal declensions. Meaning interpretation and genuine grammatical
optionality remain different: an unknown sense is an obligation, not a
free choice of whatever output looks familiar.

## K2. Construct the causee-role branches first

Use the same source-bounded kāraka decisions as PG14 V5. Denote b's retained
kartṛ branch by χ=Agt and its karman branch by χ=K. These symbols are role
labels; Agt must not be confused with ātmanepada A.

| Exact root and sense | Admitted χ | Source for b's relation |
|---|---|---|
| bhū 1010001, being; sthā 1011077, standing | K | 1.4.52, intransitive base |
| gam 1011137, movement with intended goal | K | 1.4.52, gati |
| vid 1020059, knowing; budh 1040068, understanding | K | 1.4.52, buddhi |
| pā 1011074, drinking | K | 1.4.52, ingestion |
| nī 1011049, leading | Agt | Kāśikā 1.4.52's nī/vah qualification |
| kṛ 1080010, making; hṛ 1011046, carrying | K or Agt | 1.4.53 |
| pac 1011151, cooking; dā 1030010, giving; kṛṣ 1060006, furrowing | Agt | outside the admitted 1.4.52 classes; retained kartṛ route |

Let R(F) be the set of admitted χ values. For a chosen χ, construct

\[
 K_F(\chi)=\{c:\ c\text{ is the retained base karman}\}
      \cup\{b:\chi=K\}.
\]

Each member carries its designation source. For example, c can carry
1.4.49 while b carries 1.4.52 or 1.4.53. A set of two noun strings without
those roles is not enough to decide which karman is expressible.

Every admitted causal frame has at least one karman. For bhū/sthā it is
b; in every Agt branch it is c. Consequently these frames are transitive
at the **causative-event level**. They license karmaṇi by 3.4.69, not bhāva
merely because the base root was intransitive. This is why bhūyate's bhāva
restriction does not impose singular on causative bhāvyante.

## K3. Adopted double-karman selection relation

Siddhāntakaumudī's prose at the end of the bhāva/karma procedure states:

> बुद्धिभक्षार्थयोः शब्दकर्मकाणां निजेच्छया ।
> प्रयोज्यकर्मण्यन्येषां ण्यन्तानां लादयो मताः ।

Bālamanoramā identifies the other causal cases as gati, intransitive-base
and hṛ/kṛ, and explicitly illustrates causative gam with its causee
expressed. These statements select a karman; they do not designate a new
karman independently of K2. Word-object causatives are in the cited general
policy but outside this packet's twelve-root domain.

Define Sel(F,χ) as follows:

| Situation after K2 | Eligible expressed q |
|---|---|
| Exactly one karman | that unique karman |
| Two karmans; vid/budh cognition or pā ingestion | b or c |
| Two karmans; gam movement | b only |
| Two karmans; kṛ/hṛ with χ=K | b only |

The bhū/sthā rows have only b in this domain; temporal/spatial additions
would require the separately excluded extension. For kṛ/hṛ with χ=Agt,
only c is karman, so the first row applies. It is a type error to select
b as the expressed karman on that branch merely because b was eligible
on the alternative χ=K branch.

The complete selection relation is

\[
 \mathcal B(F)=\{(\chi,q):\chi\in R(F),\ q\in\operatorname{Sel}(F,\chi)\}.
\]

It is a dependent union, not the Cartesian product of every causee choice
with every mentioned participant. On hṛ/kṛ it has the two branches
(K,b) and (Agt,c), rather than four combinations.

## K4. Case, pada and agreement after selecting q

Karmaṇi fixes ātmanepada by 1.3.13. PG14's **kartari** beneficiary/upapada
decision is not rerun on this branch. In particular budh's active-causative
P exception 1.3.86 does not create a P passive, and 1.3.77 does not double
the passive alternatives. The active and passive constructions start from
the event/formation, not from each other's printed endings.

For a branch (χ,q), the source-based assignment is:

| Participant | Expression and case route |
|---|---|
| q | expressed karman of tiṅ; residual nominative under the admitted 2.3.46 conditions |
| Every other member of K_F(χ) | unexpressed karman; accusative by 2.3.1–2 |
| Causer a | unexpressed kartṛ of the causal event; instrumental by 2.3.18 |
| Causee b, when χ=Agt | retained unexpressed kartṛ; instrumental by 2.3.18 |

Two instrumental participants in the final clause retain different event
roles and source identities. Their identical case does not merge them.

The agreement slot is slot(q), using 1.4.105, 1.4.107 and 1.4.108 for
person, and 1.4.21–22 / 1.4.101–102 for number. The causer's person/number
does not determine tiṅ here. Selecting another eligible q can change the
verb ending even though the causative passive stem is identical.

## K5. Existence, exclusivity and composition on this domain

**Proposition K5.** Every completed input in D_C^pass has a nonempty finite
set of source-licensed branches B(F). Each branch selects exactly one
expressed karman and one agreement slot, and assigns every retained
participant its stated case. This relation contains all the alternatives
of the adopted K2–K3 source policy on D_C^pass.

**Proof.** K2's finite partition assigns either K, Agt or both to b. A
K branch has b, and an Agt branch has the supplied c, so K_F(χ) is nonempty.
There are at most two members. With one, the selector is unique. With two,
the domain partitions into cognition/ingestion, gam, or hṛ/kṛ; K3 gives
respectively both members or b alone. Thus no source option in the adopted
partition is lost, and no participant outside the karman set is admitted.
Once q is fixed, K4 has one disjoint case route for each participant and
one discourse slot for q. Finiteness follows from these finite choices. □

The passive stem construction in NIC_LOPA depends on the root/formation,
not on which eligible q was selected. Let Stem(F) be that owned construction
and End_A its PG14 ending relation. The complete written result is

\[
 \bigcup_{(\chi,q)\in\mathcal B(F)}
  \bigl(\operatorname{Case}(F,\chi,q),
        \operatorname{End}_A(\operatorname{Stem}(F),\operatorname{slot}(q)),
        \text{retained branch/source history}\bigr).
\]

By PG14 S4 this composition inherits local soundness and invariant
preservation. Relative completeness requires precisely the local readings
and domain restrictions just stated. It is not a theorem that all Sanskrit
multi-object constructions have been classified or all histories commute.

## K6. Separate basic-root comparison domain

To keep the formation distinction explicit, admit five noncausative
double-karman frames with their two roles already source-designated:

| Basic root | Principal and secondary karmans | Expressed karman | Source word construction |
|---|---|---|---|
| duh 1020004, milking/filling | milk; cow in the stated akathita reading | secondary cow | gaur duhyate payaḥ |
| nī 1011049, leading | led entity; destination | principal led entity | ajā grāmaṃ nīyate |
| hṛ 1011046, carrying | carried entity; destination | principal carried entity | ajā grāmaṃ hriyate |
| kṛṣ 1060006, drawing/furrowing | drawn entity in the source example; destination | principal drawn entity | ajā grāmaṃ kṛṣyate |
| vah 1011159, conveying | conveyed entity; destination | principal conveyed entity | ajā grāmaṃ uhyate |

This is the source's specific contextual extension for kṛṣ, distinct from
the furrowing causative profile. The table does not silently make all
furrowing uses double-karman. The exact lexical assignment of the source's
unqualified kṛṣ label is discussed in SOURCE_REVIEW; its word example is
not an independent witness for all contexts of the PG13 lexical row.

The role-selection policy comes from the same Siddhāntakaumudī passage:
secondary karman for its duh group, principal for nī/hṛ/kṛṣ/vah. The
Mahābhāṣya 1.4.51 discussion also gives the cow and goat examples. These
are finite source-selected frames, not a complete account of akathita
designation, competing apādāna readings or the whole duh-group list.

Their word chains are in DERIVATIONS. This separate domain makes a central
distinction visible: basic hṛ selects its carried entity here, while
causative hṛ with a causee-karman selects that causee. A rule dispatching
only on the unqualified spelling hṛ would conflate the two.
