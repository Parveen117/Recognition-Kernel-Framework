# PG15 — Owned ṇic-lopa and the causative passive aṅga

Author: Monty. 2 November 2026.

## L1. Formation state, not an active word with an ending removed

Work in the typed history module of [NATIVE_EXTENSION.md](NATIVE_EXTENSION.md).
An input to this chapter contains the causal frame, exact PG13 lexical
identity, the teaching affix ṇic, its owned markers and vowel, and the
source-bearing root operations already licensed in that formation. The
construction is independent of the choice of expressed karman in K3;
that choice determines case and the subsequent tiṅ slot.

There are three different objects which must not be identified:

1. The base dhātu, before causativization.
2. The derived dhātu with ṇic and its root effects, before a present vikaraṇa.
3. The kartari present aṅga with śap and the additional changes which its
   following ending/vikaraṇa licenses.

PG15 starts at the second object. It does not remove *ati/ate* from a
causative active word and replace it by *yate*. Nor does it discard the
causative formation and apply the basic passive to the original root.
Both shortcuts lose the distinction between *kriyate*, *kāryate* and
*kārayate*.

Ṇic's markers ṇ and c are unpronounced under the it/lopa construction
inherited from PG13. Its pronounced i is a separate owned occurrence.
The event below removes that i; it does not newly remove the markers,
delete every letter i, or undo all changes caused by the affix.

## L2. The local affix-class calculation

The karmaṇi request licenses A by 1.3.13. Laṭ is replaced by the selected
tiṅ ending. That ending is sārvadhātuka by 3.4.113. In bhāva/karma before
such an ending, 3.1.67 introduces yak. After its final k is marked and
unpronounced, yak has visible ya and retained kit.

Yak itself is neither tiṅ nor śit. It therefore belongs to the remaining
ārdhadhātuka class under 3.4.114. The following ending's sārvadhātuka status
does not make every intervening affix sārvadhātuka. The relevant adjacency is

\[
 [C]_{\text{causative root constituents}}
 [i]_{\text{ṇic}}
 [ya]_{\text{yak, kit, ārdhadhātuka}}
 [\tau]_{\text{tiṅ, sārvadhātuka}}.
\]

The iṭ condition 7.2.35 requires a val-initial ārdhadhātuka. Yak begins
with y, and y is outside val. Thus this yak does not acquire iṭ by that
rule. The next affix to ṇic is an aniṭ ārdhadhātuka, exactly the local
condition of 6.4.51 **ṇer aniṭi**.

Kāśikā explicitly includes *kāryate* and *hāryate* among its consequences,
and *kārayitā*, *hārayitā* among the contrasting seṭ examples. Its statement
that the lopa overrides iyaṅ, yaṇ, guṇa, vṛddhi and dīrgha is a general
conflict statement, not a claim that all five operations simultaneously
apply before this particular yak. In the present environment, 7.4.25's
otherwise available lengthening of an eligible final vowel before kit y
does not preserve ṇic's i as ī against 6.4.51.

Define the owned event

\[
 L_{\text{ṇic}}:
  C\,|\,i_{\text{ṇic}}\,|\,ya_{\text{yak}}\,|\,\tau
  \longmapsto
  C\,|\,\varnothing_{\text{ṇic}}\,|\,ya_{\text{yak}}\,|\,\tau.
\]

The target history retains the teaching affix, its source, the vanished
vowel and the causal event. The visible concatenation is Cyaτ. In the
native lift, this is an actual change of the owned state, with payload
\(1+z_L\); it is not merely multiplication of an unchanged word by a label.

In the last column the final *ya* is yak's visible constituent. The
retained *p* in sthāpya/dāpya and the first *y* in pāyya are augment
material; neither is ṇic's vowel. In nāyya the first *y* has a third
provenance: the root-vowel substitute āy. An unowned instruction such as
“delete the causative suffix including its preceding consonant” would
incorrectly erase all these distinctions.

The basic-passive rule 6.4.66 for specified ā-final roots does not see
bare sthā/dā as the final aṅga in these augmented formations. Before lopa
the derived dhātu ends in ṇic's i; after the visible lopa its retained
root/augment constituents end in p. Thus the basic *sthīyate/dīyate*
chains are not copied into *sthāpya/dāpya*. Similarly 7.4.28's riṅ for
an eligible ṛ-final aṅga gives basic *kriyate/hriyate*, not a replacement
of the already formed kār/hār in this causal chain.

For gam, 6.4.92's mit shortening belongs to the inherited formation.
6.4.93's optional length before ciṇ/ṇamul does not turn the present yak
path into an optional *gāmya* path. The vowel-initial condition of
6.4.98 is also false before yak; Kāśikā there expressly contrasts
*gamyate* and *hanyate*. None of these conditions is determined merely
by seeing the letters *gam* in the final word.

## L4. Ordinary lopa and the limits of retained visibility

6.4.51 is ordinary affix lopa. It is not luk, ślu or lup. 1.1.62 supplies
pratyayalakṣaṇa after affix lopa; 1.1.63 withdraws the stated aṅga effect
in the lumat situation. Kāśikā 1.1.63 itself gives *kāryate/hāryate* as
the contrasting non-lumat cases. This is a direct source reason not to
erase the causative root effect with the pronounced affix.

Two boundaries matter:

* Root operations already performed with visible ṇic retain their actual
  output and source history. A later kit affix does not globally undo a
  root operation which was licensed by a different, earlier affix.
* Pratyayalakṣaṇa is not a blanket re-creation of every deleted sound for
  every phoneme-sensitive rule. Kāśikā 1.1.62 distinguishes varṇāśraya
  operations and denies the unrestricted ayādi inference in its own
  examples. The data model must answer rule-relative visibility queries.

Therefore the ordered construction in L3 is sufficient: first construct
the derived dhātu with its root effects, then attach yak and apply the
owned lopa. This is a stage boundary with retained evidence, not a universal
claim that every complete derivation has the same chronological order.

An independent local observation for nī is useful. Suppose root vṛddhi
has occurred but its ayādi is still pending:

\[
 n[ai]_{root}\,|\,i_{\text{ṇic}}\,|\,ya_{\text{yak}}.
\]

Applying 6.1.78 before L produces nāy|i|ya and then nāy|∅|ya. If L is
performed first, its vowel-to-zero substitution is caused by the following
affix. For the preceding root's 6.1.78 query, 1.1.57 supplies the relevant
paranimittaka ajādeśa visibility. The ayādi query is not one of the
excluded operations listed in 1.1.58. The same owned visible endpoint
can therefore be obtained without falsely citing 1.1.62 as an unrestricted
phonemic reconstruction. This is an application of the general source
rule to this explicitly displayed local state, not a literal commentary
quotation of *nāyyate*.

The permission is query-specific: L continues to inspect whether the
owned i is actually present and does not repeatedly delete a resurrected
vowel. The two schedules have different retained histories and different
ordered residue words. Equality of their final owned pronunciation does
not imply equality of their native operators or global confluence.

## L5. Why the two y's remain

For nī, the path ends

\[
 nī\longrightarrow n[ai]\longrightarrow n[āy]
 \quad\text{before ṇic},\qquad
 n[āy]\,|\,i\,|\,ya\longrightarrow n[āy]\,|\,\varnothing\,|\,ya.
\]

For drinking pā, the path is instead

\[
 [pā]\,|\,[y]_{yuk}\,|\,i\,|\,ya
 \longrightarrow [pā]\,|\,[y]_{yuk}\,|\,\varnothing\,|\,ya.
\]

In either case there are two adjacent visible y occurrences with distinct
owners. 6.1.66 deletes an eligible y/v before **val**; the following y is
not val. Kāśikā's own counterexamples *ūyyate/knūyyate* make this boundary
explicit. This does not mean those examples are lexical witnesses for nī
or pā; they witness the guard's false case.

Nor does 8.4.64 authorize reducing the pair to one y: its deleted yam must
be preceded by a hal and followed by a yam. Here the first y is preceded
by ā, and the second y is followed by a. Neither qualifies. The commentary's
discussion of two/three y occurrences in *śayyā* likewise must not be
turned into an unrestricted identical-letter collapse.

There is a separate optional phonetic stage. Siddhāntakaumudī 8.4.46–47
explicitly retains the optional reading of consonant doubling. Applying
8.4.47 to the first y after ā in the displayed pair can introduce a
third y. In that new state the middle y has a preceding hal and a
following yam, so 8.4.64 can remove it, returning the pair; retaining it
gives a triple-y reading. This does not license reducing the original
pair to a single y. The conventional word tables choose the non-doubling
branch; no exhaustive set of phonetic realizations or recursive doubling
scheduler is claimed.

Thus the declared word paths have *nāyya* and *pāyya*, not *nāya* and *pāya*.
Their word forms below are written rule consequences. No exact standalone
*nāyyate/pāyyate* quotation was located in the selected primary passages;
that distinction remains visible in SOURCE_REVIEW.

## L6. Local correctness and excluded extensions

**Proposition L6.** For every completed PG13 pre-yak constituent in L3,
the admitted karmaṇi yak continuation has the displayed Cya endpoint.
Exactly ṇic's owned pronounced i is removed. Its formation identity,
root/augment ownership and causal roles survive in the history.

**Proof.** L2 supplies an aniṭ ārdhadhātuka immediately after ṇic on each
row, so 6.4.51 applies. The affected owned constituent is the same ṇic
occurrence in all twelve rows. L3 lists its preceding root/augment output;
none of those sounds belongs to the removed vowel. L4 preserves the
formation effects within the declared visibility policy. L5 disposes of
the y-lopa ambiguity on the only two doubled-y rows. The remaining
concatenations give exactly Cya. Every event retains its old/new state
and source, so the erased vowel and semantic formation remain recoverable
from the history, without being pronounced. □

This proposition does not license deleting ṇic before every affix. The
seṭ contrast *kārayitā* already refutes that extension. Niṣṭhā has the
separate 6.4.52 provision and its own iṭ/lopa ordering. Other future,
ciṇ, ṇamul, san, yaṅ, perfect, accent and nested-formation cases require
their own domains and precedence. Those obligations are not hidden in
the word “passive.”
