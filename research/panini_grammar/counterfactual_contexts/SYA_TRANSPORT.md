# PG31 — Transporting owned sya interfaces into lṛṅ

Author: Monty. 3 November 2026.

## S1. A new semantic admission, a shared internal affix

The inherited vowel operations use the vṛddhi/guṇa definitions of
1.1.1/2, the ik target restriction of 1.1.3 where applicable, and
1.1.51 for ṛ's r-bearing substitute. Junction operations keep their
explicit two-place domain under 6.1.84; they are not blind replacements
of every similarly printed vowel.

Rule 3.1.33 explicitly treats lṛṭ and lṛṅ under the common lṛ identity:
both take sya; luṭ takes tās. Kāśikā gives kariṣyati/akariṣyat.
R admission comes from A, not PG16's ordinary future meaning. Thus a
past counterfactual can have sya without asserting that its event is future.

Separate three interfaces:

1. Exact root or inherited derived dhātu, qualified role/pada evidence.
2. The owned sya, its own iṭ branch, and root/affix operations.
3. The lṛṅ tiṅ member plus aṭ/āṭ and E's endings.

The middle interface is transported from PG16/17; their future semantics
and lṛṭ ṭit endings are not. This is a lift of an owned construction,
not prefixing a to a completed future word.

## S5. Restricted transport lemma

**Lemma S5.** Each selected S2–S4 branch lifts from its owned sya
interface into R with the same inner root/pada/iṭ permission and with
E's augmented ending interface. No admitted branch is gained or lost.

**Proof.** Rule 3.1.33 provides the same sya. S2 lists the eight inherited
root predicates; S3 exhausts the nine additional exact roots and their
sya-based dependent choices; S4 establishes the derived-dhātu and retained
role/ṇic conditions. None uses lṛṭ's ṭit as its deciding hypothesis.
The changed hypotheses are isolated to aṭ/āṭ and the final tiṅ path
in E. Their targets are distinct from root-facing iṭ and ṇic.
For the two vowel-initial inputs E1 gives the combined vṛddhi/guṇa path
explicitly. Later root/sya phonetics are read only at their lawful stage.
Thus the same joint branches compose with all nine new ending paths. □

This is not a universal commutation theorem for every grammatical event,
nor a licence to feed a late C-factor spelling back into an earlier query.
