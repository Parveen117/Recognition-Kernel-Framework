# PG31 — Interpreted contexts before lakāra selection

Author: Monty. 3 November 2026.

## D1. Full-history inputs

Begin on N1's native carrier with an interpreted frame

\[
 f=(\ell,\sigma,\eta,e,a,\beta,p,n,u,\tau,\gamma,q,K,\mathcal E).
\]

Here ℓ/σ fix the exact lexical identity and sense; η is basic or the
inherited owned ṇic formation; e/a are event/actor identities; β contains
the qualified pada and causal-role evidence; p/n determine agreement;
u/τ supply the utterance and temporal interpretation; γ is the addressed
semantic context; q records the relevant upapada occurrences and their
clause association; K is the supplied kriyātipatti evidence; and E retains
the source policy, stage, exception decisions and remaining obligations.
Original markers, present constituents, deleted owners and ordered Smriti
remain in the history. A word does not determine these fields backwards.

The time adapter distinguishes intended past, present and future reference
relative to u. It is linguistic input, not a physical clock or a scalar
interpretation of lambda. In a counterfactual the intended event need not
have an actual timestamp. Absence of an observed occurrence is not K.

Completed semantic predicates give T/F cuts; unresolved predicates give
the separate ? channel. A grammatical option has positively established
premises. It is not an unresolved truth value turned into two answers.

## D2. Contextual and morphological gates

Use the request labels L=laṭ, O=non-āśīs liṅ, F=lṛṭ and R=lṛṅ.
An ordinary temporal request relation Q(τ) is an explicitly supplied
interface in the one place where 3.3.143 preserves it. PG31 does not
construct all its possible luṅ/liṭ/luṭ words. A request can therefore be
licensed even when a particular word adapter is not in this packet.

The new context families are 3.3.142–151 and the counterfactual operators
3.3.139–141. For the exclusive 152 boundary, admit the exact affirmative
uta/api certificate of PG28 C1; for the classical cause/result example,
admit PG28 C5's exact 3.3.156 dependency with K added. These two explicit
adapters extend PG28's former non-counterfactual boundary. They do not
retroactively enlarge every PG28/29/30 theorem or all later liṅ meanings.
Counterfactual desire, blessing and competing āśaṃsā interpretations
still require their own source/scope decision.

The new R word domain is classical, unprefixed, unaccented, finite kartari,
with one resolved controller and the inherited non-doubling/devoiced-pause
policy. For causatives retain the causer, causee/base agent, admitted
karmans, source-qualified causee-role branches and the causer controller.
PG17's stem and role restrictions survive; only the admitted outer
lakāra/context is extended. The discourse upapadas here are not automatically
new verbal prefixes or new 1.3.77 beneficiary conditions.

## D3. Meaning is not token matching

Garhā is supplied censure; anavakḷpti is a supplied incredulity or
impossibility assessment; amarṣa is intolerance; citrīkaraṇa is surprise.
These are the speaker's interpreted attitudes, not mathematical proofs
that the described action is wrong, impossible or physically achievable.
Traditional examples are evidence for their grammatical analyses, not
endorsement of their social judgments.

Keep these source distinctions:

- kiṃvṛtta includes the appropriate inflected/derived kim expressions;
  **kiṃ kila as a whole** has a distinct 3.3.146 role.
- yad in 3.3.147 is the supplied indeclinable usage; yac ca is the
  addressed two-word expression; yatra is its distinct alternative.
- yadā/yadi enter 3.3.147 through the named vārttika. They are not
  spelling aliases of yad, yac ca, or PG29's locative yati condition.
- astyartha in 3.3.146 concerns an expressed being/existence predicate
  such as asti/bhavati/vidyate associated with the addressed event.
  Mere appearance of those letters elsewhere does not select F.

The finite context matrix resolves one of the named families and its
explicitly reviewed overrides. Unreviewed simultaneous triggers remain
an overlap obligation; no universal “highest sūtra number wins” algorithm
is asserted. The surprise-plus-incredulity/yadi intersection is separately
admitted because Nyāsa/Padamañjarī explicitly analyze it.

## D4. What changes and what is inherited

PG16's eight basic roots give 12 R profiles. PG17's nine further roots
give 15 R profiles and 20 joint pada/iṭ branches. Its twelve causal
formations give 21 profiles. Thus there are 48 profile possibilities
across completed semantic frames, with 53 branch rows. Each has nine
agreement positions. Optional iṭ does not create a new position key.

The 17 basic entries and 12 causal base entries overlap in six identities,
so the packet uses **23 distinct lexical IDs**, not 29 distinct roots.
Different senses, homonyms, extra prefixes and causal role multiplicity
are not silently added. A 3.3.147 future K frame supplies an R-admission
witness for every qualified profile; no assertion that all profiles or
both padas are simultaneously available to one utterance follows.

PG30's māṅ suppression is outside this R domain. It cannot be used to
remove the aṭ/āṭ required here. PG27's earlier vac correction is already
in the inherited 10,627 baseline and is not subtracted again.

Unbuilt sectors include passive/bhāva and PG18 ciṇvat R, other basic
root families, san/yaṅ, Vedic/accent variants, universal sentence parsing,
general external sandhi and unrestricted cross-context precedence.
