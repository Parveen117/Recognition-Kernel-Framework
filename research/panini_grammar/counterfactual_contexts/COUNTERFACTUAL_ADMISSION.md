# PG31 — Effective liṅ-nimitta and kriyātipatti

Author: Monty. 3 November 2026.

## A1. The three premises of 3.3.139

After C's exceptions retain an effective liṅ certificate κ. It identifies
the **licensed context**, not an actually selected O word. Kāśikā defines
kriyātipatti through nonfulfillment caused by an impediment or deficient
conditions. The action's nonfulfillment is supplied interpreted evidence;
it is not failure of a grammar rule, a corpus search or a software test.

On the admitted future frame 139 requires

\[
 Future(\tau)\land\kappa\land K(e).
\]

When these hold prescribe R. Without κ, failure alone does not license
R. Without K, an ordinary hypothetical cause/result relation retains its
O/F alternatives. Unknown Future or K remains Open. A present frame
receives no R from the operators constructed here.

The policy covers C's effective contexts and D2's two explicit PG28
certificates. Tattvabodhinī 139 records disagreement over extending this
to earlier āśaṃsā liṅ contexts. The disagreement is not resolved by
declaring every liṅ occurrence everywhere eligible.

## A2. Past reference and the exclusive boundary

Rule 140 extends the relevant prescription to past reference. Rule 141
is parsed **vā ā utāpyoh**; ā marks a boundary excluding 152 itself.
Before 152, on reviewed liṅ contexts, past K optionally admits R alongside
the otherwise obtained relation. The admitted affirmative 152 certificate
has obligatory R in past K; so does the admitted cause/result 156
certificate. No automatic claim about every later rule follows from its
number alone.

Let S be the effective requests before the K transformation, b the
**source-certified scope band**, and κ the effective eligibility.
For completed frames define

\[
 A(S,\kappa,b,\tau,K)=
 \begin{cases}
 S,&\neg\kappa\text{ or }\neg K,\\
 \{R\},&\kappa\land K\land Future(\tau),\\
 S\cup\{R\},&\kappa\land K\land Past(\tau)\land b=Pre152,\\
 \{R\},&\kappa\land K\land Past(\tau)\land b=Admitted152Plus,\\
 S,&\kappa\land K\land Present(\tau).
 \end{cases}
\]

The present row means only “no new R from A”; it does not validate an
inconsistent semantic frame. Unknown inputs retain conditional obligations,
not their union as licensed output. For 143, S includes only the appropriate
Q(τ). Equal request labels from distinct sources retain provenance without
being counted twice as morphological positions.

## A4. Two events, not one sentence-wide failure flag

For the admitted PG28 cause/result frame retain H(e_c,e_f), both event
owners/controllers and separately addressed K evidence. Kāśikā 139
describes knowledge of the future dependency and its nonfulfillment.
Padamañjarī notes that a qualified action, such as eating with ghee, can
fail even when unqualified eating occurs. K therefore attaches to the
**interpreted event with its qualifiers**, not every verb with a shared
root or every clause in the sentence.

When both addressed events satisfy the future K premises, PG28 C5's
{O,F}×{O,F} becomes {R}×{R}. For past K the selected 140 extension has
the same singleton pair. If only one event's K obligation is discharged,
A acts only there; the other remains under its own completed/open
certificate. No arbitrary one-site assignment is claimed consistent with
every causal story.

A supplied future “if he had come, he would have cooked” frame can use
**agamiṣyat** and **apakṣyat** on their qualified P profiles. These
are already P1 positions, not two extra counts. The two events retain
distinct owners even in examples that use the same lexical root twice.

## A5. Admission theorem

**Theorem A5.** On D's completed, consistent, source-certified families,
C followed by A gives the declared effective relation. R occurs exactly
when the selected liṅ certificate, K and past/future conditions hold.
Future is obligatory; past optionality is exactly the admitted Pre152
band. F-only/L-only exceptions receive no R from erased preliminary O
permissions.

**Proof.** C7 fixes S, κ and b after the reviewed exceptions. A2's
disjoint completed cases are 139/140/141's prescriptions. When κ is false,
the sources on 142/146/151 explicitly deny this R route. Every positive
R has the three premises; every admitted positive premise falls into a
future/past row. A4's event addresses prevent certificate reuse at an
unrelated owner. □

This is source-relative grammar on supplied evidence, not a universal
theory of counterfactual truth or of translating English “would”.
