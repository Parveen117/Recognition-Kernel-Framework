## C2. Censure: optionality and temporal reference

Rule 142 supplies laṭ across times. It does not require present event
reference and does not preserve ordinary past/future alternatives in the
selected Kāśikā/Kaumudī policy. Padamañjarī records a different Bhāṣya
analysis through habitual action and non-intended completion; that policy
is archived rather than silently merged into this selector.

For 143, ca retains L beside O, while vibhāṣā preserves Q(τ). It is
wrong to expose every past and future lakāra regardless of τ. A
future-general frame can give {O,L,F}; it does not thereby admit past
liṭ. The cross-time L comes from ca, not merely ordinary present laṭ.

In 144 vibhāṣā no longer continues. Explicit O/F replaces the ordinary
temporal family, and O's explicit mention prevents inherited L from being
carried in. Thus {O,F,L} is not this row's relation. The same F morphology
may express ordinary future elsewhere and a special attitude across times
here. Retain its semantic origin.

## C3. Two attitudes, each admitting both moods

At 145 garhā ceases. Both O and F are available for **either** anavakḷpti
or amarṣa. The commentaries expressly reject a yathāsaṃkhya assignment
O→incredulity, F→intolerance. Kiṃvṛtta is neither required nor excluded.

The 146 exception gives F alone in its stated upapada environment. It
removes 145's O permission. Kāśikā, Kaumudī, Nyāsa and selected later
commentaries explicitly conclude **no lṛṅ**, because the effective
liṅ-nimitta is absent. A preliminary 145 match is not a current certificate
after this exception.

## C4. jātu/yad and yac ca/yatra

Rule 147 gives O alone, overriding the preceding F alternative. Its
vārttika supplies yadā/yadi on the same qualified attitude domain.
It does not make every conditional sentence containing yadi optative.

Rule 148 gives O alone for yac ca/yatra. There is no yathāsaṃkhya
allocation of the two expressions to the two attitudes. Separating 148
from 147 matters: **only yac ca/yatra** continue into 149–150. Carrying
jātu/yad into all later rows changes the relation.

The same jātu therefore selects L with supplied censure under 142,
or O with supplied incredulity/intolerance under 147. Token identity does
not choose the rule. Adjacency is not a substitute for the retained
upapada-to-event association.

## C5. The semantic resets and the surprise remainder

Rule 149 resets the semantic field to censure; previous attitudes are
not required. With yac ca/yatra it gives O alone across times. Rule 150
extends that O prescription to surprise, again with those expressions.
Both are effective liṅ contexts for A's separate K/temporal conditions.
Surprise alone does not prove K.

Rule 151 concerns the **remaining upapada domain of surprise**. Śeṣa
does not mean everything other than surprise. Exclude yac ca, yatra and
yadi; then prescribe F alone. This F is not subject to the ordinary
FutureAnadyatana→luṭ selection of PG16. There is no effective O certificate
from this row, hence no R from it.

## C6. yadi needs a positive source route

The literal sources give a surprise/yadi O example as 151's negative
control. Nyāsa/Padamañjarī explain that **incredulity and surprise are
both understood**: once 151's F is excluded, 147's yadā/yadi extension
supplies O on its own attitude premises. Tattvabodhinī also names it.
Thus the admitted mixed frame has

\[
 Surprise\land Incredulity\land Yadi\longmapsto
 \{O\}_{147\text{-vārttika}}.
\]

This is not the invalid inference “151 does not apply, therefore O”.
For surprise+yadi without a supplied attitude, retain the failed 151
guard and an alternative-route obligation. Padamañjarī also reports a
Bhāṣya interpretation in which yadi conveys sambhāvanā and 154 supplies
O. That is a separate policy, not automatic conversion into PG28's
qualified capacity input.

For the admitted mixed frame, any later R derives from the effective 147
certificate plus A's premises, not from 151. Source phrases “no lṛṅ
here” stay scoped to 151's F-only prescription; they do not erase the
independently explained 147 route.

## C7. Bounded completeness

**Proposition C7.** On D's resolved families under the selected policy,
C1–C6 give exactly the declared effective requests and liṅ certificates.

**Proof.** Rows 142–144 supply the reviewed censure cases and their exact
optionality. Row 145 supplies both moods for either attitude; 146 removes
O, while 147/148 remove F in their separately resolved environments.
Rows 149/150 reset the attitude and retain only 148's upapadas. Row 151
is the surprise remainder excluding yadi; C6 supplies the positive mixed
route. Each removal/restoration has an explicit source witness. Negative
and ? channels complete the partial selector without universal rejection
of other constructions. □
