# PG31 — aṭ/āṭ and nine conditional endings

Author: Monty. 3 November 2026.

## E1. Augmentation on the addressed aṅga

The selected lṛṅ is ṅit. Rule 6.4.71 supplies initial aṭ to the
consonant-initial aṅga; 6.4.72 supplies āṭ instead to an ajādi aṅga.
The ṭ marker places the augment at the beginning under 1.1.46 and is
removed with its provenance retained. These are morphology rules on
lakāra identity: even future-reference R has the augment. No inference
that “a means past time” enters the construction.

All S2–S4 inputs are consonant-initial except basic edh/iṣ. For them,
6.1.90 gives the āṭ+initial-vowel vṛddhi junction:

\[
 [\bar a]_{\text{āṭ}}|edh|i_{\text{iṭ}}|sya\to aidh|i|sya,
\]
\[
 [\bar a]_{\text{āṭ}}|eṣ|i_{\text{iṭ}}|sya
 \to aiṣ|i|sya.
\]

For iṣ, select the inherited root-facing upadhā-guṇa iṣ→eṣ before
the āṭ junction, then apply ā+e→ai. Ajādi status holds in either
root view. Although direct ā+i also reads ai, that equality does not
replace the selected history or license a fresh guṇa query on late ai.
Edh's initial e likewise
gives ai. Thus the factors are **aidhiṣy**, **aiṣiṣy**, not
a+edhiṣy or a+eṣiṣy. Kaumudī 72's full aidha imperfect A series
supports this augment interface; it is not itself a quoted lṛṅ paradigm.

Māṅ is excluded from this R domain, so 6.4.74 suppression does not
apply. Root-facing iṭ and A first-singular ending iṭ remain distinct
from initial aṭ/āṭ.

## E4. Late reading and visibility

The thematic a keeps these ending contacts apart from the root/sya
junctions. S's root guṇa and ayādi are early owned operations. Later
8.2.30 c→k, 8.2.18 r→l, 8.3.59 ṣatva and 8.4.55 devoicing retain
their directed views. The added initial a/ā does not create an intervening
sound in any of those local root/sya contacts.

A3pl's n before t retains the inherited 8.3.24 anusvāra and 8.4.58
dental-restoration history. Syand's internal n has its own such history.
A restored late n is not fed backwards into an earlier ṇatva decision.
P3pl's word-final n instead has E2's final-n protection. The selected
devoiced pause gives final t and ḥ; external sentence sandhi is unbuilt.

## E5. Worked continuations

**Basic kṛ, P3du, future K.** Admission supplies R. The owned chain is

\[
 a_{\text{aṭ}}|kṛ|i_{\text{iṭ}}|sya|tas
 \to a|kar|i|sya|tām\to \mathbf{akariṣyatām}.
\]

The i comes from 7.2.70, not a lexical reclassification as universally
seṭ. The dual tām is distinct from 2du tam.

**Basic edh, A2du.**

\[
 \bar a|edh|i|sya|\bar{a}thām
 \to aidh|i|sya|iythām
 \to aidh|i|sya|ithām
 \to \mathbf{aidhiṣyethām}.
\]

Āṭ/initial e, ending-owned ā→iy and sya's ṣatva have three different
targets. The ending final m is retained.

**Basic iṣ, P1sg.** The one permitted iṭ branch and vowel-initial
augmentation give aiṣ|i|sya|am. The two thematic/ending a vowels
give one a under 6.1.97: **aiṣiṣyam**. Neither optional no-iṭ luṭ
nor present iccha is transported here.

**Vṛdh, P3sg versus A3sg.** Optional P before sya requires no iṭ
by 7.2.59: a|vardh|sya|t gives **avartsyat** after devoicing.
A retains iṭ: a|vardh|i|sya|ta gives **avardhiṣyata**.
The root's lexical identity survives their distinct readouts.

**Kṛp, A1sg.** The ūdit choice gives a|karp|sya|i or
a|karp|i|sya|i. The final i is the ending in both. Root r→l,
the conditional ending junction and, only in the second path, affix
ṣatva give **akalpsye /akalpiṣye**. P has no corresponding iṭ=1
branch. Equal counts of “i” in unrelated words are not branch evidence.

**Causal nī, A2pl.** Retain root-owned nāy and ṇic-owned i,
then add outer iṭ+sya and dhvam:

\[
 a|nāy|i_{\text{ṇic}}|i_{\text{iṭ}}|sya|dhvam
 \to a|nāy|ay_{\text{ṇic}}|i|sya|dhvam
 \to \mathbf{anāyayiṣyadhvam}.
\]

The causee-role/pada certificate is inherited, and the causer agrees.
No ṇic-lopa or dhvam→dhve is licensed.

## E6. Complete bounded word theorem

For C define the explicit reading tuples, in 3sg/3du/3pl/2sg/2du/2pl/
1sg/1du/1pl order,

\[
 R_P(C)=(Cat,Catām,Can,Caḥ,Catam,Cata,Cam,Cāva,Cāma),
\]
\[
 R_A(C)=(Cata,Cetām,Canta,Cathāḥ,Cethām,Cadhvam,Ce,Cāvahi,Cāmahi).
\]

**Theorem E6.** For every completed admitted R frame in D, S selects
exactly its qualified inner branches. E constructs all nine words on
each branch, with preserved ownership/history and the selected sound
policy. P lists 432 positions and 477 branch readings.

**Proof.** S5 establishes all 12 + 15 + 21 profiles and 12 + 20 + 21 branches.
E1 partitions consonant-initial versus the two vowel-initial inputs.
The original pada/member selects exactly one row of E2/E3. Each row
has its positive guard and the explicitly excluded ṭit alternatives.
E4 justifies the late root-facing readout on every slot without backwards
feedback. Thus each admitted joint branch has one selected word in each
slot, and every P row has that construction. Multiplication by nine
gives 432 and 477. This is written exhaustion of the bounded definition,
not a claim of independent software or corpus validation. □
