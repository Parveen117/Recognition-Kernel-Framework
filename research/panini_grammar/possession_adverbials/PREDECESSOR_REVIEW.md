# PG37 — Review of the two faster predecessor batches

Author: Monty. 4 November 2026.

## R1. User's quality concern

The user asked whether batches of roughly 300 had compromised the work,
because the previous two runs reached 1,348 much faster than earlier
20–30 increments. The decisive distinction is the **constructed layer**.
PG35/36 construct bounded semantic/affix-selection contracts. They do not
add complete finite paradigms. Their own documentation records zero new
positions and deferred tests/replay. Speed is therefore not evidence that
complete derivational work has become equally cheap.

## R4. What this review supports

The previous batches retain their recorded source evidence, row accounting,
ownership distinctions and explicit limitations. A 300-ID batch is not
itself a correctness defect. These checks do **not** establish that every
semantic interpretation is correct, every gaṇa is normalized, or every
word is fully derived. Full phonology, accent, feminine/declension,
unreviewed precedence intersections, implementation and independent tests
remain separate work. No success percentage or “no compromise whatsoever”
claim is justified by a row count or hash match.

PG37 continues that same bounded layer, with new source-specific gates,
addressed operations, explicit finite clause tables, native continuation,
literal archive, coverage union and implementation handoff.
