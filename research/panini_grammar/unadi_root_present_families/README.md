The printed *Daiva* supplies two distinctions that are invisible in an unaccented root key. Its verse 7 distinguishes two *styai* teaching forms; they project to the same 36 unprefixed finite words. The two class-one *naṭ* entries also agree in their entire 36-position schedules, while the dental-initial curādi *naṭ* has the factor *nāṭay*. Verse 149 gives *śāmayate* for considering something and *śāmyati* for becoming calm. The latter lengthening belongs to *śyan*, as illustrated under 7.3.74. The textual note attached to *samī* 04.0132 identifies it as an alternative to the ordering under *masī*. Its source status is retained on every reading.

The common endings are explicit in the machine relation. The past has prefixed *a* on these consonant-initial roots. For the dual-pada root *māh*, ‘measure’, examples include *māhataḥ* and *māhete* in the present dual, and *amāhat* and *amāhata* in the past singular. The earlier short-i noun cites this long-vowel root. Short *mah* ‘honour’ and curādi *mahay* retain their own records.

Every chosen word agrees with its pinned archived counterpart. The archive has **606 position–word readings** on the same 540 new requests. Of the additional 66 readings, 44 contain *tātaṅ* in the blessing use of *loṭ*. The other 22 have final *d* where the project’s chosen pause convention writes *t*. These boundary and mood alternatives are recorded for comparison under their source conditions. They add no ordinary finite positions.
