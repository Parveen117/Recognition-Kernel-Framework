# PG949 · The feminine of the offering participle

Whitney, *Sanskrit Grammar* §449h–i (printed p. 166), gives **júhvatī** from √hu. Read the example under the class restriction in §449h. The following paragraph (§449j) records nasal feminines for certain other bases. Its examples do not supply a nasal feminine for this root. The selected root is the unprefixed simple juhotyādi root 1030001 on the offering sense, with an actor and the parasmaipada present participle. The eating sense, intensive and prefixed bases have independent admission requirements. [Whitney](https://gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de/gretil_elib/Whi889__Whitney_SanskritGrammar.pdf) · [PG948 derivation to juhvat](../juhvat_participle_and_antelope/README.md)

Śatṛ supplies the ugit condition for ṅīp under 4.1.6. PG948's reduplicated ju-hv-at feeds this feminine operation. Rule 7.1.78 blocks num after the abhyasta base across the feminine formation. The śap/śyan condition of 7.1.81 is absent after class-three ślu. Neither that rule nor the neuter option of 7.1.79 supplies a nasal here. The resulting ī belongs to ṅīp. In the neuter dual *juhvatī* the corresponding ī belongs to the direct dual ending. Moving the feminine to the accusative singular gives *juhvatīm*, while moving the neuter from nominative to accusative dual preserves *juhvatī*. This pair distinguishes the histories by a further licensed operation.
