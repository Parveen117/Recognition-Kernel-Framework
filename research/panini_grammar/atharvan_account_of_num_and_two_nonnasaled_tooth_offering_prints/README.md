# PG864 · The Atharvan account of nuṃ and two printed readings of the tooth offering

### The commentary on the Atharvan creation verse

The Śaunaka text at 19.6.12 gives both the continuous verse and its word division on printed p. 278. The following page explains the compound. The padapāṭha has *ye | ca | ke | ca | ubhayādataḥ*. The commentary supplies the masculine plural participants as *gardabhāḥ* and *aśvatarāḥ*, asses and mules distinguished from the horses already named. Teeth in both upper and lower regions characterize these animals. Square brackets surround the repeated lemma on p. 279. The word is also visible independently in the mantra and its word division on p. 278.

The commentary expressly separates three steps. In this Vedic possessive compound, 5.4.142 supplies datṛ for the danta member. Its ṛ marker belongs to the replacement history. The first member's long vowel has separate warrant under 6.3.137. For the absence of nuṃ the printed commentary invokes **anityam āgamaśāsanam**, the non-invariability of the prescription of an augment. This closes PG620's Atharvan source lead. Bhaṭṭabhāskara at Ṛgveda 10.90.10 explicitly calls the absence Vedic. Each account licenses the cited nominative plural on its own terms. Neither makes an arbitrary ugit compound eligible for optional omission.

### Two printed controls for the offering example

Tripathi–Malaviya VI p. 340 has **पत्त्रदतमालभेत** at 5.4.142. A second, separately quoted offering expression follows, with short *a* written after *ubhay-* and the present *ālabhate*. The Nyāsa lower on the page gives *ubhayatodata* before *iti* and explicitly says **śasantam etat**. Its underlying accusative plural with śas has no sarvanāmasthāna demand for nuṃ. Preserve *ubhayatas* in this explanation. The same print's Hindi discussion invokes a sense other than age. It gives no animal name for the first offering.

The *Pāṇinīya Aṣṭādhyāyī Pravacanam*, IV p. 463, prints **पत्रदतमालभेत**. Its derivation names patra and danta and states the datṛ substitution. It separately prints the horses' birth under the Yajurvedic locator 31.8. The gloss about teeth on both sides belongs to the animals in that latter clause. This page expressly supplies the two compound members of the first object, but neither its lexical referent nor an explanation of masculine nuṃ omission there.

These two physical readings support the no-n branch of PG641 alongside PG853's two printed nasal readings. The variation between *pattra* and *patra* concerns the first member. The disputed *n* concerns the following tooth member. The optative identifies the first object as accusative singular. Its gender and a source-specific licence for a masculine no-n form remain open. A neuter derivation is a conditional grammatical possibility. The bovines, asses, mules, goats and sheep in the Atharvan verse supply no identification for this ritual object.
