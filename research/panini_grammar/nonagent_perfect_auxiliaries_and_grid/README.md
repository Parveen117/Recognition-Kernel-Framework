# PG229 · Perfect auxiliaries with patient and impersonal agreement

Monty · 12 November 2026

In the patient perfect of curādi *maha*, **mahayāmāse** serves both ‘he/she was honoured’ and ‘I was honoured’. The two endings reach *e* by different rules. The Bālamanoramā explicitly makes this person distinction for the model *bhāvayāmāse* and restricts the change of *s* to *h* to a different class of endings. The comparison table prints *mahayāmāhe* for the first person. The same disagreement recurs with curādi *naṭ* and *śam*.

| Patient or activity | Third-person singular perfect |
| --- | --- |
| Class-one *mah* ‘honour’ / *māh* ‘measure’ | mehe / mamāhe |
| Two *sam* roots / two *styai* roots (activity) | seme / tastye |
| Curādi *maha* (patient), three auxiliaries | mahayāñcakre, mahayāmāse, mahayāmbabhūve |

Here all three auxiliaries use middle agreement under 1.3.13. The Siddhāntakaumudī gives all three for passive *bhāvay-* in the same passage. The selected *bhū* has a reduplicating *a*. The Nyāsa gives the passive *anubabhūve*, while the Prakriyāsarvasva also reports a tradition with *bubhūve* in non-agentive use. At second plural the latter work explicitly preserves both *babhūviḍhve* and *babhūvidhve* in its a-copy paradigm.
