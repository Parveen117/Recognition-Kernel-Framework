# PG944 · √hu when it means eating

Kṣīrataraṅgiṇī 754 explicitly glosses *adanam* as *bhakṣaṇam*, eating. It distinguishes *dānam* as the casting of an offering. Record 1591 labels its head हु॒ अदने and prints distinct actor and patient tables. Record 1592 labels its head दाने. Both identify juhotyādi √hu. This is a sense split within a root. A different reported reading, *ādāna* with long ā, means taking. Dhātupradīpa 770 reports taking alongside giving. It supplies no warrant for changing the short vowel of the eating gloss. PG7's pinned lexical string and the PG22/931 actor extension use the giving/taking/pleasing scope. PG938, PG941 and PG942–943 likewise do not admit food as an argument under this eating sense. [Record 1591 and all three commentaries](https://samsaadhanii-concordance.vipran.in/dhatu/1591-hu-axane-hu1) · [PG931 actor scope](../hu_remaining_actor_categories/README.md)

Take the unprefixed simple root with eater and food, without an offering, a prescribed receptacle or the ritual incorporation analysis. For kartari the eater controls the P ending. For karmaṇi the food controls the A ending by 1.3.13. A paired-food request with one eater therefore selects *juhoti* when the verb expresses the eater, and *hūyete* when it expresses the two foods. For a pair of eaters and a single food the corresponding readings are *juhutaḥ* and *hūyate*. These are constructed agreement diagnostics. The source supplies the cells. Neither an impersonal use nor an actor A request follows from the two published tables. The Vedic offering-case example of PG941 cannot by itself establish a Vedic instrumental for food eaten in this sense.
