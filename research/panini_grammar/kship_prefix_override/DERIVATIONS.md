# PG109 — Kṣip morphology with owned operations

Monty · 10 November 2026

## H06. Class-six śa and the four present-system labels

3.1.77 supplies **śa**, whose apit sārvadhātuka status gives ṅit work
through 1.2.4; 1.1.5 prevents root guṇa. The base is kṣipa-, not kṣepa-
and not class-four kṣipya-. Retain the inherited thematic ending routes
for laṭ, laṅ, loṭ and ordinary vidhi-liṅ; the latter loses its nonfinal
s under 7.2.79. First-person thematic length comes from the present
ending interface, not a new lexical long vowel. Loṭ 2sg has hi-lopa.

Loṭ 1sg kṣipāṇi has ending-owned n→ṇ: original root ṣ is the trigger,
with i, p and ā permitted interveners under 8.4.2. Laṭ/loṭ third plural
instead have dental n: n before t first becomes anusvāra by 8.3.24,
then 8.4.58 restores dental n too late for earlier ṇatva. Final n in
laṅ/lṛṅ is protected by 8.4.37. Never run a global ṣ-to-ṇ substitution.

## H07. Aniṭ future and conditional interfaces

The val-initial sya/tās interfaces remain aniṭ; 7.3.86 gives light
upadhā i→e. Thus kṣep+sya and kṣep+tās yield kṣepsya- and kṣept-.
Tās third-person ḍā/rau/ras replacements and its remaining finite
endings follow the pinned luṭ contract; kṣeptā is a finite request here,
not a counted agent noun. Conditional lṛṅ has aṭ and its distinct event
failure/attitude certificate, despite sharing sya with ordinary future.

Sya s remains dental: root p intervenes after the iṇ vowel e and is
neither num, visarga nor śar under 8.3.58. The earlier root ṣ cannot
license arbitrary distant ṣatva.

## H08. Blessing and perfect preserve different root grades

P āśīrliṅ has kit work under 3.4.104, no present śa, and no guṇa:
kṣip+yāsuṭ yields kṣipyāt etc. Its apparent ya is not class-four śyan.
The nine ending paths are inherited P blessing interfaces, with retained
yāsuṭ s except its independently prescribed junctions.

Liṭ copies only the root. Halādi-śeṣa 7.4.60 retains initial k in the
copy, and 7.4.62 changes it to c: ci+kṣip. The original kṣ survives.
Strong 3sg/2sg/1sg have i→e, giving cikṣepa/cikṣepitha; weak apit kit
endings retain i, giving cikṣipatuḥ/cikṣipuḥ/cikṣipathuḥ/cikṣipa and
cikṣipiva/cikṣipima. Initial kṣ does not satisfy the root-final conjunct
exclusion in 1.2.5. Thal-iṭ is mandatory; it is not ending īṭ.

First-singular ṇal retains 7.1.91 ṇit-work ON/OFF histories. Both read
cikṣepa: kṣip is neither ik-final for 7.2.115 nor a-upadhā for 7.2.116.
Three requests therefore have two histories but only one spelling each.

## H09. Halanta sic-vṛddhi, and three restricted s-lopa slots

Luṅ has cli→sic and aniṭ P. **7.2.3** gives consonant-final root i→ai;
7.2.1's ik-final premise does not fit root-final p. The explicit source
akṣaipsīt fixes this grade. No class-four puṣādi/aṅ, śal-final ksa,
neighbouring kṛṣ option or causative caṅ belongs to this request.

Ending īṭ under 7.3.96 intervenes in 3sg/2sg; sic s remains in
akṣaipsīt/akṣaipsīḥ. In 3du/2du/2pl, p-s-t meets 8.2.26, so sic s
is deleted: akṣaiptām/akṣaiptam/akṣaipta. In 3pl/1sg/1du/1pl it
remains, producing akṣaipsuḥ/akṣaipsam/akṣaipsva/akṣaipsma. The p
separator prevents ṣatva throughout; no ṣṭ replacement is licensed.
Source A akṣipta is archived as a control, not a P branch.

## H10. Prefix boundaries, counts and retained histories

Prefixes remain outside root copy and grade. With initial aṭ in laṅ,
lṛṅ and luṅ, final prefix i becomes y by 6.1.77: abhy-, praty-, aty-
before the augment-owned a. Thus abhikṣipati contrasts with abhyakṣipat,
and abhicikṣepa retains the copy below the prefix. Each perfect first
singular has two labelled ṇit-work histories with the same word.

There are **270 requests, 270 readings, 273 paths and thirty complete
paradigms**: 90 requests per prefix. Root-facing iṭ occurs in nine
positions; initial aṭ in 81; ending īṭ in six. Sic occurs in 27, with
nine restricted s-lopa positions. These are independent counters, not
new requests. The cumulative register reaches **22,271**; this does not
assign a whole-grammar completion percentage.
