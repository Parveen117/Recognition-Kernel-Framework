# PG201 · The fish word, its two feminines and the three case tables

Monty · 11 November 2026

Ujjvaladatta gives two feminine words for the fish under Uṇādi 4.104. On the *ṅīṣ* branch, *matsya* belongs to *gaurādi* and loses its y before ī, giving **matsī**. In the absence of *ṅīṣ*, the a-stem forms **matsyā** with *ṭāp* and keeps y. The Kāśikā and Bhāṣya at Pāṇini 6.4.149 support the restricted deletion. The Kāśikā retains y in *matsyacarī* and in *mātsyam* ‘its flesh’. The Kośa also lists both feminine fish words together at 4.104.

In isolation *matsyā* can be the instrumental singular of *matsī* or the nominative singular of *matsyā*. Likewise *matsyām* can be locative singular of *matsī* or accusative singular of *matsyā*. **Matsyāḥ** has seven stem–case–number readings across the three profiles. The reverse index lists each reading under its spelling.

### Two accents on the masculine

At Uṇādi 4.2, the seventh assignment names **mad + syan** and gives the initially accented *mátsya*. At 4.104, the sixth assignment uses **mad + sya**. The Tattvabodhinī explains the root-final d becoming t before s and explicitly gives final accent here, *matsyá*. Perusūri’s verses 276–277 also contrast the two accents. Both histories yield the same unaccented word. The separate *sya* field at the extension rule 4.105 still needs a witness for an additional root.

The Zürich/VedaWeb entry *matsya_6526* has two initially accented masculine examples. *Mátsyam* at Ṛgveda 10.68.8b is the fish in a simile and matches the written accusative singular once accent is removed. *Mátsyāsaḥ* at 7.18.6b names the Matsya people. The source notes a play on the word for fish. That occurrence has a Vedic plural ending. Neither occurrence supplies a feminine form.
