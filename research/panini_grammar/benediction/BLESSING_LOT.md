# PG20 — Blessing loṭ and the optional tātaṅ branch

Author: Monty. 2 November 2026.

## L1. Retain the ordinary loṭ construction

3.3.173 admits loṭ on the new āśīs frame. Its tiṅ remains
sārvadhātuka; PG19 E/T/A/C's root, vikaraṇa, voice, ending and
readout arguments therefore apply on the matching morphological inputs.
This is a correspondence of derivations with **different initial meaning
records**, not a retroactive mutation of an old completed utterance.

The ordinary I_P/I_A constructors and the separately derived kṛ/krī
tuples retain every condition: hi is apit, first-person āṭ makes the
ending pit, A duals have their own 7.2.81 query, and Bhāva is 3sg.
The twenty-two causal active profiles, thirteen causal Karma profiles,
four basic Karma profiles and two Bhāva entries retain D4's precise roles.

This gives 54 nine-slot profiles and two single-slot Bhāva inputs:
**488 ordinary blessing-loṭ positions/readings**. Their words coincide
with the corresponding PG19 loṭ words. The new contribution at this
stage is their source-qualified āśīs admission and the optional branch
below, not 488 previously absent strings.

## L2. What 7.1.35 actually replaces

On āśīs frames, **tu or hi** can optionally become **tātaṅ**.
These are the owned P 3sg and P 2sg endings, not every substring tu
in a plural word. A endings and all first-person/dual/plural P endings
are outside the target. The ordinary tu/hi continuation remains positive.

The replacement is of the **whole** ending. Although 1.1.53 usually
gives a ṅit substitute an ultimate-letter target, Kāśikā 1.1.53/7.1.35
and Kaumudī explain why tātaṅ's ṅ has another purpose: its guṇa/vṛddhi
prohibition. The source consequently adopts 1.1.55's whole-substitution
reading. It is not a general rule that every ṅit substitute acts globally.

The final ṅ is an it under 1.3.3 and disappears by 1.3.9. The last
teaching a is a pronunciation aid, as Bālamanoramā explicitly states;
the pronounced ending is **tāt**. It is not *tāta* or a tiṅ-lopa.
Kāśikā further states that its ṅit prescription **displaces the pitness
that might have come from the original tu by sthānivadbhāva**. This
source-specific override must not be replaced by a universal claim that
pit and ṅit can never coexist: PG19 P vidhiliṅ has a different prescription.

## L3. Hi-luk does not erase its new substitute

Choose the optional ending before deciding which stem/hi-luk query is
applicable. On the ordinary thematic 2sg branch, 6.4.105 removes hi.
On the tātaṅ branch the ending is tāt, not hi. Kāśikā 7.1.35's
verse explicitly uses the repeated mention of hi in the lopa rule to
exclude an automatic sthānivad-lopa of this substitute. Thus bhavatāt
survives; it does not collapse back to bhava.

The same target discipline applies to kṛ's 6.4.106 hi-luk: the
original hi is retained in history, but the tāt branch does not present
an actual hi to be deleted. A visible-empty ordinary ending cannot be
used as the input of a later indiscriminate string replacement.

## L4. Tātaṅ changes the athematic stem query

For a thematic interface B∣a, śap/śa/śyan or the retained causal
śap history remains intact. The new outer ṅit condition does not
undo an earlier operation at a different affix boundary. The optional
word is **Batāt** for either eligible singular slot.

Kṛ and krī expose a different consequence:

| Root / P slot | Ordinary branch | New ending query | Tātaṅ branch |
|---|---|---|---|
| kṛ 3sg | karotu | ṅit, inherited pit displaced | kar∣u → kur∣u; kurutāt |
| kṛ 2sg | kuru, with hi-luk | ṅit; actual tāt remains | kurutāt |
| krī 3sg | krīṇātu | ṅit, consonant-initial tāt | śnā ā→ī by 6.4.113; krīṇītāt |
| krī 2sg | krīṇīhi | ṅit, consonant-initial tāt | krīṇītāt |

Kṛ uses PG6/PG19's owned 6.4.110 weak-root procedure and blocks
vikaraṇa-u guṇa at the new ending boundary. Krī retains its root-side
śnā witness and applies the weak consonant case to that affix's vowel.
These four words are written consequences of the general tātaṅ and
athematic procedures; they are not claimed as four literal quotations
under 7.1.35.

A procedure that completes strong kar-o or krī-ṇā first and merely
attaches tāt cannot obtain these branches. N6 gives the precise local
failure of commuting the ending refinement past the stem query.

## L6. Full local relation and accounting

For an eligible P singular profile set

\[
 \operatorname{Lot}_{20}(F,p)=
 \{\operatorname{ordinary}_{19}(F,p),\operatorname{tāt}_{20}(F,p)\}.
\]

For every other admitted profile retain the one ordinary PG19 reading.
The syntax/meaning hypothesis āśīs and actual tu/hi target are required;
missing evidence is Open, not another surface alternative.

**Theorem L6.** The 488 blessing-loṭ positions have exactly **530
readings** on the declared domain. Forty-two singular profiles have
two readings; the remaining 446 have one.

**Proof.** L1 gives 488 ordinary readings. L5 lists eight basic and
thirteen causal P profiles, each with exactly two eligible singular slots:
21·2=42 added readings. L2–L4 derive their distinct optional tāt words
and exclude the remaining slots; the original branch remains admitted.
Thus 488+42=530. D preserves controllers, and N3 preserves the typed
source/choice relation under zero-specialization. □
