# PG20 — Owned āśīrliṅ endings, s occurrences and dh/ḍh

Author: Monty. 2 November 2026.

## E1. Augmented endings and the actual first sound

Use the original tiṅ series in slot order
(3sg,3du,3pl,2sg,2du,2pl,1sg,1du,1pl). The lakāra owner, original
ending, marker prescriptions and each augment remain distinct objects.
The inherited ṅit-lakāra substitutions 3.4.99–101 do not assert that
every phonological ṅit effect is inherited from the lakāra's letter.

P receives yāsuṭ under 3.4.103–104; A receives sīyuṭ under 3.4.102.
The t/th occurrences receive their separate suṭ under 3.4.107 in **both
padas** where applicable. The teaching u in these augment names is not
an additional pronounced stem vowel. Their ṭ fixes initial placement
at their respective recipients by 1.1.46.

The augmented P ending begins with **y**. The pratyāhāra val starts at
v and excludes y; thus 7.2.35's val-initial premise is false. This is
not a special seṭ-root exception guessed from bhūyāt. Kāśikā **7.2.42**
explicitly explains the absence of a P-liṅ counterexample by yāsuṭ's
non-val initial. The same guard applies to the derived causal boundary.

A's sīyuṭ begins with s, a val sound. Its ordinary iṭ query is therefore
live, subject to the exact lexical/derived-root and prohibitory conditions.
If iṭ is present, the next phoneme for a **jhal-initial** kit query is
i, not s. A rule must inspect its actual augmented ending view.

## E2. Kit is not a generic property of the mood

3.4.104 is P's special **kit prescription in place of 3.4.103's ṅit**.
Kāśikā/Nyāsa attach it to the liṅ ending as a whole, not solely to
the augment. It is an override, not an accumulation of both prohibitions.
Its effect on vac and jāgṛ in B3 demonstrates why the distinction matters.

For A, 3.4.104's inherited P restriction is false. Nor can 1.2.4's
apit-sārvadhātuka rule be applied after 3.4.116 has excluded that class.
The actual local A kit rules are:

| Source | Relevant A condition | PG20 application |
|---|---|---|
| 1.2.11 | root's final consonant adjacent to ik; following liṅ jhal-initial | tud before aniṭ sīyuṭ |
| 1.2.12 | ṛ-final root; following liṅ jhal-initial | kṛ/hṛ before aniṭ sīyuṭ |
| 1.2.13 | gam; following A liṅ jhal-initial | optional kit on basic karmaṇi gam |

The rules do not make nī/krī/dā A kit. A preceding iṭ also defeats
their jhal condition, including the ciṇvat augment when it is visible
to this query. Preserve the rule and its addressed input, not one global
“weak mood” bit.

## E3. Why the optative s-lopa cannot be reused

7.2.79 inherits **sārvadhātuke**. It does not delete the non-final s
of these ārdhadhātuka endings. In particular the s of yāsuṭ, the s of
sīyuṭ and the s of each suṭ must first be retained with their owners.
The final words can lose particular s occurrences under **8.2.29**;
that is a different source, scope and sound environment.

P's ordinary ending series is

\[
 (t,tām,us,s,tam,ta,am,va,ma).
\]

3.4.100 removes the relevant i; 3.4.101 gives tām/tam/ta/am;
3.4.99 supplies uttama-s lopa; 3.4.108 gives jus for jhi. Jus loses
its initial teaching j by 1.3.7 but retains final s under 1.3.4.
The following table retains the two different s owners:

| Slots | Before the decisive s operation | Selected consequence |
|---|---|---|
| 3sg | R∣yās∣s_suṭ∣t | both s disappear by the final-conjunct 8.2.29 route; Ryāt |
| 3du, 2du, 2pl | R∣yās∣s_suṭ∣tām/tam/ta | the yāsuṭ-s disappears before the jhal context; suṭ-s remains |
| 2sg | R∣yās∣s_ending | the augment-s disappears in the final cluster; ending-s takes visarga |
| 3pl, 1sg, 1du, 1pl | R∣yās∣us/am/va/ma | yāsuṭ-s remains; the first two next sounds are vowels, the latter two are not jhal |

For 3sg adopt Bālamanoramā/Tattvabodhinī 3.4.104's explicit account:
remove suṭ-s as a member of the final conjunct, then yāsuṭ-s in that
same final-conjunct setting. Do not use the tempting “first delete
yāsuṭ-s by the jhal route, then delete suṭ-s” explanation: those
commentaries explain the conflict with 8.2.23 under that ordering.
This is a declared local source schedule, not a universal Tripādī resolver.

For 3du/2du/2pl, the surviving s is **suṭ's**, even though a compressed
table retaining yāsuṭ-s could print the same word. At 3pl the yāsuṭ-s
prevents an adjacent ā/us seam: PG19's 6.1.96 kuryuḥ path cannot be
copied to āśīrliṅ kriyāsuḥ.

## E4. Complete P constructor

Let R be B/C's owned, already-selected pre-yāsuṭ root constituent.
R is not an arbitrary present stem. With the inherited devoiced-pause
and final-s policies define

\[
 B_P(R)=(Ryāt,Ryāstām,Ryāsuḥ,Ryāḥ,Ryāstam,Ryāsta,
          Ryāsam,Ryāsva,Ryāsma).
\]

The yāsuṭ y is a consonant, so there is no automatic e/āy seam rule
on a root such as de. Nor is y a val sound that deletes a preceding y:
the causal nāy/pāy constituents produce nāyyāt/pāyyāt on the selected
non-doubling policy. Each repeated letter retains its original owner.

## E5. A endings and the two distinct ṣ contexts

Liṅ is not ṭit, so PG19's 3.4.79/80 loṭ-e routes do not apply.
3.4.105 replaces jha by ran; 3.4.106 replaces the first-person ending
iṭ by a, not an augment elsewhere. Its t is the source's pronunciation
aid. The ordinary ending series is

\[
 (ta,ātām,ran,thās,āthām,dhvam,a,vahi,mahi).
\]

After adjoining sīyuṭ and the appropriate t/th suṭ, 6.1.66 deletes
the augment's y before consonants, including the initial suṭ-s, but
retains it before vowels. In 3du/2du the s from suṭ follows the ā
inside the ending: sīy∣ā∣s∣tām and sīy∣ā∣s∣thām.

There are two separate ṣatva questions under 8.3.59:

- The initial s of sīyuṭ reads the actual preceding root/augment sound.
  It becomes ṣ after iṇ/ku: kṛṣ-, neṣ-, pakṣ-, edhiṣ-. It remains
  dental in laps-, tuts- and dās-.
- In 3sg/2sg, suṭ-s follows the ī exposed by y-lopa, so it becomes ṣ;
  8.4.41 then gives ṣṭ/ṣṭh. In 3du/2du it follows ā, so **st/sth**
  remain dental. There is no global replacement of all s or all t/th.

Let Z be B/C's owned readout factor through the **first** s/ṣ, with the
remaining sīyuṭ ī not yet written. For a permitted δ∈{dh,ḍh} define

\[
 B_A(Z,δ)=(Zīṣṭa,Zīyāstām,Zīran,Zīṣṭhāḥ,Zīyāsthām,
           Zīδvam,Zīya,Zīvahi,Zīmahi).
\]

Z is a factorization of ordered phonetic output, not permission to feed
late k/ṣ substitutions back into earlier root operations. Final n of ran
is retained and does not undergo final-n ṇatva (8.4.37).

## E6. The complete second-plural dh/ḍh relation

The selected source account of 8.3.78–79 distinguishes:

| Owned environment before ṣīdhvam | δ relation |
|---|---|
| directly following an iṇ-final aṅga, without an intervening iṭ | {ḍh}, 8.3.78 |
| following iṭ which itself directly follows iṇ | {dh,ḍh}, 8.3.79 |
| neither condition | {dh} |

The second row is 8.3.79's optional qualification, not a claim that an
iṭ segment always blocks 8.3.78 by physical separation. Nyāsa explains
that an ending-owned augment can be included in its recipient's reference,
so 8.3.78 would otherwise be mandatory in the relevant ṣīdhvam case;
8.3.79 supplies the option. Nor is iṭ simply incorporated into the root
to force a new mandatory iṇ-final reading. Kāśikā contrasts mandatory
cyoṣīḍhvam, optional laviṣīdhvam/laviṣīḍhvam, and dental pakṣīdhvam.
The relevant iṇ excludes velar k and the aspirated stop dh; dh is not
the single h phoneme. Thus edhiṣīdhvam and pakṣīdhvam remain dental.

The query reads the actual iṭ origin and preceding owned constituent.
PG20 applies this rule to both i_val and i_cv: 6.4.22 hides i_cv from
the stated ābhīya ṇic-lopa query, not from this Tripādī query. The
consequences for the newly derived causal cells are written applications,
not independently quoted words. No extra dh/ḍh choice is added outside
2pl. Source examples containing prefixes/Vedic or other lakāras are not
new admitted lexical domains.

## E7. Ending exhaustion theorem

**Theorem E7.** For each admitted owned R or Z interface, E4 or E5/E6
specifies every selected āśīrliṅ slot and every admitted 2pl alternative.

**Proof.** The original 3×3 series is exhausted by E3 and E5. Their
suṭ targets, final-s distinctions and y environments are disjoint
addressed cases. E6 exhausts the selected dh context without affecting
other slots. E1/E2 keep the class, augmentation and kit premises separate;

listed interfaces, while D preserves the controller restriction. N3 gives
