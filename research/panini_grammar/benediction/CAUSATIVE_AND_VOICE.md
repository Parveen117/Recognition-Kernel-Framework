# PG20 — Āśīrliṅ across causation, karman and Bhāva

Author: Monty. 2 November 2026.

## C1. P causative: yāsuṭ licenses ṇic-lopa

Start from PG13–PG15's owned causal C_root∣i_ṇic, including PG18's
exact class-4 śam addition. The outer P āśīrliṅ ending is ārdhadhātuka
and begins with yāsuṭ. E1 proves that it takes no ordinary val-iṭ.
The aniṭ ārdhadhātuka condition of **6.4.51** therefore holds:

\[
 C_{root}|i_{ṇic}|yās|t\longrightarrow
 C_{root}|\varnothing_{ṇic}|yās|t.
\]

The lopa is the source-stated exception to ordinary iy/yaṇ/guṇa/vṛddhi/
length operations on ṇic; do not first manufacture ay from its i. The
causal event, original ṇic and earlier root changes survive in history.
The full result is **B_P(C_root)**. Thus kāryāt here is causal kartari
P; a similar-looking passive word elsewhere does not determine its voice.

Prior root strengthening, mit shortening and puk/yuk remain owned events.
The 1.1.57 visibility principle used in PG15/PG18 prevents treating the
remaining C_root letters as a new bare root for an extra unrestricted
strengthening after lopa. This is a query-specific continuation, not an
automatic re-derivation from the printable substring.

## C2. A causal kartari: ordinary iṭ preserves ṇic

The nine admitted active A profiles begin with val-initial sīyuṭ and
take ordinary i_val under 7.2.35 at the derived-dhātu boundary. A bare
root's aniṭ tag is not a prohibition on that larger causal input.
There is no kartari ciṇvat choice. The visible i_val makes 6.4.51's
aniṭ condition false, and E2's jhal-initial A kit conditions are false:

\[
 C_{root}|i_{ṇic}|i_{val}|sīy
 \to C_{root}|e_{ṇic}|i_{val}|sīy
 \to C_{root}|ay_{ṇic}|i_{val}|sīy.
\]

Ordinary 7.3.84/6.1.78 supplies e/ay; 1.1.6 protects i_val. The
first s takes 8.3.59 after i, giving Z=C_root·ayiṣ. Before i_val
the realized causal constituent ends in y∈iṇ, so E6 gives the two
**2pl-only** readings Zīdhvam/Zīḍhvam. Every other slot is single.

## C4. Causal Karma(q): re-open the actual sīyuṭ ciṇvat domain

Here the expression cut selects A by 1.3.13. There is no yak before
ārdhadhātuka āśīrliṅ. Unlike PG19's vidhiliṅ, this is the sīyuṭ
domain expressly admitted by **6.4.62**; Kaumudī/Tattvabodhinī state
the ārdhadhātuka limit directly.

Use PG18's source-qualified ciṇvat transport with **actual sīyuṭ** as
its target. Eligibility reads the original vowel of the owned ṇic.
Every one of these thirteen causal Karma frames therefore has:

- **O:** ordinary i_val; retained ṇic; Z=C_root·ayiṣ.
- **C:** jointly prescribed ciṇvat and i_cv; the adopted 6.4.22 view
  hides i_cv only from 6.4.51's relevant query, so ṇic is lopped;
  Z=C_root·iṣ. No second i_val is independently appended.

The actual ending remains āśīrliṅ; it does not become ciṇ or lose its
tiṅ under 6.4.104. Transport is restricted to the aṅga operations that
PG18 V4 proves. Its special augment is visible to the later first-s
ṣatva and dh/ḍh queries. No deletion of ṇic deletes that different i_cv.

The two mit roots gam/śam additionally have **short/long C branches**
through the transported 6.4.93 condition. O retains gamay/śamay and has
no extra length toggle. Kāśikā 6.4.62 literally gives
**śamayiṣīṣṭa/śamiṣīṣṭa/śāmiṣīṣṭa**. The gam trio is the written
application of its inherited am-final mit witness and the same rule.

Kṛ/hṛ's ordinary sīyuṭ branch gets no 7.2.70 future-sya iṭ. Its
ṝ/ṛ kit rule is live. The C i_cv changes that jhal query, while
ciṇvat supplies the stated vṛddhi, giving kār/hār. The original

constituents have appeared. Pac is neither originally vowel-final nor
one of 6.4.62's named consonant-final exceptions, so it has no C branch.

Gam's 7.2.58 ordinary iṭ permission requires P in the relevant finite
context; Karma(q) has A, so no i_val is supplied. Rule **1.2.13**
instead offers kit. With kit, **6.4.37** deletes original anudātta
gam's nasal before jhal-initial sīyuṭ: ga∣sīy, giving **gasīṣṭa**.
Without kit, m remains until 8.3.24 gives anusvāra: **gaṃsīṣṭa**.
Kāśikā 1.2.13 illustrates the contrast with prefixed saṃgam kartari;
PG20's unprefixed karmaṇi forms are written consequences of the same
root/jhal/kit rules plus 1.3.13, not literal copies of that source example.
Neither branch is ciṇvat: basic gam is ineligible. Its P yāsuṭ path
has a non-jhal y and therefore preserves m in gamyāt.

3.4.104 kit and no 1.2.17 liṅ permission: that latter rule concerns
**sic**. Therefore ordinary sthā remains ā, while C supplies yuk.
These Bhāva inputs do not acquire extra person/number slots.

## C8. Finite continuation theorem

**Theorem C8.** The admitted causal active sector has **198 positions /
207 readings**, causal Karma **117 / 270**, and basic Karma/Bhāva
**38 / 69**.

**Proof.** Active causal has thirteen single P paradigms and nine A
paradigms with one additional 2pl reading: 22·9=198 and 198+9=207.
Causal Karma has eleven two-branch roots and two three-branch mit roots,
giving 28·9=252 readings before the late sound choice. Thirteen O
branches and five C branches add one 2pl reading each: 252+18=270,
over 13·9=117 positions. Basic Karma has two kṛ branches, two hṛ,
one pac and two gam: 7·9=63, plus two C second-plural alternatives.
The two Bhāva inputs have two 3sg readings each, so 63+2+4=69 over
4·9+2=38 positions. E7 supplies each tuple, D supplies the controller,
and N3–N4 preserve the dependent branch relation and its zero-reading. □
