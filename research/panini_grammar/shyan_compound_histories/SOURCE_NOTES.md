## S1. Positive word construction and exact addresses

Kāśikā 6.1.13 explicitly supplies both कौमुदगन्धीपुत्रः and its पति
counterpart; 6.3.139 prints the pair without the former field's broken line
between कौमुदगन्धी and पतिः. The spelling is therefore adopted from
the coherent witnesses, without normalizing the archive itself.

The 1.1.45 definition distinguishes the replacement relation in a prescription
from the resulting vowel in a later reference. Accordingly 6.1.13 prescribes
य्→इ at the ष्यङ् owner, 6.1.108 gives पूर्वरूप for the internal
vowel pair, and 6.3.139 lengthens the resulting first-member vowel. The
whole stem's other यण् sounds are not additional targets.

The input stem's earlier गोत्र/feminine derivation is attested by 4.1.78
and the 6.1.13 explanation. PG51 starts after that qualified preparation.
The earlier lexical compound, descent and feminine steps are not separately
credited as completed new histories or full paradigms.

## S2. The tree distinction is in the source

Kāśikā's exclusion of पुत्रकुल as the immediate उत्तरपद is not a
ban on every larger word containing that string. Padamañjarī 6.1.13
expressly contrasts the route in which **कारीषगन्धीपुत्र** has already
been formed and its कुल is then composed. H03/H04 adopt that exact
distinction, with the actual two constituents and the queried node retained.

The two syntactic compositions are supplied inputs. Their different rule
outcomes do not establish arbitrary associativity or semantic equivalence
of all compound rebracketings. They establish why an implementation cannot
discard brackets and then recover every application from leaf order alone.

## S3. The feminine-host exception and the अति boundary

All three 6.1.13 commentary fields explain the expanded feminine first member
and the restriction when that feminine bearer is subordinate. PG51 uses
their shared admission/exclusion contrast. The whole word's meaning and
the inner bearer remain separately addressed.

The pinned Kāśikā displays **अतिकारीषगन्ध्या** and corresponding
long-आ compounds. The Nyāsa field discusses 1.2.48 shortening, and its
appended Bālamanoramā-labelled section explicitly gives **अतिकारीषगन्ध्यः**
and a short first-member vowel. These are not silently rewritten into one
uniform literal witness. They agree on the relevant exclusion from 6.1.13;
the full अति derivation and exact citation policy remain a named next task.

The wording also differs in the described bearer: Kāśikā writes
अतिक्रान्ता, while the appended discussion uses अतिक्रान्तः. Input
gender and any later feminine suffix therefore need examination before
treating every vowel difference as a copying error. PG51 makes no such
global emendation.

The outer genitive subordinate-first designation does not itself settle
this inner स्त्रीप्रत्यय prominence question. Treating those two uses
of subordination as one boolean would contradict the positive examples.

inside its nested example; the adopted कारीषगन्ध्या family is established
by its surrounding discussion and the Kāśikā witnesses. No new sound rule
is inferred from those textual defects.
