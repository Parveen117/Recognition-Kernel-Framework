# PG841 · Sima in six hymns and the three agreement genders

The [seven-case panel](CASES.tsv) takes free *sima* in the whole/all sense, with the gender of its head and a supplied number and case. Kāśikā ad 1.1.27 includes this base and gives *simasmai*. The inherited construction is the pronominal a-stem with a separate feminine ṭāp input. **Endings and boundaries.** The base has short i. It is distinct from the word *sīmā* ‘boundary’ and the Vedic particle *sīm*. There is no lexical-root derivation, preverb, voice, finite ending or new inner affix on this lexical request. With M *jas*, F dual *au/auṭ* or N dual *au/auṭ*, 7.1.17–19 each yield long śī on their own original ending and recover *sime*. The M accusative plural has śas, giving *simān*. The N plural has short śi (7.1.20), nuṃ and length, giving *simāni*. With no r or ṣ trigger, both this n and the instrumental n of *simena* are dental. In the singular obliques the pronominal substitutes give *simasmai, simasmāt, simasmin*. The voiced final *-d* has its own pause option in the selected PG420 policy. In the feminine obliques, āp, the short stem vowel under the syāṭ prescription, and plural suṭ retain different owners. Compare singular *simasyāḥ* with plural *simāsām*. The PG420 competing analyses for the feminine genitive plural keep their history and converge on the same spelling. Do not project the pitch of any one mantra onto every companion. No general Vedic plural ending or unquoted vocative series is inferred.
