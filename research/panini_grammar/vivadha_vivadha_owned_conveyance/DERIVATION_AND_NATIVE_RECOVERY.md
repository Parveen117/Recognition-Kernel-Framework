# Source placement, optionality and the carrier in Rājataraṅgiṇī

## Rule placement and actual means relation

Kāśikā and Bhāṣāvṛtti give both bases in the sūtra
under the inherited *tena harati* relation and
derive masculine and feminine examples. The pinned
project source has this two-base wording. In the
Mahābhāṣya's address 1755 the short-base sūtra is
followed by vārttika 5561 admitting *vīvadha*.
Tattvabodhinī 1200 treats the vārttika as stating
what the partial-change principle already supplies
and ascribes the inclusion in the wording to the
author of the vṛtti. Retain all three addresses.
They agree here on admitting both bases. Historical
priority of the wordings stays Open.

The source requests carry a nominal input suited
to tṛtīyā, *vivadhena* or *vīvadhena*, a
conveyer and something conveyed. Kāśikā gives
both terms for path and for *paryāhāra*.
Nyāsa explains the latter through conveying
around a course or circuit. These two sense
addresses remain distinct as inputs to the same
word paradigm. A mere location or a reference
to the sound spelling supplies no instrument
role. Arbitrary instrumental bases and automatic
transfer of 4.4.17's option to 4.4.16 or 4.4.18
are Excluded by the source predicates.

The supporting predicate *harati* is unprefixed
hṛñ, 01.1046, haraṇe, in the supplied taking or
conveying sense. Its P3sg kartari form provides
the example relation. It is separate from the
nominal etymon and its ṭhak or ṣṭhan. This fibre
introduces no new finite request. Śabdakalpadruma
also supplies a qualified trade use, with actual
*vyavaharati* in its definition, short *vivadha*,
ṭhak, grain/rice and an explicit three-gender
label. Its supplied adjectival neuter uses that
short-base branch. Extending the wider trade
sense to every source path needs its own evidence.

## Two suffixes and four paths

The selection sup is deleted under 2.4.71. Under
4.4.17 the specific ṣṭhan is optional. Where
it is unselected, the inherited 4.4.1/15
ṭhak serves. Retain ṣ and n on the first
suffix and k on the second as original teaching
markers. In both paths the sounded ṭha has its
own substitution to ika under 7.3.50. The base
loses only its final a at the taddhita boundary
under 6.4.148. In ṭhak, the original k triggers
7.2.117–118 and the first vowel receives the
vṛddhi ai under 1.1.1 and 1.1.49–50.
No ñ, ṇ or k vṛddhi condition is supplied by
ṣṭhan. The initial i and ī therefore survive
on their respective ṣṭhan paths.

Each feminine path loses the taddhita's now
final a before the sounded ī at its own
boundary. The print of the long-base feminine
is explicit in the Puṣpā Kāśikā and
Bhāṣāvṛtti witnesses. Sanskrit Documents
prints short *vivadhikī* twice in its Kāśikā
display. Its actual short string remains pinned
as a print observation. The long form is
Accepted on its independent positive sources.
A second spelling report carries no additional
case-reading credit.

In the a-stems, instrumental singular retains
the ending's dental n and genitive plural
retains dental nuṭ. In neuter the num of
nom/acc/voc plural also stays dental. There
is no active r/ṣ/ṛ or ṝ trigger in these
stems. The accusative plural's final n is
ending-owned. Feminine genitive plural is
ī-nām with its own nuṭ. The a-stem ablative
pause choice inherits PG691 and PG692's
voicing/devoicing at a single grammatical key.
These dental and pause distinctions are
included in the exact factored table.

## The attested feminine and the competing interpretations

The Tokyo collated text and Sanskrit Wikisource
both give *vaivadhikī* at Rājataraṅgiṇī
6.308. They differ elsewhere in the first
pāda. Tokyo's *krīḍācaṅkrameṇa* and
Wikisource's OCR-form *krīḍācaḍkramaṇe*
remain literal source records. The secure
feminine nominative singular designation
belongs in apposition to *valgābhidhā* and
*vigrahavāhinī*. Valgā is the agent of
*akārayat*, with the monastery as what she
caused to be made. The maintenance of the
*vaivadhikī* case address does not depend on
assigning all other words in the verse.

The carrying epithet and the genitive queen
support Stein's contextual bearer reading as
reported by Sringeri Belur. The *paṅgvā*
sequence can be analysed with a genitive
before the voiced initial of *vigrahavāhinī*.
Sringeri Belur reports its own hawker/lame
Valgā interpretation separately, while
Śabdakalpadruma and Monier-Williams also
supply the trade gloss. Keep the published
verse reading, the positive porter analysis
and the trade interpretation at their own
source addresses. The verse's physical
means and the first vowel of its underlying
nominal base cannot be decided from the
shared ai. Monier-Williams's Harṣacarita
reference stays an Open work-level lead.
