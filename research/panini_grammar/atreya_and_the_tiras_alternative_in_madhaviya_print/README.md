# PG835 · Ātreya and the tiras alternative in the Mādhavīyadhātuvṛtti

The two printed texts of the Mādhavīyadhātuvṛtti locate the dispute inside **tanādi ḍukṛñ karaṇe**. Its treatment of *tiras* follows the treatment of *adas* and precedes that of *upāje/anvāje*. This identifies the work, section and argument behind the later attribution to Mādhava. The concordance transcription identifies the author as Sāyaṇa. Its own introductory verse 13 names him and calls his work Mādhavīya.

**Who licenses what.** In the concealment frame, *tiras* before simple √kṛ can receive gati under 1.4.71–72. On the gati branch, 8.3.42 optionally gives s. The writer also mentions jihvāmūlīya and visarga for the alternate sound route. Ātreya carries the gati guard into the s prescription, so a non-gati input cannot take that s edge. The unnamed grammarians carry no such guard into that sound rule. They cite *tiraskāra* in the sense of contempt. The author expressly reports that he does not find this view in the Bhāṣya, Vṛtti, Nyāsa, Padamañjarī and the like. This is his bibliographic judgment, and the Osmania apparatus in PG781 remains separate evidence about an edited text. Counting the two modern scans as two independent manuscript lines would require a genealogy.

**The literal example and the grammatical prediction.** Both Mādhavīya printings give the lyap word *tiraskṛtya* inside the *kecit* discussion. Their wording does not itself display non-gati *tiraskṛtvā*. PG376 already derives that spelling on the broadened sound policy with same-agent prior action, the non-gati phrase and retained ktvā. PG781 independently locates the ktvā word in the Osmania Kāśikā. Lifting the sound restriction can change s while the compound/7.1.37 gate for lyap continues to inspect its own input. The contempt noun contributes a lexical s witness. Its nominal role and affix must come from their own evidence. PG487 preserves the bhāva/ghañ and agent/aṇ analyses.

**Editorial reading.** The printed *anuvṛttiṃ* completes the object of *necchanti*. The reporter is singular (*āha*), the scholars reported are plural (*kecit*). Thus the damaged segment has a positive print reading without converting the report into a universal endorsement by its reporter. The electronic Tattvabodhinī source remains byte-pinned with its damaged text. The personal identity of the unnamed scholars, an explanation for the original lyap example, and the manuscript relations remain open.
