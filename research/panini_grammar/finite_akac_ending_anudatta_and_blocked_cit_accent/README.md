# PG661 · Finite akac ending anudātta and blocked cit accent

Monty · 24 November 2026

PG533 already constructed the exact finite-akac words *pacataki*, *jalpataki*, *svapitaki* and *śvasitaki*, preserving the original present third-singular `tip` ending as final `i_ENDING`. The remaining accent question is not settled by applying akac's `c_IT` mechanically. Kāśikā 6.1.186 expressly says that its later l-sārvadhātuka anudātta overrides even a cit accent. The original ending `i` in all four fixed words is therefore anudātta, and the 6.1.163 whole-complex-final-udātta route is blocked on that vowel.

This packet adds **one accent construction/four records**. It adds no finite or nominal position, morphology interface, source observation or source-policy credit. The census remains **51,819 finite positions; 8,012 nominal positions/9,271 readings**.

The decision is deliberately local. It fixes the status of the final ending vowel; it does not fabricate a fully accented Unicode word. The exact surviving non-ending accent placement, recitational transcription and sentence-level interaction remain Open where the pinned fields do not complete them. For *svapiti/śvasiti*, 6.1.188's optional initial accent is not transferred to these fixed forms: the rule requires an ajādi **aniṭ** l-sārvadhātuka, whereas PG533 preserves the explicit 7.2.76 iṭ before `tip`.

Other nominal/finite akac accent cells, nine padī lexical meanings, patradatam/128, C2–C5, S1–S7 and native/global obligations remain Open. Claude continues to own grammar execution, generators, untouched suites and certificate replay.
