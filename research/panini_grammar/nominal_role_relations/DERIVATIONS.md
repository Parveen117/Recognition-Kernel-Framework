# Written derivations

## H6. Putra is a two-direction ambiguity

From SELF_WISH(son) derive putrīyati or putrakāmyati. From OBJECT_MODEL(son, pupil) derive putrīyati by 3.1.10. Reverse recognition of putrīyati therefore yields two role frames in this bounded domain. Supplying the extra argument as patient resolves the behavioral reading. Lack of an expressed patient by itself does not establish self-wish.

## H7. Write the four P series once per profile

The factors are putrīy, putrakāmy, vastrakāmy, prāvārīy, prāsādīy and paryaṅkīy. With PG122’s śap-ending maps these give putrīyati, aputrīyat, putrīyatu and putrīyet in 3sg. Carry two semantic histories behind every cell for putrīy; all other selected profiles currently have one admitted frame.

## H8. Agreement in two A profiles

The factors śyenāy and puṣkarāy give śyenāyate and puṣkarāyate. The 3du forms śyenāyete and puṣkarāyete agree with two acting referents. Changing the incorporated comparison standard to dual is not the operation that creates that verbal agreement.

## H9. Store the bound and the free participant

For prāsādīyati kuṭyām the stored nominal standard is prāsāda and the overt locus is kuṭī. For putrīyati chātram the nominal is putra and the overt patient is chātra. State records identify the associated case and its role, including the independently established commentary extension. A one-word readout may leave the free participant uninstantiated.

## H10. No lexical or position inflation

Count one slot per nominal/formation/pada/lakāra/agreement key. The reappearance of putrīyati under a second semantic frame contributes an additional labelled history to each of 36 cells. It contributes neither an extra spelling nor a new position. Kāmyac produces a distinct derivational profile: its 36 cells are genuine new finite requests.
