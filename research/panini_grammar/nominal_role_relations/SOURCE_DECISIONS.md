# Source decisions

## H1. Self-wish has two affix choices

3.1.8 and 3.1.9 exhibit putrīyati and putrakāmyati for wanting a son for oneself. Kāśikā also gives vastrakāmyati. The nominal supplies the object sought, and the wisher is its intended beneficiary. Distinguish the alternative affixes from two meanings of the same affix.

## H2. Object-standard behavior

Kāśikā 3.1.10 illustrates someone treating a pupil like a son, and treating a blanket like a wrap. The incorporated nominal specifies the comparison standard. The patient is a separate argument, displayed as chātram or kambalam. It does not control the verbal person/number.

## H3. Place-standard behavior

Kāśikā adds adhikaraṇāc ca as an extension: prāsādīyati kuṭyām and paryaṅkīyati mañcake. The hut or platform is the locus whose treatment is compared with the palace or couch. The typed comparison role and overt locative distinguish the two argument positions. Mark the extension separately from the text of 3.1.10.

## H4. Agent-standard behavior

Under 3.1.11 Kāśikā gives a crow behaving like a hawk and a kumuda behaving like a puṣkara. Kyaṅ is ṅit and selects A. The actual actor supplies agreement; the incorporated standard does not. The Mahābhāṣya places the crow example in its discussion of why the affix remains available in the absence of any final s to delete.

## H5. Distinguish ī, ā and the kāmya segment

7.4.33 gives ī before kyac on the admitted a/ā-final hosts; 7.4.25 lengthens the vowel in the selected kyaṅ stems. Kāmyac retains its initial k and inner kāmya; the original a of putra or vastra precedes k. These three formations have different internal boundaries even when their outer endings match.
