## H02. Pada must be chosen again for the prefixed request

Kāśikā **1.3.17** expressly overrides residual P for ni + viś: **niviśate**, with **nyaviśata** despite intervening aṭ. Its augment-membership account supports the laṅ application; the ni token remains an upasarga after its i becomes y. Thus all 36 viś positions here are **kartari A**, not passive and not optional P/A. The outer pra does not remove the inner ni relation to viś.

For pac and kṛ select the principal action-result as not accruing to the agent under 1.3.72; incidental compensation does not reverse that decision. Kṛ also excludes the special senses in 1.3.32 and the other applicable special-pada contexts. There is no reciprocal-action request, causative, san or karmakartṛ request. Residual **1.3.78** then supplies P to the six other selected families. A semantic frame requiring another pada is outside this request grid, not an optional spelling in it.

## H03. Mandatory gad before the residual option

Under **8.4.17**, pra's r licenses mandatory ṇ in the following ni before the named gad. Kāśikā gives **praṇigadati** and expressly permits aṭ intervention, **praṇyagadat**. Nyāsa explains why an augment of the vikaraṇa-containing aṅga cannot simply be treated as an augment of the bare gad in every query. Padamañjarī supports the permitted intervention and contrasts a further intervening pada in **pranyupagadati**.

The current request has no extra upa or other intervening pada. Its laṅ aṭ is admitted by the specific commentary account. The **śeṣa** condition in 8.4.18 excludes gad from that residual option. It would empty 8.4.17's mandatory domain to apply optional dental n here merely because gad otherwise satisfies the letter guards. H03 handles gad in all four lakāras; suffix changes do not erase the named root identity.

## H04. Optional ni depends on upadeśa, not the latest word

Kāśikā, Nyāsa and Padamañjarī **8.4.18** identify the remaining root as neither k-initial nor kh-initial nor ṣ-final **in its teaching**. In this bounded [pra, ni] domain, with the prescribed adjacency/intervention already established, use this decision relation:

| Original root query | Result for prefix ni |
| --- | --- |
| Named gad of 8.4.17 | ṇ mandatory; no residual dental branch |
| Remaining pac or viś | ṇ / n, two real rule alternatives |
| kṛ or kṣi, initial k | n only |
| khād, initial kh | n only |
| piṣ, final ṣ | n only |

The source supplies **pranicakāra, pranicakhāda, pranipekṣyati** as negative controls and **praṇiveṣṭā** as a positive control. Copy c in a perfect does not turn original k/kh into an eligible teaching. Conversely viś's later ś→ṣ does not make it an original ṣ-final root. Piṣ's later ṣ→k before future s does not remove its original exclusion. Pac's ṣ-it marker cannot be mistaken for piṣ's root sound.

For the laṅ cases retain the inherited aṭ-intervention analysis of 8.4.17 and the 8.4.2/Padamañjarī account; the vowel is not an additional lexical pada. The same ni owner is realized as ṇy or ny before aṭ under 6.1.77. Arbitrary additional prefix chains, yaṅ-luk designations, āṅ intervention and Vedic separated constructions are not generalized from these tables.

Siddhāntakaumudī and Tattvabodhinī **8.4.18** explicitly distinguish this option from declining saṃhitā: dhātu/upasarga saṃhitā is obligatory in the selected construction. Both alternatives are fully joined words. This is a word-level option, not a policy that must be chosen once for an entire paradigm.

## H05. Each nasal has an owner and an observation stage

The addressed target of H03/H04 is **prefix[1].n**. In **pranikṣiṇoti**, that nasal remains dental while **śnu.n** becomes ṇ after the root ṣ under 8.4.1–2. Original k blocks one target's special prefix rule; it does not prohibit every ṇ in the word. In piṣ the śnam nasal is before root ṣ, so that ṣ cannot trigger it backwards. Pra's r cannot reach it through ni's intervening n. The allowed interveners of 8.4.2 do not contain a plain dental ni-n.

Before a consonant the weak śnam n in piṣ becomes anusvāra by 8.3.24. It remains anusvāra before ṣ, which is outside yay. In a third plural, an ending n first becomes anusvāra before t and is later restored as dental n under 8.4.58; that later n does not re-enter an earlier ṇatva query. A pada-final n in the admitted laṅ forms remains dental under 8.4.37. The same readout discipline applies to kṛ and kṣi's third plurals.

Neither ni nor śnam is an invented num augment. A lexical/compound kṣubhnādi exception under 8.4.39 requires its own evidence. No general “contains ṣ, therefore no ṇ” shortcut follows from that list. Earlier rule contracts for 8.4.14–18 and 1.4.58–60 remain pinned; the present packet adds their concrete combined requests, not new sūtra IDs.
