# PG202 · A new 4.105 dictionary reading and the two māḍhi stems

Monty · 12 November 2026

Śabdakalpadruma gives a previously unindexed example under **Uṇādi 4.105**. The entry for *māḍhi* (3.689b, L27028) explicitly prints the root *māh*, quotes *anyebhyo ’pi dṛśyante* and names **ktin**. The same dictionary explicitly uses *itvan* at that address for *petvam*. In the project’s five-pāda edition the inherited list contains nine affixes, including *itvan*, and has no *ktin*. The new reading has its own source record. The four primary fields *sya, ḍha, ḍaṭ, āṭac* remain open.

The next headword, **māḍhī** (L27029), expressly adds *ṅīṣ* to *māḍhi* and cites *kṛdikārāt*. Its gloss is *dantaśirā*, a dental structure. The short-i entry has several senses, including the vein of a leaf and the name of a region. Other dictionaries also record dental and non-dental senses. Monier-Williams specifically derives its ‘honouring’ gloss from short-vowel *mah*. The Dhātupāṭha distinguishes *māh* ‘measure’ (01.1042) and *mah* ‘honour’ (01.0831).

### Where the two dictionary derivations meet

The full condition quoted by the Kāśikā at 4.1.45 is **kṛdikārād aktinaḥ**. A short-i kṛt formed with *ktin* is excluded from this provision. The Kaumudī explains why *paddhati*, which does end in *ktin*, has to be named independently in the class. Thus the two adjacent dictionary entries alone leave a missing step if one tries to derive the long vowel by that general clause.

The same Kāśikā list gives a distinct condition for an i-stem denoting a living being’s bodily part. The Nyāsa illustrates it with *dhamaniḥ / dhamanī*. This supplies a grammatical comparison for the dental sense of *māḍhī*. Its application to this particular word is our conditional reading. Śabdakalpadruma itself cites the kṛt clause. The dental evidence supplies no basis for extending the long vowel to the district reading.

The two source-given stems now have a written case table with **48 positions and 52 readings**. The short stem follows the archived *mati* model and the long stem the *nadī* model. For example, *māḍhī* is nominative singular of the long stem and the nominative, accusative or vocative dual of the short stem. Both stems admit the locative singular *māḍhyām*. The short stem also admits *māḍhau* there. These are model-based case readings, with two separate lexical histories.

Zero new finite positions. Cumulative **41,333**.
