**sic 1060170** (class 6, U, aniṭ) and **dṛś 1011143** (class 1,

## H02. The full 1.3.67 guard

Kāśikā says the special rule is useful even when the principal result
does not accrue to the agent; 1.3.74 already covers the other case.
All six requests here select OTHER principal result. The special A
guard requires the following separately retained witnesses:

| Witness | Selected value |
| --- | --- |
| Current formation | ṇic is present |
| Earlier construction | aṇic, no earlier ṇic |
| Earlier role of q | karman, not instrument or merely a named participant |
| Current agreement controller | that same q, not a new causer c |
| Action comparison | the qualified same-action witness is supplied |
| New independent object | absent in the selected reduced construal |
| Ādhyāna | absent; nostalgic/longing recollection is excluded |

Use the Kāśikā–Nyāsa reduced construal: q's exceptional accommodation
first motivates causative attribution; the expressed causee/causer
transaction is then withdrawn, but ṇic remains. No new action is
introduced at the selected endpoint. The earlier object and present
controller IDs survive this change in construal. This is not a generic
claim that any passive patient may be relabelled an agent.

## H03. Independent negative controls and genuine source differences

Archive Kāśikā's separate controls: no ṇic; a previously ṇic
gaṇayati frame; an instrument dātra instead of karman; mounting versus
sprinkling despite the same elephant; a new object; a different causer;
and excluded ādhyāna. Each fails its own witness, even if the remaining
spelling or participants coincide. Incomplete witnesses produce HOLD.

Do not turn all these local rejections into a universal P theorem:
other rules may independently admit A. Bhāṣya discusses gaṇayati and
explicit remaining objects differently; its full field is preserved.
Kaumudī gives both withdrawn-causation and attributed-causation analyses.
The selected Kāśikā–Nyāsa endpoint with causees unexpressed is one
declared policy, not a resolution of every interpretation. Other
readings and nested/lexical ṇic cases remain outside the counted grid.

The same-action certificate is intensional and source-qualified; it
does not merely compare root names or real-world participant sets.
Likewise a first/second-person slot changes agreement on the supplied
current controller, not the role-identity witness.

## H04. Karmavat work has a different domain

Kāśikā 3.1.87 limits karmavat transfer to a former object's own
action/state. It explicitly separates kartṛstha from karmastha work.
Kāśikā 1.3.67 calls ruh kartṛstha-kriyā and dṛś kartṛstha-bhāva:
these require the special extension. The sprinkling frame can also
have karmastha-kriyā evidence. Retain this difference rather than
setting one blanket passive flag on the three A requests.

For the karmavat overlap, 3.1.89's ṇi-vārttika explicitly blocks
yak and ciṇ, and gives kārayate-type retained-ṇic construction.
Thus the present stem uses outer śap, not passive yak. That prohibition
does not automatically block **ciṇvat**: 3.1.87 and the 6.4.62 frontier
keep that later issue separate. PG93 writes only the four present
labels. No future, blessing, perfect or aorist is credited prematurely.
