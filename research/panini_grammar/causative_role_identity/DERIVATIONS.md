## H06. Root strength and the owned h/p option

Select the exact lexical root and one ṇic under 3.1.26, with ṇ/c
marker history and visible i retained separately. Root light-upadhā
guṇa **7.3.86** gives ruh→roh, sic→sec and dṛś→darś; ṛ's a+r
resolution retains its original owner. These are not final-ik vṛddhi
cases under 7.2.115, nor a-upadhā cases under 7.2.116.

For ruh, **7.3.43** optionally replaces final root h by p before
ṇic: roh/rop. Pin Kāśikā, Kaumudī and Nyāsa, which distinguish
this from the different root rup in delusion. The p is a replacement
of root h, not a puk augment or a second root. Each ruh key retains
two paths; the choice is independent of the qualified pada and slot.
For sic the lexical initial ṣ has the admitted s readout; root c
remains c. No current basic-present siñc or paśy stem is imported.

## H07. Derived dhātu and retained ṇic

By 3.1.32 the root constituent plus ṇic forms the derived dhātu.
Apply outer **śap 3.1.68** to that whole constituent. Guṇa of
ṇic's final i and 6.1.78 ayādi produce its separately owned **ay**;
the śap a is another owner. The layouts are
`ā | roh/rop | ay | a`, `upa | sec | ay | a`, and
`darś | ay | a`. A ending choice does not delete ṇic.

The original lexical aniṭ tag cannot suppress this sārvadhātuka
construction, and there is no root-facing iṭ in these four labels.
Ṇic is retained when the causative transaction is unexpressed at
the selected A endpoint. That retained morphology is exactly why a
word-only surface cannot recover the earlier event-role construction.

## H08. Thematic P and A endings

Reuse the pinned thematic present, secondary, ordinary loṭ and
vidhiliṅ maps on the final śap a. A has thematic ant in 3pl,
not the athematic at used for the preceding bhuj/yuj packets.
Thus ārohayante/upasecayante/darśayante. The A dual interfaces
give -ayete/-ayethe; A laṅ 1sg gives -aye.

P ordinary loṭ 2sg uses **6.4.105 hi-luk**, giving -aya.
A 2sg uses -ayasva; A first persons give -ayai/-ayāvahai/-ayāmahai.
First-person long-a work belongs to the thematic or ending seam,
not to an invented long root. A vidhiliṅ has -ayeta, -ayeyātām,
-ayeran, -ayethāḥ, -ayeyāthām, -ayedhvam, -ayeya, -ayevahi,
-ayemahi. Ordinary loṭ adds no separate blessing/tātaṅ option.

## H09. Prefixes and the past augment

Only laṅ has past **6.4.71 aṭ** in this packet. Its location is
after prefixes and before the derived root constituent. Ā+a coalesces
to ā; upa+a gives upā by 6.1.101; unprefixed dṛś gives adarś-.
Consequently ārohayate/ārohayata can differ only at the ending,
and A laṭ/laṅ 1sg ārohaye has the same visible word under two
different context histories. Surface absence of an extra vowel does
not mean the past-augment event was absent.

All 54 laṅ keys retain initial aṭ, with 72 paths because of ruh's
option. The prefix is not included in root guṇa or h/p replacement.
Root sic and aorist affix sic are separate names/owners; no aorist
affix is present in this block.

## H10. Finite closure and the unfilled complement

Six profiles × four labels × nine slots gives **216 positions**,
108 A and 108 P, in 24 full paradigms. The 72 ruh positions
have two readings each; the other 144 positions are singleton:
**288 readings and 288 paths**. There are no coincident extra paths
in this block. The profiles are disjoint from PG13's twelve-root
unprefixed ordinary-causal domain and from the earlier san/passive
dṛś domains because formation, prefix or role frame differs.

The cumulative register becomes **18,671+216=18,887**. The bounded
ten-label plan has 540 keys; the exact 324-key complement is recorded
as UNWRITTEN, not as forms. Its first obligation is to separate
ordinary causative and kartṛstha future interfaces from the possible
karmavat/ciṇvat work on upa+sic. Grammar execution remains deferred.
