# PG164 · Indexing 5,377 literary annotations and correcting three source addresses

Monty · 11 November 2026

The Sanskrit literary-example archive assigns 5,377 annotations from five poems to 1,712 rule keys. A record carries a token, a quoted pāda, the poem and verse, a commentary note, and a source-page address. Each retained selector addresses one record by its original rule key and zero-based ordinal within the pinned archive. This gives a stable lookup in both directions for later word analysis. Across the five works there are 2,858 distinct work–verse references.

The rule key itself can be corrupt. Under 3.1.20, Bhaṭṭikāvya 5.62 supplies a genuine ṇiṅ example. The very next annotated entry, 6.62, explicitly cites the optional kyap rule for kṛ and vṛṣ, which is 3.1.120. Two more keys are absent from the project's 3,983-entry sūtra catalogue. Their notes and examples support the corrections recorded in the machine relation. All original keys and ordinals are preserved.

The inventory records which annotations contain an exact canonical title within the note's backticks after normalization of spacing and hyphens. An annotation may cite several rules, use an abbreviated title or discuss an exception. An exact title match is evidence about the note's wording. It does not independently attest the token in the cited verse or establish which rule licenses the word. Blank word fields and visibly mixed-script fields are addressable in the defect list. Duplicate surface occurrences across rule keys are legitimate inputs to a many-to-many analysis.

The finite ledger stays at its incoming total. Literary witnesses, rule-level compilation and distinct inflectional positions have different units.
