# PG122 — Select the input that becomes a denominative

Monty · 11 November 2026

## H01. Source policy and input types

Use the restrictive vārttika/Bhāṣya reading of 3.1.13 adopted in
Kaumudī 1.3.90. Kyaṣ is licensed here from lohita, its expressly
admitted feminine lohinī, and a certified ḍāc-final imitation.
Kaumudī places śyāma in the bhṛśādi kyaṅ construction instead.
The positive grid uses six base witnesses and eleven pada profiles.
The source addresses are commentary field/entry labels. None is a
Dhātupāṭha root number. Finite agreement belongs to the subject of
the resulting state or sound event.

Kāśikā 3.1.13 also discusses an open class and gives additional
kyaṣ examples. Record this as a different source policy. Do not
infer P for every adjective, mental state or vowel-final noun.
The stage selects the ordinary unprefixed, unaccented kartari domain.

## H02. The nominal colour inputs

Lohita requires the supplied change from an earlier non-red state to
a red state (abhūtatadbhāva). Supply the corresponding source-qualified
feminine base **lohinī** for the second colour reading. Kaumudī
1.3.90 invokes the liṅgaviśiṣṭa convention and explicitly writes
lohinīyati/lohinīyate. This is an extension of a named base. One
cannot infer that replacing any state adjective by any feminine
derivative licenses kyaṣ. The base's gender is separate from the
finite subject's person and number.

For śyāma supply the chosen becoming-dark frame. The selected
Bhāṣya/Kaumudī reading assigns this remaining colour term to kyaṅ
under 3.1.12. Its ṅit status supplies A under 1.3.12. The rule
1.3.90 on kyaṣ is inapplicable to this different affix.

## H03. Three attested sound constructions

The Bhāṣya tail at 3.1.13 supplies paṭapaṭāyati/-te, damadamāyati/-te
and maṭamaṭāyati/-te. Each request supplies an actual avyakta
sound imitation and a ḍāc formation governed by 5.4.57. Under the
Kaumudī reading at 1.3.90 the new-colour test on lohita does not
extend to the ḍāc word. The sound may be produced repeatedly without
a witness that an entity has newly turned into that sound. The query
concerns the stipulated event and source construction, independent
of the apparent repetition of the printed letters.

The Nyāsa account derives the selected paṭapaṭā input from paṭat.
For the two further Bhāṣya examples accept the cited complete ḍāc
base as source input. Their unobserved pre-duplication token histories
are not reconstructed from their spellings. Retain this difference
in the machine record.

## H04. Two explanations of pada, one set of forms

Kāśikā 1.3.90 asks how A remains available in this non-ṅit kyaṣ
construction and takes A as optional, with residual kartari P by
1.3.78. Kaumudī states the surface option as optional P. Use the
Kāśikā cause account for the machine labels and preserve the other
statement in the source archive. The two commentarial descriptions
do not multiply the readings. Kyaṅ alone has its own ṅit A cause.
An apparent -āya stem carries insufficient information to infer either
the affix identity or the P entitlement.

## H05. Marker interpretation and boundaries

The sūtra names kyaṣ and the traditional fields discuss whether its
initial k needs separate justification. Bhāṣya 3.1.13 debates kit,
the lengthening rule and collective ky reference, then rejects the
need for k in its revised wording. Kaumudī 7.4.25 states the lengthening
before qualifying y-initial affixes; the Kāśikā includes the k/ṅit
qualification. Store the canonical affix identity, surviving ya, and
the stated source policy. There is no pronounced k or ṣ in these
readings. In particular 1.3.90 is an affix-class query after marker
disappearance. The ḍāc witness and the colour-change witness cannot
be substituted for one another. A missing witness gives HOLD.
