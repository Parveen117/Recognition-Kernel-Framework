## H07. The paṭat witness is an inner derivation

Under the cited 5.4.57 reading, duplication can be selected in view
of the intended ḍāc. Nyāsa 3.1.13 places it before the suffix. At
the repeated-word seam, 6.1.100 takes the form of the following p;
Bālamanoramā's account gives a single p there. The ḍit ṭi-lopa
removes the terminal portion of the second paṭat, leaving the source
ḍāc base paṭapaṭā. Then add kyaṣ. Do not treat this repetition as
a san desiderative copy or as the outer finite augment. For the two
other sound bases H03 records the source stopping point.

## H09. Retain the outer augment and endings

Laṅ adds aṭ under 6.4.71 before the entire derived formation.
Paṭapaṭā P 3sg is **apaṭapaṭāyat**: the augment occurs once before
the first sound member. Lohinī A laṭ 1sg **lohinīye** contrasts
with laṅ 1sg **alohinīye**. The former has the ṭit ending route;
the latter has the a+i junction. In P loṭ 2sg retain the hi-lopa
history; **lohitāya** can coincide with the written citation stem.
P loṭ 1sg keeps dental n in these selected stems. The ś/ṭ segments
in distinct source forms do not themselves license ṇatva here.

## H10. Count the finite requests

Each of the five kyaṣ bases has two padas; śyāma has one. Four
lakāras and nine person-number slots give (5×2+1)×4×9 = 396
positions and singleton readings. The elementary paṭat derivation,
six nominal witnesses, alternative source statements and blocked
śyāma P proposal have zero independent position credit. The
cumulative written register reaches **28,103**.
