# PG946 · One instrumental plural in two Ṛgvedic hymns

The word *juhūbhiḥ* occurs at Ṛgveda 1.58.4 and 10.21.3. Sāyaṇa gives two different referents. At 1.58.4 the fire goes among trees equipped with its own tongues. His gloss is *svakīyābhiḥ jihvābhiḥ*. At 10.21.3 his first interpretation takes *dharmāṇaḥ* as the priests sustaining the sacrifice and *juhūbhiḥ* as offering vessels full of oblations. His *yadvā* introduces a second reading of the larger clause with rays abiding in Agni and a comparison involving poured offerings. The written ladle construction selects the first interpretation here. The second interpretation is preserved as a reading of the clause and supplies no extra instrumental word. **Morphology and syntax.** Both instrumental plural requests use feminine *juhū* + bhis. The consonant-initial ending preserves ū. The final s has the ordinary visarga readout in the isolated word. They have one unaccented word spelling. One occurrence predicates equipment of the fire and the other predicates ritual service with vessels. **Which kvip source is meant?** At 1.58.4 the commentator cites *huvaḥ śluvacca*, quoting a reference printed in this transcription as 2.218. He accounts for lengthening through *ca*, reduplication through ślu-like treatment, and gives final accent in his lexical explanation. In the repository Uṇādi concordance, identical rule text and kvip occur at **2.61**, key **2061**. There are 157 entries in its first pāda, putting 2.61 at cumulative entry **218**. These three anchors align this individual citation. They do not establish how every rule was numbered in the commentator's edition. PG945's Kāśikā vārttika at 3.2.178 remains a distinct source route. The citation supports Uṇādi kvip in this lexical explanation without borrowing a ślu affix from a finite present. [Pinned Uṇādi register](../unadi_concordance/CONCORDANCE.tsv)

Sāyaṇa illustrates the noun by *hūyate āsu*, with a plural locative, “in these”. This places the act of offering in them. The missing expressed food or offering in this short explanation does not identify the particular intransitive incorporation policy of PG941. It therefore supplies contextual evidence for the finite string while leaving direct attribution of PG942's impersonal derivation Open. In 1.58.7 the word division separately has *juhvaḥ*. The gloss there names the officiants. It supplies no nominative-plural ladle credit here.
