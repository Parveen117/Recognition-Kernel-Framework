# PG207 · Two styai teaching forms across all ten desiderative lakāras

Monty · 12 November 2026

The two teaching forms *styai* (01.1058) and *ṣṭyai* (01.1059) share the bare present *styāyati* in Deva’s account. Each has **both** senses, ‘make a sound’ and ‘form an aggregate’. Puruṣakāra on *Daiva* 7 expressly rejects distributing those senses by the order of the roots. It identifies the first divergent forms as the unprefixed desideratives *tistyāsati* and *tiṣṭyāsati*. The conditioning *i* comes from the reduplicative syllable *ti*. The same commentary reports a different basic present, *ṣṭyāyati*, for the second root in Kṣīrasvāmin and Śākaṭāyana. We keep its attribution explicit.

| Construction, third singular | Original dental s | Root taught with ṣ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Basic present, Deva | styāyati | styāyati |
| Desiderative present | tistyāsati | tiṣṭyāsati |
| Desiderative future | tistyāsiṣyati | tiṣṭyāsiṣyati |
| Desiderative aorist, pause reading | atistyāsīt | atiṣṭyāsīt |

Under 6.1.64 the second root has a substituted *s*. After the reduplicative *i*, 8.3.59 affects that substituted sound and 8.4.41 gives the adjacent retroflex stop. The original dental *s* of the first root fails the substitution condition. The *s* contributed by the desiderative suffix remains dental after *ā* in both roots. This lets a reader distinguish the two sounds of *tistyāsiṣyati* even when the future adds a third sibilant.

Both roots now have all **90** requested *parasmaipada* positions. In every corresponding pair the two sets of written words are disjoint. The grammatical source supplies the present example directly. The other words extend the source-specific choice through the previously written āśīrliṅ, perfect, aorist and other ending schedules. The compound perfect preserves its auxiliary and nasal-boundary alternatives.
