# PG32 — Ordinary luṅ and addressed prohibitive continuations

Author: Monty. 3 November 2026, Asia/Calcutta.

## D1. Input before word formation

Start on N1's full retained-history carrier. A completed input supplies
the exact lexical entry and sense, basic kartari formation, event and
actor, controller/person/number, pada evidence, semantic request,
māṅ/sma dependencies, commentary policy and phonetic policy. Unknown
dependencies remain Open. No clock or assertion of an event is inferred
from a lakāra's sound or from an initial a.

Mud is class 1, not class 10's saṃsarga/ṇic entry. Vand retains the

listed padas do not mean free choice within one fixed event.

## D2. Three request profiles

Use PG30's exact lexical particle māṅ, including its retained marker,
and its addressed association with a clause. An intervening bhavān is
permitted; a particle belonging to another clause supplies no evidence.
The homographic root, pronoun and alternative aṅit mā are not silently
reclassified as this particle.

| Profile | Completed admission | Lakāra | Initial augment |
|---|---|---|---|
|L+|supplied ordinary bhūta-general luṅ request; no superseding special context|luṅ, 3.2.110 under 3.2.84|aṭ/āṭ, 6.4.71–72|
|L−|addressed māṅ, with or without associated sma|luṅ, 3.3.175–176|blocked, 6.4.74|
|J−|addressed māṅ with associated sma|laṅ, 3.3.176|blocked, 6.4.74|

L+ is a construction for an admitted luṅ request, not an assertion that
every past utterance has only luṅ. The other temporal/epistemic rules
are not resolved here. J− has prohibitive meaning; ordinary anadyatana
past laṅ is not added to the domain. L− is counted once even when
admitted in two particle contexts. A prohibition does not assert that
its event happened in the past.

The controller is the supplied kartari actor. Bhavān takes third-person
agreement even when addressed. All nine grammatical slots are written;
their use still requires an appropriate discourse/controller frame.

## D3. Exactly what is new

L+ has ten root/pada profiles, or 90 positions. L− has eight **new**
profiles, or 72 positions; PG30 already supplies its kṛ/hṛ P profiles.
J− likewise has eight new profiles /72 positions, with kṛ/hṛ P reused.
Thus PG32 adds **234 positions**, not 270. Existing counterpart forms
and repetitions across contexts are not counted again.

Within the adopted Nyāsa-compatible dh/ḍh policy, only īh L± 2pl
has two readings. The register therefore contains **236 readings**.
Kaumudī's wider augmented-aṅga policy and its explicit rival reading
are recorded separately in S; they are not pooled as free variation
under one supposedly undisputed rule.

## D4. Boundaries

No passive/bhāva luṅ, causal caṅ, ksa, aṅ, ciṇ, prefixed-pada exception,
Vedic augment option, accent implementation or full sentence sandhi is
constructed. The non-doubling phonetic profile and devoiced pause are
inherited. The source's other word forms are witnesses to a rule or a
policy, not automatically new admitted paradigms.

The grammar register is written work. It is not an executable oracle,
an independent corpus check or a claim that all applications of these
sūtras are closed. Tests, generator/certificate replay, navigation and
operator suites remain deferred under the owner's written-first instruction.
