# PG32 — Sic, its suppression, and three different augment owners

Author: Monty. 3 November 2026.

## L1. Select and retain the affix owners

For D's luṅ requests, 3.1.43 supplies cli and 3.1.44 substitutes sic.
The adopted Kāśikā account treats sic's i as an enunciative vowel and
c as the accent marker; pronounced s remains affix-owned. The contrasting
Kaumudī wording is retained in S. Sic is ārdhadhātuka under 3.4.114;
the following tiṅ is separately sārvadhātuka under 3.4.113. Neither
the lakāra's ṅ nor an apit ending makes every intervening affix ṅit.

The five seṭ roots edh/vand/mud/īkṣ/īh receive root-facing iṭ on
sic by 7.2.35. Its actual i defeats the jhal-initial guard of 1.2.11;
mud consequently has upadhā-guṇa mud→mod by 7.3.86. Vand preserves
its root-owned nasal; its upadhā is not the eligible short vowel of
mud. Edh, īkṣ and īh receive no analogous invented strengthening.

Kṛ/hṛ remain aniṭ under 7.2.10. In A, 1.2.12 makes the **jhal-initial
sic** kit after ṛ, so 1.1.5 withholds guṇa and the root remains kṛ/hṛ.
In P the A restriction is false and 7.2.1 instead gives kār/hār.
The future-only ṛ-root iṭ of 7.2.70 cannot be transported from PG31.

Bhū P is the different L4 branch. The listed roots do not satisfy the
other cli-substitute domains: no caṅ-triggering derived root, no admitted
puṣādi/dyutādi/ḷit profile, and no aniṭ ik-upadhā śal-final input for
ksa. In particular seṭ īh/īkṣ do not obtain ksa by their spelling alone.

## L2. Ātmanepada: nine actual endings

Original A tiṅ members are ta, ātām, jha; thās, āthām, dhvam;
iṭ, vahi, mahiṅ. Luṅ is not ṭit: neither 3.4.79 nor 3.4.80 replaces
them by present/future endings. Here the final i is the surviving
first-singular **ending**, not the root-facing iṭ augment.

| Slot | Ending construction | Sic fate |
|---|---|---|
|3sg|ta|after short ṛ, 8.2.27 deletes s; after affix-i it remains|
|3du|ātām unchanged; preceding sic is not short-a-final|s retained|
|3pl|jha→ata by 7.1.5, whose at substitutes only for jh|s retained|
|2sg|thās; final s has pause visarga|same root distinction as 3sg|
|2du|āthām unchanged|s retained|
|2pl|dhvam, with its final m retained|8.2.25 deletes sic-s before dh|
|1sg|iṭ→i by marker removal|s retained|
|1du|vahi|s retained|
|1pl|mahiṅ→mahi|s retained|

The tiṅ augment īṭ of 7.3.96 requires an apṛkta ending (the single-
phoneme affix of 1.2.41) which is also hal. Ta/thās are not single-sound
endings. Ending i is a vowel, so it fails the hal condition.
Consequently **none of these A slots
gets īṭ**. In particular edhiṣi is not derived with a long ī ending.

Before ta/thās, seṭ sic has i to its left, but this is affix-owned iṭ,
not a short-vowel-final root/aṅga satisfying 8.2.27's selected account.
Kāśikā's alāviṣṭām/alāviṣuḥ contrast makes that ownership restriction
explicit. Rule 8.2.28 requires following **īṭ**, which is absent here.
Rule 8.2.26 requires preceding jhal, also absent before this sic-s.
Thus s survives: edh|i|s|ta→edhiṣṭa, not edhita.

Retained s after i or ṛ takes 8.3.55/57/59 ṣatva. Adjacent t/th
then receives 8.4.41 ṭ/ṭh. There is no āśīrliṅ sīyuṭ or suṭ here.
At 2pl, s was already deleted before dh, so ṣṭutva is not a substitute
explanation for the remaining dh/ḍh question. S specifies that question.

For a seṭ root readout B∈{edh,vand,mod,īkṣ,īh}, define the unaugmented
tuple, subject to the permitted δ at 2pl,

\[
L_A^{set}(B,\delta)=
(Biṣṭa,Biṣātām,Biṣata,Biṣṭhāḥ,Biṣāthām,
 Bi\,\delta\,vam,Biṣi,Biṣvahi,Biṣmahi).
\]

In the sixth entry the notation means concatenation Bi + δ + vam,
without a word break. For r∈{kṛ,hṛ} the corresponding tuple is

\[
L_A^{ṛ}(r)=(rta,rṣātām,rṣata,rthāḥ,rṣāthām,
 rḍhvam,rṣi,rṣvahi,rṣmahi).
\]

The original root and deleted sic remain in Smriti at rta/rthāḥ/rḍhvam.
Deletion of the realized s is neither failure to select sic nor erasure
of its entire derivational identity.

## L3. Ordinary kṛ/hṛ P from the inherited sic constructor

PG30 F2 supplies the nine unaugmented P words. Retain its root vṛddhi,
jus at 3pl, īṭ at 3sg/2sg, ṣatva and ṣṭ contacts. Under L+, add
the independently licensed aṭ, giving

\[
(akārṣīt,akārṣṭām,akārṣuḥ,akārṣīḥ,akārṣṭam,akārṣṭa,
 akārṣam,akārṣva,akārṣma)
\]

and the corresponding ahār- series. These are new ordinary augmented
profiles. The already-written L− and J− P profiles are linked, not
counted a second time. A's kṛta and P's kārṣīt cannot be obtained by
changing an ending after one shared unqualified root-strength choice.

## L4. Bhū: sic-luk is not a global ban on its historical effects

2.4.77 deletes sic by luk after bhū in P. Retain the luk event and
its source identity. No iṭ attached to an absent root-facing sic is
inserted. At consonant-initial sārvadhātuka tiṅ, 7.3.88 withholds
bhū-guṇa. Before the vowel-initial 3pl/1sg endings, 6.4.88 supplies
root-final vuk: bhū|v|ant and bhū|v|am. Its k places v at the end
under 1.1.46; vuk is not the first-person dual ending v.

Two different source restrictions are necessary:

1. **Īṭ:** adopt Kaumudī/Bālamanoramā 7.3.96's actual, extant sic
   reading (Kāśikā also withholds īṭ in abhūt through its stated exception).
   Deleted sic does not supply this guard. Thus bhūt, not bhūīt.
2. **Jus:** do not justify its absence merely by “sic was deleted”.
   Kāśikā 3.4.110 explicitly says pratyayalakṣaṇa would make jus
   available after sic-luk. The rule restricts this lopped-sic case to
   **ā-final** roots. Bhū is ū-final, so the restriction excludes it.
   Realized sic still gives kārṣuḥ through 3.4.109.

Thus bhū uses 7.1.3's ant at 3pl; vuk plus final nt simplification
under 8.2.23 gives bhūvan. P i-lopa, the tām/tam/ta/am substitutions
and first-person s-lopa are the inherited 3.4.99–101 operations. The
whole unaugmented tuple is

\[
(bhūt,bhūtām,bhūvan,bhūḥ,bhūtam,bhūta,bhūvam,bhūva,bhūma).
\]

The pinned Kaumudī 3.4.110 field prints bhūt in the 2pl neighborhood;
the actual tha→ta rule yields bhūta. Preserve the source bytes and this
adjudication separately. No source error becomes a new grammatical branch.

## L5. Initial augmentation and prohibition

L+ inserts aṭ for bhū/vand/mud/kṛ/hṛ. For edh/īkṣ/īh, 6.4.72
supplies āṭ and 6.1.90 takes the initial vowel to vṛddhi:
edh→aidh, īkṣ→aikṣ, īh→aih at that outer junction. This does not
change the root-facing iṭ, kit or sic query history.

L− blocks that **candidate** aṭ/āṭ operation by 6.4.74 before word
realization. Affix iṭ, eligible P īṭ and bhū's vuk survive. The
result is not constructed by deleting the first character of a completed
past word: deleting a from aidhiṣṭa cannot recover edhiṣṭa.

For each completed inner branch, the request-qualified outer constructor
therefore has exactly the L+ or L− readout written in P. The source
labels and rejected augment candidate remain recoverable even when the
observer later reports only the word.
