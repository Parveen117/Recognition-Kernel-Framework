## N2. Suppression is addressed to a candidate operation

Let F build an admitted root/pada/lakāra/ending branch with the owned
constituents and query views required in L/J. Its output is not a bare
word. Let I+ append the licensed aṭ/āṭ event, and let I− record its

\[
C_\pm=\operatorname{Read}\,\Omega_\pm I_\pm F.
\]

They are typed on different semantic inputs with matched inner
morphology, not two competing truth claims about one event. Their source
labels differ. Both preserve F's root-facing iṭ, A kit, P īṭ and
bhū-vuk query records wherever those have been admitted.

For each of these addressed inner predicates q, L proves
q(I±h)=q(h). Its corresponding diagonal cuts therefore satisfy

\[
Q_q^\pm I_\pm=I_\pm Q_q.
\]

**Proof.** I± adds or rejects only the candidate owned by the initial
aṅga augmentation. It changes none of the stored owner/lexical/ending
fields or earlier query views determining q. The basis calculation is
thus identical on both sides. This is a source-specified local schedule,
not a universal assertion that aṭ commutes with every grammar operation. □

This proves why māṅ may coexist with sic-iṭ, P īṭ or bhū-vuk.
An observer that compresses all these owners to one “augment present”
bit cannot implement the owner-selective operation: edh L− needs
affix-iṭ while bhū L− needs root-final vuk. A blanket augment-deletion
routine would violate the explicitly retained constituents.

Word surgery is not the construction. For example Read Ω+I+ gives
aidhiṣṭa; deleting its initial a yields idhiṣṭa, not edhiṣṭa.
This disproves that particular deletion algorithm. It does **not** prove
that no lookup or decoder can exist on this small finite word inventory.

\[
