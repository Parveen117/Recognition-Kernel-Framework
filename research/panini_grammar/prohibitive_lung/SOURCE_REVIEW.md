## S2. The adopted dh/ḍh policy must be named

Nyāsa 8.3.79 distinguishes the owner of iṭ: in luṅ it belongs to
**sic**, not to the lakāra ending. Its alavidhvam example therefore
does not acquire mandatory 78 merely by treating augment-i as the
root's final iṇ. Rule 79 permits dh/ḍh when that iṭ follows iṇ.
This packet adopts that root-qualified account, consistent with PG20's
declared origin-sensitive query, now applied to the luṅ owner.

| Actual owned environment at luṅ 2pl | Adopted result | PG32 application |
|---|---|---|
|iṇ-final root, no intervening iṭ after sic-s deletion|ḍh by 78|kṛ/hṛ: kṛḍhvam/hṛḍhvam|
|sic-iṭ immediately after iṇ|dh or ḍh by 79|īh: its h is in iṇ|
|sic-iṭ after a non-iṇ root ending|dh|edh/vand/mud/īkṣ|

An aspirated stop **dh is not h**, and kṣ ends in ṣ, not an iṇ
sound. Hence īh differs from edh and īkṣ. The ordinary initial aṭ/āṭ
does not change any of these root-final tests. Laṅ has no license from
78–79; thus kurudhvam in J− keeps dh despite its adjacent u.

There is an explicit rival source policy. Kaumudī **7.1.5** prints
aidhiḍhvam under 78, then says that under the reading excluding iṭ
from this iṇ the form is aidhidhvam. Its ensuing sixteen-form remark
also includes optional consonant doubling, which PG32 excludes. The
policies are not one free optional 78 on every seṭ root.

PG32 adopts **aidhidhvam /edhidhvam** in the [word register](PARADIGMS.md).
The rival augmented-aṅga
policy would give aidhiḍhvam/edhiḍhvam and, by the same policy's
application, the ḍh readings for vand/mud/īkṣ. Only the edh pair is
explicit in that selected source field; the further applications would
be derivations, not quotations. Those rival-policy readings are not
included in 236. Īh's two readings are included because 79 positively
licenses them **inside the adopted policy**. No consensus claim is made.

## S3. Literal anomalies and different analyses

1. Kāśikā 3.1.43/44 calls i enunciative. Kaumudī 3.1.44 says
   `इचावितौ`. Retain both and use Kāśikā's enunciative-i account here;
   do not infer an accent or generally derivable marker theorem from
   the shared pronounced s.
2. Kaumudī 3.4.110 includes `अभूत्` in the expected 2pl neighborhood.
   The retained 3.4.101 tha→ta construction supplies **abhūta**.
   The raw field is archived without silently correcting its letters.
3. Kaumudī 3.4.106 contains āśīrliṅ forms and then aidhiṣṭa/
   aidhiṣātām; 7.1.5 continues the luṅ paradigm and later the
   conditional. This is a source reading sequence, not permission to
   assign every form in the field to its heading's rule.
4. The 7.3.96 commentaries give different grammatical analyses of
   asti/sic and explicitly prevent īṭ after lopped sic. PG32 adopts
   the extant-sic guard and retains the alternative explanation; it
   does not equate historical sic-luk with universal invisibility.
5. Kāśikā 3.2.111's supplied field has a luṅ label beside laṅ
   examples. It is archived as an excluded temporal-neighbour control;
   PG32 does not construct ordinary laṅ from that damaged label.

Missing requested fields are explicitly marked empty. Empty strings,
source headings, a software comparison and a derived full paradigm are
not interchangeable evidence types.

## S4. Word witnesses versus authored continuations

Kāśikā supplies akṛta/akṛthāḥ/ahṛta/ahṛthāḥ under 8.2.27,
akṛṣātām/akṛṣata as the contrasting surviving-s cases, akṛḍhvam
under 8.3.55, and mā bhavān īhiṣṭa/īkṣiṣṭa under 6.4.74.
Kaumudī supplies mā bhavān bhūt and mā sma bhavat/bhūt, while its
3.4.110, 6.4.88 and 7.3.96 passages ground the bhū ending/augment
distinctions. Its edh fields ground the A ending chain and the explicit
dh-policy disagreement. These are selected witnesses, not independent
full-paradigm evidence for all eight roots.

The complete vand/mud/īkṣ/īh series and most source-context/pada
transports are **authored applications of the selected rules**. No
unperformed generator run, grammatical test or independent external
validation is implied. The current archive supports reproducible reading;
it is not a newly generated certificate of Sanskrit completeness.
