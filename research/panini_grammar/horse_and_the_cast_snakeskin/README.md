# PG982 · The horse Raghu asks for and the skin a snake casts off

Mādhava quotes a line about a horse immediately after deriving *nirmokaḥ*. In the Benares edition the explanation crosses from printed p. 344 to p. 345. The quotation belongs to Raghu’s reply to Indra in *Raghuvaṃśa* 3.64–65. Raghu draws back his arrow. If Indra judges the horse must be kept, Raghu asks that his father nevertheless receive the full fruit of the sacrifice. The privative adjective *amocyam* agrees with the masculine accusative *aśvam*. *Manyase* gives the judgment to the second-person addressee. The horse bears the property under discussion, and the requested beneficiary of the ritual is yet another participant. Adjacent lettering in *amocyamaśvam* supplies the two accusatives at their own word boundaries. ## Three positions in the comparison and its compounds

1. In *Mahābhārata* 7.143.4–5 the preceding verse has the armour dislodged by arrows. In the comparison the snake is the subject of casting off and *nirmokam* is its accusative object. At 12.274.50 *nirmokaṃ bhujageṣu* again gives the singular accusative of the same noun. Its final nasal meets bh in the running text. Two occurrences supply one grammatical key. [Droṇaparvan](https://bombay.indology.info/mahabharata/text/UD/MBh07.txt) · [Śāntiparvan](https://bombay.indology.info/mahabharata/text/UD/MBh12.txt)
2. In *Raghuvaṃśa* 16.17 the pieces of cast skin left by serpents cling to images of women on pillars and serve as breast cloths. *Nirmokapaṭṭāḥ* is masculine nominative plural. Paṭṭa furnishes its gender and outer ending. The instrumental *phaṇibhiḥ* supplies the agents of *vimuktāḥ*, which agrees with the pieces.
3. In *Mahābhārata* 12.139.27–30 a settlement is identified through its furnishings. *Sarpanirmokamālābhiḥ* names the garlands used in marking its huts, instrumental plural. Mālā supplies the feminine ending. Sarpa identifies whose discarded skins make up the garlands. Neither inner word takes the outer instrumental. [Śāntiparvan](https://bombay.indology.info/mahabharata/text/UD/MBh12.txt)

## Exact case register

For each request prepend the literal base L to the indicated ending. The columns contain singular, dual and plural in order. Masculine bases are **amocy**, **nirmok** and **nirmokapaṭṭ**. N is dental n in the first and third bases and ṇ in the second. In *nirmokeṇa* the trigger r reaches the eligible ending through m, the vowel and k. The ṭṭ of the outer *paṭṭa* breaks that path. The explicitly final n of the accusative plural remains dental. For the feminine use **sarpanirmokamāl** and the literal endings in the last column. Its l intervenes before the genitive plural n.
