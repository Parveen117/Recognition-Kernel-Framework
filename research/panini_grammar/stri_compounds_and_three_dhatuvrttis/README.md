# PG225 · Three Dhātuvṛtti readings of strī and the abstracts of its compounds

Monty · 12 November 2026

Kṣīrasvāmin’s own *Kṣīrataraṅgiṇī* gives **ṣṭyāyati** alongside **styāyati**. His derivation of *strī* expressly names **ḍraṭ at 4.165**. At that same number the project’s primary five-pāda text reads *ḍaṭ*. Both readings now have exact source addresses. The separate missing assignment of *ḍaṭ* at **4.105** is still open.

Maitreya’s *Dhātupradīpa* gives *kustrī* and *duḥstrī*. The three sources for their abstracts record different consonants and, in one case, a different meaning.

| Witness | First abstract | Second abstract | What the witness specifies |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mādhavīyadhātuvṛtti 577 | kaustram | daustram | *Aṇ* under the *yuvādi* rule, in the sense of state or conduct. |
| Dhātupradīpa 613, digital transcription | kausram | dauḥsram | Both have *sr* where the other witness has *str*. Its wording identifies an abstract; the transcription supplies no derivation for dropping *t*. |
| Monier-Williams | kaustra | dauḥstra | The second entry glosses discord between women; its head retains the visarga and the *t*. |

The Kāśikā under 5.1.130 lists *kustrī* and *sustrī*. The digital Gaṇapāṭha nearby prints *kustrī, sustri, daḥstrī*; the last two spellings are transcribed as given. The independently named *duḥstrī* belongs to its own witness.

Two head-final *strī* compounds and three neuter abstract heads yield **120 case–number positions, 127 readings**. The feminine tables admit the expanded and contracted accusatives found in *strī*. Two independently tabulated compounds, *kulastrī* and *vārastrī*, provide all 48 corresponding control cells. The apparently t-less abstract citations remain two source observations outside the new declensions.
