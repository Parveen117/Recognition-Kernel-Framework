## H06. Different present constructions under the same root spelling

For rudhādi 1070017 use 3.1.78 and mit placement 1.1.47:
**bhu | na | j**, with weak 6.4.111 giving **bhu | n | j**.
Reuse PG90 H06–07's exact strength/endings schedule, replacing only
the lexical root and its independently qualified pada cause.
There are 144 present-system positions: 19 strong and 125 weak.
The three A profiles each have only the three ordinary loṭ first
persons strong; the P profile has its laṭ/laṅ singulars and loṭ
3sg/first persons strong.

The weak infix n gives ñ before retained j, and ṅ when j→g/k at a
consonant seam. Thus bhuñje/bhuñjvahe, bhuṅkte, bhuṅkṣe and
bhuṅgdhve have different recipient histories. Strong bhunaj gives
bhunakti/bhunakṣi and bhunajmi. In P laṅ 3sg/2sg, 6.1.68 deletes
the apṛkta ending, leaving selected pausal **abhunak** at two keys.
Ordinary loṭ P 2sg is **bhuṅgdhi** by hi→dhi 6.4.101.
A non-short-a 3pl uses 7.1.5 at, hence bhuñjate, not thematic ant.

For tudādi 1060153 use **3.1.77 śa**, not śnam or śap. Its apit
sārvadhātuka status under 1.2.4 blocks root-u guṇa by 1.1.5;
the 36 present-system positions have **bhuj+a**. The thematic endings
give bhujati, abhujat and bhujatu; **6.4.105 hi-luk** gives bhuja.
Thematic a lengthens before eligible first-person endings; 7.2.80's
optative interface gives bhujet/bhujeyuḥ/bhujeyam. No present nasal
is inferred from the shared lexical spelling. Both vikaraṇas are
absent from the six ārdhadhātuka constructions below.

## H09. Perfect copy bu and coincident ṇal work

Apply 6.1.8 to original bhuj. Abhyāsa 7.4.60 retains its first
consonant and vowel; **8.4.54** gives copy bh→b, hence **bu**.
That deaspiration belongs only to the copy: the original root still
begins bh. Neither present vikaraṇa participates; no ām auxiliary
is introduced. The two lexical roots have the same written perfect
stem system but retain different source identities.

All A and the six weak P slots have apit liṭ kit under 1.2.5,
blocking guṇa: bubhuje, bubhujatuḥ, etc. P 3sg/2sg/1sg have u→o:
bubhoja/bubhojitha. Iṭ is present at P 2sg/1du/1pl and A
2sg/2pl/1du/1pl by 7.2.13/35; these are **18 positions/paths**.
Thal has no aniṭ alternative: the root is neither vowel-final nor
a-vowel-bearing in upadeśa under 7.2.61/62. Ire's i is ending-owned.
J before iṭ is not iṇ, so perfect bubhujidhve has dental dh only.

At each P 1sg, 7.1.91 gives two ṇit-work histories but one word
bubhoja: neither final-ik 7.2.115 nor a-upadhā 7.2.116 applies,
and light-u guṇa remains o. Consequently 45 perfect keys/readings
carry **47 paths**. This is inherited history policy, not an extra
surface-word alternative.

## H10. Sic aorists with the corrected jhal guard

Use cli→sic 3.1.43–44. Neither root is śal-final for ksa 3.1.45,
nor irit/ḷit or a selected gaṇa member for aṅ. Do not transport
yuj's irit option just because its final j and vowel resemble bhuj.
All 45 aorist keys have one sic history.

P sic is aniṭ and has **7.2.3 u→au**. Ending īṭ 7.3.96 occurs
only at 3sg/2sg, giving abhaukṣīt/abhaukṣīḥ. It is not root-facing
iṭ. The 3pl uses jus and yields abhaukṣuḥ. At 3du/2du/2pl,
**8.2.26 deletes sic-s between root j and ending t**, producing
abhauktām/abhauktam/abhaukta. There is no residual ṣ to retroflex t.

A aniṭ sic is kit under 1.2.11 and retains u. Rule 8.2.26 applies
at 3sg/2sg; 8.2.25 applies at 2pl: abhukta, abhukthāḥ,
abhugdhvam. Root g remains voiced before dh. Vowel-facing A dual
and plural endings retain sic; A 1sg uses ending i, never P ending
īṭ. Dental dh is compulsory in the selected domain.

Thus sic-lopa has **15 new positions/paths**: nine A and six P.
All laṅ/lṛṅ/luṅ requests have initial aṭ, totaling 135 positions.
Five profiles × ten labels × nine slots closes **450/450**, with
450 readings and 452 paths. Cumulative positions: **18,221+450=18,671**.
H04's six earlier replacements add no credit to that sum.
