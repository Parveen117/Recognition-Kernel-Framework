# PG963 · From grinding grain to making a flour animal

The same root enters two independent analyses. On the lexical reading *sañcūrṇana* the thing ground is a direct object. When the output of grinding supplies material for an effigy, it is the maker's verb and the name of the effigy that determine the outer agreement.

## What the case identifies

Mādhavīya 1053 on √piṣ distinguishes three examples. *Corasya pinaṣṭi* has a genitive on the injury reading construed as a residual relation under 2.3.56. With the object expressed directly, *coraṃ pinaṣṭi* takes accusative even with injury. *Dhānāḥ pinaṣṭi* also takes accusative, here for grain being pounded. Thus an animate object by itself is insufficient for the genitive. Kāśikā on 2.3.56 gives *caurasya* and *vṛṣalasya* with *pinaṣṭi*, and gives the grain counterexample. Its expressed nonresidual counterexample uses a different verb. Retain both thief nouns and the verb in each witness. The explicit *coraṃ pinaṣṭi* contrast is supplied by Mādhavīya. The finite verb in these examples belongs to the already constructed actor grid. The new syntax interface is the three-way case contrast for this root. The earlier rule, affix inventory, grain example, thief noun forms and present verb receive zero additional word credit. ## What the material modifies

Manu 5.37 supplies two masculine accusative singular objects of the optative *kuryāt*. Medhātithi's exposition supplies a third accusative singular with feminine head *pratikṛti*, a likeness. The table distinguishes the full words at pause from the source spelling before the next consonant.

In the verse the material stands immediately before *paśu* inside the compound. A sup ending appears only at the outer noun boundary. In the gloss the commentator explicitly places *piṣṭa-maya* inside the name of the likeness. The lexical material noun *piṣṭa* permits mayaṭ for its product under 4.3.146, underlying maya after removal of the final marker. The *kan* exception at 4.3.147 requires a conventional name of the specified kind. The descriptive gloss supplies no such condition. With the final feminine i-stem, accusative am yields *pratikṛtim* (6.1.107). In the two masculine u-stems the same operation yields *paśum*. Each word has final m at pause and anusvāra before the following consonant under 8.3.23. No mayaṭ is inserted into the verse's two literal words.

Medhātithi also offers an alternative in which flour is the material used to make a cake or other offering. There *piṣṭena* is instrumental. On his ghee interpretation ghee itself can occupy the animal's sacrificial place. Neither of these alternatives licenses silently replacing either verse token by the longer word in the third row. The independent *paśum* in the verse's negative clause is a control. It has a different role from either material compound.
