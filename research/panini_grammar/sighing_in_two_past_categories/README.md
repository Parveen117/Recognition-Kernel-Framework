# PG977 · Why Rāma’s sigh is an aorist

The last predicate of Bhaṭṭi 6.34 belongs to Rāma. Jayamaṅgalā on printed p. 111 glosses the breath as long and explains the short radical vowel by the restriction on vṛddhi. The print marks the end of the sic section immediately after this verse. The gloss uses niḥśvasitavān to explain the action. The verse has **ni + a + śvas**, with yaṇ at the ni/a boundary. The initial śv cluster belongs to the root. In particular the gloss licenses no inserted visarga on the verse’s preverb. PG975 owns the adjacent impersonal samabhāvi and the distribution of muhuḥ. ## Two derivations with the same singular output

Fix adādi śvas (śvasã prāṇane), simple kartari parasmaipada, one preverb ni, a breathing actor, no patient or derivational suffix. For the affirmative luṅ request, cli is replaced by sic. Its iṭ branch keeps the radical a short under 7.2.5. Apply the ending history for the active iṣ-aorist, including the singular apṛkta environments, then join ni to the augmented base. Kāśikā at 7.2.5 explicitly gives the unprefixed singular. The Hyderabad Mādhavīya entry gives the dual, second singular and first singular in its post-blessing series. Its electronic third singular in that series reads aśvasat. Record that disagreement at its own locus. The Kāśikā and the poem support -īt here. The Mādhavīya’s earlier imperfect series separately contains both -īt and -at. The origin of the post-blessing spelling is Open. [Hyderabad, entry 719](https://sanskrit.uohyd.ac.in/scl/dhaatupaatha/files/mA719.html) · [Kāśikā register](../anga_affix_abhyasa/SOURCE_EXCERPTS.json) · [PG217 short-grade ending schedule](../unadi_root_aorist_constructions/PARADIGMS.md)

For laṅ supply a non-today past context. Its initial aṭ is again on the base inside ni. The rudādi present system gives two apṛkta-ending policies in third and second singular. Under 7.3.98 the ending receives īṭ. Under 7.3.99 Gārgya and Gālava admit ending-owned aṭ. Both paths retain the independently required past augment after ni. Thus 3sg nyaśvasīt and 2sg nyaśvasīḥ each have a laṅ derivation as well as a luṅ derivation. In the third plural the contrast is directly visible in nyaśvasan versus nyaśvasiṣuḥ. Person and number refer to the breather throughout. The [written register](FORMS.tsv) uses citation spelling. Two of its 18 positions have direct poetic evidence for this preverb and category, and 16 are written extensions. The third and second singular each admit two imperfect readings, giving 20 readings across the two panels. Sandhi, multiple tokens, the unprefixed comparison, and the different subjects contribute no extra position. Direct literary attestations of the other cells, the transmission of the disputed third singular, other prefixes, accent and other categories remain Open.
