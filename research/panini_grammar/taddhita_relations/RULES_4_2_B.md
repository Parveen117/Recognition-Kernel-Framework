# PG35 — 4.2.67–145: place names and the śaiṣika selector

Author: Monty. 4 November 2026.

Use the conventions of [4.2 A](RULES_4_2_A.md) and [DOMAIN](DOMAIN.md).
`C` is the disjoint union of 67–70's **witnessed** relations, each requiring
the resulting place to bear the relevant name. It is not a Cartesian product
of every noun with four meanings. `S` is a supplied, admitted śeṣa relation
under 92, ordinarily one of the explicit relations in 4.3.25–133. Its case
comes from that relation. A lexical selector by itself never licenses a word.

The source-qualified labels Northern, Eastern, Vāhīka, Uśīnara, etc. are
historical grammatical classifications. This packet supplies no modern
geographical classifier. Likewise `vṛddha` is a grammatical designation with
its own evidence, not a guess from spelling alone.

## The ordered seventeen-way contract of 4.2.80

Membership is keyed by `(rule, gaṇa ordinal, literal source member)`. The two
कुमुदादि entries therefore cannot overwrite each other in a dictionary.
The first member below is a finite positive admission witness for every
clause. Further members require the same archived Kāśikā entry and any
attached member condition. Several memberships can produce several histories.

शर्करा illustrates why simple last-rule selection fails: the source admits
ठच् and कक् through 80, the restored अण् with optional लुप् through 83, and
ठक्/छ through 84. Those six labelled results must not be turned into twelve
by applying 83's लुप् to every suffix. The six source words are controls;
their complete accent/feminine/declension constructions are not counted here.
