# PG35 — Dependent semantic selection on the retained carrier

Author: Monty. 4 November 2026.

## N1. Carrier and extension

Continue PG34's cut field \(C_\Sigma\), derived iota and dagger. Add
events for semantic admission, scope changes, witnessed membership,
candidate suppression, explicit affix alternatives, addressed nominal
changes and typed transfer. Set

\[
B_{35}=C_\Sigma\langle z_e,z_e^*:e\in E_{\le35}\rangle,
\qquad
M_{35,\lambda}=(M_{34,\lambda}\otimes_{B_{34}}B_{35})
 \oplus\bigoplus_{h\in H_{35}}e_hB_{35}.
\]

Use finite words, finite support and finite typed blocks. New histories are
disjoint from the inherited corner. Sending the new event letters to zero
and retaining the old ones gives a retraction to \(B_{34}\); the induced
retraction on the extended old corner shows that this extension has not
identified two previously distinct old module vectors. Extend the inherited
pairing and use \(\langle x,y\rangle=\sum_hx_h^*y_h\) on new coordinates.
Coordinate-unit evaluation proves nondegeneracy there. Dagger reverses
event words; no Hilbert metric or physical clock is introduced.

A history includes the complete D2 input, its proof tokens, opened semantic
scope, exact gaṇa ordinal, original affix tree, each optional branch's
source, all target addresses and pending realization obligations. A final
letter observer is a later map, not the primitive carrier.

## N2. Admission before selection

For each gate g, let \(P_g^T,P_g^F,P_g^?\) be its coordinate cuts on
histories with a recorded decision. They are self-adjoint orthogonal cuts
with local sum I. A request missing a decisive membership or accent proof
lies in the ? cut, not the F cut. The selection map factors through the
semantic-admission and scope cuts before lexical affix cuts.

For the residual A route define its admission on the completed local
decision histories: the semantic gate is T, the scope is correct, and
every applicable suppressing exception is proved absent or explicitly
defeated. No ? decision is converted to a positive default. This gives
an evidence-monotone discipline: adding missing evidence may settle Open,
but erasing evidence cannot create a newly certified A path.

This is an internal property of the declared evidence relation. It does
not prove that an externally supplied semantic annotation is true.

## N3. Dependent branching proposition

**Proposition.** Selection histories are a dependent union over admitted
inputs and clauses, not a Cartesian product of base, meaning, suffix,
replacement and elision choices.

**Construction and proof.** For each admitted q let \(J(q)\) be precisely
the source-labelled branches surviving C2. Its branch j contains the
selected affix and that clause's associated operations. Form the disjoint
histories \((q,j,\mathsf{operations}(q,j))\), with no independent operation
bits. In 4.3.1–3, the single-referent replacement is defined on खञ्/अण्
fibres and absent on छ. In 4.2.83, optional लुप् is defined on the restored
अण् fibre and not on the other शर्करा affixes. In 4.3.94, the four base
names determine four paired suffix clauses, not sixteen pairs. These
dependent domains make the unwanted combinations ill-typed. □

The same word observer may identify several accepted histories (for
example when an accent is not displayed). Such observational equality
does not identify their original upadeśas or their future rule eligibility.

## N4. Scope-separation proposition

Let \(P_S\) and \(P_M\) project onto the current śeṣa and material
views. The 4.3.134 transition creates an M history retaining its past S
record, but its **current** candidate view is M. The current cuts satisfy
\(P_SP_M=0\); this is not deletion of past scope history.

**Proposition.** An S-only selector cannot be applied merely by persistence
of an earlier affix choice after the 134 transition.

**Proof.** Its source block requires \(P_S\). Composing that block after
the transition's M-target cut gives zero. A specific material rule, or a
typed transfer such as 156, can authorize its own operation, but must carry
that separate witness. Thus 124's हल→ठक् belonging history does not prove
a ठक् material history. The same proof distinguishes 135's restricted
PartOf gate from the broader ModificationOf gate. □

## N5. Owned deletion and transfer

An elision event is labelled by `(rule, history, affix-token address,
kind, query view)`. At 4.3.168 the target is the inner nominal छ/यत्;
the newly selected outer material affix is another token. At 4.2.64 and
4.3.107 the two लुक् events on a Katha history likewise have different
addresses. A map that retains only a final string cannot reconstruct these
addresses in general. Nor can it recover whether a जम्बू fruit's absent
suffix underwent लुक् or लुप् and which agreement obligations survive.

For a transfer t define a typed map only on the supplied source-selection
witnesses. It copies the permitted base/affix/operation fields, retains
their provenance, and attaches the destination semantic witness. The
source and destination proof requirements remain separately readable.
For 100 the domain is a ruler/country pair with a proved plural-name
match; the output includes the country base treatment even when the
requested ruler relation is singular or dual. For 156 the input is a
completed bought-with witness, never an unresolved placeholder.

Consequently transfer is not a global equivalence of source and target
meanings. It cannot convert an arbitrary ownership statement into a
material derivation or infer ancestry from residence.

## N6. Zero-reading theorem at the selection layer

For each legal typed event \(e:h\to h'\), use
\(U_e(e_ha)=e_{h'}(1+z_e)a\). Finite branch sums use distinct labelled
target histories. Let \(\epsilon:B_{35}\to C_\Sigma\) take the empty-word
coefficient; let \(\pi_0\) retain every history coordinate while applying
\(\epsilon\) to its coefficient. The empty-word coefficient of a product
is the product of the empty-word coefficients because degrees are
nonnegative. Thus \(\epsilon\) is a unital dagger homomorphism.

Write \(\mathsf{Select}_{35,\lambda}\) for C1's finite admitted construction
on a fixed finite input/branch family. Then

\[
\pi_{0,\mathrm{out}}\mathsf{Select}_{35,\lambda}
 =\mathsf{Select}_{35,0}\pi_{0,\mathrm{in}},
\qquad
\epsilon\langle x,y\rangle_{B_{35}}
 =\langle\pi_0x,\pi_0y\rangle_{C_\Sigma}.
\]

**Proof.** Semantic, lexical, scope and precedence cuts depend on retained
history and are unchanged by \(\pi_0\). Every labelled event factor has
\(\epsilon(1+z_e)=1\). The homomorphism preserves finite composition and
branch sums, while dagger compatibility gives the pairing identity.
On unit-start legal paths, each labelled selection history therefore
recovers its C1 coefficient-one path, including its original affix and
deletion target. Open inputs remain Open. Arbitrary signed coefficients
can cancel; no support-preservation theorem for all signed inputs follows. □

The recovered object is the written **selection relation**, not 313
complete paradigms or a new Sanskrit execution result. This construction
adds no physical quantum-gate interpretation, general factual-truth
guarantee or application performance claim.
