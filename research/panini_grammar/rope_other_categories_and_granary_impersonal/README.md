# PG961 · The remaining third-person forms for the rope and the impersonal granary

Kāśikā at 3.1.87 expressly enumerates four consequences of treating this subject like the original object. They are yaK, A endings, actual ciṇ and ciṇ-like operations where licensed. It exemplifies the aorist with wood splitting of itself, *abhedi kāṣṭhaṃ svayam eva*. In the rope example of PG960, √chid retains its lexical identity and the represented agent is the rope that gives way. This licenses the remaining third-person requests on the same role grade. The ordinary future, agent-future and blessing do not gain an optional iṭ here. The 6.4.62 root guard fails on consonant-final √chid. The plural and dual are semantic controls on the subject, while singular ciṇ is a control on the original ending. The eighteen entries are grammatical extensions. The rope witness explicitly exemplifies only present singular. PG956 owns the homophonous passive entries and its resolution of the imperfect/aorist augmented contact. PG960 owns the twelve third-person entries in the four present-system categories. Together the rope panels cover 30 third-person positions, ten per number. Discourse with a first- or second-person speaker in this role and evidence for actual usage beyond the present example remain Open. **Why the granary can be instrumental.** Kāśikā explicitly preserves what the original subject itself licenses under the *-vat* comparison, giving *bhidyate kusūlena* and referring to the impersonal la-affix on an intransitive. In the corresponding personal construction the granary bears nominative case. When the event itself is expressed, the granary takes the instrumental of the unexpressed agent and the finite is impersonal 3sg A. The grammatical subject role survives the restricted object-like transfer. This explains why agreement is no longer a test for counting granaries in the impersonal clause. The original unprefixed rudhādi root is √bhid, splitting, 07.0002. Preserve PG355–360's passive grid and the earlier personal wood example. Only the specifically impersonal present singular is credited here.

Padamañjarī presents a rival explanation. Even without a visible conscious splitter one might supply an external cause, or attribute the action to the object. For the instrumental example it explicitly tests supplying *ātmā* as a silent patient. It then asks how that proposal explains *bhettavyaṃ kusūlena* and *īṣadbhedaṃ kusūlena*. Their neuter forms provide an action reading beyond that particular masculine omitted-patient explanation. The latter predicate expresses ease. They are two cited nonfinite tests. This stage assigns neither a new nominal case contrast nor another finite grid to them. The commentary gives reasons for retaining 3.1.87 and its restricted transfer. The Cāndra example of a nominative granary in PG960 stays on its own school grade.
