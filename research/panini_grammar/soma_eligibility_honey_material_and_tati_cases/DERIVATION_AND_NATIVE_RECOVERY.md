# PG747 · From the support construction to the recited case

## Two kinds of Soma and the honey drops

At 4.4.137 the object of the eligibility predicate is soma in the accusative. The derivative designates the person entitled to it. In KS 5.2 the relative plural agrees with the Brāhmaṇas, and the following exclusion of an ineligible person's share confirms the direction of entitlement. Select plain **ya** from this rule. Final-a deletion in the base (6.4.148) gives som-ya. Its plural gives somyāḥ, observed as somyās before t. In 4.4.138 the same base and token type are carried forward with a new semantic test. The commentary groups origin, modification, constituent and predominant material under mayaṭ, with their respective support cases. Only the material example licenses the present primary case. In RV 8.24.13 pibāti governs madhu, with neuter accusative somyam. The Pada has final m. Sāyaṇa explicitly identifies its composition, and the verb and head support this case even though the displayed Vedaweb analysis labels the adjective NOM. The N singular is syncretic. The swara of the citation is somyám. A hypothetical yat token from the preceding default would lose the grammatical accent distinction specified by Kāśikā at 137.

At 139 the soma and its explicit plain ya both expire. Madhu supplies the material, and the chapter’s yat furnishes the ending, with t as it (1.3.3, 1.3.9). Guṇa before this taddhita (6.4.146) and the v-final substitution before suffixal y (6.1.79) give madhu → madho → madhav-ya. Masculine accusative plural agrees with stokān at PS 1.88.2. In PG745 the exact TS madhavyaḥ has possessive month/body history and a different case. The base and affix spelling are insufficient to choose between these histories.

## Wealth, syllables and stars

At 140 the same grade/glide steps give vasav-ya. The new samūha relation and the retained mayaṭ sense have distinct support frames. Two supplementary clauses add own-sense derivation for vasu and for chandas already denoting a collection of syllables. The latter gives chandas-ya. The checked MS passage gives o śrāvaya (4), astu śrauṣaṭ (4), yaja (2), ye yajāmahe (5) and vaṣaṭ (2). Its ensuing identification of Prajāpati totals seventeen. The exact quoted adjective remains a grammatical citation with no MS word-position credit.

The AV instrumental in a request to fill two hands has the a-stem plural -aiḥ; RV 4.55.8 has the genitive -asya with īśe. Kāśikā interprets both as simple wealth terms and locates the AV under its own numbering. Sāyaṇa explains groups of riches in both texts and explicitly invokes 140 in the AV. Each reading has a positive source. The distinct material interpretation survives as a grammatical branch. Neither of these two contexts independently establishes it.

Rule 141 ends the collection frame. Gha has the substitution iya under 7.1.2. Final-a treatment gives nakṣatr-iya. The two recipients at VS 22.28 have the same dative-plural ending -ebhyaḥ on simple and derived bases. The first occurrence is a comparator, while the second carries the suffix event. Uvaṭa and Mahīdhara discuss time-deities collectively. That ritual grouping by itself does not select a grammatical collection suffix.

## Two bases, two relations and the short i-ending

Rule 142 addresses sarva and deva separately. Attach tātil, delete its terminal l by 1.3.3/9, preserve l in the accent query (6.1.193). Unlike an affix beginning in y or a vowel, the t-initial body retains the base-final vowel. Both witnessed accusatives have -tim. The preceding vowel carries the accent in sarvátātim and devátātim. In RV 3.19.2 Sāyaṇa explicitly gives the affix and accent treatment. His semantic gloss uses plural devān. RV 10.36.14 asks Savitṛ for everything desired.

Rule 143 changes both base set and predicate. With śiva, śam or ariṣṭa the genitive is construed with the maker noun kara; the corresponding verbal paraphrase has an accusative object of kṛ. The kṛ predicate explains the semantic role, and contributes zero finite forms. Rule 144 keeps the base roster and suffix and substitutes the state relation. At the śam + tāti joint, the pada division preserves m; nasal notation at the dental contact varies across presentations. PS 5.36.1–8 supplies śivatātir before astu, from nominative śivatātiḥ. The prayer supports well-being as the wished-for state. Kāśikā indexes this same passage under both relations. In PS 5.18.2 the two different bases take -bhiḥ, with final r on the first in connected speech. Interpreting what is brought as welfare gives a state reading; the verse does not itself name an affix or determine the gender from -bhiḥ.

In RV 8.18.7 the Pada ends śam-tāti in short i. Sāyaṇa expressly takes it as the peace-bringing modifier of neuter mayas, and the annotation gives N ACCsg. The modern notes discuss a neuter noun and an indeclinable patterned on śam. Only the nominal analysis has a case coordinate. RV 1.112.20 has śam-tātī with preserved final ī, predicated of the Aśvins, whom Sāyaṇa calls two makers. In RV 10.60.7–9 -tātaye is a dative singular in a purpose series alongside jīvātave and mṛtyave. Sāyaṇa at verse 8 glosses preservation and calls the affix own-sense. Kāśikā supplies both general constructions; Vasu cites the refrain in the maker discussion. A grammatical nominative lemma at either citation gives no additional primary reading. Repeated refrains, spellings at a phonetic boundary and competing interpretations of one written ending receive one case credit each per panel row.

## Typed zero specialization

Use PG14 N1–N5 on its cut field CΣ, free ordered residue *-algebra B, finite-support history modules Mλ(X) and pairing ⟨eₕa,eₖb⟩ = δₕₖa*b. X is the disjoint union of typed input frames, selected suffix histories, conditioned case histories and readouts. An input carries register, lexical owner, role, source policy and support case. Each suffix selection appends its original teaching form; realization adds exposed body, retained marker, verse/edition, intended case and ending. A surface-only alternative such as the indeclinable has an observation target, separate from the nominal count. Unknown evidence leaves an open record rather than an edge to a credited case.

On each admitted edge e from h to h′, Tₑ(eₕa) = eₕ′(1+zₑ)a. Its adjoint on that extended history restores h with coefficient (1+zₑ*)b. On the complementary source grades the selection is zero. On an admitted edge ε((1+zₑ)a)=ε(a); on the complement both maps vanish. For each typed selector D, realization R and case readout W this gives π₀,out WλRλDλ = W₀R₀D₀ π₀,in by composition. The pairing specializes coefficientwise and every admitted zero history has a constant-coefficient input and a corresponding lifted path. The two wealth interpretations still end in distinct history summands, even when the census forgets that distinction. The absent MS case has no new path after specialization.
