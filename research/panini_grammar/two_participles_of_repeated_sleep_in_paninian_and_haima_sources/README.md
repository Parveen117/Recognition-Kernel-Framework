# PG858 · Two participles of repeated sleep in Pāṇinian and Haima sources

The Siddhāntakaumudī at Aṣṭādhyāyī 1.2.19 pairs the simple-root participles śayitaḥ and śayitavān and then gives śeśyitaḥ and śeśyitavān for the intensive with yaṅ deleted. The second pair already appears in PG46's pinned rule excerpts. This stage accounts for the two forms and their agreement, and locates a parallel explanation in a different grammatical tradition.

### Which final vowel reaches iṭ

Fix unprefixed śīṅ svapne, repeated sleeping, yaṅ followed by luk, an intransitive completed event and its agent. The two niṣṭhā requests are separate (1.1.26, 3.2.102). Kta can denote the agent of an intransitive verb under 3.4.72. Ktavatu denotes the agent under 3.4.67. The next table admits the same participant by two derivations and requires its grammatical gender, external case and number as inputs. It does not assign a patient to sleeping.

At the boundary the pieces are śeśy + i + ta and śeśy + i + tavat. Ktavatu contributes its own tavat and its ugit history for subsequent agreement. It is not constructed by adding matup to the other participle. A present-time kta with another semantic condition, or an expressed object after a preverb, requires a separate input. The digitized Bālamanoramā at 1.2.19 displays initial dental s in its quoted *seśyitavān* and explains the absence of guṇa. Record this spelling at that source. Neither the Kāśikā rule nor the visually checked agreeing sources license an optional switch from ś to s here.

**Siddhahema 4.3.27.** The left column of the printed Madhyamavṛtti with the Vacūri on Jain Quantum host page 169 distinguishes the text layers.
