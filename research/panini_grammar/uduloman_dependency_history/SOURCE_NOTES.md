## S2. The gate changes because the syllabic position changes

Kāśikā 1.1.64 defines टि from the last vowel through the end of its
addressed host. Kāśikā 6.4.144 therefore deletes **अन्** from the
न्-final भ host before the admitted इञ्. The surviving म् joins the
suffixal इ, giving the source's औडुलोमि stem.

Before this event the graded proposed इञ् formation has vowels
**औ उ ओ अ इ**. Its penultimate syllable has short अ and no following
consonant cluster. After deletion the sequence is **औ उ ओ इ**: the
already long ओ now occupies the penultimate position. The basic weight
distinctions are supported by 1.4.10/11/12. Nothing in this event converts
अ to ओ or makes the first syllable's heaviness sufficient.

Nyāsa's decisive ordering reads **तत्र प्रागेव ष्यङ इञि तस्यैव टिलोपः**;
Padamañjarī gives **तस्मादिञ्येव टिलोपः, ततो गुरूपोतमत्वम्**. Their
sequence is the positive local derivational evidence. The initially false
weight gate blocks premature ष्यङ्; a later printed bad result does not
make that proposal an admitted grammar path.

## S3. Preservation is contextual, not a blanket न्-retention rule

The initial following suffix is इञ्. Kāśikā 6.4.167 conditions its
अन् preservation on अण्, and 6.4.168 conditions its preservation on
य-initial तद्धित outside भाव/कर्म. Neither is licensed merely because
the main host ends in अन्. Nyāsa 6.4.168 identifies the positive
सामन्य/वेमन्य witnesses' यत् prescription; those are different admitted
contexts, not counterexamples to the present initial इञ् deletion.

Padamañjarī's unwanted औडुलोमन्या arises when a proposed early य
environment is allowed to preserve the undeleted ending. The local answer
rejects that earlier admission: the necessary heavy penultimate did not yet
exist. It does not announce that preservation rules are generally weaker
than deletion rules.

## S4. Added suffix and replaced suffix keep different evidence

Nyāsa/Padamañjarī discuss an added ष्यङ् separated from the base by
इञ्. Their argument preserves the intervention even after the इ is
deleted. Nyāsa expressly cites **1.1.57 अचः परस्मिन् पूर्वविधौ**;
Kāśikā's full field supplies the general clause's vowel-replacement,
following-cause and preceding-operation conditions.

That deleted इ is not active pronunciation and is not the original base's
deleted अन्. The comparison must retain the source-qualified consumer
view, event kind and cause. The actual added-affix word path still performs
the enabling टि-lopa before the heavy-penultimate check. Its later
intervention witness is not retroactively made the cause of that event.

The replacement route instead changes the original इञ् owner. A witness
for deletion of a separate intervening इ cannot be imported from the other
policy. Both routes retain the standard ष्यङ्/चाप् presentation used for
the bounded stem comparison; the commentary's alternate marker-design and
accent debate are not separately reconstructed.

## S5. Visibility and source defects remain explicit

Kāśikā 6.4.22 distinguishes the admitted same-conditioning domain from
different-conditioning cases. It supports retaining the consumer and cause
of an earlier-view query, not universal access to every historical spelling.
The present source's explicit आनुपूर्व्य establishes the local word
construction. It is not a proof of all late-preservation schedules, universal
non-reapplication or global confluence.

The Nyāsa 4.1.78 field repeats part of the इ-lopa/1.1.57 passage and
contains visibly damaged wording. Those repetitions and spellings remain
literal. The argument used here is its explicit intervention clause and
its equally explicit ordering answer, also supplied by Padamañjarī.

The Padamañjarī 6.4.168 key is empty in the pinned file. It contributes
no independent commentary and no nonempty-field credit. Nyāsa and
Padamañjarī 6.4.144 were read for the surrounding preservation exceptions;
their additional examples do not replace the direct 4.1.78 word-specific
ordering witness.

Full accent, declined paradigms, general compound/semantic admission and
