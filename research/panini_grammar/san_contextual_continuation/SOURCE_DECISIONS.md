## H01. Lexical selection and the two events of desire

Select jñā **1090043**, śru **1011092**, smṛ **1010919** and dṛś
**1011143**. All four selected underlying roots are P and aniṭ in the
lexical source. Jñā is the ninth-class avabodhana root. Its ghaṭādi
placeholder 1010923 and curādi 1100258 are not additional roots in this
block. The selected smṛ is the ādhyāna entry cited as 807 by Nyāsa at
1.3.57; the related cintā entry 1011082 is not counted simultaneously,
and the fifth-class variant 1050015 is outside this selection. Preserve
the source's smṛ mit marker and dṛś irit teaching without inventing
new finite alternatives from them. PG79's lexical distinction is reused.

Under 3.1.7 the desired action is the object of desire and the desirer is
also the actor of that desired action. The inner action and outer desire
event therefore remain distinct, with an explicit actor-identity witness.
Choose the san member of the rule's optional san/phrase construction.
The alternative paraphrase receives no additional word-position credit.
An instrumental action rather than an object of desire, different actors,
absence of desire, and another icchā-san on an icchā-san formation are
not admitted. The commentary's āśaṅkā extension is outside this packet.

San attaches to the root; prefixes retain their own ordered relation.
Kāśikā's pra example explicitly prevents treating the entire prefixed
string as the reduplication recipient. Rule 3.1.32 gives the completed
san formation its own dhātu status. The outer tense/mood certificate
qualifies the desire event. It does not by itself date the desired action.

śuśrūṣate, susmūrṣate and didṛkṣate. The rule supplies A in the
selected desire domains even where earlier root-specific A conditions
are absent. This is not a general A tag for the four underived roots.

## H03. Anu+jñā blocks one A cause, not all A causes

Rule 1.3.58 negates the immediately preceding 1.3.57 entitlement.
Kāśikā explicitly limits the resulting prohibition to the transitive
case; Kaumudī and Bhāṣya preserve the independent intransitive case.

| Profile | Inner frame | Admission and retained cause |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ANU_JNA_TRANSITIVE_P | Transitive anu+jñā desire, agent ⊗ object, no other certified A cause | 1.3.58 cancels the 1.3.57 entitlement; residual P by 1.3.78 |
| ANU_JNA_ENGAGEMENT_A | Knowledge-guided engagement by a supplied means, no karman; agent ⊗ instrument | 1.3.45 survives through the same-cause continuation 1.3.62 |

The source gives putram anujijñāsati against sarpiṣo'nujijñāsate and
auṣadhasyānujijñāsate. PG79 supplies the intransitive engagement and
2.3.51 genitive-instrument account; nominal inflection is not generated
again here. The absence of karman is a property of the inner action,
not a denial that that action is the object of the outer desire.
Neither a prefix-only prohibition nor a generic copy of a bare A flag
implements this distinction. A conflicting or missing cause witness is HOLD.

## H04. Prati/āṅ must be actual upasargas

Rule 1.3.59 selects **PRATI_SHRU_P** and **ANG_SHRU_P**, with actual
prati/āṅ in the verbal prefix relation of 1.4.58–59 and no surviving
independent A cause. Residual P follows 1.3.78. The āṅ marker is removed;
the surface prefix is ā and its original identity remains in history.

Kāśikā expressly contrasts *devadattaṃ prati śuśrūṣate*. Here prati
is karmapravacanīya in its nominal relation, not the upasarga that would
trigger 1.3.59. This is a zero-credit contextual control on the already
counted SHRU_HEAR_A profile, not a ninth table. Missing category evidence
is HOLD; string matching on prati is insufficient.

## H05. Same-cause continuation and two independent iṭ interfaces

Kāśikā 1.3.62 requires the same active nimitta that licensed the inner
root. Its śad/mṛ counterexamples show why a bare A flag is insufficient:
an outer śit affix beyond san is not the inner root's required adjacent
śit environment. Those examples receive no PG87 table credit. The
positive use here is 1.3.45 → 1.3.62 in H03, with that cause retained.

At **root → san**, all selected roots are aniṭ under 7.2.10. San is
kit for śru/smṛ under 1.2.9 and for dṛś under 1.2.10; 7.2.11 also
supports the selected ik-final prohibition. Jñā is not assigned an
invented ik-based kit tag. Smṛ's later ṝ from 6.4.16 does not create
the original-upadeśa ṝ entitlement to optional iṭ in 7.2.41. Kāśikā
there explicitly rejects that inference for comparable derived ṝ.

At **completed san-dhātu → sya/tāsi**, 7.2.35 supplies required short
iṭ to the new polysyllabic derived root. The inner aniṭ decision is
retained at its own interface. The outer affix addresses the derived
root's real final a, deleted by 6.4.48; the former san-n marker does
not make it n-final at this later affix-prescription stage. Bālamanoramā
6.4.48 expressly explains this time-of-prescription distinction with
chikīrṣitam. Do not substitute ṇic-lopa 6.4.51 or ya-lopa 6.4.49.

This packet has seven labels per profile. Āśīrliṅ, liṭ and luṅ need
their own outer-affix, ending and option decisions; their 216 requests
are enumerated without predicted readings. Other san roots, source
extensions, voices, derived formations and Vedic uses remain outside.
