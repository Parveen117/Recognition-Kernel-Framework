# PG789 · Derivation and native recovery

## Parts and whole

At 5.2.42 the source numeral is not merely adjacent to several objects. It counts the parts belonging to one whole. The inherited `tad asya` relation supplies the bearer, and `tayap` denotes that whole. Thus `pañca + tayap` gives `pañcataya`, not five independent entities. The Kāśikā directly supplies `pañcatayam`, `daśatayam`, `catuṣṭayam` and feminine `catuṣṭayī`; its 4.1.15 discussion directly supplies `pañcatayī` and `daśatayī`. The feminine long ī belongs to the `tayap` history and is not a gender inferred from the counted members.

At 5.2.43, two and three first enter the same parts-to-whole construction. The optional operation is replacement of the selected `taya`, not selection of a second unrelated suffix. Consequently `dvitaya/dvaya` and `tritaya/traya` are accepted alternatives with different surface histories. Sthānivad treatment retains the `tayap`-conditioned feminine and optional pronominal designation: `dvayī/trayī`, and nominative plural `dvaye/dvayāḥ`, `traye/trayāḥ`. These alternatives are related; they are not a Cartesian licence to mix suffix histories within one derivation.

At 5.2.44 `ubha` admits the same whole relation whether its numerical status is obtained directly or by the commentary's rule division. The following `ayac` replacement is obligatory. No retained `ubhataya` branch is constructed. `ubhayaḥ`, `ubhayam` and the Taittirīya `ubhaye` are three case/number addresses of this one derived stem. The Vedic phrase names the two classes, gods and humans, as the constituents; it does not turn the affix into a dual ending.

## Surplus relation

At 5.2.45 a numeral with material before final `daśan` receives `ḍa` when it states what is extra in a hundred, thousand or their named combination. Marker deletion and the prescribed loss of final `an` yield the a-stem readout. `ekādaśam` with `śatam` and with `sahasram` therefore has two contextual positions. `dvādaśam` supplies the corresponding two controls. `ekādaśam kārṣāpaṇaśatam` passes the same-unit condition. Eleven māṣas over a hundred kārṣāpaṇas fails it. Bare ten, five over a hundred and eleven over thirty fail separately; none is reclassified as an exclusion from Sanskrit.

The `śatasahasram` example retains two semantic readings: a thousand of hundreds with eleven hundreds extra, or a hundred of thousands with eleven thousands extra. One surface position does not erase the independently owned unit relation.

## Accent

The `c` marker of optional `ayac` records an accent different from retained `tayap`; the commentary invokes 6.1.163 for the ordinary affix-final result. Rule 5.2.44 then explicitly assigns udātta to the initial of the obligatory `ayac` after `ubha`. The Taittirīya token is retained as the direct Vedic record. These records do not fabricate a complete recitational paradigm.

## Recovery on the full carrier

Reuse PG14's full operator/history carrier `CΣ`, its derived involution, and `B=CΣ⟨zₑ,zₑ*⟩`. Let the input grades be numeral/base and member relation `I`, semantic admission `Q`, affix and replacement history `H`, nominal case/number `W`, accent `A`, and source policy `S`. Each matching written edge acts as

`Tₑ(eₕb)=eₕ′(1+zₑ)b`,

with the corresponding adjoint edge. The projections `πI, πQ, πH, πW, πA, πS` retain type and ordered history while applying the augmentation `ε(zₑ)=0`. Hence

`πout Tₑ = Tₑ,0 πin`

for `Q←I`, `H←Q`, `W←H`, `A←H` and `S←W`; composition and the orthogonal pairing commute with specialization.

This proves typed zero-specialization rather than mere deletion. The zero image of `dvaya` still records replacement of an admitted `tayap`, so it cannot merge with retained `dvitaya`. Obligatory `ubhaya` cannot acquire a zero-incidence `ubhataya` branch. The two `śatasahasram` readings remain orthogonal by their unit histories even though their word projection agrees. Mixed māṣa/kārṣāpaṇa input has zero incidence on both the decorated and specialized surplus carriers. Frozen PG37 contracts and PG14 operator structure are reused, not rewritten.
