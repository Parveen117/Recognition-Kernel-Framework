# PG231 · A ten-pāda kīnāśa reading and the two kliś entries

Monty · 12 November 2026

Kṣīrasvāmin gives a further reading for **kīnāśa**. His divādi *kliś* entry cites a ten-pāda variant with **kal** and a prescribed long ī. The next citation, with **al**, derives the different word *keśa*. The five-pāda 5.56 still has **kan**. The ten-pāda citation supplies neither an additional function for the five-pāda *k* nor a Yama gloss at its own location.

| Formation | Written choices | Why the two roots converge |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *ktvā*, prior action | kliṣṭvā / kliśitvā | 7.2.50 licenses both choices for both roots. Even with *iṭ*, 1.2.7 preserves *kit* and the short radical *i*. |
| *kta* and *ktavatu* | kliṣṭaḥ / kliśitaḥ, kliṣṭavān / kliśitavān | Both *niṣṭhā* suffixes participate. The three genders have their own case forms. |
| *vuñ*, habitual agent | kleśakaḥ, kleśikā, kleśakam | The Kāśikā on 3.2.146 explicitly includes both roots. The ordinary strengthened vowel is *e*. |

The usual starting conditions differ. For ū-marked class nine, optional *iṭ* before *ktvā* was independently available, while *niṣṭhā* would encounter a prohibition. For class four, ordinary *iṭ* would be obligatory. The shared rule explains why the actual word pairs coincide.

The nine gendered tables give **216 nominal positions and 372 readings**. Both archived root entries agree on their separately recorded *ktvā* alternatives. A second table supplies the masculine, feminine and neuter nominative singulars for all three suffixes. The two uninflected *ktvā* words have their own record.
