## H02. Karman, intransitivity and residual pada

Kāśikā 1.3.34 expressly makes karman a kāraka, not a synonym for activity. **Kroṣṭā vikurute svarān** supplies a sound object; **vikaroti payaḥ** is the negative comparison. Siddhāntakaumudī's **cittaṃ vikaroti kāmaḥ** likewise does not pass the sound-object guard. A noisy subject or the mere occurrence of a word for sound is insufficient. The object type must be established for this verbal event.

Rule 1.3.35 separately admits intransitive vi+kṛ. Kāśikā's **vikurvate saindhavāḥ** and **odanasya pūrṇāś chātrā vikurvate** describe their respective intransitive activities; the satiety expression does not turn odana into this verb's karman. Siddhāntakaumudī's “undergo alteration” explanation and Bālamanoramā's dhātvarthopasaṅgraha remark allow an internally included meaning without an external karman. Therefore certified intransitivity and a missing overt object are different premises. The latter is HOLD until meaning/role evidence is supplied.

The intransitive admission is recorded with **zero new finite-position credit**. It may use the same A morphology under its own semantic and temporal certificates, but it is not another 90-word table here. A and an agent controller do not imply passive voice. Both counted profiles keep their object roles; the intransitive domain has no external object factor.

Rules 1.3.33/34 explicitly cover the principal-result-elsewhere case. They defeat residual P under 1.3.78 in this domain; 1.3.72 is not a requirement that the principal result accrue to the agent. Conversely, failure of one contextual guard alone does not prove P: all other applicable A grounds must also be excluded. An unknown semantic guard remains HOLD. The seven special senses of 1.3.32 are excluded from these exact profiles, and its pratiyatna-linked 2.3.53/6.1.139 genitive/suṭ prescriptions are not transported here.

## H04. Two source policies for kṛ A sic-luk

Kāśikā 3.1.79 says the separate mention of kṛ, already in tanādi, restricts other tanādi work; it specifically excludes the optional sic-luk of 2.4.79 and gives **akṛta, akṛthāḥ**. Its pinned field prints **सिचो लुङ्** at that point. The adopted operation is **luk**, identified by 2.4.79 and the explicit **लुग्** quotation in Padamañjarī, not another selection of the luṅ lakāra. The damaged spelling stays unchanged in the archive.

Bālamanoramā 2.4.79 expressly describes sic-luk producing **akṛta**, then explains why the no-luk route does not leave audible s. Siddhāntakaumudī 6.4.109 supplies the latter route through 8.2.27. Padamañjarī 3.1.79 challenges Kāśikā's restriction and argues for rejecting the supposedly necessary separate mention of kṛ. Siddhāntakaumudī/Bālamanoramā at 3.1.79 instead discuss non-universality of gaṇa effects. These positions cannot all be compressed into one uncontested kṛ-specific ban.

| Policy | Primary basis | 3sg/2sg | Other seven slots |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| K | Kāśikā 3.1.79 | Sic retained, then 8.2.27 s-lopa | Ordinary sic history |
| B | Bālamanoramā 2.4.79 with the SK no-luk explanation | Optional complete sic-luk or ordinary sic followed by 8.2.27 | Ordinary sic history |

Rule 2.4.79 is restricted to the original **A ta/thās** singulars. Kāśikā excludes P ta by its association with thās. It does not permit deletion of all sic cells or A dhvam. The two policies yield the same nine A words; H08 preserves their different root/affix histories. K/B are source-labelled explanatory alternatives, not a claim that each commentator accepts both policies.

Other literal cautions: Nyāsa 1.3.33 opens with **तमधितक्रे** in the pinned electronic field. Adopt **adhicakre** from Kāśikā and the independently derived liṭ constructor; do not repair the archive silently. The archive also retains damaged prose in Padamañjarī's sense discussion. No newly collated scan or universal source consensus is claimed.
