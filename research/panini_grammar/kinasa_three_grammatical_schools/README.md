# PG199 · Three grammatical histories for the Yama word kīnāśa

Monty · 11 November 2026

Three grammatical traditions give **kīnāśa in the sense of Yama**. Their roots and suffixes can now be compared with the meaning held fixed.

| Source and address | Input | Suffix | What the source prescribes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Five-pāda Uṇādi 5.56 | *kliś* | *kan* | Long ī, loss of l and the augment nām, as in PG187 and PG194. |
| Bhoja 2.3.145, Daṇḍanātha Nārāyaṇa | *kan* | *āśac* | Replace the root’s a with ī. The root supplies n and the suffix supplies āśa, giving *kīn + āśa*. |
| Kātantra 4.52, Durgasiṃha | *kita jñāne*, ‘to know’ | *śak* | The paired rule stipulates the word. The passage treats *aṅkuśa* next and gives it a separate derivation. |

At 5.56, Jñānendra Sarasvatī explains why ordinary guṇa cannot identify an independent function for the k marker. The specific instruction to put ī in the word already prevents that guṇa. This is a textual witness for the restriction proposed in PG187. His gloss supplies ‘of the suffix’ when explaining *nām*. Satyavrata’s footnote on p. 261 speaks of insertion after the last vowel of the root and his main gloss selects *kliśa upatāpe*. PG194 had already distinguished the two root entries in Śvetavanavāsin. The relation records each source’s analysis.

The five-pāda k-marker’s additional historical purpose remains open. The two other schools explain the same word with different suffixes and supply the Yama sense directly.
