# PG235 · The ten-pāda miser, the hair word and the explicit judgment on k

Monty · 12 November 2026

The 1943 Daśapādī edition gives **kīnāśaḥ** at **9.1** and glosses it *kadaryo janaḥ*, a miserly person. The gloss assigns the **agent** role and allows either divādi *kliś* or kryādi *kliśū*. This supplies a source-local meaning alongside the Vedic ploughman and the Yama reading already documented. Monier-Williams independently records all three.

| Ten-pāda entry | Printed main text, 1943 | Kṣīrasvāmin’s cited reading | Word and meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 9.1, compared to five-pāda 5.63 in this edition | *kan* | *kal*, literal citation ‘99 ṅapāṭhaḥ’ | *kīnāśa*, miser in the 1943 commentary |
| 9.2, compared to five-pāda 5.35 | *an* | *al*, literal citation ‘92’ | *keśa*, hair, explained under both agent and instrument |

At 9.1, editorial footnote 2 rejects the claim that the marker *k* is needed for a *kit* operation here. The editor cites Ujjvaladatta’s statement that its effect is unobserved. This narrows the evidence for the primary five-pāda 5.56: the explicit ī, loss of l and *nām* are documented, and the available sources still identify **no additional independent effect for k**.

The two head-specific paradigms add **48 nominal positions and 50 readings**. For example, the instrumental singulars are *kīnāśena* and *keśena*. The instrumental case of *keśa* is independently available whether the noun itself received an agent or an instrument explanation.
