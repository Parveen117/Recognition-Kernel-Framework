## H02. Three distinct kit and iṭ interfaces

Āśīrliṅ is ārdhadhātuka by 3.4.116. In **P**, 3.4.103–104 give yāsuṭ
and prescribe **kit to the liṅ ending**, replacing the ordinary ṅit status.
The resulting y-initial boundary is outside val, so 7.2.35 supplies no iṭ.
Vad is yajādi: 6.1.15 consequently changes root v to u, and 6.1.108 gives
the former member at u|a. The root interface is **ud**, as the source's
udyāt explicitly witnesses. Kit is essential; a generic “weak mood” flag
cannot derive this contrast with the ordinary vidhiliṅ of PG81.
The resulting root-u also retains the kit-conditioned guṇa prohibition of
1.1.5; it is not strengthened to o merely because it is now a short ik upadhā.

In **A āśīrliṅ**, sīyuṭ begins with val s and 7.2.35 gives iṭ to the
udātta seṭ root. P's 3.4.104 kit is unavailable; 1.2.11–13 do not provide
kit for this a-upadhā root, and the actual iṭ boundary also fails their
jhal-initial guard. Thus **vad|iṭ|sīyuṭ**, with no saṃprasāraṇa. There is
no kram-only 7.2.36 restriction or bhāva/karma ciṇvat option in this kartari
domain. All **45 A blessing positions** have root-facing iṭ; the six P
positions do not.

For **liṭ**, 3.4.115 gives ārdhadhātuka tiṅ. Vad is not saṃyogānta.
All the counted A endings and the six counted P dual/plural endings are
apit, hence kit by 1.2.5. The iṭ query remains independent: 7.2.35 supplies
it at A 2sg/2pl/1du/1pl and P 1du/1pl, within the inherited slot restrictions.
The 7.2.13 niyama does not prohibit it for vad. This gives **23** iṭ-bearing
perfect positions: 21 A and two P. No additional iṭ belongs before a
vowel-initial ending; A irec's i is its own ending sound.

For **sic**, all **51** requests receive iṭ under 7.2.35. Neither the
outer luṅ ṅ nor an apit tiṅ makes intervening sic kit. The A-specific kit
rules 1.2.11–17 supply no vad licence; 3.4.104 is a different mood and pada.
Consequently root saṃprasāraṇa is absent in both sic branches. H04 supplies
their different root-strength decisions.

## H03. Weak perfect: change the root before copying it

Adopt the explicit Kāśikā 6.1.17 and Kaumudī/Bālamanoramā vad account
in the publisher's **3.1.54 field**: at kit liṭ, root saṃprasāraṇa precedes
reduplication. Thus vad → u|ad → ud under 6.1.15/108; 6.1.8 then copies
the admitted one-vowel root, with 7.4.60 removing the copy's noninitial d.
The interface **u_copy|ud_root** coalesces by 6.1.101 to the factor **ūd**.
Original root d survives; the copied d and root a have different deletion
histories. The long ū is a copy/root seam, not lexical long ū or an augment.

The resulting root ud has u, not the upadhā a needed by 6.4.120's
e-contraction. Its source-prescribed weak route is therefore not an optional
ved- stem. A later spelling query does not restore the removed root a to
manufacture a second contraction branch.

All 51 counted perfect requests use this weak route. The source explicitly
supplies **ūdatuḥ, ūdathuḥ, ūda, ūdiva, ūdima** in its P discussion.
Their kit status likewise withholds guṇa of the resulting root-u under 1.1.5.
Applying the same kit-root/copy interface to the separately admitted A
endings gives the full A tuple in H06. Those A words are written rule
inferences, not falsely labelled verbatim paradigms from that P passage.

The same source also gives **uvāda/uvada** and **uvaditha** in P singulars.
There the root is not kit and 6.1.17 operates on the **copy**; the root
retains vad with its own strong-grade choice. These controls have zero
position credit because PG81 did not request P singulars. They are not
alternative readings for any counted weak cell. PG80's jñā-specific
copy-before-final-ā-lopa schedule is not a universal order to impose on vad;
the present source explicitly places this root substitution before copying.

## H04. Named vad vṛddhi and the limits of the source's container key

Kāśikā, Bālamanoramā and Tattvabodhinī **7.2.3** explicitly retain vad's
named prescription in P sic: **vād**, not optionally vad. Rule 7.2.4 blocks
the general hal-final vṛddhi before iṭ, but Bālamanoramā 7.2.4 distinguishes
the independently named vad/vraj provision. Their explicit mention also
blocks the ordinary light-a option of 7.2.7. The source's **avādīt** and
Bālamanoramā's vad passage confirm this combination. No 7.2.5 m-final or
other exclusion is imported from kram.

The inherited **parasmaipade** condition matters. In A sic it is false,
so root a stays short: **vad|iṭ|sic**, while P has **vād|iṭ|sic**. Changing
an ending after constructing one common stem would lose this distinction.
Both branches remain seṭ; iṭ presence alone cannot decide root strength.

The electronic **Kaumudī/Bālamanoramā 31054** field contains a running
lexical transition from hve to vad and śvi after its main discussion.
Preserve the whole literal field. Its container key does **not** make vad
eligible for 3.1.54's optional aṅ: Kāśikā 3.1.53–54 limits that rule to
lip/sic/hve. Likewise the adjacent śvi options are not vad alternatives.
The vad subsection controls its own lexical illustrations, not the entire
preceding rule's root scope. No new source-image collation is claimed.
