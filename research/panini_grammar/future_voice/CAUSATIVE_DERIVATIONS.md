# PG18 — Causal karmaṇi futures with retained or lopped ṇic

Author: Monty. 2 November 2026.

## C1. Enter through the owned derived dhātu

D5 supplies the causal event, its source-qualified (χ,q) branch and A
agreement with q. Start at the PG13–PG15 state C_root|i_ṇic, before
outer-affix guṇa or lopa. Root substitutes, puk/yuk, ṇic and the
future affix are distinct owners. A printed causative present with its
ending removed is not this state.

V2's eligibility uses the original ṇic vowel, including when its
realization will later be empty. All thirteen admitted causal frames
therefore have O and C. Karma(q) is fixed before that morphological
option; it is not selected by whichever spelling results.

## C2. Ordinary branch: the retained-ṇic construction

The O branch has i_val by 7.2.35 at the derived-dhātu boundary. The
bare root's aniṭ tag does not prohibit it on that larger input. The
6.4.51 aniṭ guard is false, as in PG17. Then

\[
 C_{root}|i_{ṇic}|i_{val}|A
 \to C_{root}|e_{ṇic}|i_{val}|A
 \to C_{root}|ay_{ṇic}|i_{val}|A,
 \qquad A\in\{sya,tās\}.
\]

Rules 7.3.84 and 6.1.78 change ṇic-i; 1.1.6 protects augment i.
Thus the interfaces are C_root·ayiṣya and C_root·ayit. The active
causative's pada profile is not copied: even bhū/sthā/budh, whose
selected kartari causatives were P-only, have A here by 1.3.13.

## C3. Ciṇvat branch: lopa without erasing the augment

The C branch supplies i_cv by 6.4.62, excludes a second ordinary
augment by V3 and reads 6.4.51 through V5's visibility law. Hence

\[
 C_{root}|i_{ṇic}|i_{cv}|A
 \longrightarrow C_{root}|\varnothing_{ṇic}|i_{cv}|A.
\]

The original ṇic, its causal contribution and lopa event remain in
history. No e/ay is produced from its now-deleted realization: Kāśikā
6.4.51 expressly treats this lopa as an exception to the listed
vowel operations on ṇic. The output still has the **different** i_cv.

The established root constituent is not recomputed by stripping all
formation history and treating its letters as a new basic root.
For a fresh preceding-vowel query, ordinary paranimittaka vowel-lopa
visibility under 1.1.57 retains the relevant earlier ṇic boundary;
it does not turn C_root into a newly exposed bare aṅga for another
unrestricted round of 7.2.115/116. The inherited root strengthening
was already an owned formation event. This is the same boundary issue
PG15 resolves for causal passive lopa, now with an outer sourced iṭ.
Kāśikā 1.1.57's paṭayati example explicitly uses retained vowel-lopa
visibility to block a newly apparent 7.2.116 condition. It supplies
the general query principle used here, not a literal quotation of
the causal gam future.

In particular the basic-dā yuk of R3 is not inserted again after the
causative dā+p_puk constituent. Its p survives, giving dāpiṣyate,
not a new dāy- recipe. The inherited nī āy and pā yuk owners also
survive. Their new words are nāyiṣyate and pāyiṣyate respectively.

## C4. Mit introduces a further conditional branch

Rule 6.4.93 gives an optional long upadhā for a mit root in the
specified ṇic/ciṇ or ṇamul environment. V4 transports the ciṇ
case; it does not import the ṇamul domain. The source explicitly
gives **śamita/śāmita/śamayita** and the corresponding sya trio.
The third form is the O branch; the first two are the C branch's
short/long alternatives, not three unrelated iṭ options.

The inherited gam entry has the explicit am-final gaṇasūtra mit
witness used in PG13. Its C branch likewise admits the 6.4.93
length alternative **gam/gām**. The ordinary branch remains gamay-.
The ṇic boundary and the original short/upadhā witness are retained
for this dedicated query; the source's specific length operation is
not replaced by an automatic reapplication of final-vowel vṛddhi.
This does not assert that 1.1.57 applies uniformly to lengthening:
Kāśikā 6.4.93 expressly distinguishes dīrgha from a shortening option
and cites 1.1.58's exception in its further nested-formation examples.
For the single-ṇic domain here, use its directly stated mit condition
and 6.4.62's explicit transport of that length alternative.
The resulting gami-/gāmi- futures are written consequences of the
general mit construction, not claimed as separately quoted examples.

Thus for these two entries the joint relation is

\[
 \{(O,\text{retained }ṇic),
   (C,\text{lopa},\text{short}),
   (C,\text{lopa},\text{long})\}.
\]

A fourth “O plus optional 6.4.93 length” branch is not licensed:
actual sya/tās is neither ciṇ nor ṇamul, and O has no ciṇvat
transport. Other admitted causal entries have no mit length option
in this selected domain. Nested ṇic and yaṅ cases in the commentary
remain excluded.

## C5. The added śam frame and its formation

The new base is **śam 1040098**, upaśama, with an intransitive event
and a conscious causee as declared in D5. Causative 3.1.26 adds ṇic;
7.2.116 gives śām, and the am-final gaṇasūtra mit condition with
6.4.92 gives the admitted short śam|i_ṇic. The class-1 seeing root's
special mit prohibition is not imported into this class-4 entry.

Kāśikā/Nyāsa 6.4.62, Kaumudī/Bālamanoramā 6.4.93 and the
retained lexical/gaṇasūtra records provide this causal mit procedure.
The ordinary future is **śamayiṣyate/śamayitā**. The C alternatives
are **śamiṣyate/śamitā** and **śāmiṣyate/śāmitā**. They retain
the same causal role frame and q controller.

These forms are not obtained by calling the basic intransitive śam
directly eligible for 6.4.62: that consonant-final basic root is not
one of han/grah/dṛś. The ṇic formation is the eligibility witness.

## C7. Worked meaning-to-form continuations

**Causal bhū, several expressed causees.** D5 designates those causees
karman, so the causal event has Karma(q), A, 3pl. O gives
**bhāvayiṣyante**; C gives **bhāviṣyante**. In luṭ the two are
**bhāvayitāraḥ/bhāvitāraḥ**. Basic bhū's Bhāva domain, by contrast,
admits only bhaviṣyate/bhāviṣyate and bhavitā/bhāvitā, never these
plurals. The shared bhāviṣya interface does not erase expression type.

**Causal kṛ, expressed first-person karman.** The appropriate PG15
(χ,q) branch supplies q with first singular. O gives kārayiṣye and
kārayitāhe; C gives **kāriṣye/kāritāhe**. The latter still encodes
the causal event despite the absence of pronounced ṇic-ay.

**Causal sthā, two addressee causees.** The role frame gives A 2du.
O yields sthāpayiṣyethe/sthāpayitāsāthe; C yields
**sthāpiṣyethe/sthāpitāsāthe**. Root-final puk-p survives ṇic-lopa.
Basic Bhāva sthā's yuk-y belongs to a different derivation.

**Causal gam, third singular causee.** The admitted trio is
**gamayiṣyate/gamiṣyate/gāmiṣyate**, or
**gamayitā/gamitā/gāmitā** for luṭ. Basic karmaṇi gam instead has
gaṃsyate/gantā. Its bare aniṭ/P-only-iṭ behavior is not copied to
the derived causal boundary.

## C8. Causal factorization theorem

**Theorem C8.** For each completed D5 role branch, C2–C6 constructs
all selected morphological future branches and their owned interfaces.
Composing with PG16's nine A ending paths yields 13·2·9=234 positions
and (11·2+2·3)·2·9=504 branch readings across these profiles.

**Proof.** Every derived ṇic boundary satisfies V2. O is the unique
retained-ṇic interface by C2. C has the source-relative lopa of C3;
the admitted mit partition gives two C lengths precisely for gam/śam
and one C interface for the remaining eleven. C6 exhausts the inputs.
All outputs meet the thematic-sya or owned-tās interface, whose nine
ending paths PG16 proves. D5 supplies q and A independently of spelling.
N3 preserves the composed guards, histories and pairing under typed
zero-specialization. □

This is a written, source-relative construction. It neither executes
504 cases nor independently attests every expanded word in a corpus.
