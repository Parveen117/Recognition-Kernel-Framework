of tāsi's i, while preserving Jayāditya's idit analysis. PG17's

the spelling **ṭilopasya siddhatvād eva**, even though its nearby

wording is not silently corrected in the archive or used as a clean
independent proof. The manuscript declares its policy and uses the
explicit 6.4.22 rule/view plus the witnessed hantā; the wider textual

The original PG16 ending interpretation is otherwise unchanged:

## S4. Ciṇvat's coupled augment and limited transport

The full Kāśikā **6.4.62** supplies ci's cāyiṣyate/ceṣyate,
dā's dāyiṣyate/dāsyate, causal śam's three forms, han's
ghāniṣyate/haniṣyate, grah's grāhiṣyate/grahīṣyate and dṛś's
darśiṣyate/drakṣyate, with their luṭ counterparts. The source also
discusses sic, sīyuṭ and lṛṅ: those contexts are not counted here.

Kaumudī says that this iṭ is tied to the ciṇvat choice. Its
Bālamanoramā and Tattvabodhinī explanations distinguish the outer
option from the chosen branch's priority over ordinary val-iṭ, and
state why that priority prevents the ordinary augment from defeating
ṇic-lopa. Nyāsa/Padamañjarī provide the same relevant explanation.
V3–V5 are this restricted adopted construction, not a new general
priority algorithm for every sūtra conflict.

Tattvabodhinī reads the original-vowel condition constituentwise so
that a derived ṇic-final dhātu qualifies without being independently
listed as a whole upadeśa. Nyāsa invokes the derived-dhātu teaching
under 3.1.32. The different formulations remain attributed; the common
causal example is not a reason to erase their explanatory difference.

Kāśikā and the commentaries restrict the transport to aṅga operations.
Nyāsa explicitly excludes both the chapter-2 root substitutes and the
following-ending ciṇ-luk of 6.4.104. Han's retained n also respects
7.3.32's ciṇ exception; it is not generated by a generic ṇit recipe.

## S5. Augment origins, am and inherited root constituents

Kāśikā **7.2.37** directly states that grah's lengthening applies
to the ordinary iṭ, not ciṇvat iṭ, and supplies grāhitā/grāhiṣyate
as contrasts. The distinction is independently repeated under 6.4.62.
This is the strongest literal source witness for origin-sensitive
queries beyond the causal lopa argument.

Kāśikā **6.1.58** gives dṛś's am as the exception to light-upadhā
guṇa before the specified jhal/non-kit affix. Its C branch starts with
i_cv instead and takes ordinary guṇa, as Nyāsa 6.4.62 expressly
explains. The stored Nyāsa misnumbers the am rule **6.1.57**; the
pinned sūtrapāṭha locates it at **6.1.58**. It also has other evident
transcription errors in tense labels and example words. Raw text is
preserved; our event IDs use the pinned sūtras and the matching Kāśikā.

Basic gam's A path uses the P restriction in Kāśikā 7.2.58 and its
explicit saṃgaṃsyate counterexample. Nṛt's 7.2.57 option has the
s-initial/non-sic condition without a P restriction. The source rules,
not an unqualified root seṭ/aniṭ label, decide these new voice cases.

The twelve causal root constituents are inherited from PG13–PG15.
No disappearance of outer ṇic is allowed to delete earlier puk/yuk
or to recompute the causal event as a basic one. The ordinary
1.1.57 boundary and the dedicated 6.4.93 length query are kept distinct.
The former source's paṭayati example expressly blocks a newly apparent
7.2.116 condition after vowel-lopa. The latter source distinguishes
its length operation and the 1.1.58 exception; neither justifies one
uniform visibility value for all vowel queries.
Kāśikā 6.4.51 supplies lopa's relation to ṇic's vowel operations;
6.4.92/93 and their commentaries supply the mit construction.

The śam trio is explicitly quoted. The corresponding causal gam
short/long trio is a **written extension** using its exact mit witness
and the general 6.4.93 condition; no literal gam-future trio is claimed
in the cited passages. Other expanded causal words likewise retain
their inherited source qualifications, especially kṛṣ's exact-word gap.
