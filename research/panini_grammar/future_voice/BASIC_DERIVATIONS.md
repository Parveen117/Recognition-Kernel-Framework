## R1. Notation and the ordinary augment guards

All arrows act on N1's native, owned histories. O and C denote V2's
ordinary and ciṇvat branches. A final lṛṭ interface is B·a; a luṭ
interface H is the final readout factor used in Hā/Hāse/etc. These
are output factorizations, not permission to move Tripādī before
earlier morphology. D fixes the expression and allowed agreement slots.

The ordinary iṭ relation is:

| Entries | O before sya | O before tās |
|---|---|---|
| bhū, edh | i_val, 7.2.35 | i_val, 7.2.35 |
| grah | i_val→ī, 7.2.35/37 | i_val→ī, 7.2.35/37 |
| kṛ, hṛ, han | i by 7.2.70 | none, 7.2.10 |
| nṛt | i or none, 7.2.57 | i_val, 7.2.35 |
| sthā, nī, dā, pā, ci, dṛś, pac, labh, gam | none | none |

Nṛt's 7.2.57 rule is s-initial/non-sic, not a P-only rule. Its
present Bhāva A domain therefore has nartiṣyate/nartsyate. The short
u-it rule 7.2.56 is not substituted for this s-initial condition.

Basic gam is particularly important: 7.2.58 requires P in the relevant
finite setting, so it cannot supply i on the present A branch. Kāśikā
contrasts the A saṃgaṃsyate example. Its unprefixed karmaṇi consequence
here is **gaṃsyate**, with the source's P gamiṣyati kept separate.
Gam is consonant-final and is not one of 6.4.62's three named exceptions;
there is no basic C branch merely because its root has a mit note.

## R3. Vowel-final branches: strengthening and owned yuk

For bhū, O has guṇa bhū→bho and bho+i→bhav+i under 7.3.84 and
6.1.78. C transports 7.2.115 vṛddhi bhū→bhau, then 6.1.78 gives
bhāv+i_cv. Kaumudī 6.4.62 expressly gives **bhaviṣyate/bhāviṣyate**
and **bhavitā/bhāvitā**. Their bhāva meanings retain default 3sg.

For kṛ/hṛ, O sya gets the special 7.2.70 augment and root guṇa
kar/har. O tās has no i and gives kart/hart. C gives kār/hār
by 7.2.115 with 1.1.51's r, followed by i_cv in both futures:
**kāriṣyate/kāritā**, **hāriṣyate/hāritā**. The original ṛ-final
eligibility is not recomputed from the consonant-final visible kar/har.

Nī and ci have no ordinary iṭ: guṇa gives ne/ce before sya/tās,
with 8.3.59 producing neṣya/ceṣya. In C, vṛddhi gives nai/cai;
6.1.78 before i_cv yields nāy/cāy. Their y is an āy substitute's
part, not a yuk inserted into an original ā-final root. Kāśikā 6.4.62
gives ci's **cāyiṣyate/ceṣyate** and **cāyitā/cetā**.

For original ā-final sthā/dā/pā, O retains ā before s/t. C admits
the **yuk of 7.3.33**, whose retained y joins the root/aṅga owner
before the separately owned i_cv. Thus sthā+y+i+sya gives
**sthāyiṣyate**, and dā/pā similarly give dāyiṣyate/pāyiṣyate.
The luṭ factors are sthāyit/dāyit/pāyit. Kāśikā explicitly gives
dā's pair dāyiṣyate/dāsyate and dāyitā/dātā.

The ordinary ā in sthāsya/dāsya/pāsya supplies no 8.3.59 trigger;
the C branch's i_cv does. The present-passive substitutions such as
sthīya/dīya/pīya are not antecedent stems for these futures.

## R4. Han and grah: two different transported effects

Han O sya receives i by 7.2.70, giving **haniṣyate**. O luṭ gives
**hantā**, with the n-retention proof in R6. C transports h→gh by
7.3.54 and a-upadhā vṛddhi by 7.2.116:

\[
 han+i_{cv}+sya \longrightarrow ghān+i_{cv}+sya
 \longrightarrow ghāniṣyate.
\]

The luṭ word is **ghānitā**. The model is ciṇ, for which 7.3.32
excludes the n→t operation; no ghāti- is manufactured by treating the
transport as generic ṇit. Chapter-2 han→vadh also stays outside V4.
These are the literal contrast pairs under 6.4.62.

Grah O has ordinary i_val and 7.2.37 lengthens it, producing
**grahīṣyate/grahītā**. C transports a-upadhā vṛddhi but keeps
its different augment short: **grāhiṣyate/grāhitā**. Kāśikā 7.2.37
expressly excludes the ciṇvat augment from its lengthening. An iṭ
origin label therefore changes an actual derivation, not just provenance
printed in a footer.

## R5. Dṛś and the consonant-root controls

Without iṭ, dṛś sees a jhal-initial, non-kit affix. Rule 6.1.58
supplies am, an exception to the ordinary light-upadhā guṇa:

\[
 dṛś+sya \to dṛ+a_{am}+ś+sya
 \to draś+sya\quad(6.1.77)
 \to draṣ+sya\quad(8.2.36)
 \to drak+sya\quad(8.2.41)
 \to drakṣya\quad(8.3.59).
\]

Am's m marker locates a by 1.1.47; the root ṛ→r yaṇ and augment a
retain their separate ancestry. Before tās, 8.2.41's following-s
condition fails. After the earlier ending morphology, 8.4.41 gives
the **draṣṭ** junction and **draṣṭā**. Kāśikā 6.1.58 and 6.4.62
supply these ordinary examples.

With i_cv, the following affix is vowel-initial for the 6.1.58 query,
so that am route is inapplicable. Ordinary 7.3.86 guṇa gives darś,
followed by i_cv: **darśiṣyate/darśitā**. Nyāsa explicitly states
that guṇa is independently available; iṭ is the useful ciṇvat effect
for this root. Its stored cross-reference to 6.1.57 is corrected to
the pinned 6.1.58 without editing the raw source quotation.

Pac and labh reuse the PG16 owned junctions: pac→pak before s/t;
labh→lap before s, but labh+t→labdh in luṭ by the distinct
8.2.40/8.4.53 path. Hence pakṣyate/paktā and lapsyate/labdhā.
Edh retains its ordinary seṭ interface. Nṛt retains PG17's
nartiṣya/nartsya versus obligatory nartit before tās.

For basic gam, no iṭ or am is admitted here. Root m before s/t takes
8.3.24 anusvāra. Before s it stays ṃ, giving **gaṃsyate**; before
dental t, 8.4.58 gives n, yielding **gantā** after the earlier luṭ
morphology. The late n does not feed backward into 6.4.24.

## R6. The local han luṭ nasal obligation

PG16 left the wider nasal-A/tāsi adjudication open. Han now requires
an explicit local choice. We retain its selected Vāmana/Bālamanoramā
pronunciation-aid i analysis and the **6.4.22 view including the bha
domain**. Under that view 6.4.143's ṭi-lopa is asiddha to the earlier
6.4.24 query on the same ending-facing aṅga.

The displayed 3sg history has han+tās+ḍā→han+t+ā. But 6.4.24
does not inspect the newly shortened raw string hant as if the
ṭi-lopa had always been there: its view still has hantās, whose
upadhā is ā, not n. Its n-upadhā guard fails. Han's n therefore
survives in **hantā**. In the other eight slots the s-deletion or
substitution does not expose n as the relevant early upadhā either.

Kāśikā 6.4.62 supplies hantā directly. The 3.1.33 commentaries
also preserve Jayāditya's idit-blocking analysis and their disagreement
about exceptional asiddha visibility. We do not assert the two policies
are globally equivalent. S3 records a problematic sentence in the
digital Padamañjarī text; the adopted guard above follows the explicitly
declared 6.4.22 policy, not a silent emendation of that sentence.

## R7. Interface/ending factorization

**Lemma R7.** For every R2 branch, composing its owned root/affix
history with PG16's A ending paths gives every admitted slot in P.

**Proof.** The sya output is thematic a-final; all root-specific
junctions in R3–R5 precede the unchanged ending-facing thematic part.
Each tās branch retains the root-facing t while its ās/ending
junction follows PG16 E3/E5. Han's sole new early nasal concern is
discharged by R6; gam's n is late. The late phonetic readouts therefore
factor as P's B/H arguments without changing operation order. D2
restricts Bhāva to 3sg; D3 admits the nine Karma slots across frames.
These cases exhaust R2. □

This proves a finite source-relative factorization, not arbitrary
commutation of early and Tripādī rules.
