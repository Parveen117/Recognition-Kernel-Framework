# PG18 — Ciṇvat is a guarded transport with its own iṭ

Author: Monty. 2 November 2026.

## V1. Actual future affixes, not a passive present plus suffix

On D's native frames, 3.1.33 supplies actual sya or tās. The affix is
ārdhadhātuka; the original finite ending remains sārvadhātuka. The
future construction does not first attach yak under 3.1.67 and then
append sya/tās. Nyāsa/Padamañjarī 3.1.33 explicitly explain the
sya-family's relation to the general yak/śap route; the same future
affixes apply in bhāva/karma.

Keep the selected PG16 teaching-i policy for tāsi. Affix iṭ, teaching
i and the A first-person ending iṭ are three distinct objects. Every
substitution or lopa acts on an owner and retains its original data.

## V2. Exact condition of 6.4.62

In bhāva/karma contexts before the named affixes, 6.4.62 optionally
transports ciṇ-like **aṅga operations** and supplies its own iṭ.
PG18 admits only its sya/tās cases. The source's sic/sīyuṭ/lṛṅ
examples are preserved as context, not new domains of this packet.

The eligibility predicate is

\[
 E_{cv}(F)=\operatorname{BhāvaOrKarma}(F)\ \wedge\
 \operatorname{FutureAffix}(F)\ \wedge\
 \bigl(\operatorname{OriginalFinalAc}(F)\vee
       \operatorname{NamedHanGrahDṛś}(F)\bigr).
\]

The ac condition queries original teaching evidence. Kṛ's later kar
does not lose eligibility: Kāśikā explicitly uses this example to
explain *upadeśe*. For a causative, the original final vowel of the
owned ṇic supplies the witness. Tattvabodhinī distinguishes “ending
in a vowel which is present in upadeśa” from the stronger and wrong
requirement that the entire derived word have an independent dictionary
upadeśa. Nyāsa gives a related 3.1.32 account. Both support the causal
inclusion; their formulations are retained separately in the source record.

If E_cv is true there are two grammatical choices: O, the ordinary
future-affix procedure; and C, ciṇvat. If false, only O is admitted.
If its evidence is unknown, the undecided branch is Open, not another
positive optional form.

## V3. The ciṇvat choice and its augment are coupled

Kāśikā says that when ciṇvat applies, this iṭ applies. Kaumudī and
Padamañjarī explicitly explain their sanniyoga, the joint prescription.
Use i_cv for the occurrence introduced by 6.4.62 and i_val for the
ordinary 7.2.35 occurrence. The C branch has i_cv; it is not the product
of an optional ciṇvat flag and an independently optional i_cv flag.

The O branch consults its own iṭ sources. For bhū it has i_val;
for basic dā it has none; for kṛ before sya it has the 7.2.70
augment; for nṛt the 7.2.57 alternatives survive. Therefore “C off”
does not mean “all iṭ off”.

The commentaries on 6.4.62 give a source-specific priority explanation:
once C is chosen, its augment applies whether an ordinary val-based
augment was contemplated or not; after i_cv, the val-initial condition
for another such augment is defeated. Hence there is **one augment**,
not i_cv+i_val. Their description of this priority as nitya does not
remove 6.4.62's outer *vā*. The C/O choice remains optional.

## V4. Limited transport, not ciṇ substitution

The future affix does not become actual ciṇ. The selected aṅga
operations are those witnessed by the traditional ciṇ procedure:

| Transported operation | Source and addressed target |
|---|---|
| Final-vowel vṛddhi | 7.2.115 on its eligible aṅga vowel |
| a-upadhā vṛddhi | 7.2.116, with its own conditions/exceptions |
| ā-final yuk | 7.3.33, owned root/aṅga-final augment |
| han h→gh | 7.3.54 on the original han-h |
| mit length alternative in a causal frame | 6.4.93, not an unrestricted vowel-length option |

Ordinary guṇa, ayādi and late sound operations still have their own
guards. Dṛś's C branch, for example, needs the special iṭ; its darś
comes from ordinary 7.3.86 guṇa, not an invented ṛ-upadhā vṛddhi.

Kāśikā/Nyāsa expressly exclude the chapter-2 han→vadh and iṇ/iṅ
root replacements from the transport: they are not aṅga-adhikāra
operations. Nyāsa also excludes **6.4.104 ciṇo luk** on a following
ending: the comparison transports the preceding aṅga's operations,
not every effect anywhere around a ciṇ occurrence. Thus no tiṅ-luk,
aorist affix replacement or aorist time interpretation is introduced.

This is a source-bounded operation relation. It is not a universal
“copy the complete ciṇ pipeline” algorithm, nor a rule-order shortcut.

## V5. Two iṭ origins have different visibility to ṇic-lopa

Take an inherited causal state C_root|i_ṇic followed by a future affix.
After augmentation the raw owned-letter display can be identical:

\[
 C_{root}|i_{ṇic}|i_{augment}|sya.
\]

But the lopa query must distinguish its histories:

| Chosen branch | Augment source | 6.4.51's following-affix view | ṇic result |
|---|---|---|---|
| O | 7.2.35 i_val | i-initial; its aniṭ guard fails | retained, then i→e→ay |
| C | 6.4.62 i_cv | this ābhīya augment is asiddha under the adopted 6.4.22 reading | lopa; owner/history retained |

This is the explicit use made in Kāśikā's verse and the Nyāsa,
Padamañjarī, Bālamanoramā and Tattvabodhinī explanations. They also
address the possible interference from ordinary i_val: V3's priority
removes that alternative augment on the C branch. Merely hiding i_cv
while leaving a second i_val present would not prove the desired lopa.

Asiddha here means invisible **to this query**, not absent from the
event's output or every later rule. The final C word still contains its
short i_cv, protected by 1.1.6. It remains visible to the later 8.3.59
sya-s→ṣ operation. The deleted ṇic remains a distinct retained owner;
its lopa is not deletion of i_cv.

PG17's kartari continuation is unaffected: its ordinary i_val blocked
ṇic-lopa. PG18 changes the expression domain and supplies the new
6.4.62 choice with the additional source-visible history.

## V6. Grah shows that augment origin matters outside lopa too

The pinned Kāśikā **7.2.37** explicitly limits its i→ī lengthening
to the ordinary augment in the inherited context. It contrasts
**grahītā/grahītum** with ciṇvat **grāhitā/grāhiṣyate**.
Kāśikā/Nyāsa 6.4.62 make the same distinction.

Thus the O future gives grah+i_val→grah+ī, while the C future has
grah→grāh and **short** i_cv. Neither “lengthen every i after grah”
nor “all iṭ occurrences are interchangeable” is the stated rule.
The teaching marker, insertion source, following affix and query remain
in the native history and in its zero-reading.

## V7. Local branch theorem

**Proposition V7.** On completed D-domain frames, V2–V6 supplies the
source-licensed C/O relation, a single sourced augment on each positive
augment branch, and the stated lopa/lengthening query outcomes. It
preserves actual future-affix and ending identity.

**Proof.** V2 partitions eligibility. A positive C choice requires its
coupled i_cv by V3; the same source's priority excludes the duplicate
val augment. V4 restricts the transported targets. V5 and V6 then use
the relevant source/visibility fields, not the raw-letter display.
O retains the ordinary guards constructed in PG16/PG17 and R below.
Every admitted branch has its source witness; all other hypotheses
remain Excluded or Open under their failed/undischarged conditions. □

The general scheduling of all ābhīya operations, all ciṇ analogies
and all commentary policies is not solved by this bounded theorem.
