## D2. Bhāva is not agreement with an instrumental agent

Rule 3.4.69 admits bhāva for an intransitive event, and karman expression
for a transitive event. Kāśikā expressly excludes a transitive root's
bhāva reading in that stated construction. We do not silently remove an
object from a supplied transitive event to admit it into the bhāva sector.
Rule 1.3.13 prescribes A for both expression types.

For Bhāva the process itself is expressed. The actor, if overt, is
unexpressed kartṛ and takes instrumental under 2.3.18. Kaumudī 3.1.67
explains why the verb takes **third person, singular**: the expressed
bhāvanā is not coreferential with yuṣmad/asmad; its non-substantival
meaning does not present dual/plural number, so singular is the default.
This is not an assertion that the real world contains exactly one actor
or one counted occurrence. The explanation concerns the expressed sense
and applies here without transferring that rule's yak affix.

Consequently an event performed by “you”, “me”, or several instrumental
agents still has the Bhāva 3sg slot. For the admitted bhū frame,
tvayā bhaviṣyate and asmābhiḥ bhaviṣyate have the same verbal agreement;
each also has the ciṇvat alternative bhāviṣyate constructed below.
The agent's person/number is retained as evidence, not copied into tiṅ.

## D3. Basic karmaṇi selects its karman controller

Our basic transitive frames supply one intended, already designated
karman q and an agent a. Rules 3.4.69 and 1.3.13 give Karma(q), A.
The expressed q takes the admitted nominative route under 2.3.46;
the unexpressed a takes instrumental under 2.3.18. Agreement follows
q through 1.4.105/107/108 and the inherited person/number rules.

All nine slots are possible across completed discourse frames. For
example, selecting an addressee as the seen q gives **drakṣyase** or
**darśiṣyase**, not the agent's slot. Selecting several third-person
karmans gives the third plural. These are frames, not a claim that every
individual noun can occupy every person in one fixed sentence.

The restricted one-karman frames do not attempt the whole double-object
grammar. Ci's source inventory labels it D; PG18 selects its ordinary
collecting frame with one expressed collection/object and leaves any
additional akathita-object construction outside. Nī and hṛ similarly
retain the one-object sense here. Kāśikā 6.4.62's explicit ci future
examples support the morphological entry, not every possible role frame.

## D5. Causal karmaṇi and its dependent role relation

The twelve inherited causal frames are PG15 K1–K4's bhū, kṛ, nī, hṛ,
pac, gam, sthā, dā, pā, vid, budh and kṛṣ, with their exact entries,
conscious bhū/sthā causees and all stated source qualifications. Reuse
their relation B(F)={(χ,q)} of causee status χ and eligible expressed
karman q. Its rules are not laṭ-restricted; its stem continuation is
replaced here by the future construction, not by a new role assignment.

In every case the causal event is transitive. An intransitive **base**
does not make the derived causal event a Bhāva input. The causal causer
remains instrumental; q controls tiṅ. Other unexpressed karmans remain
accusative, and a causee retaining kartṛ remains instrumental, exactly
as in PG15. The active-causative exceptions and beneficiary conditions
do not turn into P passive options.

Add one exact frame: **śam 1040098**, class 4, upaśama. A conscious
causee b becomes calm, and distinct causer a causes that event. Rule
1.4.52 designates b karman; 3.1.26 supplies one owned ṇic. Thus b is
the sole expressed karman, a instrumental, and the future is A with
slot(b). The causative stem and mit witness are proved in C5. This is
not class-1 śam in seeing or the class-10 nitya-ṇic entry.

## D6. Domain exhaustion and composition

For a completed basic frame D4 decides the expression domain: four
Bhāva entries with one agreement slot, twelve Karma entries with nine
slots across frames. For causal frames D5 first decides (χ,q), then
slot(q). Incomplete lexical, role or future evidence remains Open.

**Proposition D6.** Every admitted completed frame has a nonempty
finite expression/controller relation; each branch fixes A and exactly
one agreement slot. The relation does not use the later word spelling.

**Proof.** D2 supplies the default Bhāva slot. D3 has one stipulated
karman. The inherited causal cases satisfy PG15 K5, and the added śam
case has one causee-karman by 1.4.52. Their union exhausts D4–D5.
Each participant's case and slot follows the displayed source route.
No future affix operation changes those event roles. □

Morphological counts below factor out repeated causal role witnesses
with the same word morphology; the native histories do not erase them.
Nominal declension is supplied by the nominal grammar, not reconstructed
in this finite verbal packet.
