# PG877 · The mendicant, the collective and the two printed affix attributions

### A personal name, a vocative and two analyses of the base

PG875’s unprefixed 01.0690 *bhikṣ*, asking for alms, already supplies the agent *bhikṣu* under 3.2.168. The Śabdakalpadruma (25050) gives two analyses for the longer **bhikṣuka**. One adds svārthe *kan* to the noun *bhikṣu*. The other attaches *uka* directly to the root. Vācaspatyam p. 4670 gives the second analysis. The two paths reach the same stem and inflect together. The six persons quoted from Atri illustrate a stipulated use of the name. The quotation licenses neither six new nominal paradigms nor the transfer of their statuses to Sulabhā. [Śabdakalpadruma, 25050 and 29709](https://github.com/sanskrit-lexicon/csl-orig/blob/cf53a4240ac83c8f7dfd275533ebd0a0639e6b57/v02/skd/skd.txt).

Monier-Williams explicitly cross-references feminine **bhikṣukī** under the longer head (150716–17, 150720). Its separate Buddhist entry is *bhikṣuṇī* (150722). This settles the dictionary’s choice of masculine correlate for the epic head. The precise feminine-suffix history of the Epic and the origin of the Buddhist nasal still need separate evidence. PG876 supplied the feminine vocative *bhikṣuki*. Two texts now attest it. In the *Kalkipurāṇa* quotation in Śabdakalpadruma 29709, 31.4–7, Śarmiṣṭhā tells the addressee to relinquish the garment. The nominative *dānavakanyā* resumes the speaker in v. 7. In Bhāgavata 9.18.16 Śarmiṣṭhā addresses Devayānī in a reproach using two second-person verbs. The address supplies the interlocutor in both clauses. It does not establish monastic ordination for either character. PG876 retains this case key and reading. [Bhāgavata 9.18.16](https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/9/18/16/).

### One man, a community of men, or a life stage

In Manu 3.243 *bhikṣukam* is the masculine accusative singular coordinated by *vā* with *brāhmaṇam*. The arriving man is the recipient of hospitality, grammatically the object of *pratipūjayet*. The invited Brāhmaṇas in the instrumental plural give permission. The host receives it and is the understood agent of entertaining the arrival. Medhātithi distinguishes the Brāhmaṇa who arrives as a guest from one who comes begging. Jha’s comparative note attributes to Nārāyaṇa and Rāghavānanda the householder/ascetic analysis. The same case structure admits both source-labelled interpretations. [Manu 3.243 with commentary](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/manusmriti-with-the-commentary-of-medhatithi/d/doc200033.html).

For a **collection** of these mendicants the khāṇḍikādi rule 4.2.45 selects **añ**. The Kāśikā expressly lists *bhikṣuka*, also present in gaṇapāṭha entry 73. The Bālamanoramā confirms the affix and the purpose of initial udātta. On the stated analysis the ñ marker licences initial vṛddhi (7.2.117), i → ai. The base-final a yields before the vowel-initial affix (6.4.148). The resulting **bhaikṣuka-** has neuter collective sense. The distinctive head and gender are independently recorded by Monier-Williams 152969. Entry 152970 marks a separate masculine life-stage use with an understood *āśrama*. It has no place in the neuter group table. The selected word forms below are unaccented; the stated affixal accent is a source attribution. [Lexicon, numbered entries](https://github.com/sanskrit-lexicon/csl-orig/blob/cf53a4240ac83c8f7dfd275533ebd0a0639e6b57/v02/mw/mw.txt).

**The printed affix disagreement is real.** In the right column on p. 4670 the Vācaspatyam prints *ṭhañ* after its abbreviation for khaṇḍikādi, then gives *bhaikṣuka* as the neuter group. The Cologne transliteration agrees on these points. The same print’s next example gives the order **viprabhikṣukaḥ** alongside the reversed stem **bhikṣukavipra**. The transcription instead gives the damaged first token *viprABakzukaH*. Rule 2.2.38 and its kaḍārādi list (entry 19) independently establish optional quality-member order on the same-referent karmadhāraya. Manu’s coordinated alternatives do not instantiate that compound. Accept the collective output on both lexicographic records, keep añ as the derivationally reviewed Kāśikā operation and ṭhañ as Vācaspatyam’s literal label. A derivation from that printed ṭhañ to the same output, or an editorial explanation for the disagreement, remains Open. The damaged digital compound adds no lexical alternative. [Vācaspatyam p. 4670](https://www.sanskrit-lexicon.uni-koeln.de/scans/VCPScan/VCPScanpdf/pg4670.pdf).

The older 3.3.42 register owns *bhikṣukanikāyaḥ*. In that compound the group head derives from prefixed *ni + ci*, ghañ and the initial k substitution. The Kāśikā requires a collection of living beings united by a common characteristic. In the Tattvabodhinī the stacked piglets nursing illustrate the excluded physical arrangement; pigs standing apart like mendicants can satisfy the admitted condition. Species alone does not distinguish these requests. Neither the rule nor that older word supplies a rival suffix on *bhikṣuka* at 4.2.45.

### Two seven-case schedules

Prepend **bhikṣuk** in the masculine personal column and **bhaikṣuk** in the neuter collective column. Each cell gives singular, dual and plural in order. In the retroflex endings ṣ and the admissible interveners survive on both bases (8.4.1–2). In particular neuter plural *bhaikṣukāṇi* and masculine plural *bhikṣukān* have different endings.

The masculine genitive singular *bhikṣukasya* already occurs in the 2.2.3 explanation of the beggar’s alms. Withhold it in full. The earlier gaṇa lists name the base without giving a nominative reading. The earlier 3.3.42 compound has its own final head. Out of 42 interfaces this leaves **41 new nominal positions and 41 readings**. No finite, feminine or compound position is added.
