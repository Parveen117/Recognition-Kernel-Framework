Author: Monty. 4 November 2026.

Use [Domain](DOMAIN.md), the twelve rule tables and

## C1. Admission before combination

Retain root and affix origins, action/participant identities, original case
functions, compound members and source policy. Evaluate the requested row's
positive gates and decisive exclusions. A false gate rejects that clause;
unknown evidence leaves it Open. Select only legal dependent clauses, then
apply each operation at its addressed token. Effects include authority,
designation, case selection, compound membership/order, root substitution,
affix selection, deletion and local lexical stipulation.

Sound sameness does not prove morphological identity. यत्, क्यप् and ण्यत्
retain different histories; so do क/अण्, खश्/खच् and the formative versus
finite-ending सिप्. The source's नानुबन्धकृतमसारूप्यम् at 3.1.94 concerns
the affix-competition query, not deletion of markers from the historical
carrier or a denial of their other consumers.

## C2. Roles, case and compound eligibility

Assign a participant role in an admitted action. Check whether that relation
is already expressed under 2.3.1. Select the applicable case rule, including
its discourse and lexical conditions. A genitive does not by itself authorize
a genitive compound: apply 2.2.8 and the local permissions/prohibitions to the
correct relation after case selection.

|Frame|Case/compound consequence|
|---|---|
|याजकादि relation|2.2.9 restores a compound otherwise blocked by 16|
|Part fraction in 2.2.3|Its genitive-TP branch specifically survives 2.2.11's ordinal block|
|Present-purpose or locus क्त|2.3.67/68 restore genitive; 2.2.12/13 still block the corresponding compound|
|Both actor/object genitives for one kṛt|2.3.66 selects object genitive under the chosen policy; 2.2.14 can still prohibit compounding|
|Distance expression and its standard|2.3.35/36 target the distance word; 34 targets the associated nominal|
|KP particle|2.3.8 is refined by 9–11; designation does not imply universal accusative|

Thus distant-word case and standard case do not compete for one token.
Similarly, a purposive dative under 2.3.14 requires a genuinely omitted purpose
verb's object; it cannot be inferred from any missing verb. Time-span
instrumental at 2.3.6 additionally requires attainment and completion, not
merely elapsed time or abandonment of the action.

The शेष construal in 2.3.52–57 is not an unconditioned optional case toggle.
Keep the intended relation distinct from ordinary object presentation. At
2.3.58–60, preserve the rule's more specific दिव्, prefix and register choices.
The actual kṛt prescription, not present-time discussion alone, admits 2.3.67.

## C3. Compound versus phrase histories

For an admitted उपपद request, 3.1.92 identifies the prescribed relation and
2.2.19 licenses the non-finite compound. The word need not already possess
every eventual ending. Preserve the original participant and सुप् history
through the separate 2.4.71 internal-affix deletion.

The **कुम्भकार** witness has an object-related कुम्भ token, कृ root and
3.2.1 अण् selection; its उपपद compound is a different event from internal
सुप् loss and from later grade/ending realization. The final source witness
does not erase those intermediate addresses. The **शब्दकार** control does
not receive 3.2.20 ट merely from a habitual reading: 3.2.23 blocks that route.

For the later अम् group, 2.2.20 requires exclusive co-prescription. 2.2.21
admits its optional compound/phrase branches even in the specified shared
prescriptions, and 22 extends the later group to क्त्वा. With 3.4.59's admitted
उच्चैस् + कृ frame, the compound branch supports उच्चैःकृत्य through the
qualified 7.1.37 ल्यप् and 6.1.71 तुक् requirements; the phrase branch retains
उच्चैः कृत्वा. The 3.4.47/59 clauses remain archived controls without newly
constructed-ID credit in this packet.

PG42's separate तिरस् + कृ analysis remains intact. Adding a general गति
compound clause at 2.2.18 does not resolve the extra तिरस्कृत्वा source reading
or authorize a non-गति/गति-conditioned-स् cross-pair.

## C4. Number, order and gender

Retain all original referents when a compound receives collective single-entity
treatment. This differs from PG42's 1.2.69 neuter retention/optional singular
analysis. Determine collective versus individual construal before choosing
the associated gender and inflection.

At 2.4.12, Nyāsa defines बहुप्रकृति through plural-denoting/plural-inflected
inputs. **बदराणि + आमलकानि** can produce the collective बदरामलकम् although
there are only two written member names. A two-item बदरामलके does not satisfy
that condition. Do not implement बहुप्रकृति as “more than two string nodes.”

At 2.4.16, approximate enumeration preserves two coupled paths:

|Choice|Modifier and compound witness|
|---|---|
|Collective|उपदशम् as अव्ययीभाव with दन्तोष्ठम्|
|Individual/plural|उपदशाः as बहुव्रीहि with दन्तोष्ठाः|

The modifier cannot be selected independently of the number branch. Exact
enumeration under 15 blocks the collective route. Dvandva member ordering at
2.2.32/33 chooses a qualified first member; it does not automatically sort
every remaining member. The specified राजदन्तादि, प्रिय and निष्ठा alternatives
retain their own lexical conditions.

Gender is grammatical and relation-dependent. 2.4.27's first-member gender
applies to the noncollective अश्ववडव branch beyond the displayed dual. The
Vedic 2.4.28 clause uses its separately declared sources. Neither becomes a
general prohibition on last-member gender outside its domain.

## C5. Root and affix origin

The contextual authority 2.4.35 and the option 3.1.31 can operate on an
intended आर्धधातुक derivation before its final suffix choice. An implementation
that waits for a visible following suffix would miss the source's भव्यम्-type
dependency. Retain the intended class as evidence and then select the licensed
root and suffix in their declared order.

|Apparently similar inputs|Different owned operation|
|---|---|
|इण्, इक् and इङ्|Root identity controls गम्/गा/गाङ् and their precise domains|
|इङ् + णिच् + सन्/चङ्|2.4.51 checks both affix-relative positions, not just a visible णिच्|
|च्लि directly deleted|2.4.80's adopted Nyāsa clause has no सिच् token to supply its specific accent|
|च्लि changed to सिच् then deleted|Original सिच् and its permitted consumers remain a different history|
|आम् perfect plus auxiliary|2.4.81 deletes the primary following लि/लिट् token, not the auxiliary's distinct लिट्|
|खश् versus खच्|श् supplies सार्वधातुक status; खच् has the addressed 6.4.94 णिच्-upadhā shortening condition|

For 2.4.80, Nyāsa explicitly motivates च्लि rather than सिच् deletion by
excluding a सिच्-specific accent consequence such as 6.1.187. This is why
recording only “aorist sign absent” is insufficient. Vasu's broad gloss of लि
does not authorize deletion of every finite ending. At 81, literal आम् inside
जगाम, शशाम or the homonymous root does not satisfy the affix-origin gate.

## C6. Explicit pair relations

कर्म/सुप् inheritance in early 3.2 is selected by the actual semantic frame.
Do not require an object of intransitive रम् at 3.2.13 merely because 3.2.1
began with कर्मणि. Conversely, do not relax a later explicit object restriction
into arbitrary nominal adjacency.

|Rule|Admitted object/root pairs for खश्|
|---|---|
|3.2.29|नासिका–ध्मा; नासिका–धेट्; स्तन–धेट्|
|3.2.30|नाडी–ध्मा; नाडी–धेट्; मुष्टि–ध्मा; मुष्टि–धेट्|

The first relation is neither a simple two-by-two ordered pairing nor the full
four-pair product. The second explicitly admits all four named pairs. Other
rules, such as 3.2.41, use their own ordered correspondence. A common “expand
all options” operation would therefore be grammatically incorrect.

At 3.2.36 the negative in असूर्यंपश्य remains related to seeing, as in not
seeing even the sun. The named compound does not move negation onto an
ordinary “non-sun” object. At 3.2.44, अण् and खच् own respectively the
क्षेमकार/क्षेमंकर histories and specifically block हेत्वादि ट; one may not
combine the grade of one suffix with the मुम् behavior of another.

## C7. कर्मकर्तृ and qualified transfer

3.1.87 requires an original object presented as actor of the same root's
object-centered action. A paraphrase using a different root, an instrument
acting, or the word स्वयमेव alone does not supply that evidence. The transfer
feeds its named verbal consumers; the actor's own properties and other
participants are retained. It does not turn every instrument into an object
or assign accusative to a कुसूल participant by analogy.

Existing 1.3.67 instead provides its qualified णिच्/pada route for
actor-centered examples too. Its special A license is not permission to
import all 3.1.87 यक्/चिण् effects. Source-licensed 3.1.88 is another explicit
exception: तपस्-related तप् is admitted despite the stated action difference,
while 3.1.65 still blocks चिण् in its domain.

At 3.1.62/63, optional चिण् belongs to the specified A third-singular त and
qualified कर्मकर्तृ history. Other persons and ordinary passive clauses do
not gain that option. 3.1.89's prohibitions address transferred यक्/चिण्,
preserving दुह्'s separately licensed 63 option and other lawful effects.
3.1.90 jointly selects श्यन् and P within the यक् domain; the alternative
has its ordinary यक्/A history. Nyāsa identifies the excluded लिङ् example
as **आशीर्लिङ्**. Do not exclude ordinary विधिलिङ् just because both are named
लिङ्, or extend the P option to future/perfect/aorist forms outside the domain.

## C8. Meaning, accent and realization limits

सन् of 3.1.5/6 is not ordinary same-agent desire सन् of 7. कण्ड्वादि verbal
entries are not their nominal homonyms. 3.1.149's समभिहार admits skilled
performance even once and excludes repeated poor performance; it is not
3.1.22's generic repetition/intensity condition.

At 3.1.103, keep identical अर्य surfaces in separate sense histories with
different accent: स्वामिन् final उदात्त, वैश्य initial उदात्त under the adopted
Nyāsa-supported vārttika. At 3.4.117's Vedic control, different consumers may
receive the prescribed सार्वधातुक and आर्धधातुक treatments in one attested
history. This requires consumer-indexed observations, not an exclusive global
bit inferred from one spelling.
