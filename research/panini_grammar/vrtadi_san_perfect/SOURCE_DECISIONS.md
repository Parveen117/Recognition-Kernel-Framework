# PG121 — Primary word and auxiliary word

Monty · 11 November 2026

## H01. The input is a parokṣa desire

Continue the PG118–120 unprefixed kartari comparison. The same agent
desires an action of the specified lexical root and would perform that
action. Supply the inherited non-today parokṣa context under 3.2.115,
including its first-person witness when the speaker is the agent. Use
the ordinary non-Mantra domain. The past/perfect context qualifies
the desire, independent of whether the desired action took place.

## H02. The primary word has no same-word taṅ

Kāśikā 3.1.35 admits ām on these san-derived, multi-vowel dhātus.
Kāśikā 2.4.81 removes the primary lakāra. Tattvabodhinī on that
rule places luk at the **lakāra stage**, in place of the tiṅ
substitutions. It treats the ām word as atiṅanta, with pada status
explained through nominal/avyaya and sup-luk. Nyāsa 1.3.63 likewise
describes sup and sup-luk on the primary, followed by a new liṭ on
the auxiliary. The primary thus has no taṅ member whose class could
survive as an ending substitute. This differs from PG119's luṭ ḍā.

## H03. Apply the commentary's same-word reading to san

Mahābhāṣya and Kāśikā at 7.2.59 give a general prohibition with
the licensed A condition in the same word. Kāśikā 7.2.60 explicitly
carries the same qualification to kṛp. H02 fails the same-word A
condition for *every* auxiliary choice, including kṛ with A. Therefore
the selected primary branch is aniṭ for all five roots. This conclusion
is a written inference obtained by combining the two source analyses.
The archived passages do not quote these five complete perfect tables.

The selected stems may equal a PG118 P stem in sound. Their iṭ decision
here has the cause **absence of same-word taṅ**, with no P tiṅ on the
primary. Define a separate primary stem record for that cause. For
syand and kṛp the inherited ūdit option likewise has no A witness here.

## H04. The kṛ auxiliary asks a different question

The ām base has the pada entitlements of the san formation (1.3.12/62
and 1.3.92–93). The P/A coordinate in these comparison requests selects
one of those entitlements. Source 1.3.63 passes the applicable ām-base
entitlement to auxiliary kṛ, with the restrictive pūrvavat reading.
Tattvabodhinī explicitly distinguishes the ām base's property from an
A ending on the ām word. As and bhū in the selected kartari package
have P as in PG88. Actual finite endings occur only in the auxiliary.

| ām-base entitlement | Inner iṭ | kṛ auxiliary | bhū auxiliary | as auxiliary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| P | absent | P | P | P |
| A | absent | A | P | P |

Two words can be contiguous under 3.1.40 and undergo sandhi without
becoming a single pada for 7.2.59. The explicit 1.3.63 transfer is
directed from base to auxiliary. It supplies no reverse transfer to
the root-to-san iṭ query.

## H05. Status of the inference

This table adopts the Kāśikā same-pada reading together with
Tattvabodhinī's pre-tiṅ luk and PG88's kartari auxiliary selection.
The Bhāṣya at 7.2.59 illustrates intervening affixes inside one word,
not a middle auxiliary in a second word. A primary seṭ perfect would
require an additional account of that boundary. Independent direct
attestation of these compound readings remains open. The selection
is recorded on each new history so a competing textual analysis could
be represented separately without changing the PG118–120 finite forms.
