# PG188 · The ten Uṇādi ṛ-stems across case and number

Monty · 11 November 2026

All ten stems named together at Uṇādi 2.95 now have a written declension in their selected gender and sense. Each has eight case or address rows and three numbers. All **240 nominal forms** agree, cell by cell, with the pinned word tables. The visible contrast falls in the vowel immediately before *r* in the strong forms.

| Selection | Nominative duals |
| --- | --- |
| Listed for lengthening under 6.4.11 | *naptārau, neṣṭārau, tvaṣṭārau, hotārau, potārau* |
| Short *a* before *r* in this environment | *bhrātarau, jāmātarau, mātarau, pitarau, duhitarau* |

The Kāśikā gives the five long duals, and also gives the short duals of mother and father. Siddhāntakaumudī explains why Uṇādi derivatives ending in tṛn or tṛc require the named list in this rule. Thus the competing analyses of *bhrātṛ* still predict *bhrātarau* in this comparison. For *jāmātṛ*, all 24 forms can be specified while the individual suffix marker remains unresolved. In *jāmātarau* the lexical ā before *t* and the short vowel after *t* have separate grammatical histories.

The oblique forms expose a second contrast. *Pitṝn* and *mātṝḥ* are both accusative plurals. Their different endings reflect the selected genders. Locative singular *pitari* and *hotari* share a short vowel, despite the difference between *pitarau* and *hotārau*. The singular address, such as *he hotaḥ*, has its own condition.

The declension archive labels its *potṛ* entry as a grandson. The Uṇādi commentary calls *potṛ* a type of officiating priest, and the priest reading is independently identified in PG183. Here the archive supplies the word endings, and the primary lexical text supplies the selected sense. Every row preserves the archive locator so this difference stays visible.
