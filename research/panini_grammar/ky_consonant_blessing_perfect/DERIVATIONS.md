## H06. P blessing's three ordered histories

For samidh and P 3sg, write samidh-y-yāt. Retain both sounds to give samidhyyāt. The optional yam reduction gives samidhyāt. Alternatively choose morphological ky-lopa first, leaving samidh-yāt with the one y belonging to the blessing. The same pair of readings and three paths occurs with dṛṣad and across the nine P endings. Projection can forget their difference; the typed histories cannot be merged before the rules run.

## H07. A blessing tests the pre-iṭ host

A 3sg gives samidhyiṣīṣṭa or samidhiṣīṣṭa. In 2pl the retained branch gives samidhyiṣīdhvam or samidhyiṣīḍhvam. The deleted branch gives samidhiṣīdhvam. A corresponding three-way support appears for dṛṣad; its bare d and the dh of samidh have the same exclusion from iṇ.

## H08. Primary words of the perfect

Preserve the four primary members samidhyām, samidhām, dṛṣadyām and dṛṣadām. For each input profile the first two or the last two are alternatives, never four. P selects kṛ-P, bhū-P or as-P; A selects kṛ-A, bhū-P or as-P. Auxiliary grade, copying and ending are the PG88 maps pinned here. The conditioning here is 6.4.50 on ky, not the disputed san/7.2.59 environment of PG121.

## H09. Boundary versus morphological options

samidhāñcakre and samidhāṃcakre are two standard boundary realizations from deleted-y samidhām + cakre. samidhyāñcakre and samidhyāṃcakre retain the denominative y. Before āsa the primary final m remains visible. The full primary and auxiliary words survive in every perfect path, also when the normalized text is written without a break.
