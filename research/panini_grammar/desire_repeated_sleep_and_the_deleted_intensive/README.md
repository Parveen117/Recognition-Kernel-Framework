# PG857 · Desire to sleep, repeated sleep, and deletion of the intensive suffix

The Mādhavīya on śīṅ svapne (entry 682, printed p. 339) puts two ordinary-looking middle forms next to four forms of an intensive whose suffix has disappeared. Further down the same paragraph, the author distinguishes the impersonal of this last stem from the impersonal of simple śīṅ. The root means ‘sleep’ throughout this comparison. All credited inputs are unprefixed, in laṭ.

### Two nine-position middle presents

For san, the sleeper desires to perform the action (3.1.7). The middle selection of the marked underlying root carries over by 1.3.62. The root takes iṭ. Accordingly the effective following onset is a vowel, and the condition for imposing kit on san in 1.2.9 fails. Guṇa of ī supplies e, then ay before i (7.3.84, 6.1.78). With desiderative reduplication and ṣ after i the resulting stem is śiśayiṣa-. The Kāśikā on 1.2.9 itself cites the third singular as the counterexample to a broader kit rule.

For yaṅ the input denotes frequent or intense performance of the action (3.1.22). Yaṅ's own ṅ selects middle under 1.3.12. Its surviving y-initial, ṅ-marked grade meets 7.4.22. The Mādhavīya explicitly orders substitution of ay for root-final ī before doubling. The copy now has a, lengthened under 7.4.83, while the base retains the two adjacent y's from śay + ya. Thus śāśayya- and the desiderative stem enter the same present-ending schedule with different lexical and affix histories. Kāśikā 7.4.22 supplies the intensive third singular independently. The Nyāsa on that rule also places the substitution before reduplication and names the lengthening rule.

### What the deleted suffix leaves visible

On the yaṅ-luk branch of 2.4.74 the copy has guṇa of its i-vowel by 7.4.82, giving śe. There is no surviving yaṅ-initial y to trigger the preceding table's change of the root to śay. In the ordinary agentive third person the active endings now distinguish strong from weak slots. The text restricts the special simple-root guṇa and ruṭ rules to the root named with its marker. The independently licensed guṇa of 7.3.84 at a strong ending still applies.
