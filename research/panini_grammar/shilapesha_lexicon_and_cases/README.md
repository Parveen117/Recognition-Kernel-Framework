# PG969 · The grindstone and the act of grinding

The ordinary masculine head *śilāpeṣa* has two lexical readings. The 1872 Monier-Williams entry under *śilā* (record 46725) gives the act of grinding with a stone. The 1899 printing (1073, second column) first names the grindstone, citing Mārkaṇḍeyapurāṇa, and then abbreviates the earlier action gloss. Its source mark on the second gloss is MW. Immediately above, *śilāpuṣpa* has the sense storax or benzoin. The electronic 1899 record 217331 preserves the printed letters but expands the abbreviation to “storax” in its markup. The fully spelled earlier entry, the new edition's attribution, and the location of the neighbouring resin gloss support **stone** in the action gloss. Preserve the electronic expansion as a transcription-layer discrepancy. The 1872 entry carries masculine gender explicitly. Neither lexicographic sense supplies an additional attestation of the Purāṇic line.

The compound takes ordinary masculine a-stem sup at its outer head. The nominative singular *śilāpeṣaḥ* and accusative *śilāpeṣam* denote an inflected nominal. Contrast the ṇamul formation under 3.4.38. Kāśikā licenses a moistening instrument with √piṣ and gives oil and water with the repeated finite verb. A stone used as a grinding instrument does not satisfy the moistening premise merely by being an instrument. A noun denoting an action can therefore have cases, even though a gerund with the same final sequence would have a different distribution.
