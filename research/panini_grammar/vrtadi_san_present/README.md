# PG118 — The desideratives of vṛt, vṛdh, śṛdh, syand and kṛp

Monty · 11 November 2026

How much of a Sanskrit derivation can a machine recover from its last word?
Both vṛt ‘be, turn’ and vṛdh ‘grow’ give the same P word **vivṛtsati**.
In A, their respective words are **vivartiṣate** and **vivardhiṣate**.
PG118 records the lexical entry, intended action, selected pada and the
root-to-san decision, so both derivations remain available to later queries.

The four present-system lakāras supply **360 positions / 432 readings /
432 paths** across 40 complete nine-slot paradigms. The written-position
register rises from 26,807 to **27,167**. The two extra readings per slot
across the ten profiles belong to the A profiles of syand and kṛp.

Read the [admission rules](SOURCE_DECISIONS.md), [formation and endings](DERIVATIONS.md),
[complete word tables](PARADIGMS.md) and [operator construction](DOMAIN_AND_NATIVE.md).
The [machine-readable state](STATE.json) links each word to a typed stem branch,
slot and context. The [source fields](SOURCE_EXCERPTS.json), [dependency pins](SOURCE_PINS.json)
and [implementation handoff](COMPANION_REVIEW.md) identify what supports it.

This stage supplies a written specification for implementation. Python 3.12 checks
cover the static records and their agreement with the tables. Execution of the
grammar and certificate replay remain deferred.
