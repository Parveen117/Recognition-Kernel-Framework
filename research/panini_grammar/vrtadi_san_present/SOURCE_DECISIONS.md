# PG118 — Admission and source policy

Monty · 11 November 2026

## H01. The input describes an intended action and an act of desiring

Supply an exact Dhātupāṭha entry, its selected sense, and two events.
The action of the lexical root is the object of desire. Its actor is
identical to the desirer under 3.1.7. Choose the san construction and
supply unprefixed kartari and the controller of outer agreement.
The finite tense or mood qualifies the desire event. The question of
whether the desired action actually takes place is independent.

This packet counts laṭ, laṅ, loṭ and ordinary vidhiliṅ of the derived
dhātu, with the inherited outer context codes in STATE.json. Each
selected lexical root has a P and an A profile. An unknown actor
equation or unknown lexical identity gives HOLD. A known actor mismatch,
another formation or a prefix falls outside this domain. The alternatives
of 3.1.7 include a paraphrase, which is outside the single-word grid.
A later analyser may receive a word alone and return several candidates.

## H03. Determine iṭ at the root-to-san boundary

In P, 7.2.59 blocks iṭ for the first four and 7.2.60 for kṛp.
In A, the first three take short iṭ under 7.2.35. Syand and kṛp are
ūdit; 7.2.44 leaves both seṭ and aniṭ in A. On the P path the
specific prohibition overrides that option. Kāśikā and Nyāsa discuss
the force of the explicit count of four for syand. The short u marker
of the first three entries supplies no corresponding ūdit alternative.

Kāśikā reads the ending condition through the grammatical same-word
environment. In P loṭ second singular this prohibition still applies
when hi disappears. Its explicit *vivṛtsa tvam* and *cikḷpsa tvam*
are controls on the selected ordinary imperative. A search for a final
audible personal ending would give the wrong interface decision.

## H04. Distinguish explicit examples from tuple extensions

Kāśikā 1.3.92 and 7.2.59 give P vivṛtsati for vṛt and vṛdh,
and give P śiśṛtsati and sisyantsati. The A spelling vivardhiṣate
uses the inherited undoubled spelling policy for the printed
vivarddhiṣate. At kṛp, the Kaumudī procedure stored under **1.3.89**
explicitly gives cikḷpsati, cikalpiṣate and cikḷpsate. The san rules
governing that passage are 1.3.92–93 and 7.2.60. Nyāsa 7.2.60 and
Kaumudī support the vocalic ḷ reading despite inconsistent typography
in the Kāśikā electronic fields. The literal fields remain pinned.

For syand, the ūdit option and its P override give A sisyandiṣate and
sisyantsate. Apte's lexical entry also explicitly lists these alternatives.
The remaining person/number forms are written extensions through H08–H09.

## H05. Vṛh and earlier bare-root requests receive zero credit

The archive labels 1010852 vṛh ‘grow’ as a pāṭhabheda of **1010836
bṛh**. Its editorial note attributes the reading to the Mādhavīya
Dhātuvṛtti and says it is absent from Siddhāntakaumudī. Both records
give P and have an empty antargaṇa field. Kāśikā's explicit list of
five establishes the present stage's boundary independently. Vṛh has
no place in this five-root san grid. PG116's wider dyutādi aorist
question across editions still requires collation of the actual witnesses.

The two lexical records are archived as editorial controls. PG17/PG31
already cover selected **bare-root** future/conditional requests, and
PG116–117 cover basic luṅ. None is a new san word here. Wider senses,
kyaṣ, and the remaining outer lakāras require their own decisions.
