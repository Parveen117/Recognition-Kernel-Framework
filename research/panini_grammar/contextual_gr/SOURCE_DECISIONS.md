the supplied proposition/acknowledgement frame, with an agent and content.

The vad-specific human/joint-speaking restrictions do not survive the

## H03. Optional l targets the root's r before its verbal affix

Kāśikā 8.2.21 prescribes optional r→l at an **ajādi verbal-affix**
boundary: nigirati/nigilati, nigaraṇam/nigalanam and nigārakaḥ/nigālakaḥ.
Kaumudī gives girati/gilati and the four future witnesses
**garitā / garītā / galitā / galītā**. Nyāsa, Bālamanoramā and
Padamañjarī qualify the affix by the root prescription; this is not a
global replacement of every r standing before a printed vowel.

In the present system the actual śa interface is vowel-initial. In future
and conditional, iṭ makes sya/tās vowel-initial; both short i and long ī
satisfy the same guard. The option acts on the root's r, whether r accompanies
the present i substitution or future a-guṇa. It does not target prefix or
ending r. Thus **avagalitārau** keeps the r of the luṭ ending: the root's
l option does not license *avagalitālau*.

The source calls this vyavasthitavibhāṣā and distinguishes nominal **gala**
“neck” with obligatory l from **gara** “poison” with obligatory r. Those
lexicalized nominal controls are not additional finite branches. Its girau/
giraḥ discussion likewise excludes an unqualified nominal-ending transfer.
This packet selects the two readings at the declared finite verbal domains,
not a universal r/l option for every word connected with gṝ.

The source's ṇic-lopa/causal passive discussion retains a separate
sthānivad/ordering obligation. The present packet needs no deleted-ṇic
permission: its vowel-initial verbal interface is extant. No causal, passive,
yaṅ or nominal table is added here.
