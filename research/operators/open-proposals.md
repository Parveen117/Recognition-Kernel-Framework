# Proposals that still need a rule

A name can point to a good experiment before it specifies an operator. These examples explain why some prominent names in the [source register](sources.md) have no separate place in the count of 107.

## Tamil form, flow, coupling, phase and silence

The Tamil algebra note gives Φ the role of creating a form, Ω that of transforming it, Λ that of combining it with context, ⊙ that of phase and 𝓛 that of silence. To turn such a role into a map we need an example domain, a guard and a stated action. The written rule for joining consonant and vowel and the two noun paradigms meet that requirement. Their outputs can be inspected in Tamil script. [Algebra note](sources.md#s-t10) · [Uyirmei](grammar.md#uyirmei) · [Guarded inflection](grammar.md#tamil)

Two stronger equations require a choice. If $i$ is a central scalar and $\odot=i\mathcal L$, then $[\odot,\mathcal L]=i\mathcal L^2-\mathcal L i\mathcal L=0$. The same note proposes a nonzero commutator. Both statements cannot describe one algebra with that meaning of $i$. T56 gives separate charts with explicit actions and obtains different commutator readings. This makes an experimental question possible once a chart is fixed. [Tamil axioms and derived relations](sources.md#s-t10) · [T56 B1](../../theorum/56_canvas_operators_nativized_theorem.md)

## Hebrew letters, templates and energies

The root–template example needs three ordered consonants and an explicit pattern. Gematria needs a value dictionary and a summation rule. Each forgets different information. For example, swapping two letters leaves their value sum unchanged, whereas swapping the same letters between two positions of a root can change the written output. Assigning a quantum energy to each letter would additionally require a state preparation, observable, units and readings for independent words. These are different levels of specification. ## Cosmic continuum and a shared phase

The notes propose phase coherence between scales and a feedback network. If two oscillators run at the same rate but start a quarter turn apart, their quarter-turn offset persists under free evolution. A reference, an observation of the difference, and a corrective action make the offset measurable and adjustable. The catalogue gives a local feedback rule with a chosen gain. A cross-scale prediction would additionally require specified clocks or observables on each scale, the link between them, its calibration and an expected difference from independent evolution. [Network proposal](sources.md#s-c03) · [Scale hierarchy](sources.md#s-c01) · [Phase correction](phase.md#phase-feedback)

## A single universal operator, spectral claims and bridges

The ΩΛ proposal lists differential, transport and integral pieces. To check self-adjointness of their sum requires compatible domains, boundary conditions, the kernel of the integral term and a pairing. The Hodge operator itself has a defined action in the finite complex on its card. Identifying its gap with a four-dimensional Yang–Mills mass gap requires an additional construction. Likewise, the black-hole notes specify roles for collapse, the micro-core and their coupling. A quantitative collapse prediction needs initial data, an evolution map, a physical observable and units. The bridge notes identify desired correspondences while leaving concrete maps and the quantities they preserve to be specified. [ΩΛ proposal](sources.md#s-a21) · [Hodge and Yang–Mills note](sources.md#s-a32) · [Micro-core proposal](sources.md#s-a07) · [Bridging operators](sources.md#s-a31) · [Hodge example](fields.md#hodge)

The “future arrow” and “statistical layer” are more tractable when the inputs are explicit. A transition kernel plus an initial law yields a forecast. A sampling law plus a summary statistic yields a distribution of summaries. Different transition or sampling laws can give different answers on the same starting observations. The cards name the specified finite models and leave the choice of law visible. ## Two checks on proposed operator identities

- For a unitary involution $A$ on a finite Hilbert chart, $G\geq0$ implies $AGA=A^*GA\geq0$. If this also equals $-G$, every quadratic reading of $G$ vanishes, so $G=0$. A nonzero sign-changing sector therefore needs a signed form here. The repository already records this distinction. [Aghora source](sources.md#s-w051) · [T56 B4](../../theorum/56_canvas_operators_nativized_theorem.md)
- For the undeformed $D=q\,d/dq$ and the constant rotation $T_\phi f(q)=f(e^{i\phi}q)$, both $DT_\phi$ and $T_\phi D$ send $q^n$ to $n e^{in\phi}q^n$. They commute on polynomials, and on analytic power series in their common domain. A proposed nonzero commutator needs a different action or an additional term whose effect can be shown. [Arithmetic source](sources.md#s-w107) · [Logarithmic derivative](arithmetic.md#q-derivative)
