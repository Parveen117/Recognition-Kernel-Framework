# SP1 Sanskrit passage register

Author: Monty. Checked against the linked texts on 1 November 2026.

These short passages identify the philosophical motivation of SP1. The
English glosses below are our concise working glosses, not quotations from a
modern translation. They do not translate an ancient text into a quantum
law. [SOURCES.md](SOURCES.md) gives the mathematical lineage separately.

## Spandakārikā 1.1

> यस्योन्मेषनिमेषाभ्यां जगतः प्रलयोदयौ ।
> तं शक्तिचक्रविभवप्रभवं शंकरं स्तुमः ॥१.१॥

IAST: *yasyonmeṣanimeṣābhyāṃ jagataḥ pralayodayau / taṃ
śakticakravibhavaprabhavaṃ śaṃkaraṃ stumaḥ //*

Working gloss: praise of Śaṃkara, from whose opening and closing the world's
withdrawal and manifestation arise, and from whom the power of the circle of
energies proceeds. Kṣemarāja's commentary treats these aspects as inseparable;
this is not a specified mechanical oscillation or a frequency in hertz.

Source: [GRETIL text and Kṣemarāja commentary, VSpk_1.1](https://gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de/gretil/corpustei/transformations/html/sa_vasugupta-spandakArikA-comm.htm),
based on M. K. Shastri, Srinagar, 1925; input Oliver Hellwig. Its header records
the attribution as Vasugupta **or Kallaṭa Bhaṭṭa**, so SP1 does not settle that
authorship question. Devanagari here renders the source's IAST, including
anusvāra in *śaṃkaraṃ*; it is not a new critical edition.

## Śivasūtra III.9–11

| Passage | Sanskrit | IAST | Working gloss |
|---|---|---|---|
| III.9 | नर्तक आत्मा॥९॥ | nartaka ātmā | The self is the dancing actor. |
| III.10 | रङ्गोऽन्तरात्मा॥१०॥ | raṅgo'ntarātmā | The inner self is the stage. |
| III.11 | प्रेक्षकाणीन्द्रियाणि॥११॥ | prekṣakāṇīndriyāṇi | The senses are the spectators. |

Source: [Śivasūtravimarśinī, section III, aphorisms 9–11](https://mail.sanskrit-sanscrito.com/en/shiva-sutra-vimarshini-iii-1-11-trika-scriptures-non-dual-shaivism-of-kashmir/555),
Sanskrit text with Kṣemarāja's commentary, presented by Gabriel Pradīpaka.
These are yogic recognition passages, not Pāṇini's phonetic Śivasūtras.
SP1 borrows the question of observer access; it does not identify a detector
with the spiritual self or the senses with a matrix theorem.

## Pratyabhijñāhṛdaya 1–2

> चितिः स्वतन्त्रा विश्वसिद्धिहेतुः॥१॥
> स्वेच्छया स्वभित्तौ विश्वमुन्मीलयति॥२॥

IAST: *citiḥ svatantrā viśvasiddhihetuḥ / svecchayā svabhittau viśvam
unmīlayati.*

Working gloss: free consciousness grounds the world's manifestation; by its
own will it unfolds the world on its own ground. These concern consciousness
in Kṣemarāja's system, not a computational claim about a simulator.

Source: [GRETIL, Kṣemarāja, Pratyabhijñāhṛdaya, sūtras 1–2](https://gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de/gretil/1_sanskr/6_sastra/3_phil/saiva/pratyabu.htm),
encoded by Marino Faliero, July 1998. Devanagari is transcribed from its IAST.
The further discussion of retained impressions used as a research prompt is
in the commentary on sūtra 11; it is not quoted here as an affine memory law.

## Śivatāṇḍavastotra, verse 1, short excerpt

> चकार चण्डताण्डवं

IAST: *cakāra caṇḍatāṇḍavaṃ* — performed the fierce dance.

Source: [Sanskrit Documents, verse 1](https://sanskritdocuments.org/doc_shiva/shivtAND.html),
traditionally attributed to Rāvaṇa. The linked edition also records textual
variants. Its rhythm motivates a question about repeated protocols; no beat,
mantra syllable or divine activity is assigned a quantum gate by this text.

The Tamil Naṭarāja image is a separate iconographic source, linked in
[SOURCES.md](SOURCES.md). These contexts remain distinct. The modern affine
equations, assumptions and proofs belong to [THEOREM.md](THEOREM.md).
