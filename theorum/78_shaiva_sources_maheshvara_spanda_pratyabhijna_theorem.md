### T1 The Māheśvara sūtras are an interval code on the two-channel carrier

Read S1 as a token order: 43 sound tokens (42 sounds; *ha* occurs twice,
in sūtras 5 and 14) closed by 14 it-markers, nine plus five
(*navapañcavāram*). By 1.3.3 every marker is a consonant, by 1.3.9 it is
elided: the marker lives in the **memory channel** of theorum/58, the
initial sound in the **recognized channel**, and 1.1.71 is the map

```text
pratyāhāra(x, m) = the sound tokens from the first occurrence of x
                    through the end of the sūtra closed by m
```

— a pratyāhāra is a recognized initial plus a remembered, elided marker,
exactly the shape 1.1.62 gives theorum/58 T3 (*pratyayalope
pratyayalakṣaṇam*). Certified on this definition:

- `ik` (1.1.3, 6.1.77) is theorum/58's `IK = (i, u, ṛ, ḷ)`; theorum/58's
  ten-vowel carrier is the 1.1.69 savarṇa closure (long forms) of `ac` with
  `ṛ ḷ` omitted — omitted by theorum/58's own declaration, not by the sūtras
  (recorded boundary).
- The 42 pratyāhāras of the traditional Aṣṭādhyāyī list (41 names; `aṇ`
  twice) are all intervals of the token order with the two *ha* positions;
  `aṇ` is the only name denoting two sets (1.1.51 short, 1.1.69 long); the
  sets contain sound tokens only — no marker is ever a member.
- There are 305 (initial, later marker) position pairs and 294 distinct
  sound sets. Every coincidence (ten groups, eleven redundant pairs) comes
  from the fourteenth sūtra *ha l*, whose only sound is already present:
  marker *r* and marker *l* give the same set from any initial at or before
  the first *ha*, and *yal* equals *hal* as a set.
- `hal` = every consonant (34 tokens, 33 sounds); `yar = hal \ {h}`;
  `val = hal \ {y}` and ends on the **second** *ha*.

### T2 The repeated *ha* is necessary (the fourteenth beat)

**Theorem.** Let the six Aṣṭādhyāyī classes be read as sets of the 42
sounds with *ha* counted once:

```text
ac  (6.1.77)   = the nine vowels
aṭ  (8.4.2)    = ac ∪ {h, y, v, r}
yaṇ (6.1.77)   = {y, v, r, l}
yar (8.4.45)   = all consonants except h
val (6.1.66)   = all consonants except y
hal (1.3.3)    = all consonants
```

No linear order of the 42 sounds makes all six sets intervals. Hence any
interval code of these six classes with one position per sound is
impossible; a sound must be repeated, and the Māheśvara order repeats
exactly *ha*, which realizes all six (and all 42) as intervals.

**Proof.** (i) *Contraction lemma.* Partition the sounds into atoms by
their membership vector across the six sets. If a sound order makes every
set an interval, keeping one representative per atom still makes every
set an interval: the representatives lying between two representatives of
a set `S` represent atoms whose sounds lie inside the interval of `S`,
hence belong to `S`. So it suffices to search orders of atoms. (ii) There
are six atoms: the vowels; `h`; `y`; `{v, r}`; `l`; the remaining 28
consonants. The certificate enumerates all 720 atom orders: none makes all
six sets intervals. (iii) The written reason, visible in the search: `hal`
is an interval; `yar = hal \ {h}` and `val = hal \ {y}` are intervals, so
`h` and `y` are the two ends of `hal`; `aṭ` contains both ends and is an
interval, hence contains `l ∈ hal`, contradicting `l ∉ aṭ`. (iv) With the
two *ha* positions distinguished (`h₁` in `aṭ` and `hal`, `h₂` in `val`
and `hal`) an order exists, and the Māheśvara order itself is one. ∎

The global statement that fourteen markers is the least possible number
is a published classical theorem (W. Petersen, *A mathematical analysis
of Pāṇini's Śivasūtras*, J. Logic, Language and Information 13, 2004) and
is **not** re-derived here; what is certified is the local necessity of
the repetition, on the framework's own carrier and by exhaustive
computation.

S4 is cited as the Śaiva statement that the alphabet (*mātṛkā*) is a
ground of cognition and a wheel (*cakra*); nothing beyond the interval

### T4 The Pratyabhijñā recognition defect (MR-03 extension)

S5 defines recognition as the cognition "sa evāyam": a present cognition
identified with a remembered one. MR-03 already gives the carrier: a
history `γ` lifts to `(Ω_ω(γ), γ)`, and two histories with the same
projected composite are the same lifted morphism iff their memories agree
(Definition 6.1). Define the **recognition defect**

```text
δ(γ, η) = Ω_ω(γ) − Ω_ω(η)        for γ, η with the same composite in C.
```

**Theorem.** Let `C` be a commutative monoid of arrows (one object) and
`ω_B(b, a) = bᵀ B a` a bilinear memory cocycle (every bilinear form on a
commutative monoid satisfies the cocycle identity and is normalized).
Write `B = S + A` with `S` symmetric and `A` antisymmetric. Then

1. for two orderings `γ, η` of one multiset of elementary arrows,
   `δ_B(γ, η) = δ_A(γ, η)` and `δ_S(γ, η) = 0`: the symmetric part is
   recognition-blind, the defect is carried by the antisymmetric (turn)
   part alone;
2. for a normalized 1-cochain `α` and the coboundary change
   `ω' = ω + α(b∘a) − α(b) − α(a)` of MR-03 §8, `δ'(γ, η) = δ(γ, η)`
   whenever `γ, η` use the same arrow multiset; across different
   decompositions of one composite the defect shifts by
   `Σ_{a∈η} α(a) − Σ_{a∈γ} α(a)`.

**Proof.** For the path `a₁, …, a_k` the lift folds left, so
`Ω_B = Σ_{j<i} B(a_i, a_j)` by bilinearity. For symmetric `S` this is the
sum over unordered pairs, invariant under reordering; `Ω_B = Ω_S + Ω_A`
gives (1). For (2), each composition step contributes
`α(c_i) − α(c_{i−1}) − α(a_i)` with `c_i = a₁ + … + a_i`, which telescopes
to `α(c_k) − Σ_i α(a_i)`; the composite `c_k` is common to `γ` and `η`, so
the shift of `δ` is the difference of the arrow sums, zero for equal
multisets. ∎

Certified on arrows `(p, q)`, `p, q ≤ 2`, three cocycles (the turn cocycle
`q′p`, a symmetric one, a generic one), all multisets of up to four
generators and all their orderings: cocycle identity, normalization,
associativity (MR-03 3.1), (1), (2), and the shift formula on
`(1,0)+(1,0)` versus `(2,0)` (shift 2, as predicted). MR-03 §9's
calibration `ω(b, a) = ba` is symmetric, hence blind to reorderings; the
turn cocycle gives `δ(xy, yx) = 1` with equal composite `(1,1)` — the
explicit MR-03 Theorem 6.2 witness.

S6's vocabulary is recorded, not claimed: *bījāvasthāpana* (seed-deposit)
is the role of `Ω`, *vilāpana* (dissolution) the role of the forgetful
functor `π`, *vimarśana* (recognition) the test `δ = 0`. Recognition across
different elementary decompositions of one composite needs a fixed memory
gauge; that is an open obligation, not a theorem.
