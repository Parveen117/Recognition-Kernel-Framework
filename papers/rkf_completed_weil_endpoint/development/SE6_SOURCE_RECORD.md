## External primary sources and what is not new

Masatoshi Suzuki, [Aspects of the screw function corresponding to the
Riemann zeta function](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.03682), published in
Journal of the London Mathematical Society (2023),
[doi:10.1112/jlms.12785](https://doi.org/10.1112/jlms.12785).
The official article's Sections 4.2--4.3 record small-support positivity
and Yoshida's earlier positive finite-codimensional restrictions.
Those general principles are not claimed as new in SE6.

Suzuki, [Weil's quadratic form via the screw function,
arXiv:2606.09096v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09096v3), was checked at
its introductory normalization, prior-work discussion and Theorem 1.4.
It supplies current primary context for localized Weil operators and
their small-aperture behavior. SE6 does not consume its conditional
global spectral construction or infer RH from a finite aperture.

Rainer Andreas Mittermeier,
[Compact Weil Event Cells from Suzuki's Screw Function](https://zenodo.org/records/22687244),
10 September 2026. The author's primary repository abstract was inspected
for duplication: finite-window parity kernels, event-cell enclosures and
finite Gram certificates already have a developed route. The full paper
was not audited or imported. SE6 claims no new universal event-cell
method and does not consume that preprint's numerical results.

NIST DLMF [5.7.6](https://dlmf.nist.gov/5.7.E6), checked at the official
entry, underlies the positive digamma difference used in SE4.
SE6 evaluates only finite rational lower sums, retaining the omitted
positive terms. No approximate Gamma table is imported.

## Specific work supplied here

SE6 attaches the established aperture strategy to the corrected source
with explicit constants and a native joint observer. It proves:

- an exact operator pairing of prime events and odd completion poles;
- failure of every fixed prime truncation that keeps the full boundary
  on the unbounded carrier, plus a moving-window response identity;
- a quantitative moment mass bound and native reserve conversion;
- contraction on the whole odd aperture of radius 1/16;
- a floor on the infinite complement of fifteen native moment readings
  at radius 1/2, where prime 2 is active;
- an exact fifteen-dimensional Schur reduction with a proved positive
  pivot and an explicit complement-solve error bound.

The finite-dimensional Schur identity itself is standard and already
part of the framework. What is supplied now is a concrete source-bound
positive complement, not an assumed pivot. Its moment dimension is
fifteen; it is not the historical five-label endpoint.

The exact Python replay verifies the scalar budget. It does not test
the universal analytic proof, evaluate the Schur matrix, or supply the
unbounded-aperture limit. No external review or novelty priority is claimed.
